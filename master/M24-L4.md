# M24-L4 – Ultrasound, Pulse Echo and Acoustic Impedance {#L4}

**Course area:** M24 – Bachelor: Medical Physics and Imaging

**Level:** Bachelor · Elective

**Prerequisites:** M4: sound waves and reflection; M16: measurement uncertainty. [Previous lesson](M24-L3.md).

**Learning objective:** Derive the pulse-echo depth formula, calculate intensity reflection from impedances and state model limitations.

## Question and Observation {#S1}

An ultrasound transducer sends short acoustic pulses into a medium. Interfaces between different acoustic properties generate echoes whose travel time and intensity can be measured. A B-mode image assigns echoes to depths and beam directions; its brightness is therefore not a direct density map. Different echo delays also arise from different acoustic path lengths.

## Physical Model and Equations {#S2}

If a pulse travels to an interface and back at an assumed constant sound speed $c$, its round trip over time $t$ obeys $2d=ct$. Acoustic impedance $Z=\rho c$ is the product of mass density and sound speed. For a plane interface at normal incidence between lossless media, the fraction of reflected intensity is $R_I$ in the squared impedance expression. The amplitude reflection coefficient is not squared and may carry a sign.

$$
d=\frac{ct}{2},\qquad Z=\rho c,\qquad R_I=\left(\frac{Z_2-Z_1}{Z_2+Z_1}\right)^2.
$$

The frequently assumed soft-tissue value of about $1540\,\mathrm{m/s}$ is a useful teaching approximation, not a universal value for every tissue. Nonuniform sound speed produces depth errors. Shorter pulses improve axial resolution, whereas higher frequency often increases tissue attenuation. Lateral resolution depends strongly on aperture, focusing and diffraction. Real interfaces may also be oblique to the incident beam.

## Worked Example and Teaching Diagram {#S3}

For $t=100\,\mathrm{\mu s}$ and $c=1540\,\mathrm{m/s}$, the depth is $d=1540\cdot100\cdot10^{-6}/2=0.077\,\mathrm m=7.7\,\mathrm{cm}$. If $Z_1=1.50$ and $Z_2=1.65$ in the same MRayl units, $R_I=(0.15/3.15)^2\approx0.00227$, or about $0.227\%$. The diagram separates echo time from interface reflection.

## Self-Assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find the depth for $t=80\,\mathrm{\mu s}$ and $c=1540\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
2. What is $R_I$ if $Z_1=Z_2$?
3. Why can a constant assumed sound speed cause errors?

### Solutions

1. $d=1540\cdot80\cdot10^{-6}/2=0.0616\,\mathrm m=6.16\,\mathrm{cm}$.
2. $R_I=0$: in the ideal model there is no reflection due to an impedance jump.
3. Tissues exhibit different sound speeds; mapping time to distance with a single assumed speed may assign an incorrect depth.

## Validity Limits and Quality {#S5}

The intensity expression assumes plane waves, normal incidence and lossless interfaces. Multipath propagation, speckle, scattering, absorption and speed gradients require richer models.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Pulse-echo depth contains a factor of one half for the return path. Ideal normal-incidence reflection depends on acoustic impedance contrast.
