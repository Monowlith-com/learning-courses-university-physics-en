# M36-L5 – Heavy-Ion Collisions, Quark–Gluon Plasma and Flow {#L5}

**Course area:** M36 – Master: Particle Physics, Collision Experiments and Accelerators

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M27: many-body theory; M30: QCD.

**Learning objective:** Explain elliptic flow as a Fourier moment and evaluate a normalized angular model.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Ultrarelativistic nucleus–nucleus collisions can briefly produce strongly interacting matter at extreme energy density. Experiments such as ALICE study collective particle distributions and jet modifications to constrain quark–gluon plasma properties. An anisotropic distribution is a class of observable, not by itself a complete phase diagnosis.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

For azimuth $\varphi$ relative to event plane $\Psi_2$, define $v_2=\langle\cos 2(\varphi-\Psi_2)\rangle$. A simplified normalized single-harmonic model is $f(\varphi)=(1+2v_2\cos2(\varphi-\Psi_2))/(2\pi)$. Its integral from zero to $2\pi$ equals one. For this single-harmonic truncation $|v_2|\le1/2$ is needed for $f\ge0$; measured distributions use more harmonics and corrections.

$$
v_2=\left\langle\cos\bigl(2(\varphi-\Psi_2)\bigr)\right\rangle.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

Take $v_2=0.10$ and $\Psi_2=0$. The unnormalized angular density is proportional to $1.2$ at $\varphi=0$ and to $0.8$ at $\varphi=\pi/2$, a ratio of $1.5$. The polar curve is illustrative and is not derived from measured data.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What is $v_2$ for a perfectly uniform azimuthal distribution?
2. Compute $(1+2v_2)/(1-2v_2)$ at $v_2=0.2$.
3. Why does elliptic flow alone not uniquely establish quark–gluon plasma formation?

### Solutions

1. $v_2=0$.
2. $1.4/0.6=7/3\approx2.33$.
3. Other effects, system size and modelling assumptions must be controlled.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The illustration ignores event-plane fluctuations, nonflow correlations, collision geometry and higher harmonics. Hydrodynamic claims require multiple observables and model comparisons.
