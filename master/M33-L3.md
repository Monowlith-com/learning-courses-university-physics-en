# M33-L3 – Molecular Potentials, Morse Oscillator and Franck–Condon Factors {#L3}

**Course area:** M33 – Master: Atomic, Molecular and Optical Physics

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M12: potential wells and harmonic oscillators; M33-L1; basic molecular bonding.

**Learning objective:** Account consistently for electronic energy and nuclear repulsion and quantify Morse-oscillator anharmonicity.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

In molecules, heavy nuclei usually move more slowly than electrons. The Born–Oppenheimer approximation first solves the electronic problem at fixed nuclear positions. If the electronic Hamiltonian excludes internuclear repulsion $V_{nn}(R)$, the nuclear potential curve is $U(R)=E_{el}(R)+V_{nn}(R)$; if it includes that term, it must not be counted twice. Nuclear motion takes place on this potential surface while nonadiabatic couplings remain small.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

A bound diatomic molecule can be approximated by a Morse potential $U(R)=D_e[1-e^{-a(R-R_e)}]^2$ with zero at its minimum. $D_e$ denotes dissociation energy from that minimum and $R_e$ the equilibrium separation. Levels measured from the minimum are $E_v=\hbar\omega_e(v+1/2)-[\hbar\omega_e]^2(v+1/2)^2/(4D_e)$ while bound states exist. Adjacent level spacings decrease with $v$. For vibronic transitions, the Franck–Condon factor $q_{v\prime v}=|\langle\chi^{(e)}_{v\prime}|\chi^{(g)}_v\rangle|^2$ describes vibrational overlap alone; without electronic transition dipoles, populations, and selection rules it is not a complete line intensity.

$$
E_v=\hbar\omega_e\left(v+\frac12\right)-\frac{(\hbar\omega_e)^2}{4D_e}\left(v+\frac12\right)^2.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For a constructed example take $\hbar\omega_e=0.20\,\mathrm{eV}$ and $D_e=5\,\mathrm{eV}$. The dimensionless anharmonicity is $x_e=\hbar\omega_e/(4D_e)=0.01$. For the $v=0\rightarrow1$ transition, $E_1-E_0=\hbar\omega_e(1-2x_e)=0.196\,\mathrm{eV}$. A harmonic oscillator would predict $0.200\,\mathrm{eV}$. ## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find $x_e$ for $\hbar\omega_e=0.10\,\mathrm{eV}$ and $D_e=2.5\,\mathrm{eV}$.
2. What is $E_1-E_0$ with those values?
3. When should $V_{nn}$ be added separately to $E_{el}$?

### Solutions

1. $x_e=0.01$.
2. $0.10(1-0.02)=0.098\,\mathrm{eV}$.
3. Only when internuclear repulsion is not already included in $E_{el}$.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The Morse model is useful for an individual bond coordinate, not general polyatomic motion or crossings between electronic surfaces. The exact Morse-level expression applies only to the finite set of bound states. Translating Franck–Condon overlaps into intensity estimates further assumes an approximately coordinate-independent electronic transition dipole (Condon approximation).
