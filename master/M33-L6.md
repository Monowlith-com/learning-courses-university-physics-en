# M33-L6 – Atomic Traps and Quantized Motion in a Harmonic Potential {#L6}

**Course area:** M33 – Master: Atomic, Molecular and Optical Physics

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M12: harmonic oscillator; M33-L5: cooling; M31: model limits.

**Learning objective:** Compute vibrational energies and the ground-state spatial width of a harmonic atom trap.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Optical dipole traps, magnetic traps, and ion traps confine particles in spatial potentials. Near a stable minimum, a second-order Taylor expansion often suffices. Along one coordinate this produces a harmonic oscillator. Its quantized motion differs from a perfectly stationary classical atom: even the ground state has finite spatial spread.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

With $V(x)\simeq V_0+\tfrac12m\omega^2(x-x_0)^2$, the energies are $E_n-V_0=\hbar\omega(n+1/2)$ and the ground-state spatial standard deviation is $\sigma_x=\sqrt{\hbar/(2m\omega)}$. Here $x_0$ is the trap minimum, and the quadratic approximation applies near that minimum. Greater particle mass or stronger confinement (larger $\omega$) reduces the quantum spatial spread.

$$
\sigma_x=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}},\qquad E_n-V_0=\hbar\omega\left(n+\frac12\right).
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

Consider a hypothetical atom of mass $m=1.44\cdot10^{-25}\,\mathrm{kg}$ with $\omega/(2\pi)=1.0\,\mathrm{kHz}$. Then $\sigma_x=\sqrt{\hbar/(2m\cdot2\pi\cdot1000)}\approx0.241\,\mathrm{\mu m}$. The energy spacing is $\Delta E=h\cdot1.0\,\mathrm{kHz}$. The diagram sketches a parabolic trap and a qualitative ground-state probability density.

*Quantization of trapped motion leaves a finite spatial spread even in the ground state.*

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. How does $\sigma_x$ change if the trapping frequency is quadrupled?
2. What is $(E_0-V_0)/(\hbar\omega)$?
3. Why might the harmonic model fail far above the bottom of the trap?

### Solutions

1. The spatial width is halved.
2. $(E_0-V_0)/(\hbar\omega)=1/2$.
3. Higher-order potential terms, finite trap depth, and couplings become important.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

Not every trap is harmonic; coupled three-dimensional potentials require normal modes and principal axes. A classical thermal ensemble does not have the same spatial distribution as the quantum ground state. The numbers are fabricated, not an experimental trap prescription.
