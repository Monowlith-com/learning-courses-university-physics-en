# M21-L6 – Remote Sensing with Active and Passive Sensors {#L6}

**Course area:** M21 – Bachelor: Geophysics and Climate

**Level:** Bachelor · Elective

**Prerequisites:** M6: electromagnetic waves; M7: optics; M17: inverse models. [Previous lesson](M21-L5.md).

**Learning objective:** Differentiate active and passive sensors and calculate a basic time-of-flight range.

## Question and Observation {#S1}

Remote sensing infers properties of a target without physical contact. Passive instruments detect available radiation, such as reflected sunlight or thermal emission. Active systems transmit a signal and receive its scattered return. Even a precise recorded signal does not directly provide a unique material property: calibration, a forward model and often inversion are needed.

## Physical Model and Equations {#S2}

For an ideal monostatic radar, the propagation delay $\Delta t$ between transmitted and received pulses gives
$$
R=\frac{v\Delta t}{2},
$$
where $v$ is signal speed. The factor of one half accounts for the outward and return paths. In air or vacuum one often approximates $v\approx c$, but other media or substantial atmospheric delays require corrections. A passive observation can be written schematically as $y=\mathcal F(x)+\varepsilon$, with target properties $x$, radiative forward operator $\mathcal F$ and noise $\varepsilon$. Inverting $y$ does not necessarily give a unique $x$.

## Worked Example and Teaching Diagram {#S3}

With a round-trip delay $\Delta t=100\,\mathrm{\mu s}$ and the educational approximation $v=c=3.0\cdot10^8\,\mathrm{m/s}$, the radar range is $R=15\,\mathrm{km}$. ## Self-Assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find the range if $\Delta t=40\,\mathrm{\mu s}$ and $v=3.0\cdot10^8\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
2. Why is there a factor of $1/2$ in the radar range formula?
3. Give one reason two physically different surfaces might have the same measured radiance.

### Solutions

1. $R=3.0\cdot10^8\cdot40\cdot10^{-6}/2=6000\,\mathrm m$.
2. The recorded time includes propagation to the target and back.
3. Different combinations of temperature, emissivity, illumination or atmospheric effects can give similar observations.

## Validity Limits and Quality {#S5}

The range formula assumes one identifiable return along a simple path; multipath, resolution, speckle, scattering cross section and refraction are neglected. Passive radiance alone is not a unique material fingerprint.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Active sensors create their own signals; passive sensors receive existing radiation. Both require physical interpretation and uncertainty analysis.
