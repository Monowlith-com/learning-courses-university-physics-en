# M39-L2 – Fickian Diffusion, Reactions and the Damköhler Number {#L2}

**Course area:** M39 – Master: Biophysics, Nonequilibrium and Complex Systems

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M39-L1; M31: differential equations and numerical boundary problems · [Previous lesson](M39-L1.md).

**Learning objective:** Combine flux and conservation for first-order consumption and interpret the reaction penetration length.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

In tissues or catalytic membranes, species diffuse while undergoing chemical conversion. Their spatial concentration obeys a local conservation law. A unique concentration profile requires an initial condition, boundary conditions and a reaction law.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

Fick\'s first law is $\mathbf J=-D\nabla c$. Combining $\partial_tc=-\nabla\!\cdot\!\mathbf J-kc$ with uniform $D$ gives $\partial_tc=D\nabla^2c-kc$. In the stationary one-dimensional half-space $x\ge0$, with $c(0)=c_0$ and $c(\infty)=0$, the solution is $c(x)=c_0\exp(-x/\ell)$, $\ell=\sqrt{D/k}$. The dimensionless Damköhler number $\mathrm{Da}=kL^2/D=(L/\ell)^2$ compares diffusion and reaction timescales.

$$
\frac{\partial c}{\partial t}=D\nabla^2c-kc,\qquad\ell=\sqrt{\frac Dk},\quad\mathrm{Da}=\frac{kL^2}{D}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For a constructed medium choose $D=10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m^2s^{-1}}$, $k=0.01\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$ and $L=0.20\,\mathrm{mm}$. Thus $\ell=0.10\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\mathrm{Da}=4$. At $x=\ell$, $c/c_0=e^{-1}\approx0.368$. The figure shows the stationary exponential profile and its penetration length.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. How does $\ell$ change when reaction rate $k$ becomes four times larger?
2. What is the relative concentration at $x=2\ell$?
3. What else is needed for a time-dependent solution?

### Solutions

1. Penetration length is halved.
2. $e^{-2}\approx0.135$.
3. Initial concentration profile and boundary conditions.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The exponential solution changes for finite layers with different boundary conditions, saturable enzyme kinetics, spatially varying $D$ or appreciable advection. The stated $\mathrm{Da}$ uses first-order kinetics; other definitions are used for different reaction models.
