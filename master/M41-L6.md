# M41-L6 – Pump–Probe Measurements, Timing Jitter and Instrument Response {#L6}

**Course area:** M41 – Master: Photonics and Ultrafast Optics

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M41-L3; M41-L5; M32: pump–probe instrumentation.

**Learning objective:** Combine pump, probe and jitter widths into an idealized temporal instrument response.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

In pump–probe measurements, a pump pulse launches a transient state and a delayed probe records its response. Repeated measurements scan the delay. Temporal resolution is not simply the duration of the shorter pulse: both pulse envelopes and their arrival-time jitter contribute.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

For independent Gaussian pump and probe intensity profiles with independent Gaussian timing jitter, the temporal cross-correlation is again Gaussian. Variances add. Because the conversion from Gaussian standard deviation to FWHM is the same for all three, their FWHM widths may also be combined in quadrature. This is not generally valid for asymmetric shapes or correlated jitter.

$$
\Delta t_{\mathrm{IRF}}\simeq\sqrt{\Delta t_{\mathrm{pump}}^2+\Delta t_{\mathrm{probe}}^2+\Delta t_{\mathrm{jitter}}^2}\quad\text{(independent Gaussian FWHM terms)}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

With pump FWHM of $50\,\mathrm{fs}$, probe FWHM of $70\,\mathrm{fs}$ and independent jitter of $30\,\mathrm{fs}$, one obtains $\Delta t_{\mathrm{IRF}}=\sqrt{8300}\,\mathrm{fs}\approx91.1\,\mathrm{fs}$. The figure sketches excitation and delayed probing alongside a broadened instrument response.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What is the width when timing jitter vanishes?
2. Why not simply add the pulse durations arithmetically?
3. When does quadratic addition of FWHM widths fail?

### Solutions

1. $\sqrt{50^2+70^2}\approx86.0\,\mathrm{fs}$.
2. Independent Gaussian contributions convolve, adding variances rather than widths.
3. Non-Gaussian shapes, correlations or systematic timing errors require a different response model.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

This model is not an empirical timing budget. Nonlinear sample dynamics, instabilities, pulse chirp, synchronization and shot-resolved corrections can shape the measured signal.
