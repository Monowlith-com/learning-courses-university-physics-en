# M30-L7 – Non-Abelian gauge theories and quantum chromodynamics {#L7}

**Course area:** M30 – Master: Quantum field theory and the Standard Model

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M30-L6: QED; M30-L5: renormalization group · [Previous lesson](M30-L6.md).

**Learning objective:** Explain color charge and interpret the one-loop running of the strong coupling qualitatively and numerically.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Quarks have three color states, and eight gluons mediate the strong interaction. Its gauge group is SU(3). Unlike electromagnetic U(1), gluons themselves carry color charge and interact with each other. This changes how the coupling depends on energy scale.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

In the perturbative region and with fixed $n_f$ active quark flavors, the leading running coupling is $\alpha_s(Q^2)=4\pi/[\beta_0\ln(Q^2/\Lambda^2)]$, where $\beta_0=11-2n_f/3$ for SU(3). Positive $\beta_0$ yields asymptotic freedom: the coupling becomes smaller at larger $Q$. This is a one-loop approximation; $\Lambda$ depends on convention and renormalization scheme. Perturbation theory ceases to be reliable at low $Q$. Confinement and hadronization require additional nonperturbative physics.

$$
\alpha_s(Q^2)=\frac{4\pi}{(11-\tfrac23 n_f)\ln(Q^2/\Lambda^2)}\qquad(Q\gg\Lambda).
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

As an **illustrative** calculation with $n_f=3$ and $Q/\Lambda=10$, one obtains $\beta_0=9$ and $\alpha_s\approx4\pi/[9\ln(100)]\approx0.303$. At $Q/\Lambda=20$, the same approximation gives $\alpha_s\approx0.233$. The curve shows the decrease, not precision experimental coupling values for any particular experiment.

![One-loop curve of the decreasing strong coupling versus Q divided by Lambda in the perturbative region.](../../../../media/course/U_PHY_228_EN.svg)

*Strong coupling becomes weaker at higher energy scales in the perturbative regime.*

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What is $\beta_0$ for $n_f=3$?
2. How does $\alpha_s$ behave as $Q$ increases?
3. Why must the one-loop curve not be extrapolated to $Q=\Lambda$ as a measured result?

### Solutions

1. Nine.
2. It decreases in the perturbative region.
3. The denominator tends to zero there and perturbation theory becomes unreliable.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The numerical comparison holds $n_f=3$ fixed across both illustrative scales; physical flavor thresholds require matching. Confinement cannot be derived from the simple one-loop expression and is a nonperturbative property.
