# M12-L7 – Variational Principle and Ritz Method {#L7}

**Course area:** M12 – Bachelor: Advanced Quantum Mechanics

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** Normalized states, expectation values and single-variable optimization; M12-L1–L2. [Previous lesson](M12-L6.md).

**Learning objective:** Derive the ground-state upper bound and optimize a trial state.

## Estimating Without an Exact Wavefunction {#S1}

For a complicated potential the exact ground-state wavefunction is often unknown. The variational principle nevertheless provides a controlled energy estimate using normalized admissible trial states. Instead of solving the full differential equation, one minimizes an expectation value within a chosen family. A simple family may have just one width parameter. The resulting estimate is not a lower bound and does not automatically measure wavefunction accuracy. The primary statement concerns the lowest energy of a Hamiltonian bounded below.

## Spectral Theorem and Upper Bound {#S2}

Expand a normalized trial state as $|\phi\rangle=\sum_n c_n|n\rangle$ in an eigenbasis of the Hamiltonian. Then

$$
\langle\phi|H|\phi\rangle=\sum_n |c_n|^2 E_n\ge E_0
$$

This weighted mean cannot lie below the lowest eigenvalue. For unnormalized states use the Rayleigh quotient $\mathcal E[\phi]=\langle\phi|H|\phi\rangle/\langle\phi|\phi\rangle$. Minimization over a finite linear subspace gives the Ritz eigenvalue problem. Enlarging the allowed trial space cannot increase its minimum. The argument requires a well-defined bounded-below Hamiltonian and trial functions in an appropriate quadratic-form domain.

## Example: Gaussian Trial State for the Oscillator {#S3}

For the one-dimensional oscillator $H=p^2/(2m)+m\omega^2x^2/2$ choose the normalized Gaussian $\phi_\alpha(x)=(2\alpha/\pi)^{1/4}e^{-\alpha x^2}$ with $\alpha>0$. One finds $\langle x^2\rangle=1/(4\alpha)$ and $\langle p^2\rangle=\hbar^2\alpha$, hence

$$
\mathcal E(\alpha)=\frac{\hbar^2\alpha}{2m}+\frac{m\omega^2}{8\alpha}
$$

Solving $d\mathcal E/d\alpha=0$ yields $\alpha=m\omega/(2\hbar)$ and $\mathcal E_{\min}=\hbar\omega/2$. This ansatz happens to reproduce the exact ground state because its true wavefunction is Gaussian. The figure plots energy against trial width and marks the minimum.

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Can a normalized trial energy lie below $E_0$?
2. What happens to $\mathcal E(\alpha)$ as $\alpha\to0$ or $\alpha\to\infty$?
3. Give the minimizing $\alpha$ for $m=\omega=\hbar=1$.

### Solutions

1. No, provided the state is admissible and $H$ well defined.
2. Energy diverges in both limits.
3. $\alpha=1/2$ and $\mathcal E_{\min}=1/2$.

## Optimization and Methodological Pitfalls {#S5}

Variational quality depends strongly on the trial family. Boundary conditions, symmetry, normalizability and singularities all matter. An inflexible family may give a valid but poor upper bound; excited-state estimates need orthogonality constraints relative to lower states. Numerical Ritz methods use many basis functions and solve generalized eigenvalue problems $H\mathbf c=E S\mathbf c$. A small energy error does not guarantee equally small errors in all other observables.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

The variational principle supplies controlled upper bounds on ground-state energy within admissible trial spaces.
