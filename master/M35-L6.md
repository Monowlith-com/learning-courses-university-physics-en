# M35-L6 – SSH Chain, Chiral Symmetry and Winding Number {#L6}

**Course area:** M35 – Master: Advanced Solid-State Physics and Materials Science

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M35-L1: tight binding; M34: quantum information.

**Learning objective:** Distinguish zero and nonzero winding in a specified SSH block $q(k)$.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

The Su–Schrieffer–Heeger chain contains two sublattice sites per unit cell with alternating couplings $v$ and $w$. In a suitable basis the spinless model has chiral symmetry, producing energies in pairs $\pm E(k)$. Its complex off-diagonal hopping traces a closed curve as crystal momentum traverses the Brillouin zone. Whether that curve encloses the origin distinguishes gapped bulk phases as long as the protecting symmetry is respected.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

We explicitly choose $H(k)=\begin{pmatrix}0&q(k)^*\\q(k)&0\end{pmatrix}$ with $q(k)=v+w e^{ika}$ for real $v,w>0$. Its energies are $E_\pm(k)=\pm|q(k)|$. With this basis and orientation, $\nu=(1/2\pi)\int_{-\pi/a}^{\pi/a}\!\partial_k\arg q(k)\,dk=1$ if $w>v$ and $0$ if $v>w$. The band gap is $2|v-w|$ and closes at $v=w$. Reversing the sign of the exponential reverses the orientation and sign of $\nu$, but not the criterion of whether the origin is enclosed.

$$
\nu=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{-\pi/a}^{\pi/a}\partial_k\arg q(k)\,dk.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

At $v=0.6\,\mathrm{eV}$, $w=1.0\,\mathrm{eV}$, one has $\nu=1$ and a bulk gap of $0.8\,\mathrm{eV}$. At $v=1.2\,\mathrm{eV}$ with the same $w$, one finds $\nu=0$ and a gap of $0.4\,\mathrm{eV}$. The two circles in complex $q$ space have radius $w$ and centers at real $v$. Edge modes additionally depend on termination and symmetry.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. When does the SSH gap close for positive $v,w$?
2. What is the bulk gap for $v=0.7$ and $w=1.0\,\mathrm{eV}$?
3. Why does $\nu=1$ not guarantee boundary states against arbitrary perturbations?

### Solutions

1. At $v=w$.
2. $0.6\,\mathrm{eV}$.
3. Protection depends, among other things, on symmetry-preserving perturbations and termination.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The definition requires a specified basis, unit cell and chiral symmetry. Another unit-cell convention can change the winding-number convention; bulk and boundary must be compared consistently. Symmetry-breaking terms can remove protection.
