# M41-L4 – Free-Electron Lasers and Undulator Resonance {#L4}

**Course area:** M41 – Master: Photonics and Ultrafast Optics

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M36: accelerators; M41-L3: pulse physics; M32: XFEL instrumentation.

**Learning objective:** Compute the on-axis undulator resonance wavelength in an ideal electron-beam model.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

A free-electron laser uses relativistic electrons and a periodic magnetic field rather than a bound atomic laser transition. The undulator drives small transverse electron oscillations. Phase matching between emitted radiation and electron motion selects a wavelength; laser gain requires additional physical processes.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

For an ideal planar undulator, on-axis fundamental radiation in the ultrarelativistic limit satisfies $\lambda_r=\lambda_u(1+K^2/2)/(2\gamma^2)$. Here $\lambda_u$ is the magnetic period, $K$ the dimensionless undulator parameter and $\gamma$ the electron Lorentz factor. Off-axis radiation adds an angular contribution. The resonance condition alone guarantees neither microbunching nor exponential FEL gain.

$$
\lambda_r\simeq\frac{\lambda_u}{2\gamma^2}\left(1+\frac{K^2}{2}\right)\qquad\text{(planar, on axis, fundamental)}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For illustrative parameters $\lambda_u=0.030\,\mathrm m$, $\gamma=10^4$ and $K=1$, the result is $\lambda_r=2.25\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm m=0.225\,\mathrm{nm}$. Doubling $\gamma$ at fixed $K$ would quarter the resonance wavelength. The diagram shows magnetic periods, electron trajectory and a schematic emitted wave.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find $\lambda_r$ for $K=0$ with all other parameters fixed.
2. How does $\lambda_r$ scale with Lorentz factor at constant $K$?
3. Why does the resonance formula not prove laser amplification?

### Solutions

1. $0.150\,\mathrm{nm}$.
2. $\lambda_r\propto\gamma^{-2}$.
3. Gain additionally requires field–beam coupling, beam quality and appropriate longitudinal phase evolution.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

Real facilities involve finite emittance, energy spread, harmonics, SASE or seeded operation, and nontrivial beam transport. The numerical example is not an operating specification of any XFEL facility.
