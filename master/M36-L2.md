# M36-L2 – Unstable Particles, Lifetime and Decay Law {#L2}

**Course area:** M36 – Master: Particle Physics, Collision Experiments and Accelerators

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M13: statistical distributions; M30: interactions.

**Learning objective:** Distinguish mean lifetime from half-life and compute an idealized survival probability.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Unstable particles decay randomly; an individual lifetime cannot be predicted. An ensemble with a constant decay hazard follows an exponential law. Mean lifetime and half-life are distinct descriptors. At colliders, production, flight time and detection also depend on particle motion and detector selection.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

For a constant decay hazard $\lambda=1/\tau$ in proper time, $dN/dt=-\lambda N$. Integration gives $N(t)=N_0e^{-t/\tau}$ and survival probability $P_{\mathrm{surv}}(t)=e^{-t/\tau}$. The half-life satisfies $P_{\mathrm{surv}}(t_{1/2})=1/2$, hence $t_{1/2}=\tau\ln2$. Relativistic particles require proper time to be distinguished from laboratory time through time dilation.

$$
P_{\mathrm{surv}}(t)=e^{-t/\tau},\qquad t_{1/2}=\tau\ln 2.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

In a constructed example take $\tau=2\,\mu\mathrm{s}$. After a proper time $t=2\,\mu\mathrm{s}$, the average surviving fraction of a large ensemble is $e^{-1}\approx36.8\%$; the half-life is about $1.386\,\mu\mathrm{s}$. The diagram describes a normalized ensemble, not a measurement of a particular particle.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Compute $P_{\mathrm{surv}}$ after $2\tau$.
2. Find the half-life for $\tau=4\,\mathrm{ns}$.
3. Does this proper-time expression directly use laboratory time for a relativistic beam?

### Solutions

1. $e^{-2}\approx0.1353$.
2. $4\ln2\approx2.773\,\mathrm{ns}$.
3. No; relativistic time dilation must be incorporated.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The exponential model assumes a stationary hazard without important selection effects. The number surviving is random; $N(t)$ gives an expectation, not a deterministic particle list.
