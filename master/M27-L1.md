# M27-L1 – Time-dependent perturbation theory and transition rates {#L1}

**Course area:** M27 – Master: Advanced quantum theory

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M12: stationary perturbation theory; M11: two-level systems; M26: operators. [Previous lesson](M26-L7.md).

**Learning objective:** Derive the first-order transition amplitude, identify resonance and explain why perturbation theory must be distinguished from coherent Rabi dynamics.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

A time-varying electromagnetic field can excite an atom between discrete energy levels. Stationary perturbation theory alone cannot describe the transition: explicit time dependence must enter the state evolution. Resonance depends on both the driving frequency and the energy separation.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

Write $H(t)=H_0+V(t)$ with $H_0|n\rangle=E_n|n\rangle$ and expand the interaction-picture state in unperturbed eigenstates. To first order, for $f\neq i$, the amplitude is the time integral of $V_{fi}(t)$ weighted by the Bohr frequency $\omega_{fi}=(E_f-E_i)/\hbar$. A harmonic drive $V_{fi}(t)=\hbar\Omega\cos(\omega t)$ has two exponential components with denominators proportional to $\omega_{fi}\pm\omega$. Near $\omega=\omega_{fi}$ the slowly rotating component dominates. With additional assumptions about a continuum and time coarse-graining, the treatment yields Fermi's golden rule; an isolated coherent pair instead requires a Rabi description.

$$
c_f^{(1)}(t)=-\frac{i}{\hbar}\int_0^t\!V_{fi}(t\prime)e^{i\omega_{fi}t\prime}\,\mathrm dt\prime,\qquad P_f\simeq |c_f^{(1)}|^2.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For a resonantly driven two-level system with $V(t)=\hbar\Omega\cos(\omega t)\,\sigma_x$ and $\omega=\omega_0$, the rotating-wave approximation gives $H_{\mathrm{eff}}=\hbar\Omega\sigma_x/2$ and $P_e(t)=\sin^2(\Omega t/2)$. With $\Omega=2\pi\cdot1000\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$ and $t=0.25\,\mathrm{ms}$, one obtains $P_e=\sin^2(\pi/4)=0.5$. A first-order expansion extrapolated to long times can yield an impossible $P>1$; the figure shows bounded coherent Rabi oscillations.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What condition determines resonance between two levels?
2. Find $P_e$ at $\Omega t=\pi$ in the resonant Rabi model.
3. Why is Fermi's golden rule not automatically applicable to a single closed two-level system?

### Solutions

1. The drive frequency must be near $\omega_{fi}=(E_f-E_i)/\hbar$.
2. $P_e=\sin^2(\pi/2)=1$.
3. The golden rule assumes, among other things, a suitable continuum and temporal coarse-graining; an isolated pair exhibits coherent oscillations.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The rotating-wave approximation requires $\Omega\ll\omega_0$ and near resonance. Very short pulses, large detuning, strong fields and open systems need more complete models. Probabilities must remain normalized.
