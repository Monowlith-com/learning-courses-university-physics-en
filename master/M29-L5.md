# M29-L5 – Einstein equations and the Newtonian limit {#L5}

**Course area:** M29 – Master: General relativity and cosmology

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M29-L4: curvature; M3: Newtonian mechanics; M26: tensor notation · [Previous lesson](M29-L4.md).

**Learning objective:** Identify terms in the Einstein equations and connect the weak static limit to Poisson’s equation.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

A geometric account of gravity requires field equations: matter and energy shape spacetime, while the metric determines how freely moving bodies travel. Einstein’s equations relate the Einstein tensor to stress–energy. A cosmological constant is allowed as an additional geometric term.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

With signature $(-,+,+,+)$ and the Riemann convention of M29-L4, write $G_{\mu\nu}=R_{\mu\nu}-(1/2)Rg_{\mu\nu}$. For slowly moving matter, $T_{00}$ is related to energy density $\rho c^2$. Covariant conservation $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu}=0$ is compatible with the field equations through the Bianchi identity. In a static weak field with negligible pressure, $g_{00}\simeq-(1+2\Phi/c^2)$. With $\Lambda=0$, the suitable limit recovers Poisson’s equation $\nabla^2\Phi=4\pi G\rho$ with **mass density** $\rho$.

$$
R_{\mu\nu}-\frac12Rg_{\mu\nu}+\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}=\frac{8\pi G}{c^4}T_{\mu\nu}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For a uniform-density sphere with $\rho=5500\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$, the Newtonian interior field grows linearly: $|g(r)|=4\pi G\rho r/3$. At $r=1000\,\mathrm m$ and $G\approx6.6743\times10^{-11}\,\mathrm{m^3/(kg\,s^2)}$, it equals $1.54\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The graphic shows the piecewise field strength of an idealized uniform sphere; this is a Newtonian limit, not a full solution of Einstein’s equations.

![Plot of the gravitational field magnitude of an ideal sphere: linear increase inside and inverse-square decrease outside.](../../../../media/course/U_PHY_078_EN.svg)

*The piecewise field is a consequence of the Newtonian limit for constant density.*

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What does $T_{\mu\nu}$ represent?
2. What are the units of $\nabla^2\Phi$, and do they match $4\pi G\rho$?
3. How does $|g(r)|$ scale with radius inside a uniform sphere?

### Solutions

1. The stress–energy tensor of matter and nongravitational fields.
2. $\mathrm{s^{-2}}$; $G\rho$ has the same unit.
3. It doubles when $r$ doubles, provided both points are inside the sphere.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The Newtonian limit needs weak nearly static fields, slow matter, and negligible relativistic pressure. The field equations alone do not specify a solution without a source model, boundary conditions, and coordinate choices.

## Summary and outlook {#S6}

The Einstein equations connect matter to curvature and recover Newtonian gravity in the appropriate regime. M29-L6 studies a spherical vacuum solution. [Next lesson](M29-L6.md).
