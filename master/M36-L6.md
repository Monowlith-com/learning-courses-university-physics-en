# M36-L6 – Transverse Beam Optics and RMS Emittance {#L6}

**Course area:** M36 – Master: Particle Physics, Collision Experiments and Accelerators

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M31: linear systems and numerics; M32: accelerator instrumentation.

**Learning objective:** Calculate geometric RMS emittance from centered phase-space moments and explain conditional invariance under linear transport.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

A charged particle beam is described transversely by position $x$ and small slope $x^{\prime}\simeq p_x/p_z$. In the paraxial thin-focusing-lens model the slope changes as $x^{\prime}_{\mathrm{out}}=x^{\prime}_{\mathrm{in}}-x/f$, while $x$ is unchanged at the lens. A beam is a phase-space distribution rather than a single trajectory.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

For centered variables $X=x-\langle x\rangle$ and $X^{\prime}=x^{\prime}-\langle x^{\prime}\rangle$, the covariance matrix is $\Sigma=\begin{pmatrix}\langle X^2\rangle&\langle XX^{\prime}\rangle\\\langle XX^{\prime}\rangle&\langle X^{\prime2}\rangle\end{pmatrix}$. Geometric RMS emittance is $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rms}}=\sqrt{\det\Sigma}$ (e.g. mm mrad). Under linear transport $\Sigma_{\mathrm{out}}=R\Sigma_{\mathrm{in}}R^T$, hence $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{out}}=|\det R|\varepsilon_{\mathrm{in}}$. A thin lens has $R=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\-1/f&1\end{pmatrix}$ and determinant one.

$$
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rms}}=\sqrt{\langle X^2\rangle\langle X^{\prime2}\rangle-\langle XX^{\prime}\rangle^2}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

Let $\sigma_x=2\,\mathrm{mm}$, $\sigma_{x^{\prime}}=1\,\mathrm{mrad}$ and correlation $\rho=0.6$. Then $\langle XX^{\prime}\rangle=\rho\sigma_x\sigma_{x^{\prime}}=1.2\,\mathrm{mm\,mrad}$ and $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rms}}=2\sqrt{1-0.6^2}=1.6\,\mathrm{mm\,mrad}$. ## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find RMS emittance at $\rho=0$ with the same spreads.
2. How does $\det R=-1$ change the magnitude of RMS emittance for a linear map?
3. Why is geometric emittance not automatically invariant during acceleration?

### Solutions

1. $2\,\mathrm{mm\,mrad}$.
2. Its magnitude is unchanged because $|\det R|=1$.
3. Because $x^{\prime}\simeq p_x/p_z$ scales with longitudinal momentum; normalized emittance needs separate assumptions.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

This result describes a decoupled linear 2D phase plane in fixed coordinates without significant nonlinear forces, space charge or scattering. Acceleration, coupling and nonlinear dynamics prevent a blanket invariance claim.
