# M29-L4 – Riemann curvature and geodesic deviation {#L4}

**Course area:** M29 – Master: General relativity and cosmology

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M26: differential geometry; M29-L3: covariant derivatives · [Previous lesson](M29-L3.md).

**Learning objective:** Separate coordinate-independent curvature from connection coefficients and calculate curvature on a sphere.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

When nearby freely falling worldlines accelerate relative to one another, tidal gravity is present. The mathematical object is the Riemann tensor. In flat spacetime it vanishes even if individual Christoffel symbols happen to be nonzero in a chosen chart.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

The commutator of covariant derivatives on a vector $V^\rho$ defines curvature for a torsion-free connection by $[\nabla_\mu,\nabla_\nu]V^\rho=R^\rho{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}V^\sigma$ (this fixes our sign convention). Geodesic deviation relates the curvature tensor to the relative motion of nearby freely falling trajectories. An accessible two-dimensional example is the surface of a sphere of radius $R$: its Gaussian curvature is $K=1/R^2$ everywhere. A triangle bounded by three great-circle arcs has angular excess $A/R^2$, with $A$ its surface area.

$$
K=\frac{1}{R^2},\qquad \alpha+\beta+\gamma-\pi=KA=\frac{A}{R^2}\quad\text{(sphere, great-circle triangle)}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For a sphere of radius $R=2\,\mathrm m$, $K=0.25\,\mathrm{m^{-2}}$. For a great-circle triangle with $A=2\,\mathrm{m^2}$, the angular excess is $0.5\,\mathrm{rad}\approx28.65^\circ$. The figure sketches a spherical triangle bounded by great-circle arcs, not a planar unfolding.

![Sphere with two schematic great-circle arcs and a highlighted spherical triangle; three angles are marked.](../../../../media/course/U_PHY_063_EN.svg)

*The angular excess of a great-circle triangle measures positive spherical curvature.*

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Compute $K$ for a spherical surface with $R=5\,\mathrm m$.
2. Find the angular excess for $A=1\,\mathrm{m^2}$ on a sphere with $R=2\,\mathrm m$.
3. Why does $\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\nu}\neq0$ not prove physical curvature?

### Solutions

1. $0.04\,\mathrm{m^{-2}}$.
2. $0.25\,\mathrm{rad}$.
3. Connection coefficients depend on coordinates; curvature is described tensorially.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The sphere illustrates positive spatial surface curvature, not four-dimensional Lorentzian spacetime directly. Spacetime geodesic deviation requires timelike or null directions together with a consistent sign convention.

Curvature is observable independently of coordinates. M29-L5 couples geometry to matter and energy through the Einstein equations. [Next lesson](M29-L5.md).
