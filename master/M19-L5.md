# M19-L5 – Research Questions, Literature and Blinded Analysis {#L5}

**Course area:** M19 – Bachelor: Advanced Experimental Methods and Research Practice

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** M18: laboratory reports; M19-L1: measurement model; basic statistics. [Previous lesson](M19-L4.md).

**Learning objective:** Separate source evidence from original findings and justify a prespecified analysis plan.

## Question and Model {#S1}

Research begins with a question narrow enough to determine what data could answer it. Literature review first establishes terms and methods: primary papers, textbooks, lab manuals and reviews serve different evidential purposes. Record author, title, version, passage and publication date for important claims. An item merely listed in a bibliography does not verify every equation in a course. A sound investigation also specifies competing hypotheses and possible artefacts.

## Equations and Assumptions {#S2}

A prespecified analysis plan states the primary outcome, quality thresholds, exclusion rules and test before outcomes are seen. Blinding can reduce selective interpretation induced by knowing group labels; it cannot substitute for instrument calibration. Hypotheses developed after looking at data are acceptable as exploratory analyses when labelled accordingly. For $m$ independent tests with false-alarm probability $\alpha$ each, a simplified teaching model gives
$$
P_{\ge 1}=1-(1-\alpha)^m.
$$
Independence is an assumption, not a universal property of scientific studies.

## Worked Example and Diagram {#S3}

If $m=20$ independent null hypotheses are tested at $\alpha=0.05$, the chance of at least one false positive is $1-0.95^{20}\approx0.642$. One significant finding selected from twenty exploratory tests must not be reported as if it came from one predeclared test. The figure shows a simple process: question, preregistered plan, acquisition, blinded analysis, unblinding and interpretation. The calculation is illustrative, not an empirical rate for a research discipline.

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Why is a literature hit not automatically direct verification of a formula?
2. For 20 independent tests with $\alpha=0.05$, what is the chance of at least one false alarm?
3. How do blinded and exploratory analyses differ?

### Solutions

1. Topic relevance does not replace checking the exact passage and assumptions.
2. About $64.2\%$ assuming independence.
3. Blind means hidden group labels; exploratory means hypotheses developed after inspecting data and labelled as such.

## Limits and Quality Control {#S5}

Good reporting distinguishes planned analyses, post-hoc decisions, negative findings and unperformed measurements. A research-data recommendation does not imply that every raw dataset may be published openly; privacy, contracts and disciplinary constraints can apply. Changes introduced after the results become visible must be declared as deviations. The diagram is an educational workflow, not a claim that all research follows one procedure.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Plans, results and post-hoc interpretation must be separately documented.
