# M20-L2 – Stellar Spectra, Temperature and Radial Velocity {#L2}

**Course area:** M20 – Bachelor: Astrophysics and Cosmology

**Level:** Bachelor · Elective

**Prerequisites:** M7: spectroscopy; M10: electromagnetic waves; M11: atomic transitions. [Previous lesson: Astrometry, Parallax and the Distance Ladder](M20-L1.md).

**Learning objective:** Use spectral signatures to estimate temperature and radial motion within the model assumptions.

## Question and Observation {#S1}

A star is not a monochromatic source. Its continuum depends strongly on temperature and radiation transport, while absorption and sometimes emission lines are superimposed. Line profiles and strengths respond to composition, excitation, ionisation, surface gravity and motion. Spectral classes O, B, A, F, G, K and M broadly order stars by temperature-sensitive spectral signatures. An isolated feature is not an unambiguous chemical fingerprint without an atmosphere model.

## Physical Model and Equations {#S2}

For an ideal blackbody, Wien's displacement law gives the wavelength of maximum spectral radiance per wavelength interval:
$$
\lambda_{\max}T=b,\qquad b\approx2.898\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m\,K}.
$$
For radial speeds small compared with the speed of light, a spectral-line shift satisfies
$$
\frac{\Delta\lambda}{\lambda_0}\approx\frac{v_r}{c}.
$$
Positive $v_r$ denotes recession and hence a redshift in this convention. At high speeds, or with gravitational or cosmological shifts, this non-relativistic Doppler approximation is insufficient. Blackbody peaks in wavelength and frequency representations do not occur at corresponding coordinates.

## Worked Example and Teaching Diagram {#S3}

## Self-Assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find $\lambda_{\max}$ for $T=4000\,\mathrm K$.
2. A line at $656.3\,\mathrm{nm}$ is observed at $656.9\,\mathrm{nm}$. Estimate $v_r$.
3. Why does line strength alone not uniquely measure elemental abundance?

### Solutions

1. $\lambda_{\max}\approx725\,\mathrm{nm}$.
2. $v_r\approx c(0.6/656.3)\approx274\,\mathrm{km/s}$ (receding).
3. Excitation, ionisation, pressure and radiative transfer also control line strength.

## Validity Limits and Quality {#S5}

Wien's law provides a characteristic temperature, not a complete stellar-atmosphere model. Radial velocities require multiple spectral features and calibration against instrumental drift. Inferring redshift and abundance is therefore a model-based process rather than a simple reading of colour or one line.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Spectroscopy connects thermal continua, atomic lines and motion, but it requires explicit modelling assumptions.
