# M43-L7 – Research Capstone: External Validation and Transparent Reporting {#L7}

**Course area:** M43 – Master: Model Assessment and Scientific Research Practice

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M43-L1–L6: prediction, calibration, design, uncertainty and model assessment.

**Learning objective:** Complete a small physical-model validation on pre-separated test data and document the limits of the conclusion.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

A research report turns a sequence of decisions into an auditable result: question, assumptions, measurement protocol, raw data, calibration, processing, code version, uncertainty budget, model checks and justified conclusions. **External validation** here means that the evaluation data were not used for fitting the model or choosing its parameters afterward. Transparent reporting includes negative results, alternative models and deviations from the original plan.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

For a predesignated holdout set, a simple error metric in the original measurement units is the mean absolute error: $\mathrm{MAE}=n^{-1}\sum_i|y_i-\hat y_i|$. Unlike a training residual, it estimates prediction error outside fitting if the split was genuinely independent. It does not by itself reveal the error distribution, measurement uncertainty, bias or generalization to new regimes.

$$
\mathrm{MAE}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|y_i-\widehat y_i|.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

Four **synthetic, prespecified holdout** readings are $y=(2,3,4,5)$ units, while the fixed model predicts $\hat y=(2.2,2.9,4.1,4.8)$. Absolute errors are $(0.2,0.1,0.1,0.2)$, giving $\mathrm{MAE}=0.15$ units. The scatter plot compares prediction and observation against the ideal diagonal. Both the data and the claimed holdout independence are **teaching assumptions**, not an audited research study.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Calculate $\mathrm{MAE}$ for absolute errors $(0.2,0.4)$.
2. Why is adjusting a model after seeing test errors problematic?
3. What must accompany a reproducible final report?

### Solutions

1. $\mathrm{MAE}=0.3$ in the measurement units.
2. The test set then influences model selection, so the reported metric is no longer an independent evaluation.
3. Protocol, raw data or documented access conditions, calibration, code/environment, uncertainties, deviations and sources.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

Four artificial points and one error metric cannot validate a research program. Errors may be correlated; a defensible claim needs uncertainty analysis, physical scope, safety and ethics review and possibly independent replication. FAIR principles require documented accessibility, not universal public release of all raw data.

## Summary and outlook {#S6}
The final lesson connects model predictions, measurement protocols, independently held-out test data and the limits of validation.
