# M42-L1 – Plasma Parameters, Debye Shielding and Quasineutrality {#L1}

**Course area:** M42 – Master: Interdisciplinary Physics and Research Frontiers

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M6: electrostatics; M22: plasmas; M36: charged particles. [Previous lesson](M41-L7.md).

**Learning objective:** Calculate the electron Debye length and explain when a quasineutral bulk description is appropriate.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

A plasma contains mobile positive and negative charge carriers. An electrostatic perturbation redistributes especially the light electrons and is spatially screened. **Quasineutral** does not mean exact charge cancellation in every microscopic cell: on scales much larger than the Debye length, positive and negative bulk charge densities are often close to equal. Near interfaces and sheaths, this approximation fails.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

For classical, nearly isotropic Maxwellian electrons with number density $n_e$ and temperature $T_e$, linearizing their Boltzmann response and Poisson’s equation gives $\lambda_{De}=\sqrt{\varepsilon_0 k_B T_e/(n_e e^2)}$. If $k_BT_e$ is specified in electronvolts, convert that energy into joules before substituting SI quantities. Ions can contribute to collective screening; the present formula is for the **electron Debye length**.

$$
\lambda_{De}=\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon_0 k_B T_e}{n_e e^2}},\qquad \lambda_{De}\propto\sqrt{\frac{T_e}{n_e}}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For a **synthetic** plasma with $n_e=10^{18}\,\mathrm{m^{-3}}$ and $k_BT_e=10\,\mathrm{eV}$, the formula gives $\lambda_{De}=23.5\,\mathrm{\mu m}$. Screening therefore has a characteristic length, not a sharply terminating boundary. Quadrupling temperature at fixed density doubles the Debye length. The figure sketches an exponentially attenuated potential in one direction, not a plasma simulation.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What is $\lambda_{De}$ at four times the electron density with fixed temperature?
2. Why can quasineutrality break down in a plasma sheath?
3. How does $\lambda_{De}$ change under $T_e\to9T_e$?

### Solutions

1. It halves to $11.75\,\mathrm{\mu m}$.
2. Sheaths can contain substantial charge separation and strong potential gradients.
3. The characteristic length triples.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The screening expression assumes a thermal electron response and weak perturbations suitable for linearization. Nonequilibrium plasmas, strong magnetization and multiple populations need additional assumptions. The graphic is one-dimensional and schematic.
