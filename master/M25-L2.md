# M25-L2 – Material properties, band gaps and light–matter interaction {#L2}

**Course area:** M25 – Bachelor: Interdisciplinary applications and scientific practice

**Level:** Bachelor · elective

**Prerequisites:** M6: electromagnetic fields; M7: optics; M14: band theory and semiconductors. [Previous lesson](M25-L1.md).

**Learning objective:** Connect material properties with electronic states and estimate an optical absorption threshold from a band gap.

## Guiding question and observation {#S1}

What distinguishes a transparent insulator from a semiconductor detector? Microscopic bonding and accessible electronic states determine how matter responds to charge and light. In a crystal, many atomic states form allowed energy bands. A schematic band diagram separates valence-band states from higher conduction-band states by a forbidden energy interval. A band gap is an energy, not a spatial width. Real materials also have defects, transition probabilities and temperature-dependent occupancies.

## Physical model and equations {#S2}

Photon energy is $E_\gamma=h\nu=hc/\lambda$. At an idealized direct band edge, photons with sufficient energy can promote an electron; the threshold wavelength follows from $E_\gamma=E_g$. Lower-energy photons may still be absorbed through other mechanisms, including defect states, while above-threshold photons need not be completely absorbed. Indirect transitions involve momentum exchange with phonons. Optical response also depends on refractive index, absorption coefficient, reflectance, frequency and microstructure.

$$
E_\gamma=h\nu=\frac{hc}{\lambda},\qquad \lambda_g=\frac{hc}{E_g},\qquad E_g=E_{\mathrm c}-E_{\mathrm v}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For an idealized direct gap of $E_g=1.50\,\mathrm{eV}$, using $hc\approx1240\,\mathrm{eV\,nm}$ gives a threshold wavelength of about $827\,\mathrm{nm}$. At $\lambda=620\,\mathrm{nm}$ photon energy is about $2.00\,\mathrm{eV}$, greater than $E_g$. This energy calculation by itself does not establish a particular absorption efficiency. The band diagram shows only energy ordering and a possible optical transition.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Calculate the threshold wavelength for $E_g=2.0\,\mathrm{eV}$.
2. What are the units of a band gap?
3. Why does $h\nu>E_g$ not fully predict absorption?

### Solutions

1. $\lambda_g\approx620\,\mathrm{nm}$.
2. Energy, for example joules or electronvolts.
3. Transition probabilities, state occupation, momentum, defects and sample thickness also matter.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The single-particle band picture omits excitons and complex electron correlations. The drawn bands are not a measured spectrum or a specific material’s band structure.

## Summary and outlook {#S6}

Material properties connect microscopic states to measurable optical and electrical response; the band gap is a central parameter but not the only one.
