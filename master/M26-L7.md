# M26-L7 – Methods bridge: eigenmodes, stochastic correlation and model selection {#L7}

**Course area:** M26 – Master: Advanced mathematical methods and model foundations

**Level:** Master · methodological specialisation

**Prerequisites:** M26-L1 to M26-L6; M13: ensemble statistics; M17: numerical validation. [Previous lesson](M26-L6.md).

**Learning objective:** Derive the time correlation and power spectrum of a stationary linear process and articulate the assumptions required when transferring mathematics between physical models.

## Guiding question and observation {#S1}

Advanced physics draws on several mathematical tools, but these are not interchangeable labels. Operator theory describes admissible spectra, differential geometry organizes local descriptions, variational methods control approximations and stochastic processes model fluctuations. Each technique has its own domain of validity.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

For a stationary Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process with drift $-\gamma X_t$ and diffusion strength $D$, the autocovariance is $C(\tau)=(D/\gamma)e^{-\gamma|\tau|}$. Define the two-sided power spectral density as $S(\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}C(\tau)e^{-i\omega\tau}d\tau$. Integrating the two exponential half-lines yields a Lorentzian. Fourier conventions matter: other definitions can move factors of $2\pi$. This correlation spectrum is not automatically the energy spectral measure of an arbitrary quantum operator.

$$
C(\tau)=\frac D\gamma e^{-\gamma|\tau|},\qquad S(\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}C(\tau)e^{-i\omega\tau}\,\mathrm d\tau=\frac{2D}{\gamma^2+\omega^2}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For $\gamma=1\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$ and $D=0.25\,u^2\mathrm{s^{-1}}$, one finds $C(0)=0.25\,u^2$. At $\tau=1\,\mathrm s$, $C(1\,\mathrm s)=0.25e^{-1}\,u^2\approx0.092\,u^2$. The zero-frequency spectral density is $S(0)=0.50\,u^2\mathrm s$; at $\omega=\gamma$ it is half this value. The figure compares normalized correlation and normalized power spectrum.

![Two plots: normalized autocorrelation decaying with lag and a Lorentzian power spectrum versus frequency.](../../../../media/course/U_PHY_162_EN.svg)

*Time-domain relaxation and frequency-domain width are Fourier-related descriptions of the same stationary dynamics.*

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find $C(0)$ for $D/\gamma=0.4\,u^2$.
2. Find the positive half-power frequency of $S(\omega)$ for any $\gamma>0$.
3. Must an eigenbasis of a self-adjoint quantum operator also diagonalize the Fokker–Planck generator?

### Solutions

1. $0.4\,u^2$.
2. $\omega_{1/2}=\gamma$.
3. No; the operators generally act on different spaces, and the Fokker–Planck generator is not necessarily self-adjoint in ordinary $L^2$.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The Lorentzian applies to the stationary linear process with the specified two-sided Fourier convention. Nonlinear dynamics, colored noise, nonstationary data and different operator spaces require additional analysis.

## Summary and outlook {#S6}

The module closes with a verifiable bridge from differential equations to fluctuation spectra. Advanced specializations must still recheck domains, symmetries, boundary conditions and empirical validation. [Next lesson](M27-L1.md).
