# M30-L3 – Propagators and microcausality {#L3}

**Course area:** M30 – Master: Quantum field theory and the Standard Model

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M30-L2: field operators; M10: light cones · [Previous lesson](M30-L2.md).

**Learning objective:** Distinguish time ordering from spacelike separation and interpret the causal content of a field commutator.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Quantum field theory must account for both field correlations and relativistic causal structure. In perturbation theory, a propagator encodes correlations. It is not the probability of a classical signal being sent from A to B.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

For a free scalar field let $D_F(x-y)=\langle0|T\phi(x)\phi(y)|0\rangle$. The symbol $T$ orders operators in time; in momentum space the Feynman expression is proportional to $i/(p^2-m^2+i\varepsilon)$. Time ordering alone does not establish causality. Locality requires bosonic field operators at spacelike-separated points to commute. Under our signature, spacelike means $s^2=(\Delta t)^2-|\Delta\mathbf x|^2<0$. Thus a possibly nonzero time-ordered correlator cannot be used for faster-than-light signalling.

$$
[\phi(x),\phi(y)]=0\quad\text{for}\quad (x-y)^2<0.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For two events with $\Delta t=2$ and $|\Delta x|=3$ in units with $c=1$, $s^2=4-9=-5$. They are spacelike separated. The figure places an event outside the light cone; the field commutator does not allow a causal signal between these events.

![Spacetime light-cone diagram in ct–x coordinates showing a spacelike-separated event outside the cone.](../../../../media/course/U_PHY_152_EN.svg)

*Spacelike separation rules out a light-speed or slower causal signal between the marked events.*

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Is $\Delta t=4,\,\Delta x=3$ timelike or spacelike?
2. What does $T$ mean in $D_F$?
3. Why does $D_F\neq0$ outside the light cone not imply faster-than-light messaging?

### Solutions

1. $s^2=16-9=7>0$: timelike.
2. It orders the field operators in time.
3. Microcausality restricts signalling through the commutator, not by requiring every time-ordered correlator to vanish.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The displayed commutator relation concerns local bosonic fields; fermionic fields require the corresponding anticommutators. The schematic shows 1+1 dimensions rather than complete 3+1-dimensional spacetime.
