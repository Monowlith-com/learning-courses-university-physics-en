# M27-L8 – Identical particles and second quantization {#L8}

**Course area:** M27 – Master: Advanced quantum theory

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M13: Fermi and Bose statistics; M27-L3: spin; M26: operator algebra. [Previous lesson](M27-L7.md).

**Learning objective:** Construct Fock states, distinguish bosonic and fermionic occupation and express a one-body Hamiltonian in second-quantized form.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

For identical quantum particles, assigning persistent labels to individual particles is generally not physical. Second quantization instead tracks occupations of single-particle modes. It therefore provides the natural starting point for many-body physics and quantum field theory.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

Each mode $i$ has creation and annihilation operators $a_i^\dagger$ and $a_i$. Bosons satisfy canonical commutators $[a_i,a_j^\dagger]=\delta_{ij}$; fermions obey anticommutators $\{a_i,a_j^\dagger\}=\delta_{ij}$ and $\{a_i^\dagger,a_j^\dagger\}=0$. The number operator is $n_i=a_i^\dagger a_i$. A noninteracting system with single-particle energies $\varepsilon_i$ has $H_0=\sum_i\varepsilon_i n_i$. For fermions, $(a_i^\dagger)^2=0$, so a single mode has occupation 0 or 1. Bosonic modes may have any nonnegative integer occupation.

$$
H_0=\sum_i\varepsilon_i a_i^\dagger a_i,\qquad [a_i,a_j^\dagger]_{\mathrm B}=\delta_{ij},\qquad \{a_i,a_j^\dagger\}_{\mathrm F}=\delta_{ij}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

Two distinct fermionic modes have $\varepsilon_1=1\,\mathrm{eV}$ and $\varepsilon_2=2\,\mathrm{eV}$. The state $a_1^\dagger a_2^\dagger|0\rangle$ has total energy $3\,\mathrm{eV}$. Interchanging the creation operators changes the sign of the vector, although a physical state is not specified by arbitrary particle labels. ## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Which occupations are permitted for a single fermionic mode?
2. Find the energy when distinct modes of energies $\varepsilon_1=1\,\mathrm{eV}$ and $\varepsilon_2=2\,\mathrm{eV}$ are both occupied.
3. Why does $(a_i^\dagger)^2$ vanish for fermions?

### Solutions

1. $n_i\in\{0,1\}$.
2. $E=\varepsilon_1+\varepsilon_2=3\,\mathrm{eV}$.
3. From $\{a_i^\dagger,a_i^\dagger\}=0$ one obtains $2(a_i^\dagger)^2=0$.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The single-particle mode basis must be complete and orthonormal or generalized appropriately. Interactions introduce terms containing several creation and annihilation operators. The particle species and required symmetry of the complete state determine the algebra.

## Summary and outlook {#S6}

Second quantization gives the language of multiparticle systems. It connects directly to the next module on nonequilibrium and many-body physics.
