# M23-L4 – Quantum Wells, Density of States and Size Quantisation {#L4}

**Course area:** M23 – Bachelor: Applied Physics, Photonics and Nanotechnology

**Level:** Bachelor · Elective

**Prerequisites:** M12: stationary Schrödinger equation; M14: semiconductors. [Previous lesson](M23-L3.md).

**Learning objective:** Derive the $L^{-2}$ scaling of well energies and assess their size and limitations.

## Question and Observation {#S1}

In a semiconductor layer only a few nanometres wide, electron motion across the layer can become strongly confined. Its perpendicular motion then occupies discrete subband energies, while motion within the layer is approximately free. Quantum wells appear in lasers, detectors and nanoscale transport studies. An infinite potential barrier is a teaching model rather than a literal band-edge profile for all heterostructures.

## Physical Model and Equations {#S2}

Take $V(x)=0$ for $0<x<L$ and infinitely high walls. The time-independent equation $-\hbar^2\psi''/(2m^*)=E\psi$ admits $\psi(x)=A\sin(kx)+B\cos(kx)$. The conditions $\psi(0)=\psi(L)=0$ yield $B=0$ and $kL=n\pi$, hence

$$
E_n=\frac{n^2\pi^2\hbar^2}{2m^*L^2},\qquad n=1,2,3,\ldots\quad\text{(infinite 1D well)}.
$$

Here $m^*$ is an effective band mass, not automatically the free-electron mass. Finite walls lower bound-state energies and allow evanescent penetration. With one quantised direction and two nearly free in-plane directions, the simple parabolic-band 2D density of states forms steps at subband thresholds. Wires and quantum dots reduce the number of free directions further and possess different spectral structure.

## Worked Example and Teaching Diagram {#S3}

Using the free-electron mass $m_e$ and $L=5.0\,\mathrm{nm}$, the ideal infinite well gives $E_1\approx0.0150\,\mathrm{eV}$. Halving the width to $2.5\,\mathrm{nm}$ raises $E_1$ by a factor of four to about $0.0602\,\mathrm{eV}$. A smaller effective mass raises the quantisation energy. The plot shows the well and the first three normalised standing-wave shapes.

## Self-Assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. How does $E_1$ change if $L$ is tripled?
2. Find $E_2/E_1$ and $E_3/E_1$ in this model.
3. Why must $m^*$ not silently be replaced with $m_e$?

### Solutions

1. $E_1$ decreases by a factor of nine.
2. $E_2/E_1=4$ and $E_3/E_1=9$.
3. Effective mass follows the material-specific band curvature and may differ substantially from free-electron mass.

## Validity Limits and Quality {#S5}

The infinite-wall, constant-mass, parabolic-band and non-interacting assumptions are restrictive. Real nanostructures may display excitons, tunnelling, roughness and nonparabolic bands. The drawn wavefunctions are idealised calculations.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Confinement makes energies discrete through boundary conditions. The squared inverse-width law is a useful first estimate for nanoscale devices.
