# M38-L6 – Four-Dimensional Variational Assimilation and Covariances {#L6}

**Course area:** M38 – Master: Geophysics, Atmospheric Physics and Climate Models

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M31: inverse problems and numerics; M32: measurement errors; M38-L5 · [Previous lesson](M38-L5.md).

**Learning objective:** Interpret a quadratic 4D-Var objective under linearization and compute a scalar state correction.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Numerical weather prediction requires a coherent initial model state. Observations are incomplete and noisy; a background state from a prior forecast is also uncertain. Variational assimilation combines them under assumed error statistics. In 4D-Var observations at several times constrain a model trajectory within a finite window.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

For an increment $\delta x$, background-error covariance $B$ and observation-error covariances $R_i$, let the innovation be $d_i=y_i-H_iM_i(x_b)$. With tangent-linear mapping $U_i=H_i\,M_i^\prime$, minimize $J(\delta x)=\tfrac12\delta x^TB^{-1}\delta x+\tfrac12\sum_i(d_i-U_i\delta x)^TR_i^{-1}(d_i-U_i\delta x)$. If covariances are positive definite, the Hessian of this quadratic approximation is $\nabla^2J=B^{-1}+\sum_iU_i^TR_i^{-1}U_i$. This is an incremental mathematical framework, not a claim about a specific current ECMWF implementation.

$$
J(\delta x)=\frac12\delta x^TB^{-1}\delta x+\frac12\sum_i(d_i-U_i\delta x)^TR_i^{-1}(d_i-U_i\delta x).
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

In an original one-dimensional one-time example set $x_b=0$, $y=2$, $B=4$, $R=1$ and $U=1$. Then $J(x)=x^2/8+(2-x)^2/2$, minimized at $x_a=1.6$ with Hessian $J\prime\prime=1/4+1=1.25$. The analysis variance implied by this quadratic model is $1/J\prime\prime=0.8$. The chart shows two weighted terms and their sum.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What is the minimum for $B=R=1$, $x_b=0$ and $y=2$?
2. How does larger $R$ at fixed $B$ affect observation weight?
3. Why do $B$ and $R$ need positive definiteness for the displayed inverses?

### Solutions

1. $x_a=1$, the weighted mean.
2. The observation receives less weight.
3. To guarantee inverses and positive quadratic curvature in this simple model.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

Operational assimilation includes nonlinear models, error correlations, observation QC and approximate covariances. The given Hessian belongs only to the linearized quadratic problem. No current operational assimilation window or ensemble size is assumed.
