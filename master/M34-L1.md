# M34-L1 – Qubits, the Bloch Sphere and Projective Measurement {#L1}

**Course area:** M34 – Master: Quantum Information and Quantum Technologies

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M12: Hilbert spaces and quantum states; M33-L2: two-level systems.

**Learning objective:** Parameterize a normalized qubit state and calculate its two computational-basis measurement probabilities.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

A classical bit has one of two states. A pure qubit is instead specified by normalized complex amplitudes whose squared magnitudes yield probabilities. The Bloch sphere represents pure states on its surface and mixed states within its volume. A global phase cannot be observed, whereas the relative phase between basis amplitudes can matter physically.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

Fixing the global phase, every pure qubit is parameterized by polar angle $\theta\in[0,\pi]$ and azimuth $\phi\in[0,2\pi)$. A measurement in the $\{|0\rangle,|1\rangle\}$ basis follows the Born rule: $P(0)=\cos^2(\theta/2)$ and $P(1)=\sin^2(\theta/2)$. The Bloch vector is $\mathbf r=(\sin\theta\cos\phi,\sin\theta\sin\phi,\cos\theta)$. The density operator $\rho=(I+\mathbf r\cdot\boldsymbol\sigma)/2$ also accommodates mixed states with $|\mathbf r|\le1$.

$$
|\psi\rangle=\cos\frac{\theta}{2}|0\rangle+e^{i\phi}\sin\frac{\theta}{2}|1\rangle.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For $\theta=\pi/3$ and $\phi=0$, the probabilities are $P(0)=3/4$ and $P(1)=1/4$. The Bloch vector makes a 60° angle with the positive z-axis. The figure shows the selected xz cross-section and the z projection. For 400 identically prepared ideal trials, one expects about 300 zeros, not an exactly prescribed count.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What are the probabilities at $\theta=\pi/2$?
2. What Bloch z component follows from $P(0)=3/4$?
3. Why does the Bloch surface not represent every mixed state?

### Solutions

1. $P(0)=P(1)=1/2$.
2. $r_z=P(0)-P(1)=1/2$.
3. Mixed states generally have a shorter Bloch vector in the sphere’s interior.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The sphere represents one two-level system and does not replace the tensor product for multiple qubits. A measurement axis and basis must be specified. Frequencies fluctuate even for perfect state preparation.
