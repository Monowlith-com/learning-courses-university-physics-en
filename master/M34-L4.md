# M34-L4 – Grover Search and Quantum Amplitude Amplification {#L4}

**Course area:** M34 – Master: Quantum Information and Quantum Technologies

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M34-L3: quantum gates; M31: numerical simulation.

**Learning objective:** Compute the ideal success probability of Grover search for a single marked item.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Unstructured search among N candidates requires many classical checks in the worst case. Grover’s quantum algorithm uses an oracle phase mark and inversion about the mean (diffusion) to amplify target amplitudes. Its advantage concerns ideal oracle queries and does not automatically include state preparation, data loading or hardware overhead.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

For $M$ equally marked items among $N$ possibilities, define $\sin\theta=\sqrt{M/N}$. After $r$ complete Grover iterations, the target probability is $P_r=\sin^2((2r+1)\theta)$. An appropriate integer iteration count is roughly $r\approx\frac\pi4\sqrt{N/M}$ when $M\ll N$; excessive iterations lower success again. This rotation assumes an ideal oracle and diffuser.

$$
P_r=\sin^2\!\left((2r+1)\arcsin\sqrt{\frac{M}{N}}\right).
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For $N=4$ and $M=1$, $\theta=\arcsin(1/2)=\pi/6$. Initially $P_0=1/4$; after one iteration $P_1=\sin^2(\pi/2)=1$. After two iterations it falls back to $1/4$. The plotted ideal curve illustrates periodic amplitude rotation.

![Grover success probability for four items and one marked solution over zero to three iterations.](../../../../media/course/U_PHY_121_EN.svg)

*Amplitude amplification peaks after one iteration.*

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What is $P_0$ for $M=1,N=4$?
2. How many complete Grover iterations are ideal here?
3. Why does $O(\sqrt N)$ not imply a guaranteed total machine runtime?

### Solutions

1. $1/4$.
2. One iteration.
3. Oracle implementation, state preparation, gate noise and sampling add overhead.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The formula does not provide a quadratic speedup for arbitrary problems. The number of solutions M must be known or estimated; real machines impose noise and finite-precision costs.
