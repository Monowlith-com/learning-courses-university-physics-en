# M18-L2 – Mechanics Experiments: Moment of Inertia and Oscillation {#L2}

**Course area:** M18 – Bachelor: Physics Laboratory Practice

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** M3: rotation; M9: analytical mechanics; M16: uncertainty. [Previous lesson](M18-L1.md).

**Learning objective:** Determine a moment of inertia from a calibrated oscillation model and test the model assumptions against data.

## Research Question and Model {#S1}

Rotational dynamics relates applied torque to the body's moment of inertia $I$. This is not an intrinsic material constant: mass distribution and the rotation axis matter. A uniform thin disk about its symmetry axis has $I=MR^2/2$. One practical determination uses torsional oscillations. For small angle and an approximately linear restoring torque $\tau=-\kappa\varphi$, the motion is harmonic. Analysis must separate the inertia of the apparatus from that of the specimen.

## Equations and Assumptions {#S2}

The equation $I_{\mathrm{tot}}\ddot\varphi+\kappa\varphi=0$ implies $T=2\pi\sqrt{I_{\mathrm{tot}}/\kappa}$. Let $T_0$ be the period of the empty holder with inertia $I_0$. With an added specimen, subtraction of squared periods yields $I_{\mathrm{sample}}=\kappa(T^2-T_0^2)/(4\pi^2)$, provided the torsional stiffness and bearing state are unchanged. The difference removes constant holder inertia. Damping, large amplitudes, stiffness drift or an off-axis mounting limit this simplified equation. Calibrate the stiffness independently or against a known reference.

$$
I_{\mathrm{sample}}=\frac{\kappa}{4\pi^2}(T^2-T_0^2),\qquad I_{\mathrm{disk}}=\tfrac12MR^2
$$

## Worked Example and Diagram {#S3}

A synthetic reference disk has $M=0.20\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $R=0.15\,\mathrm m$. The ideal disk formula predicts $I=0.00225\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$. With $\kappa=0.025\,\mathrm{N\,m/rad}$ the holder-free period is approximately $1.88\,\mathrm s$. Real rigs almost always contribute holder inertia, so that number must not be compared directly with the observed period of the full assembly. For known reference inertias $I_{\mathrm{ref}}$, we have $T^2=(4\pi^2/\kappa)(I_{\mathrm{ref}}+I_0)$. Plotting $T^2$ against $I_{\mathrm{ref}}$ gives slope $a=4\pi^2/\kappa$ and intercept $b=aI_0$; therefore the holder inertia is $I_0=b/a$.

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Calculate $I$ for $M=0.20\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $R=0.15\,\mathrm m$.
2. Why do we subtract $T_0^2$ rather than $T_0$?
3. Which geometric uncertainty enters the disk formula with a factor of two?

### Solutions

1. $I=0.5\cdot0.20\cdot0.15^2=0.00225\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$.
2. $T^2$ is proportional to the total inertia whereas $T$ is not.
3. The relative radius uncertainty $u_R/R$.

## Limits and Quality Control {#S5}

Repeat timing measurements and record whether successive timing errors can reasonably be treated as independent. Repetition estimates random scatter but cannot repair an incorrect geometry model. For $I=MR^2/2$, independent uncertainties contribute approximately $u_I/I\approx\sqrt{(u_M/M)^2+(2u_R/R)^2}$. Avoid false precision from excessive displayed digits. Comparison with a reference requires agreement on rotation axis, geometry and relevant conditions.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

A difference measurement removes constant apparatus contributions but does not replace model validation or calibration.
