# M10-L7 – Relativistic Transformation of Electric and Magnetic Fields {#L7}

**Course area:** M10 – Bachelor: Advanced Electrodynamics

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** Special relativity, Lorentz factor and Maxwell equations. [Previous lesson](M10-L6.md).

**Learning objective:** Apply field transformations under a Lorentz boost and identify common invariants.

## Electromagnetism in Moving Frames {#S1}

Electric and magnetic fields are not two independently transforming objects. Their division depends on the observer’s state of motion. A field purely electric in one inertial frame can acquire a magnetic component in a moving frame. Physical predictions for a charged test particle nevertheless remain consistent. The change is a Lorentz transformation rather than Galilean velocity addition. Signs depend on how the relative boost is defined, so its direction must be stated explicitly.

## Parallel and Transverse Field Components {#S2}

Let $S'$ move at $\boldsymbol v=v\boldsymbol e_x$ relative to $S$, with $\gamma=(1-v^2/c^2)^{-1/2}$. The components parallel to $\boldsymbol v$ are unchanged: $\boldsymbol E'_{\parallel}=\boldsymbol E_{\parallel}$ and $\boldsymbol B'_{\parallel}=\boldsymbol B_{\parallel}$. For perpendicular components,

$$
\boldsymbol E'_{\perp}=\gamma(\boldsymbol E_{\perp}+\boldsymbol v\times\boldsymbol B),\quad\boldsymbol B'_{\perp}=\gamma\left(\boldsymbol B_{\perp}-\frac{\boldsymbol v\times\boldsymbol E}{c^2}\right).
$$

These are passive transformations to measurements in the moving frame. Replacing $\boldsymbol v\mapsto-\boldsymbol v$ gives the inverse transformation. Longitudinal field components do not mix.

## Example: Magnetic Field from an Electric Field {#S3}

In $S$, take a uniform purely electric field $\boldsymbol E=E_0\boldsymbol e_y$ and $\boldsymbol B=0$. An observer in $S'$ moving in $+x$ measures $E'_y=\gamma E_0$ and

$$
B'_z=-\gamma\frac{vE_0}{c^2}.
$$

For $v=0.6c$, $\gamma=1.25$. With $E_0=1000\,\mathrm{V/m}$ one finds $E'_y=1250\,\mathrm{V/m}$ and $B'_z\approx-2.50\,\mathrm{\mu T}$. The negative sign indicates the $-z$ direction. The diagram shows a boost along $x$, the original $y$ field and the induced measured magnetic component along $-z$; it is not a new physical source.

*Motion along x mixes transverse field components.*

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Which field components stay unchanged under a boost along $x$?
2. Find $\gamma$ at $v=0.8c$.
3. Which two combinations are Lorentz invariant?

### Solutions

1. $E_x$ and $B_x$.
2. $\gamma=1/\sqrt{1-0.8^2}=5/3\approx1.667$.
3. $B^2-E^2/c^2$ and $\boldsymbol E\cdot\boldsymbol B$.

## Field Invariants and Interpretation Limits {#S5}

Two combinations remain unchanged under every Lorentz boost:

$$
I_1=B^2-\frac{E^2}{c^2},\qquad I_2=\boldsymbol E\cdot\boldsymbol B.
$$

In the example $I_2=0$ in both frames, while $B'^2-E'^2/c^2=-E_0^2/c^2$. These invariants help determine whether a frame exists in which one field vanishes. A special field configuration does not justify general claims about the underlying sources. Accelerated observers require additional care in coordinate and measurement definitions. The transformation laws motivate a compact tensor formulation.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Field transformations mix electric and magnetic quantities. An antisymmetric field tensor unifies them in one mathematical structure.
