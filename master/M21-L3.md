# M21-L3 – Hydrostatic Atmosphere and Temperature Gradient {#L3}

**Course area:** M21 – Bachelor: Geophysics and Climate

**Level:** Bachelor · Elective

**Prerequisites:** M4: fluids; M5: ideal gases; M8: differential equations. [Previous lesson](M21-L2.md).

**Learning objective:** Derive the barometric pressure profile and distinguish an isothermal model from a real lapse rate.

## Question and Observation {#S1}

Air pressure generally decreases with altitude because less atmospheric mass remains overhead. Temperature also changes, so air density is not constant. Hydrostatic balance describes the vertical pressure distribution when vertical acceleration is small compared with gravity. It does not require the entire atmosphere to be motionless.

## Physical Model and Equations {#S2}

A thin layer of air satisfies $dp/dz=-\rho g$. Combining this with the ideal-gas equation $p=\rho R_sT$ gives
$$
\frac{dp}{dz}=-\frac{pg}{R_sT}.
$$
Only if temperature $T$ and gravitational acceleration $g$ are constant does integration yield
$$
p(z)=p_0\exp(-z/H),\qquad H=\frac{R_sT}{g}.
$$
For dry air $R_s\approx287\,\mathrm{J/(kg\,K)}$. With a varying temperature profile the correct expression is $\ln[p(z)/p_0]=-\int_0^z g/[R_sT(z^{\prime})]\,dz^{\prime}$. The tropospheric temperature decrease is not one universal constant.

## Worked Example and Teaching Diagram {#S3}

Take the illustrative assumptions $T=288\,\mathrm K$, $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ and $p_0=1013\,\mathrm{hPa}$. The scale height is $H\approx8426\,\mathrm m$. At $z=5\,\mathrm{km}$, $p\approx1013\exp(-5000/8426)\approx560\,\mathrm{hPa}$. One panel shows the exponential isothermal pressure curve; the other shows a separate idealised linear temperature profile, not the temperature of that isothermal column.

## Self-Assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Derive $H=R_sT/g$ from hydrostatic balance.
2. Calculate $p(H)/p_0$ for an isothermal atmosphere.
3. Why do an isothermal pressure model and a nonzero tropospheric lapse rate not describe one fully consistent atmospheric column?

### Solutions

1. Insert $\rho=p/(R_sT)$, separate variables and integrate $dp/p=-dz/H$.
2. $p(H)/p_0=e^{-1}\approx0.368$.
3. With $T=T(z)$ the pressure-equation integrand changes; a single constant $H$ no longer solves it exactly.

## Validity Limits and Quality {#S5}

Vertical accelerations, water vapour and its effect on the effective specific gas constant $R_s$, gravity changes and atmospheric circulation are omitted. The two curves deliberately illustrate different approximations.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Hydrostatic balance plus the equation of state explains the pressure decrease; temperature structure controls its detailed profile.
