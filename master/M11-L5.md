# M11-L5 – Spin and the Stern–Gerlach Experiment {#L5}

**Course area:** M11 – Bachelor: Foundations of Quantum Physics

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** Angular momentum and magnetic fields, energy levels and quantization; M9 and M11-L4. [Previous lesson](M11-L4.md).

**Learning objective:** Explain spin-½ states, magnetic moments, and beam splitting in a nonuniform magnetic field.

## Intrinsic Angular Momentum without Classical Rotation {#S1}

Spin is the intrinsic angular momentum of a quantum system. The term does not describe a tiny extended sphere spinning mechanically around an axis. For an electron the spin quantum number is $s=1/2$. Along a chosen measurement axis $z$, only two outcomes are possible for the spin component. A state prepared in a different orientation can be a superposition of the two alternatives and gives statistical frequencies over repeated identical preparations. Spin is associated with a magnetic moment. A nonuniform magnetic field can therefore separate trajectories of neutral atoms according to their moments, providing an experimental window on quantization.

## Spin Operators and Magnetic Moment {#S2}

Spin operators satisfy $[S_x,S_y]=i\hbar S_z$ and cyclic permutations, with

$$
S^2=s(s+1)\hbar^2=\tfrac34\hbar^2,\qquad S_z=\pm\tfrac12\hbar.
$$

For an electron, $\boldsymbol\mu_s=-g_s\mu_\mathrm B\boldsymbol S/\hbar$, where $\mu_\mathrm B=e\hbar/(2m_\mathrm e)>0$ and $g_s\simeq2.0023$. The minus sign arises from the electron’s negative charge: its moment is antiparallel to its spin. In a field $\boldsymbol B=B_z\boldsymbol e_z$, energy is $U=-\boldsymbol\mu\cdot\boldsymbol B$. Allowed spin energies are separated by 

$$
\Delta E=g_s\mu_\mathrm B B_z.
$$

A uniform field changes energies; splitting a particle beam spatially requires a field gradient.

## Silver-Atom Beam in a Field Gradient {#S3}

In the Stern–Gerlach experiment, a beam of neutral silver atoms passes through a strongly inhomogeneous magnetic field. The electronic ground state of silver effectively has one unpaired valence-electron contribution to the atom’s total angular momentum. Its two magnetic projections are deflected differently. In a simplified $z$ geometry,

$$
F_z\simeq\mu_z\frac{\partial B_z}{\partial z},
$$

provided the projection remains sufficiently well defined during passage. At $B=0.50\,\mathrm T$, the electron-spin Zeeman separation for a free electron is $\Delta E\simeq5.80\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{eV}$. The original Stern–Gerlach experiment used **silver atoms**, not a free-electron beam. The diagram shows source, magnet and the two separated impact regions; trajectory curvature is schematic.

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. State the two possible electron values of $S_z$.
2. Compute $\Delta E$ at $B=1.0\,\mathrm T$ using $g_s\simeq2$ and $\mu_\mathrm B=5.788\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{eV/T}$.
3. Why did the classic experiment use neutral silver atoms rather than electrons?

### Solutions

1. $S_z=\pm\hbar/2$.
2. $\Delta E\simeq2(5.788\times10^{-5})\,\mathrm{eV}=1.158\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{eV}$.
3. Charged electrons experience substantial Lorentz-force deflection; neutral atoms allow cleaner separation by magnetic moment.

## Measurement, Superposition and Historical Interpretation {#S5}

Selecting the upper subbeam after a $z$ measurement and measuring again along $z$ gives the same ideal outcome. If another axis is measured between them, subsequent $z$ measurements may again yield either result. This axis dependence distinguishes quantum spin from a rigid pre-existing classical orientation. The historical experiment predated the fully developed electron-spin theory, so the observed splitting and its later interpretation must be kept distinct. Environmental fields and finite beam widths blur real spots and limit the idealized two-beam picture.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Spin is described by operators and discrete projections; Stern–Gerlach splitting reveals magnetic quantization.
