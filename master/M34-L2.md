# M34-L2 – Entanglement, Bell States and CHSH Correlations {#L2}

**Course area:** M34 – Master: Quantum Information and Quantum Technologies

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M12: tensor products and spin; M34-L1: qubits.

**Learning objective:** Distinguish the local CHSH bound from the quantum prediction.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

An entangled two-qubit state cannot be written as a product of individual state vectors. In the Bell state |Φ⁺⟩, computational-basis outcomes are perfectly correlated even though each marginal outcome is random. Correlation by itself does not prove entanglement: appropriately chosen Bell tests reveal the difference.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

For the spin singlet $|\Psi^-\rangle=(|01\rangle-|10\rangle)/\sqrt2$, coplanar spin measurements have $E(a,b)=-\cos(a-b)$. Define $S=E(a,b)+E(a,b\prime)+E(a\prime,b)-E(a\prime,b\prime)$. A locally realistic model subject to the Bell-test assumptions gives $|S|\le2$; quantum theory allows $2\sqrt2$. This does not imply faster-than-light signaling.

$$
|S|_{\mathrm{LHV}}\le2,\qquad |S|_{\mathrm{QM}}\le2\sqrt2.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

Take $a=0$, $a\prime=\pi/2$, $b=\pi/4$, and $b\prime=-\pi/4$. The correlators in order are $-1/\sqrt2,-1/\sqrt2,-1/\sqrt2,+1/\sqrt2$. Hence $S=-2\sqrt2\approx-2.828$. The figure compares the absolute local bound with the quantum maximum and is not experimental data.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What is the maximum $|S|$ for a local CHSH model?
2. What value follows for the chosen angles?
3. Why can one not directly send a bit using the singlet?

### Solutions

1. $2$.
2. $2\sqrt2\approx2.828$.
3. Local outcomes are random; correlations appear only after classical comparison.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The CHSH bound assumes the relevant Bell-test conditions. Detection efficiency, random setting choices and loopholes matter experimentally; the statement is not about arbitrary statistical correlations.
