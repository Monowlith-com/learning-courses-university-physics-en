# M22-L5 – Molecular Motors, Work and Dissipation {#L5}

**Course area:** M22 – Bachelor: Biophysics

**Level:** Bachelor · Elective

**Prerequisites:** M5: first and second laws; M13: nonequilibrium; M22-L4: molecular elasticity. [Previous lesson](M22-L4.md).

**Learning objective:** Calculate mechanical work per step and distinguish chemical input, efficiency and dissipated energy.

## Question and Observation {#S1}

Motor proteins can convert chemical free energy into directed steps along filaments. Motion against an external load is not an equilibrium random walk: coupled reaction cycles, changing binding states and ATP turnover drive a net current. A motor can take backward as well as forward steps; the statistics depend on load and chemical conditions.

## Physical Model and Equations {#S2}

A motor moving a constant opposing force $F$ through distance $d$ performs mechanical work $W_{\mathrm{mech}}=Fd$. In a deliberately simplified cycle supplied with available chemical free energy $|\Delta G_{\mathrm{chem}}|$, the mechanical efficiency is

$$
W_{\mathrm{mech}}=Fd,\qquad \eta=\frac{W_{\mathrm{mech}}}{|\Delta G_{\mathrm{chem}}|}\leq1.
$$

Under isothermal conditions, useful mechanical work cannot exceed the supplied free energy. The difference is dissipated via heat and internal reaction pathways, not necessarily observed as a local temperature rise. Perfect coupling would permit the energetic upper bound $F_{\max}d=|\Delta G_{\mathrm{chem}}|$, but a real motor's stall load also depends on backward steps, rates and coupling efficiency.

## Worked Example and Teaching Diagram {#S3}

For illustration only, suppose each $d=8\,\mathrm{nm}$ step draws $20k_{\mathrm B}T$ of free energy. At $T=298\,\mathrm K$ this is about $8.23\cdot10^{-20}\,\mathrm J$, which corresponds to an ideal upper force of $10.3\,\mathrm{pN}$. Against a constant $F=5\,\mathrm{pN}$ load the work is $40\,\mathrm{pN\,nm}=4.0\cdot10^{-20}\,\mathrm J$ per step, or about 49% efficiency in this model. The diagram separates motor stepping from an energy-flow budget.

## Self-Assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find the work against a $4\,\mathrm{pN}$ load over one $8\,\mathrm{nm}$ step.
2. At eight net steps per second of $8\,\mathrm{nm}$ each, find the mean velocity.
3. Why does $Fd=|\Delta G|$ not automatically predict a measured stall force?

### Solutions

1. $W=32\,\mathrm{pN\,nm}=3.2\cdot10^{-20}\,\mathrm J$.
2. $v=8\cdot8\,\mathrm{nm/s}=64\,\mathrm{nm/s}$.
3. It is a reversible energetic upper bound; backsteps, internal dissipation and chemical substeps shift the true stall load.

## Validity Limits and Quality {#S5}

The model assumes constant load, a well-defined net step and one specified energy input per step. Real proteins may exhibit reverse steps, multiple reaction cycles, internal losses or variable step sizes. The value $20k_{\mathrm B}T$ is an illustrative assumption, not a universal ATP free energy.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Chemical free energy can drive directed movement against a load. Mechanical work and efficiency must be compared with a specifically defined chemical energy budget.
