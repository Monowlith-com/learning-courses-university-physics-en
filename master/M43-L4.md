# M43-L4 – Uncertainty Budgets, Covariance and Error Propagation {#L4}

**Course area:** M43 – Master: Model Assessment and Scientific Research Practice

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M32: uncertainty methods; M43-L2: calibration; M43-L3: group comparisons.

**Learning objective:** Evaluate the impact of correlated inputs on the standard uncertainty of a derived quantity.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Measurements can share calibration, environment or processing, so their uncertainty contributions are not independent. For a difference of two sensors subject to shared drift, positive correlation may cancel part of the common disturbance; for a sum it often reinforces it. A defensible uncertainty budget lists components and their dependencies.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

For $z=x_1-x_2$, first-order propagation gives $u_z^2=u_1^2+u_2^2-2\rho u_1u_2$ for known standard uncertainties $u_i$ and correlation coefficient $\rho$. This follows from $\mathrm{Var}(X_1-X_2)=\mathrm{Var}(X_1)+\mathrm{Var}(X_2)-2\mathrm{Cov}(X_1,X_2)$. For $-1\le\rho\le1$, the resulting variance is nonnegative for consistent inputs.

$$
u_z=\sqrt{u_1^2+u_2^2-2\rho u_1u_2},\qquad z=x_1-x_2.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For **synthetic** inputs with $u_1=0.2$, $u_2=0.3$ and $\rho=0.5$, the combined uncertainty is $u_z=\sqrt{0.04+0.09-0.06}=\sqrt{0.07}\approx0.265$. For independent inputs ($\rho=0$), it would be $\sqrt{0.13}\approx0.361$. The chart shows continuous dependence on correlation; it is not a correlation function estimated from data.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Compute $u_z$ for $\rho=1$.
2. Compute $u_z$ for $\rho=-1$.
3. Can correlation always be ignored for sensors with a shared calibration?

### Solutions

1. $|0.3-0.2|=0.1$.
2. $0.3+0.2=0.5$.
3. No; shared influences can materially change the uncertainty budget.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

For a linear difference the variance expression is exact. For general nonlinear functions the GUM linear approximation requires suitable local conditions; strongly nonlinear models may need Monte Carlo or other methods. The value of $\rho$ must be justified, never chosen merely for a favorable result.
