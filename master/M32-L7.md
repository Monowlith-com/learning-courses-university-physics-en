# M32-L7 – Tomography, projections and inverse problems {#L7}

**Course area:** M32 – Master: Experimental Instrumentation and Research Methods

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M31-L5: inverse problems; M2: integration · [Previous lesson](M32-L6.md).

**Learning objective:** Derive the Radon projection of a uniform disk and explain why multiple viewing angles are needed.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

In computed tomography, suitably corrected detector readings provide projections representing integrals of a spatial distribution along rays. Recovering the distribution from its projections is an inverse problem. Angular coverage and data quality determine which spatial features can be recovered reliably.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

The two-dimensional Radon transform of $\mu(x,y)$ is $p_\theta(s)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mu(s\mathbf n_\theta+t\mathbf n_\theta^\perp)\,\mathrm dt$, where $\mathbf n_\theta$ is the projection normal. For a uniform disk of radius $R$ and value $\mu_0$, the ray traverses a chord of length $2\sqrt{R^2-s^2}$. Thus $p(s)=2\mu_0\sqrt{R^2-s^2}$ for $|s|<R$, and zero elsewhere. Multiple views enable filtered backprojection via the Fourier slice theorem under suitable ideal conditions; noise and limited angular coverage complicate inversion.

$$
p(s)=2\mu_0\sqrt{R^2-s^2}\quad (|s|<R),\qquad p(s)=0\quad (|s|\ge R).
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

Consider a uniform phantom disk of radius $R=2\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $\mu_0=0.20\,\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$. At the center, $p(0)=2\cdot0.20\cdot2=0.80$ (dimensionless); at $s=1\,\mathrm{cm}$, $p=0.4\sqrt3\approx0.693$. The figure shows the disk phantom alongside its curved projection profile.

![Uniform disk with parallel measurement rays and a semicircular Radon projection curve.](../../../../media/course/U_PHY_295_EN.svg)

*The line integral of a uniform disk is proportional to its traversed chord length.*

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find $p(0)$ for $R=3\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $\mu_0=0.1\,\mathrm{cm}^{-1}$.
2. What is $p(s)$ outside a uniform disk ($|s|>R$)?
3. Why do missing projection angles introduce artifacts?

### Solutions

1. $p(0)=0.6$.
2. $p(s)=0$.
3. Insufficient directional Fourier sampling can render reconstruction unstable or nonunique.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The chord formula applies only to a uniform disk with ideal straight rays. Scatter, beam hardening, finite pixels, motion and limited-angle sampling complicate real CT. In X-ray CT the projection quantity is typically obtained from transmission readings after a logarithmic transformation and calibration.
