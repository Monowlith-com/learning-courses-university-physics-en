# M34-L7 – Quantum Sensing, Measurement Precision and Quantum Networks {#L7}

**Course area:** M34 – Master: Quantum Information and Quantum Technologies

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M32: instrumentation; M33: atomic and optical physics; M34-L2: entanglement.

**Learning objective:** Explain the ideal 1/sqrt(N) precision scaling for independent probes and distinguish it from network protocols.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Quantum technologies use controlled states for sensing and information transfer. Atomic clocks and interferometric sensors convert an unknown phase into statistical counts. Entanglement or squeezing can improve precision under suitable assumptions, but loss, dead time and technical drift preclude a universal gain. Quantum networks distribute states or entanglement and still need classical control and communication channels.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

For $N$ ideal independent probes at optimal operating point and fixed contrast, typical phase uncertainty scales as $\Delta\phi\propto1/\sqrt N$. This is shot-noise scaling; its prefactor depends on the actual sensor. Correlations can permit a $1/N$ scaling under specific ideal resource counts, but are susceptible to noise. Precision scaling says nothing by itself about security of quantum key distribution.

$$
\Delta\phi_{\mathrm{independent}}\propto\frac1{\sqrt N}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

Increasing the count of independent ideal probes from $N=100$ to $N=400$ halves phase uncertainty: in a normalized example from 0.10 to 0.05 rad. The graph shows theoretical values for several N and is not a characterization of a particular clock or sensor. Networks can coordinate measurements but do not improve the scaling without extra resources or a suitable protocol.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. By which factor does ideal uncertainty change when N is quadrupled?
2. How does ideal variance scale if standard deviation is $\propto N^{-1/2}$?
3. Why is a quantum measurement bound not a network-security guarantee?

### Solutions

1. Uncertainty is halved.
2. As $1/N$.
3. Security depends on protocol assumptions, implementation, authentication and side channels.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The formula applies to independently measured ideal probes, not every sensor. Technical noise can impose a floor. Quantum networks require low-loss components and validated protocols; topical references are no substitute for security evaluation.

## Summary and outlook {#S6}

This module links single-qubit physics to correlations, circuits, algorithms, noise, error correction and practical applications.
