# M18-L1 – Experiment Planning, Laboratory Rules and Measurement Strategy {#L1}

**Course area:** M18 – Bachelor: Physics Laboratory Practice

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** M16: uncertainty and data analysis; M17: model comparison. [Previous lesson](M17-L8.md).

**Learning objective:** Formulate a testable measurement question, document a risk decision and design a measurement plan with controlled quantities.

## Research Question and Model {#S1}

A laboratory experiment starts before any instrument is switched on: Which quantity is to be estimated or which physical relation is to be tested, and under what assumptions? A testable hypothesis specifies an observable relation, such as $T^2\propto l$ for an ideal pendulum at small amplitude. Define the independent variable $l$, observable $T$ and potential confounders including amplitude, temperature, air motion and timer resolution. The plan distinguishes calibrations, controls and the actual measurement series. Predict orders of magnitude in advance; this helps catch unit errors and inappropriate measurement ranges. All numerical data in this module are original teaching examples, not laboratory observations.

## Equations and Assumptions {#S2}

Local laboratory rules, risk assessments and supervisor instructions take precedence over general course material. Electrical, optical, magnetic, hot, pressurised and radiation-related equipment must only be used after approval and with the required controls. Record the hazard, protective measure, stop condition and responsible person before starting. A nominally low-energy measurement is not automatically safe when the device configuration is unknown. Stop when abnormal noise, odour, overheating or missing guards are observed. This lesson teaches planning; it is not a site-specific operating procedure.

$$
T=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{l}{g}},\qquad T^2=\frac{4\pi^2}{g}l\quad(\theta\ll1)
$$

## Worked Example and Diagram {#S3}

Example: A pendulum with $l=0.80\,\mathrm m$ at $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ has a small-angle period near $1.79\,\mathrm s$. Instead of timing one swing, one may, under safe and approved conditions, time ten cycles and divide by ten. With unchanged absolute start/stop error this reduces the relative timing uncertainty. Record the number of cycles and amplitude range, however: more cycles do not automatically correct systematic deviations from the model.

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Name an independent, dependent and confounding variable in a pendulum experiment.
2. Estimate the small-angle period for $l=0.80\,\mathrm m$.
3. Give four elements of a documented risk decision.

### Solutions

1. Length $l$, period $T$, and for example swing amplitude.
2. $T=2\pi\sqrt{0.80/9.81}\,\mathrm s\approx1.79\,\mathrm s$.
3. Hazard, mitigation, stop criterion and responsible person.

## Limits and Quality Control {#S5}

Before analysis, record instrument identity, calibration date where available, conditions, raw-data format and the intended uncertainty treatment. A defensible plan includes repeats, at least one control point and a predeclared rule for excluding observations. Finally ask whether the experiment could actually falsify its hypothesis: if every conceivable outcome can be redescribed afterwards as agreement, the research question needs sharpening.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

A credible measurement starts with a hypothesis, safety, calibration and a traceable data plan.
