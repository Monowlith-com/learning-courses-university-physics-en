# M17-L6 – Monte Carlo Methods and Statistical Integration {#L6}

**Course area:** M17 – Bachelor: Computational Physics and Simulation

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** M13: expectation and variance; M16: uncertainty; M17-L2: integration. [Previous lesson](M17-L5.md).

**Learning objective:** Estimate integrals by sampling, quantify statistical uncertainty and explain pseudorandom reproducibility.

## Random Samples as an Integrator {#S1}

Monte Carlo approximates integrals by averaging randomly sampled function values. With uniform samples $U_i$ on $[0,1]$, the mean of $f(U_i)$ estimates $I=\int_0^1 f(x)\,dx$. This is especially useful in many dimensions, where regular grids may demand prohibitively many points. It does not replace the correct physical probability distribution: sampling the wrong ensemble yields a systematically wrong answer, even when statistical error bars appear small. Define the target integral, sampling measure and normalization before running.

## Estimators, Variance and Error Scaling {#S2}

For independent identically distributed samples with finite variance, the simple estimator is unbiased. Its standard deviation decreases as $N^{-1/2}$, independent of the integration dimension. This familiar scaling assumes independence and finite variance. Correlations, for example in Markov-chain methods, reduce the effective sample size. Finite-sample error bars are estimates; they quantify sampling variation, not a wrong model, normalization or random-number generator.

$$
\hat I_N=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^{N}f(U_i),\qquad \operatorname{Var}(\hat I_N)=\frac{\operatorname{Var}(f(U))}{N}.
$$

$$
\operatorname{SD}(\hat I_N)=\sqrt{\frac{4}{45N}}\qquad \text{for }f(x)=x^2.
$$

## Example: Integral of x² {#S3}

The integral $I=\int_0^1 x^2dx=1/3$ can be estimated as $\hat I_N=N^{-1}\sum U_i^2$. Since $E[U^2]=1/3$ and $E[U^4]=1/5$, $\mathrm{Var}(U^2)=1/5-1/9=4/45$. For $N=10\,000$, the theoretical standard deviation of the sample mean is $\sqrt{4/(45N)}\approx0.002981$. Any individual run may lie more than one standard deviation from the exact integral. The diagram shows the $N^{-1/2}$ trend, not a guarantee for each realization.

**Executable Python example:**

```python
import random, math
rng = random.Random(42)
n = 10000
samples = [rng.random()**2 for _ in range(n)]
mean = sum(samples)/n
standard_error = math.sqrt(4/(45*n))
print(mean, standard_error, 1/3)
```

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. By what factor does Monte Carlo standard deviation decrease when $N$ quadruples?
2. Compute $\mathrm{Var}(U^2)$ for $U\sim\mathrm{Uniform}(0,1)$.
3. Why does fixing a random seed not automatically validate a Monte Carlo model?

### Solutions

1. It is halved.
2. $1/5-(1/3)^2=4/45$.
3. It only repeats a pseudorandom sequence; the sampling distribution, model and uncertainty treatment may still be wrong.

## Variance Reduction and Pitfalls {#S5}

*Importance sampling* uses an alternative density $p(x)>0$ and corrects the sample weight to $f(x)/p(x)$. For localized contributions variance can shrink dramatically, but a poor choice can increase it or make it infinite. Antithetic variates and control variates can also reduce variance under their respective conditions. A reproducible seed is useful for diagnostics but cannot replace independent repeat runs or uncertainty assessment. Convergence plots must distinguish expected standard deviation from realized absolute error.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Monte Carlo separates expected estimate and sampling spread; correlations and model assumptions remain separate error sources.
