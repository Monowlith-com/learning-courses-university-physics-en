# M35-L1 – Band Structure, Fermi Surfaces and Effective Mass {#L1}

**Course area:** M35 – Master: Advanced Solid-State Physics and Materials Science

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M14: crystals and band model; M12: quantum mechanics.

**Learning objective:** Determine the width of a one-dimensional tight-binding band and distinguish this model from real crystals.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Electrons in a periodic crystal potential form Bloch states labelled by crystal momentum within the first Brillouin zone. A band gives allowed single-particle energies versus that momentum. The Fermi surface is the constant-energy boundary of occupied states at zero temperature, not the physical outer surface of a crystal. In one dimension it reduces to Fermi points. Gaps and band filling help distinguish metallic from insulating behavior.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

For a periodic chain with lattice spacing $a$, one orbital per unit cell and real nearest-neighbor hopping $t>0$, the tight-binding model yields $E(k)=E_0-2t\cos(ka)$. The bandwidth is $4t$, between $k=0$ and $k=\pi/a$. Differentiating gives group velocity $v_g=(1/\hbar)\partial E/\partial k=2ta\sin(ka)/\hbar$. Local effective mass reflects curvature: $1/m^*=(1/\hbar^2)\partial^2E/\partial k^2$; near a band maximum $m^*$ can be negative, motivating the hole picture.

$$
E(k)=E_0-2t\cos(ka),\qquad W=4t.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

Set $E_0=0$ and $t=1\,\mathrm{eV}$. Then $E(0)=-2\,\mathrm{eV}$, $E(\pi/2a)=0$, $E(\pi/a)=+2\,\mathrm{eV}$, giving bandwidth $W=4\,\mathrm{eV}$. The figure plots the symmetric dispersion for $-\pi/a\le k\le\pi/a$ and marks an illustrative Fermi level $E_F=0$ for half filling of this ideal spin-degenerate band.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find $W$ for $t=0.6\,\mathrm{eV}$.
2. Where does group velocity vanish in the first Brillouin zone?
3. Is the Fermi surface the outer surface of a crystal?

### Solutions

1. $W=2.4\,\mathrm{eV}$.
2. At $k=0$ and $k=\pm\pi/a$; more generally at stationary band points.
3. No. It is a structure in momentum space determined by band filling and dispersion.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The one-band approximation neglects multiple orbitals, Coulomb correlations, real three-dimensional geometry and disorder. A Fermi surface in an interacting metal needs additional care; this graph is a single-particle illustration.
