# M15-L7 – Conservation Laws and Particle Transformations {#L7}

**Course area:** M15 – Bachelor: Nuclear and Particle Physics

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** M15-L6, special relativity and elementary quantum mechanics. [Previous lesson](M15-L6.md).

**Learning objective:** Balance energy, momentum and charge in particle reactions and identify interaction-dependent quantum-number rules.

## Spacetime Conservation Laws {#S1}

Energy and momentum combine into a conserved four-momentum. For initial and final particle four-momenta $p_i^\mu$,

$$
\sum_{\mathrm{in}}p_i^\mu=\sum_{\mathrm{out}}p_i^\mu.
$$

Individual particle mass is Lorentz-invariant and obeys $E^2=p^2c^2+m^2c^4$. The sum of rest masses need not be unchanged: kinetic energy can become rest mass and vice versa. Total electric charge is also conserved in known reactions. Angular momentum, including spin, must be accounted for across all final states. An apparent violation of a selection rule cannot be inferred from an incompletely reconstructed event alone.

## Charge, Baryon Number and Flavour {#S2}

In ordinary observed processes baryon number $B$ is conserved: baryons carry $+1$, antibaryons $-1$. Lepton numbers are useful bookkeeping devices, but separate lepton-flavour numbers are not exactly conserved because neutrinos oscillate. Strangeness is conserved in strong and electromagnetic interactions but may change in weak processes. Allowed channels must also obey energy availability, charge and other relevant quantum numbers. Identifying the interaction is essential: a selection rule for strong scattering need not apply to beta decay. Claims about conservation of baryon or lepton number must specify their theoretical domain.

## Example: Beta-Minus Transformation {#S3}

Beta-minus decay converts a neutron into a proton, electron and electron antineutrino:

$$
n\rightarrow p+e^-+\bar\nu_e.
$$

Charge is zero on the left and $+e-e+0=0$ on the right. Proton and neutron carry baryon number $B=1$ in Standard Model bookkeeping; electron and antineutrino have $B=0$. The produced electron has electron-flavour lepton number $+1$, the antineutrino $-1$, giving a vanishing sum in the usual production-vertex description. The figure shows a particle accounting diagram, not the internal mechanism within the nucleon. Beta decay proceeds through the weak interaction.

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What is the total charge of $n\to p+e^-+\bar\nu_e$ products?
2. State the relation connecting $E$, $p$ and $m$.
3. Is strangeness necessarily conserved in weak decay?

### Solutions

1. $0$.
2. $E^2=p^2c^2+m^2c^4$.
3. No; the weak interaction may change strangeness.

## Limits of Apparently Exact Rules {#S5}

Free neutron beta decay is energetically allowed; within a nucleus, the corresponding conversion may be forbidden by the full nuclear mass balance. Electron capture can occur when total energies permit it. Neutrinos frequently leave a detector without visible tracks, causing missing reconstructed momentum; such an inference must be checked against resolution and other unmeasured particles. Saying that a quantum number is “conserved” does not automatically mean exact in every possible new theory. Some symmetries are broken or approximate in extensions of the Standard Model.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Conservation laws constrain reactions; flavour selection rules must not be confused with universal symmetries.
