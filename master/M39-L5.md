# M39-L5 – Molecular Motors, Chemical Work and Dissipation {#L5}

**Course area:** M39 – Master: Biophysics, Nonequilibrium and Complex Systems

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M22: biomolecular energetics; M39-L3: regulatory networks · [Previous lesson](M39-L4.md).

**Learning objective:** Compare chemical free energy per model step with mechanical work and interpret the dissipation sign.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Molecular motors can transform chemical reaction free energy into directed movement and transport. A recorded step is neither evidence of lossless conversion nor proof of one-to-one ATP–step stoichiometry. A simplified energy budget separates available chemical work, load work and dissipation.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

For a hypothetical forward step of length $d$ against force $F\ge0$, take released chemical free energy as $\Delta\mu>0$ and delivered mechanical work as $W_{\mathrm{mech}}=Fd$. An ideal isothermal cycle obeys $T\Delta S_{\mathrm{tot}}=\Delta\mu-Fd\ge0$ for average forward operation. The bound $F_{\mathrm{stall}}=\Delta\mu/d$ is a reversible limit for strict one-step coupling; competing pathways and kinetics can change measured stall forces.

$$
W_{\mathrm{mech}}=Fd,\qquad T\Delta S_{\mathrm{tot}}=\Delta\mu-Fd\ge0.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For deliberately constructed $\Delta\mu=80\,\mathrm{pN\,nm}$, $d=8\,\mathrm{nm}$, and $F=5\,\mathrm{pN}$, mechanical work is $40\,\mathrm{pN\,nm}$ and $T\Delta S_{\mathrm{tot}}=40\,\mathrm{pN\,nm}$ per ideal cycle. The reversible upper-force bound would be $10\,\mathrm{pN}$. The figure represents bookkeeping only; no experimental force curve or ATP hydrolysis value was copied.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What load work is obtained for $F=4\,\mathrm{pN}$ at the same $d$?
2. What would be the reversible upper force bound if step length doubled?
3. Why does $Fd<\Delta\mu$ not automatically measure an efficiency?

### Solutions

1. $32\,\mathrm{pN\,nm}$.
2. $5\,\mathrm{pN}$ for the same budget.
3. Step statistics, chemical coupling and hidden dissipation are not established.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

This cycle model omits backward steps, futile hydrolysis, variable coupling and fluctuations. The second-law inequality does not determine a force–velocity curve. Its parameters are not measurements of a particular motor family.
