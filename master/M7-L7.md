# M7-L7 – Optical Coherence and Interferometry {#L7}

**Course area:** M7 – Bachelor: Optics

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** Interference, complex fields and time averaging. [Previous lesson](M7-L6.md).

**Learning objective:** Interpret coherence and visibility and calculate path differences in a Michelson interferometer.

## Temporal and Spatial Coherence {#S1}

Interference requires relative phase to remain sufficiently stable during measurement. **Temporal coherence** characterizes the correlation of a field with a delayed copy of itself. **Spatial coherence** describes correlations at different transverse points. Both concepts refer to statistical field states and to measurement arrangements, not absolute labels for “coherent” and “incoherent” light. A laser may have high coherence, but real lasers have finite spectral width and phase noise. With broadband light, contrast generally decreases once the arm delay exceeds the coherence time. Spatial and spectral filtering can increase contrast, usually at the expense of optical power.

## Visibility and Correlation Function {#S2}

For a stationary scalar field define the normalized first-order coherence function

$$
g^{(1)}(\tau)=\frac{\langle E^*(t)E(t+\tau)\rangle}{\langle|E(t)|^2\rangle}.
$$

For two arms of intensities $I_1$ and $I_2$, the averaged interference intensity is 

$$
I=I_1+I_2+2\sqrt{I_1I_2}\,\mathrm{Re}\!\left[g^{(1)}(\tau)e^{i\phi}\right].
$$

Visibility $V=(I_{\max}-I_{\min})/(I_{\max}+I_{\min})$ becomes $V=2\sqrt{I_1I_2}|g^{(1)}(\tau)|/(I_1+I_2)$ in this ideal model. With balanced arms, $V=|g^{(1)}(\tau)|$. A characteristic coherence length is $L_c\sim c\tau_c$ in vacuum; the exact numerical factor depends on spectral shape and how $\tau_c$ is defined.

## Worked Example: Michelson Mirror {#S3}

In a Michelson interferometer a beamsplitter creates two arms. The two beams return after reflection and interfere at the output. Moving one mirror by $\Delta x$ changes the optical path difference in air by approximately $\Delta\ell=2\Delta x$. For vacuum wavelength $\lambda=632.8\,\mathrm{nm}$, a displacement of $\Delta x=\lambda/2=316.4\,\mathrm{nm}$ gives a complete $2\pi$ phase cycle, corresponding to **one** passing interference period. If ten bright fringes pass a fixed detector, the mirror displacement is $10\lambda/2=3.164\,\mathrm{\mu m}$ in the ideal model. The figure emphasizes the doubled path change, a common source of mistakes.

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. How far does a Michelson mirror move for five fringes at $\lambda=500\,\mathrm{nm}$?
2. Find $V$ for equal arm intensities and $|g^{(1)}|=0.7$.
3. Why can unequal arm intensities reduce contrast even at perfect coherence?

### Solutions

1. $\Delta x=5\lambda/2=1.25\,\mathrm{\mu m}$.
2. $V=0.7$.
3. The interference term scales as $2\sqrt{I_1I_2}$ while the background scales as $I_1+I_2$; equality optimizes the ratio.

## Measurement Limits and Spectra {#S5}

Visibility is a direct measure of $|g^{(1)}|$ only when arm intensities and polarization states are controlled. Vibration, drift, detector bandwidth and spatial overlap affect observed contrast. A coherence time cannot be universally equated to $1/\Delta\nu$ without specifying spectral shape and definitions. A multi-peaked spectrum may cause an oscillating coherence function that a single threshold distance describes poorly. Precision measurements must additionally calibrate the refractive index of air, thermal expansion, optical alignment and the sign of mirror movement.
