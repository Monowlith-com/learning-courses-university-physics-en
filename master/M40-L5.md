# M40-L5 – Sensitivity, Specificity, Prevalence and Predictive Values {#L5}

**Course area:** M40 – Master: Advanced Medical Physics and Quality Assurance

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M13: probability; M32: experimental design; M40-L4.

**Learning objective:** Avoid confusing diagnostic metrics and quantify prevalence effects in a complete numeric example.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Image quality alone does not establish how well a classifier distinguishes two disease states. Sensitivity conditions on cases; specificity on non-cases. Positive predictive value instead asks what fraction of positive tests are true cases and therefore depends on prevalence. With a rare condition, many positive results may be false even for a useful test.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

With $TP$ true positives, $FN$ false negatives, $TN$ true negatives and $FP$ false positives, $\mathrm{Se}=TP/(TP+FN)$ and $\mathrm{Sp}=TN/(TN+FP)$. Bayes’ theorem with prevalence $\pi$ gives $\mathrm{PPV}=\mathrm{Se}\pi/[\mathrm{Se}\pi+(1-\mathrm{Sp})(1-\pi)]$. These quantities only apply to a specified target condition, threshold, test population and reference standard.

$$
\mathrm{PPV}=\frac{\mathrm{Se}\,\pi}{\mathrm{Se}\,\pi+(1-\mathrm{Sp})(1-\pi)}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

In a *synthetic* cohort of 2,000 people, 100 are positive by reference standard. With $\mathrm{Se}=90\%$ and $\mathrm{Sp}=95\%$, the counts are $TP=90$, $FN=10$, $TN=1805$ and $FP=95$. Positive predictive value is $90/(90+95)\approx48.6\%$. These values are a statistics exercise and do not describe any real test.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. How many false positives remain for $\mathrm{Sp}=99\%$ with the same 1,900 non-cases?
2. What is negative predictive value in the baseline example?
3. Why does PPV depend on population even if sensitivity and specificity stay fixed?

### Solutions

1. $19$.
2. $1805/(1805+10)\approx99.45\%$.
3. Changed prevalence alters the ratio of true to false positive results.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

Evaluation without uncertainty intervals overstates precision. Verification bias, imperfect reference standards, dependent observations and threshold selection change interpretation. Diagnostic metrics do not replace clinical judgement for individuals.
