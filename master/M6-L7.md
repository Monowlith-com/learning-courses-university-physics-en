# M6-L7 – Alternating Current, Impedance and Resonance {#L7}

**Course area:** M6 – Bachelor: Electricity and Magnetism

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** Complex numbers, harmonic oscillation and elementary circuits. [Previous lesson](M6-L6.md).

**Learning objective:** Recognise phase shifts in RLC networks; calculate impedance and resonance frequency.

## Sinusoidal Quantities and RMS {#S1}

For a sinusoidal drive, voltage may be written $u(t)=\hat U\cos(\omega t)$, with angular frequency $\omega=2\pi f$. Across a pure resistor, voltage and current are in phase. In an ideal inductor voltage leads current by $90^\circ$; in an ideal capacitor current leads voltage by $90^\circ$. Root-mean-square values are $U_\mathrm{rms}=\hat U/\sqrt2$ and $I_\mathrm{rms}=\hat I/\sqrt2$. They produce the same mean resistive heating as equal DC values. Ideal inductors and capacitors exchange energy with the source, but their mean net energy transfer over a full cycle is zero; resistive components dissipate power.

## Complex Impedance {#S2}

With a phasor convention using $e^{i\omega t}$, an ideal series RLC circuit has complex impedance

$$
Z(\omega)=R+i\left(\omega L-\frac{1}{\omega C}\right),\quad |Z|=\sqrt{R^2+\left(\omega L-\frac{1}{\omega C}\right)^2}.
$$

The current amplitude is $\hat I=\hat U/|Z|$, and phase is $\tan\varphi=(\omega L-1/\omega C)/R$, with $\varphi=\arg Z$ representing voltage phase relative to current. At **series resonance**, inductive and capacitive reactances cancel, giving $\omega_0=1/\sqrt{LC}$ and $f_0=1/(2\pi\sqrt{LC})$. For ideal constant $R$ the current amplitude is maximal: $\hat I_0=\hat U/R$. Individual component voltages across reactive elements can exceed the applied source voltage.

## Worked Example: Series Resonance {#S3}

An idealised series RLC network has $R=20\,\Omega$, $L=0.100\,\mathrm H$ and $C=10.0\,\mathrm{\mu F}$. Its resonance angular frequency is

$$
\omega_0=\frac1{\sqrt{0.100\cdot10^{-5}}}=1000\,\mathrm{rad/s},\qquad f_0\approx159\,\mathrm{Hz}.
$$

With a sinusoidal $U_\mathrm{rms}=10.0\,\mathrm V$ source, resonant current is $I_\mathrm{rms}=U_\mathrm{rms}/R=0.500\,\mathrm A$. Inductive reactance is $X_L=\omega_0L=100\,\Omega$ and $|X_C|=100\,\Omega$. RMS voltage across **each** reactance has magnitude $50.0\,\mathrm V$, but the phasors oppose one another so their sum vanishes. ## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find $f_0$ if $L=0.040\,\mathrm H$ and $C=25\,\mathrm{\mu F}$.
2. What is the phase relation between current and voltage in an ideal capacitor?
3. Find quality factor for $R=20\,\Omega$, $L=0.100\,\mathrm H$ and $\omega_0=1000\,\mathrm{rad/s}$.

### Solutions

1. $LC=10^{-6}\,\mathrm{s^2}$, so $f_0=1/(2\pi\cdot10^{-3})\approx159\,\mathrm{Hz}$.
2. Current leads voltage by $90^\circ$.
3. $Q=\omega_0L/R=1000\cdot0.100/20=5.0$.

## Power, Bandwidth and Limits {#S5}

For a linear series circuit with constant $R$, quality factor is $Q=\omega_0L/R$; weak damping makes the resonance peak narrow. Real average power is $P=U_\mathrm{rms}I_\mathrm{rms}\cos\varphi$, while apparent power is $S=U_\mathrm{rms}I_\mathrm{rms}$. High $Q$ does not create energy; the inductor and capacitor exchange stored energy while the resistor dissipates some. Real components have frequency- and temperature-dependent parameters and parasitics, shifting resonance frequency and peak height. Phasors apply to steady-state sinusoidal drive; transient switching needs the time-domain differential equation.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Impedance describes amplitude and phase together; resonance occurs when reactances cancel. The final lesson addresses measurements and uncertainties.
