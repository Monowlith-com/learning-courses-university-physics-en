# M28-L7 – Retarded Green functions and spectral-function normalization {#L7}

**Course area:** M28 – Master: Nonequilibrium and many-body physics

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M27-L8: second quantization; M28-L5: causality · [Previous lesson](M28-L6.md).

**Learning objective:** Normalize the Lorentzian spectral density of a canonical fermionic mode and identify its energy units.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Green functions in many-body theory describe the energies at which an excitation can be created or removed. Spectral density makes this information close to measurable response. Correct normalization requires distinguishing energy $E$ from angular frequency $\omega$ and single-particle response from density response.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

Use the energy variable $E$ and define $A(E)=-(1/\pi)\operatorname{Im}G^R(E)$ for exactly one canonical fermionic mode satisfying $\{c,c^\dagger\}=1$. In a single-pole model with level energy $\varepsilon$ and width $\Gamma>0$, let $G^R(E)=[E-\varepsilon+i\Gamma]^{-1}$. Its imaginary part is negative, hence $A(E)\ge0$. Substituting $u=(E-\varepsilon)/\Gamma$ gives $\int_{-\infty}^\infty A(E)\,\mathrm dE=\int\mathrm du/[\pi(1+u^2)]=1$. In interacting systems, the retarded self-energy can appear in the Dyson form $G^R=[E-\varepsilon-\Sigma^R(E)]^{-1}$. The sum rule rests on canonical operator algebra and correct high-energy behavior; it is not a universal rule for arbitrary correlators.

$$
G^R(E)=\frac{1}{E-\varepsilon+i\Gamma},\quad A(E)=\frac{\Gamma}{\pi[(E-\varepsilon)^2+\Gamma^2]},\quad \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} A(E)\,\mathrm dE=1.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For $\varepsilon=1.0\,\mathrm{eV}$ and $\Gamma=0.2\,\mathrm{eV}$, the peak value is $A(\varepsilon)=1/(\pi\Gamma)\approx1.592\,\mathrm{eV}^{-1}$. Each half-width at half maximum is $\Gamma$, giving a full width of $0.4\,\mathrm{eV}$. The plotted peak has total area one, not unit peak height.

![Lorentzian spectral curve peaking at energy epsilon with a marked full width at half maximum of two Gamma.](../../../../media/course/U_PHY_094_EN.svg)

*In the energy convention, the single-particle spectral density has unit area; its full width at half maximum is $2\Gamma$.*

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What units does $A(E)$ have if energy is measured in electronvolts?
2. Find the full width at half maximum for $\Gamma=0.05\,\mathrm{eV}$.
3. Why is this normalization not automatically valid for a density–density correlator?

### Solutions

1. $\mathrm{eV}^{-1}$.
2. $0.10\,\mathrm{eV}$.
3. Its operator algebra and spectral sum rules are different.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The angular-frequency variable $\omega=E/\hbar$ requires consistent conversion of measure and prefactor. The Lorentzian shape assumes constant width and one simple pole. Positivity and unit area here refer only to the specified fermionic single-particle convention; other spectral functions can have different signs and normalizations.
