# M23-L1 – Optical Cavities, Modes and Quality Factors {#L1}

**Course area:** M23 – Bachelor: Applied Physics, Photonics and Nanotechnology

**Level:** Bachelor · Elective

**Prerequisites:** M7: interference; M10: electromagnetic waves. [Previous module: M22-L7](M22-L7.md).

**Learning objective:** Distinguish resonance conditions, free spectral range and cavity quality factor and calculate each.

## Question and Observation {#S1}

Two facing mirrors form a Fabry–Pérot cavity. Frequencies whose round-trip phase yields constructive interference have enhanced circulating fields. The resonances depend on cavity length, refractive index, loss and beam geometry, rather than the mirror coating alone. A laser cavity selects longitudinal and transverse modes; the passive optical response can be analysed before gain is introduced.

## Physical Model and Equations {#S2}

Assume a linear cavity with constant optical path $nL$ and mirrors contributing negligible phase. Constructive interference after a round trip requires $2nL=m\lambda$, giving $\nu_m=mc/(2nL)$ for integer $m$. Neighbouring longitudinal modes are separated by

$$
\Delta\nu=\frac{c}{2nL},\qquad \mathcal F\approx\frac{\pi\sqrt R}{1-R}\quad (R\to1).
$$

The second expression is the approximate finesse of a symmetric, lossless Fabry–Pérot cavity with equal intensity reflectivities $R$, particularly when $R$ approaches unity; the exact Airy linewidth also depends on the linewidth convention. With resonance width $\delta\nu$, finesse is $\mathcal F=\Delta\nu/\delta\nu$. By contrast $Q=\nu_0/\delta\nu$ refers to the centre frequency of a single resonance. Scattering, absorption, coupling and coating phase modify the measured width. In dispersive media, the group index rather than a constant phase index determines the mode spacing.

## Worked Example and Teaching Diagram {#S3}

For $L=0.10\,\mathrm m$ and $n=1$, the spacing is $\Delta\nu=1.499\,\mathrm{GHz}$. With $R=0.90$ the approximation yields $\mathcal F\approx29.8$ and $\delta\nu\approx50.3\,\mathrm{MHz}$. The figure shows a schematic cavity and calculated resonance peaks, not measured hardware data.

## Self-Assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find $\Delta\nu$ for $L=0.20\,\mathrm m$ and $n=1$.
2. Find $\delta\nu$ if $\Delta\nu=2\,\mathrm{GHz}$ and $\mathcal F=40$.
3. Why do $Q$ and $\mathcal F$ measure different properties?

### Solutions

1. $\Delta\nu=c/0.40\,\mathrm m\approx0.7495\,\mathrm{GHz}$.
2. $\delta\nu=2/40\,\mathrm{GHz}=50\,\mathrm{MHz}$.
3. Finesse compares spacing to width; quality factor compares the resonance centre frequency to its width.

## Validity Limits and Quality {#S5}

The approximation assumes a simple low-loss cavity, constant optical length and high mirror reflectivity. Real coatings add dispersion, temperature can change the path, and transverse modes can appear. The visual is a theoretical schematic.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Cavities select discrete optical modes by coherent round-trip feedback. Free spectral range, finesse and quality factor describe distinct aspects of that response.
