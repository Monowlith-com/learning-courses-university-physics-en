# M13-L2 – Entropy and the Statistical Definition of Temperature {#L2}

**Course area:** M13 – Bachelor: Statistical Physics

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** Microstates and multiplicity from M13-L1, together with derivatives and thermodynamics. [Previous lesson](M13-L1.md).

**Learning objective:** Apply Boltzmann entropy and derive the equilibrium condition for heat exchange.

## Why Entropy Uses a Logarithm {#S1}

At equilibrium, entropy describes how many microscopic possibilities are compatible with a given macrostate. For an appropriately defined discrete energy shell, Boltzmann's expression is

$$
S=k_B\ln\Omega.
$$

Boltzmann's constant $k_B=1.380649\times10^{-23}\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}$ converts a dimensionless state count into thermodynamic units. The logarithm is essential: the multiplicities of weakly coupled subsystems multiply while their entropies add, $\Omega_{AB}=\Omega_A\Omega_B$ and $S_{AB}=S_A+S_B$. Absolute entropy still depends on a well-defined state model; counting idealized model spins alone does not establish the actual entropy of a substance.

## Thermal Equilibrium as a Maximum {#S2}

Two weakly coupled subsystems $A$ and $B$ exchange energy while their total $E=E_A+E_B$ remains fixed. The favored equilibrium maximizes $S_A(E_A)+S_B(E-E_A)$. At an interior maximum, differentiation gives

$$
\frac{\partial S_A}{\partial E_A}=\frac{\partial S_B}{\partial E_B}=\frac{1}{T}.
$$

Temperature is therefore defined by the entropy slope with respect to internal energy while other equilibrium variables are held fixed. For infinitesimal reversible heat at fixed volume, $dS=\delta Q_\mathrm{rev}/T$. In ordinary systems an initial temperature difference drives energy exchange from hotter to colder as total entropy increases. The equation characterizes the equilibrium condition; it does not determine the rate of heat transfer.

## Example: Increasing the Accessible State Count {#S3}

Suppose changing a constraint in an ideal model raises the number of equilibrium-compatible states from $\Omega_1=2$ to $\Omega_2=8$. The entropy difference depends on their ratio, not the difference of the raw counts:

$$
\Delta S=k_B\ln\frac{\Omega_2}{\Omega_1}=k_B\ln4\approx1.91\times10^{-23}\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}.
$$

The illustration shows that $S$ increases monotonically but logarithmically with $\Omega$. Each doubling of the accessible state count adds $k_B\ln2$. This calculation describes a difference between equilibrium states; without specifying a process and its energy changes, it cannot by itself give a transferred heat quantity.

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find $\Delta S$ when the accessible state count triples.
2. Why does $S_{AB}=S_A+S_B$ hold for independent subsystems?
3. What thermodynamic condition holds at thermal equilibrium?

### Solutions

1. $\Delta S=k_B\ln3$.
2. Because $\Omega_{AB}=\Omega_A\Omega_B$ and $\ln(ab)=\ln a+\ln b$.
3. The entropy slopes agree: $\partial S_A/\partial E_A=\partial S_B/\partial E_B=1/T$.

## Discrete Models and Limits of Temperature {#S5}

The derivative $\partial S/\partial E$ presumes a suitable thermodynamic limit or smoothing. In very small systems discrete energies can make a naive derivative of the raw state count meaningless. Some specially prepared systems with an upper-bounded energy spectrum admit negative Boltzmann temperatures; these are not “colder than zero kelvin” but correspond to a distinct population ordering under restrictive conditions. The kinetic energy of an ordinary ideal gas is not upper bounded. Irreversible processes require an entropy balance including entropy production; they do not obey $dS=\delta Q/T$ for arbitrary heat exchanges. Nor is thermodynamic entropy simply subjective ignorance or colloquial disorder.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

The relation among state count, entropy and temperature explains equilibrium in isolated systems. Thermal contact with a large reservoir leads to the canonical ensemble.
