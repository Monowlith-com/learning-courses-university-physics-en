# M32-L5 – Accelerators, undulators and X-ray free-electron lasers {#L5}

**Course area:** M32 – Master: Experimental Instrumentation and Research Methods

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M10: relativity; M15: beam acceleration; M32-L4: spectroscopy · [Previous lesson](M32-L4.md).

**Learning objective:** Estimate the on-axis resonance wavelength of a planar undulator and distinguish spontaneous radiation from FEL amplification.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Relativistic electrons wiggle in the alternating magnetic field of an undulator and radiate. An X-ray free-electron laser additionally uses collective interaction between the electron bunch and the radiation field to amplify emission. Beam energy spread, bunch length and alignment are among the factors affecting output quality.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

For a planar undulator viewed on axis ($\theta=0$), the approximate fundamental resonance condition is $\lambda_r=\lambda_u(1+K^2/2)/(2\gamma^2)$. Here $\lambda_u$ is the magnetic period, $\gamma=E_e/(m_ec^2)$ is the electron Lorentz factor and $K$ is the dimensionless undulator strength. Quadratic dependence on $\gamma$ makes short wavelengths possible even with centimeter-scale magnet periods. The resonance relation alone does not predict FEL gain, saturation or temporal pulse structure.

$$
\lambda_r=\frac{\lambda_u}{2\gamma^2}\left(1+\frac{K^2}{2}\right)\quad\text{(planar undulator, on axis)}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

With $\lambda_u=0.030\,\mathrm m$, $\gamma=10^4$ and $K=1$, the result is $\lambda_r=0.030\cdot1.5/(2\cdot10^8)=2.25\cdot10^{-10}\,\mathrm m=0.225\,\mathrm{nm}$. The corresponding photon energy is approximately $5.51\,\mathrm{keV}$. The diagram shows an alternating magnet array and an idealized electron trajectory, not the complete FEL amplification process.

*Magnet period and electron energy determine the ideal resonance wavelength.*

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What happens to $\lambda_r$ if $\gamma$ doubles at fixed $K$ and $\lambda_u$?
2. Find $\lambda_r$ for $\lambda_u=20\,\mathrm{mm}$, $\gamma=10^4$ and $K=0$.
3. Why does the resonance equation not by itself predict FEL gain?

### Solutions

1. Wavelength decreases by a factor of four.
2. $\lambda_r=0.10\,\mathrm{nm}$.
3. Gain depends additionally on collective beam–field coupling, bunch properties and losses.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The formula refers to an ideal planar undulator on axis. Observation angle, harmonics, energy spread and magnetic errors modify the spectrum. The LCLS technical source supports the resonance relation; the SLAC overview provides application context.
