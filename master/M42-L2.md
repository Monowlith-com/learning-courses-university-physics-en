# M42-L2 – Fusion Plasma, Triple Product and Energy Confinement Time {#L2}

**Course area:** M42 – Master: Interdisciplinary Physics and Research Frontiers

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M42-L1: Debye shielding; M24: nuclear reactions and energy.

**Learning objective:** Compute the dimensional fusion triple product and distinguish plasma gain from net power-plant output.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Thermonuclear fusion requires a sufficient reaction rate and an energy balance capable of offsetting losses. Besides temperature and fuel density, the **energy confinement time** is central: in an idealized energy balance it is stored thermal energy divided by the loss power. It is neither the plasma-pulse duration nor the residence time of any single particle.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

A common comparison figure is $nT_i\tau_E$, with ion number density $n$, ion temperature $T_i$ and energy confinement time $\tau_E$. One may report $n$ in $\mathrm{m^{-3}}$, $T_i$ in $\mathrm{keV}$ and $\tau_E$ in seconds. Any **numerical threshold** is not a universal physical constant: it depends on fuel mix, temperature, radiative losses, reaction products and the specific definition of gain.

$$
\mathcal{P}_3=nT_i\tau_E,\qquad \tau_E=\frac{W_{\mathrm{th}}}{P_{\mathrm{loss}}}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For **synthetic** inputs $n=8.0\times10^{19}\,\mathrm{m^{-3}}$, $T_i=10\,\mathrm{keV}$ and $\tau_E=0.40\,\mathrm{s}$, the triple product is $\mathcal{P}_3=3.2\times10^{20}\,\mathrm{keV\,s\,m^{-3}}$. Doubling confinement time at fixed density and temperature doubles this value. Without a reaction and loss model, neither number alone establishes ignition or net electrical power.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Compute $\mathcal{P}_3$ at $\tau_E=0.80\,\mathrm{s}$ with other values unchanged.
2. Why is energy confinement time not pulse duration?
3. Why does a higher triple product not directly give power-plant efficiency?

### Solutions

1. $6.4\times10^{20}\,\mathrm{keV\,s\,m^{-3}}$.
2. It is set by stored thermal energy and loss rate; the discharge can continue much longer with heating.
3. Heating, fueling, cooling and thermal-to-electric conversion must also be accounted for.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

This is a dimensional scaling illustration, not an ignition-threshold claim. Ion/electron temperatures, transport, impurities, heat losses and plasma stability enter full fusion modeling.
