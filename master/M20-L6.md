# M20-L6 – Exoplanets, Transit Photometry and Detection Limits {#L6}

**Course area:** M20 – Bachelor: Astrophysics and Cosmology

**Level:** Bachelor · Elective

**Prerequisites:** M7: optics; M9: Kepler motion; M16: time series and signal-to-noise. [Previous lesson: Galaxy Dynamics, Rotation Curves and Dark Matter](M20-L5.md).

**Learning objective:** Convert a transit depth into a radius ratio and explain at least three detection biases or uncertainties.

## Question and Observation {#S1}

For a suitable line of sight, an orbiting planet crosses a stellar disc and blocks part of its light, causing a periodic decrease in measured flux. A transit first measures a ratio of projected areas, not an absolute planetary radius. An independently inferred stellar radius is needed to obtain the planet's radius. The radial-velocity method instead tracks motion of the star about the system barycentre and constrains a mass-dependent quantity. Combining both measurements constrains the planet's mean density.

## Physical Model and Equations {#S2}

For a central transit across a uniformly bright stellar disc with a small planet, the relative flux reduction obeys
$$
\delta\approx\left(\frac{R_p}{R_\star}\right)^2.
$$
Thus $R_p/R_\star\approx\sqrt{\delta}$. For a nearly circular orbit and $M_p\ll M_\star$, Kepler's third law yields
$$
a^3\approx\frac{GM_\star P^2}{4\pi^2}.
$$
Transit duration adds information about orbital geometry. Radial-velocity amplitudes typically constrain $M_p\sin i$, not $M_p$ alone; transit geometry also constrains orbital inclination $i$.

## Self-Assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find $R_p/R_\star$ for $\delta=0.0004$.
2. How does transit depth change if planetary radius doubles at fixed stellar radius?
3. Why does a single flux decrease not establish an exoplanet?

### Solutions

1. $R_p/R_\star=\sqrt{0.0004}=0.02$.
2. Transit depth becomes four times larger.
3. Stellar variability, instrument artefacts and eclipsing binaries can mimic a dip; repeated transits and independent checks are needed.

## Validity Limits and Quality {#S5}

Limb darkening, grazing geometry, stellar spots and correlated noise modify transit shapes. The method is geometrically selective and, at fixed data quality, favours larger planets and shorter periods. Eclipsing binary stars can also mimic planetary signals. The graphic contains only calculated teaching-model values and no survey mission data.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Transits primarily constrain radius ratios; modelling and independent methods are needed for robust characterisation.
