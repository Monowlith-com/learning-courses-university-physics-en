# M10-L8 – Electromagnetic Field Tensor and Covariant Maxwell Equations {#L8}

**Course area:** M10 – Bachelor: Advanced Electrodynamics

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** Four-vectors, index notation, Lorentz transformations and Maxwell equations. [Previous lesson](M10-L7.md).

**Learning objective:** Define the field tensor with a fixed metric, compute two invariants and write Maxwell’s equations covariantly.

## Why an Antisymmetric Tensor? {#S1}

Electric and magnetic components mix when the inertial frame changes. A tensor with two spacetime indices encodes that mixing. Antisymmetry reduces sixteen matrix entries to six independent components: three electric and three magnetic. The tensor lets Maxwell equations retain their form under Lorentz transformations. Such calculations require an explicit metric and component definitions. Changing conventions midway through a derivation produces apparent sign errors; copying a matrix from a differently defined source does not resolve them.

## Definitions and Covariant Form {#S2}

Choose $x^\mu=(ct,x,y,z)$ and metric $\eta_{\mu\nu}=\operatorname{diag}(-1,+1,+1,+1)$. Define $A^\mu=(\phi/c,\boldsymbol A)$, $\partial^\mu=\eta^{\mu\nu}\partial_\nu$ and

$$
F^{\mu\nu}=\partial^\mu A^\nu-\partial^\nu A^\mu.
$$

It follows that $F^{0i}=E_i/c$ and $F^{ij}=\varepsilon^{ijk}B_k$. The matrix is 

$$
F^{\mu\nu}=\begin{pmatrix}0&E_x/c&E_y/c&E_z/c\\-E_x/c&0&B_z&-B_y\\-E_y/c&-B_z&0&B_x\\-E_z/c&B_y&-B_x&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$

With $J^\mu=(c\rho,\boldsymbol J)$, the inhomogeneous Maxwell equations become 

$$
\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu}=-\mu_0J^\nu.
$$

The homogeneous equations can be expressed as $\partial_\lambda F_{\mu\nu}+\partial_\mu F_{\nu\lambda}+\partial_\nu F_{\lambda\mu}=0$.

## Example: Full Tensor Contraction {#S3}

Consider $\boldsymbol E=E_0\boldsymbol e_x$ and $\boldsymbol B=B_0\boldsymbol e_z$. The only independent nonzero entries are $F^{01}=E_0/c$ and $F^{12}=B_0$, plus their antisymmetric counterparts. Lowering indices with $\eta$ changes the sign only for time-space components: $F_{01}=-E_0/c$, $F_{12}=B_0$. The *full* Einstein sum therefore gives

$$
F^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}=2\left(B_0^2-\frac{E_0^2}{c^2}\right).
$$

With $B_0=0$ the answer is $-2E_0^2/c^2$; with $E_0=0$ it is $2B_0^2$. The diagram shows the six independent matrix positions and their antisymmetric partners; each pair appears twice in the complete contraction.

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. How many independent entries does $F^{\mu\nu}$ have in four dimensions?
2. What is $F^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}$ when $\boldsymbol E=0$?
3. Which elements must be checked together when changing sign conventions?

### Solutions

1. Six: $4\cdot3/2=6$.
2. $2B^2$.
3. Definition of $F$, its matrix, potentials, metric, and the sign of the inhomogeneous Maxwell equation.

## Sign Conventions and Verification {#S5}

The displayed field-tensor matrix depends on metric signature, potential convention and the definition of $F^{\mu\nu}$. With the present choices, $\partial_iF^{i0}=-\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol E/c$. The inhomogeneous Maxwell equation therefore needs a negative sign:

$$
\partial_\mu F^{\mu\nu}=-\mu_0J^\nu.
$$

That sign follows from the explicit definition of $F^{\mu\nu}$ and the metric, rather than being an independent extra convention. The time component ($\nu=0$) reproduces Gauss’s law and spatial components ($\nu=i$) give Ampère–Maxwell.  Using the alternative definition $F\mapsto-F$ changes both signs together and is equally valid.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}
The tensor unifies electrodynamics with special relativity. Consistency must be checked against electric Gauss’s law and the full tensor contraction.
