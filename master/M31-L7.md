# M31-L7 – Physics-informed neural networks and PDE residuals {#L7}

**Course area:** M31 – Master: Numerical and Computational Physics

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M31-L2: PDE discretization; M31-L5: optimization; neural-network basics · [Previous lesson](M31-L6.md).

**Learning objective:** Formulate a PINN loss from a PDE residual and boundary data and identify training limitations.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) use a differentiable function approximation whose output is constrained by differential equations as well as available data. The name denotes a training strategy, not a guarantee of physical correctness. Residuals are evaluated at selected collocation points, and the resulting optimization may be poorly conditioned.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

For one-dimensional heat transport $\partial_tu-D\partial_{xx}u=0$, use a neural approximation $u_\theta(x,t)$. The PDE residual is $R_\theta=\partial_tu_\theta-D\partial_{xx}u_\theta$. One possible loss is $\mathcal L(\theta)=w_R\,\langle R_\theta^2\rangle_\Omega+w_B\,\langle (u_\theta-g)^2\rangle_{\partial\Omega}+w_I\,\langle(u_\theta-u_0)^2\rangle_{t=0}$. The weights trade off requirements. Automatic differentiation computes network derivatives, but a small sampled residual alone proves neither uniqueness nor correct boundary physics throughout the domain.

$$
R_\theta=\partial_tu_\theta-D\partial_{xx}u_\theta.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

In a simplified sample, let mean squared PDE residual be $0.04$, boundary loss $0.01$, and weights $w_R=2$, $w_B=3$, with no initial-condition term. The loss is $\mathcal L=2\cdot0.04+3\cdot0.01=0.11$. The figure shows the flow from input coordinates through a learned approximation to PDE and boundary losses; it is conceptual, not a trained network.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find $\mathcal L$ for $w_R=w_B=1$, residual loss $0.02$ and boundary loss $0.03$.
2. Which derivatives are needed for the heat-equation residual?
3. Why might low training loss still produce poor predictions?

### Solutions

1. $\mathcal L=0.05$.
2. The first derivative in time and second derivative in space.
3. Collocation coverage, weights, boundary constraints or optimization may be insufficient.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The cited research addresses gradient and loss-balancing pathologies; its abstract does not verify this original numeric example. PINNs are not universally more accurate or efficient than established finite-element, finite-volume or spectral methods. External validation and uncertainty analysis remain necessary.
