# M5-L8 – Conduction, Convection and Thermal Radiation {#L8}

**Course area:** M5 – Bachelor: Heat and Thermodynamics

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** Temperature gradient, energy conservation and elementary geometry. [Previous lesson](M5-L7.md).

**Learning objective:** Distinguish heat-transfer mechanisms and calculate simple conduction and radiation balances.

## Three Heat Transfer Mechanisms {#S1}

Energy can be transferred as heat by microscopic exchange within material (**conduction**), by moving fluids (**convection**) or by electromagnetic emission and absorption (**radiation**). Heat is transferred energy, whereas temperature describes the local thermodynamic state. In ordinary conduction, temperature gradients drive thermal energy from hot to cold. Convection combines fluid motion with near-wall regions where conduction is significant. Thermal radiation does not require a material medium, so it also operates in vacuum. Several mechanisms commonly act together in devices; separating them is primarily a modelling choice.

## Local and Global Heat Balances {#S2}

Fourier's law for isotropic media writes heat-flux density as $\boldsymbol q=-\lambda\nabla T$, where thermal conductivity $\lambda$ is in $\mathrm{W/(m\,K)}$. For stationary one-dimensional conduction through a plane layer of thickness $d$ and area $A$ without internal sources,

$$
\dot Q_{\mathrm{cond}}=\lambda A\frac{T_h-T_c}{d}.
$$

For an idealised grey surface with emissivity $\varepsilon$ inside a large uniform-temperature environment, the Stefan–Boltzmann law gives net radiative power

$$
\dot Q_{\mathrm{rad}}=\varepsilon\sigma A(T_s^4-T_{\mathrm{sur}}^4),\qquad\sigma\approx5.670374419\times10^{-8}\,\mathrm{W/(m^2\,K^4)}.
$$

An empirical model for free or forced convection at a surface is $\dot Q_{\mathrm{conv}}=hA(T_s-T_f)$. Coefficient $h$ depends on shape, flow and material properties rather than being a universal material constant.

## Worked Example: Wall and Radiation {#S3}

A homogeneous wall with $A=2.0\,\mathrm{m^2}$, $d=0.10\,\mathrm m$ and $\lambda=0.80\,\mathrm{W/(m\,K)}$ separates surfaces at $T_h=310\,\mathrm K$ and $T_c=290\,\mathrm K$. Its steady conduction power is

$$
\dot Q_{\mathrm{cond}}=0.80\cdot2.0\cdot(310-290)/0.10=320\,\mathrm W.
$$

Separately, an ideal black surface with $A=1.0\,\mathrm{m^2}$ at $T_s=310\,\mathrm K$ radiating to surroundings at $T_{\mathrm{sur}}=290\,\mathrm K$ loses roughly $123\,\mathrm W$ net. These are **two different model setups**: the powers should not be added into a single wall model without coupling boundary conditions and areas. ## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. How does $\dot Q_{\mathrm{cond}}$ change if thickness $d$ is doubled with all other quantities unchanged?
2. Give the units of $\lambda$ and $h$.
3. Why is it incorrect to insert Celsius readings in the $T^4$ radiation law?

### Solutions

1. Steady conduction power is halved.
2. $[\lambda]=\mathrm{W/(m\,K)}$ and $[h]=\mathrm{W/(m^2\,K)}$.
3. Stefan–Boltzmann uses absolute thermodynamic temperature; the fourth power is not invariant under a shifted temperature scale.

## Model Limits and Coupling {#S5}

Fourier's linear relation is effective for many continua, but nanoscale paths, strong anisotropy and temperature-dependent conductivities may require extended models. Surface temperatures alone may not determine the full temperature profile: contact resistance and additional layers matter. The radiation expression assumes a suitable environment and an approximation of diffuse grey emission; surfaces facing one another require view factors. The $T^4$ dependence always uses kelvin and applies to **absolute** temperatures. In coupled problems, the energy balance must hold at each interface, or the transfer model becomes inconsistent.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Conduction, convection and radiation require different material and boundary models. Complex thermal systems need a coupled energy balance across these mechanisms.
