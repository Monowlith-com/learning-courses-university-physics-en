# M29-L6 – Schwarzschild spacetime and gravitational redshift {#L6}

**Course area:** M29 – Master: General relativity and cosmology

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M29-L2: proper time; M29-L5: field equations; M20: stellar physics · [Previous lesson](M29-L5.md).

**Learning objective:** Compute a clock rate and a frequency shift between stationary observers from the static Schwarzschild metric.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Outside a spherical nonrotating mass, the vacuum geometry is described by the Schwarzschild solution. A stationary clock in this static field measures different proper time per unit Schwarzschild coordinate time depending on radius. Light exchanged between stationary emitters and receivers consequently changes frequency.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

Let $r_s=2GM/c^2$ and $d\Omega^2=d\theta^2+\sin^2\theta\,d\varphi^2$. Outside the source the Schwarzschild metric is $ds^2=-(1-r_s/r)c^2dt^2+(1-r_s/r)^{-1}dr^2+r^2d\Omega^2$. For a stationary clock at fixed $r,\theta,\varphi$, $d\tau/dt=\sqrt{1-r_s/r}$. Along a light ray in the static geometry, the corresponding Killing energy is conserved. For emission at $r_e$ and reception by a stationary observer at $r_o$, $f_o/f_e=\sqrt{(1-r_s/r_e)/(1-r_s/r_o)}$. For an observer very far away, the denominator approaches one.

$$
\frac{f_{\infty}}{f_e}=\sqrt{1-\frac{r_s}{r_e}},\qquad r_s=\frac{2GM}{c^2},\quad r_e>r_s.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

A stationary emitter at $r_e=10r_s$ sends a light signal. A stationary receiver at infinity measures $f_\infty/f_e=\sqrt{0.9}\approx0.94868$, a decrease of about $5.13\%$. The plot shows this factor versus $r/r_s$. The curve applies only to the frequency measurements of stationary observers in the exterior region.

![Curve of the frequency ratio at infinity versus radius in Schwarzschild-radius units; the value at ten radii is marked.](../../../../media/course/U_PHY_258_EN.svg)

*A signal emitted deeper in a static gravitational field is measured at lower frequency far away.*

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find $f_\infty/f_e$ for $r_e=4r_s$.
2. What is the dimension of $r_s$?
3. Can a stationary timelike worldline in Schwarzschild coordinates extend through $r=r_s$?

### Solutions

1. $\sqrt{3/4}\approx0.8660$.
2. Length; $GM/c^2$ has units of metres.
3. No; a massive observer cannot remain stationary at the horizon.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

Schwarzschild describes a spherical exterior with no rotation or charge. The coordinate singularity at $r_s$ is not a physical curvature singularity. Real emitters and observers can also have Doppler shifts.
