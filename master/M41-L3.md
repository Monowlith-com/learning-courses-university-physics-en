# M41-L3 – Ultrashort Pulses, Time–Bandwidth and Dispersion {#L3}

**Course area:** M41 – Master: Photonics and Ultrafast Optics

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M41-L2; M7: Fourier optics; M31: numerical Fourier methods.

**Learning objective:** Compute the minimum spectral bandwidth of a transform-limited Gaussian pulse.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

A short light pulse is not a single monochromatic oscillation. Its envelope results from coherent superposition of frequency components. For a specified pulse shape, shorter temporal duration requires wider spectral bandwidth. Spectral phase may broaden the pulse without changing the spectral-intensity bandwidth.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

For a transform-limited, unchirped Gaussian pulse, the product of intensity full widths at half maximum in time $\Delta t$ and ordinary frequency $\Delta\nu$ is $2\ln 2/\pi\approx0.441$. The coefficient does **not** apply to arbitrary pulse shapes or RMS widths. For $\Delta\lambda\ll\lambda_0$, $\Delta\lambda\approx\lambda_0^2\Delta\nu/c$. Dispersive propagation modifies relative spectral phase and may introduce temporal chirp.

$$
\Delta t_{\mathrm{FWHM}}\,\Delta\nu_{\mathrm{FWHM}}=\frac{2\ln 2}{\pi}\simeq0.441\quad\text{(transform-limited Gaussian pulse)}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

A transform-limited Gaussian pulse with $\Delta t=100\,\mathrm{fs}$ has $\Delta\nu\approx4.41\,\mathrm{THz}$. Near a carrier wavelength of $800\,\mathrm{nm}$ this corresponds locally to about $9.42\,\mathrm{nm}$ spectral width. The diagram separates temporal and spectral intensity; the two horizontal axes are not interchangeable.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What minimum $\Delta\nu$ corresponds to a $50\,\mathrm{fs}$ Gaussian pulse?
2. Why can spectral-phase chirp lengthen a pulse without changing $\Delta\nu$?
3. Does the coefficient $0.441$ apply to a sech-shaped pulse?

### Solutions

1. About $8.82\,\mathrm{THz}$.
2. Spectral components acquire changed relative group delays or phases.
3. No. The time–bandwidth product depends on pulse shape and width convention.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

This does not determine pulse energy, peak intensity or practical compressibility of a laser system. Nonlinear propagation, spatiotemporal coupling, residual chirp and measurement resolution modify the simple Fourier limit.
