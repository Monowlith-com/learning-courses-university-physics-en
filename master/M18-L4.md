# M18-L4 – Electrical Measurements: Characteristics and RC Time {#L4}

**Course area:** M18 – Bachelor: Physics Laboratory Practice

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** M6: DC circuits; M16: calibration and uncertainty. [Previous lesson](M18-L3.md).

**Learning objective:** Account for instrument loading and estimate an RC time constant from a voltage trace.

## Research Question and Model {#S1}

An observed current–voltage characteristic includes the measuring instrument. An ideal voltmeter draws no current, whereas a real one has finite input resistance. Loading can be important for high-impedance circuits. An ammeter also adds resistance to the current path. Draw an equivalent circuit identifying source, specimen, leads and meter before interpreting any measurement. Local electrical safety rules and approved limits still apply at low voltage; this course does not specify the assembly of unverified equipment.

## Equations and Assumptions {#S2}

For an ohmic resistor $U=RI$ while its response remains linear. An ideal capacitor discharging through a resistor obeys $\mathrm dU/\mathrm dt=-U/(RC)$, hence $U(t)=U_0\exp(-t/\tau)$ with $\tau=RC$. Charging from an ideal constant-voltage source gives $U_C(t)=U_0(1-\exp(-t/\tau))$. After one time constant the charging voltage has reached $1-1/e\approx0.632$ of its final value. Cable and input capacitance can change the effective time constant. Always state whether the trace represents charging or discharging.

$$
\tau=RC,\qquad U_C(t)=U_0\left(1-e^{-t/\tau}\right)
$$

## Worked Example and Diagram {#S3}

Synthetic example: $R=100\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$ and $C=10\,\mathrm{\mu F}$ give $\tau=1.00\,\mathrm s$. Charging ideally toward $U_0=5.00\,\mathrm V$ yields $U_C(1\,\mathrm s)\approx3.16\,\mathrm V$. Do not assess a fitted trace solely by visually overlaying it on data points; also inspect residuals $U_{\mathrm{obs}}-U_{\mathrm{model}}$. A systematic time offset may indicate trigger delay rather than an incorrect resistor. Record time-zero definition and sampling rate.

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Calculate $\tau$ for $R=100\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$ and $C=10\,\mathrm{\mu F}$.
2. What is $U_C(\tau)$ for $U_0=5\,\mathrm V$?
3. Why does finite voltmeter input resistance matter?

### Solutions

1. $\tau=100000\cdot10^{-5}=1.00\,\mathrm s$.
2. $5(1-e^{-1})\approx3.16\,\mathrm V$.
3. Meter input resistance loads the test device and can change voltage and effective time constant.

## Limits and Quality Control {#S5}

For independent relative uncertainties in $R$ and $C$, the relative uncertainty of $\tau=RC$ is approximately their quadrature sum. Correlated temperature dependence requires a different treatment. The exponential model assumes constant parameters and a linear circuit. Non-exponential residuals may indicate leakage, instrument loading or additional elements, but cannot uniquely diagnose them without controls. One fitted time constant therefore does not prove a particular physical circuit.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Electrical data require a documented equivalent circuit, time reference and residual check.
