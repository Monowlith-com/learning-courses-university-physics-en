# M33-L2 – Two-Level Atoms and Coherent Rabi Oscillations {#L2}

**Course area:** M33 – Master: Atomic, Molecular and Optical Physics

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M12: quantum time evolution; M26: density matrix; M33-L1.

**Learning objective:** Find a resonant π-pulse duration from the Rabi frequency and distinguish coherent dynamics from decoherence.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

An atom driven between two selected states by a narrow optical field can often be approximated as a two-level system. Under coherent resonant excitation, the excited-state population oscillates periodically. Measured fluorescence also depends on decay out of the excited state.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

In a rotating frame and under the rotating-wave approximation, the coherent drive may be written $H/\hbar=(\Delta\sigma_z+\Omega\sigma_x)/2$. Here $\Delta$ is the angular-frequency detuning and $\Omega$ the resonant Rabi angular frequency. Starting in the ground state, on resonance with no dissipation one obtains $P_e(t)=\sin^2(\Omega t/2)$. A π pulse satisfies $\Omega t_\pi=\pi$ and ideally inverts the population. Decay and dephasing require optical Bloch equations with relaxation terms.

$$
P_e(t)=\sin^2\!\left(\frac{\Omega t}{2}\right),\qquad t_\pi=\frac{\pi}{\Omega}\quad(\Delta=0).
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For $\Omega/(2\pi)=1.0\,\mathrm{MHz}$, $t_\pi=0.50\,\mathrm{\mu s}$. At $0.25\,\mathrm{\mu s}$ the ideal excited-state population is $P_e=1/2$. The graphic follows $P_e$ over two cycles. Note that $\Omega$ is an angular frequency, so the $2\pi$ conversion matters.

![Sinusoidal-squared excited-state population with marked π and 2π pulses.](../../../../media/course/U_PHY_301_EN.svg)

*Undamped resonant Rabi driving: a π pulse inverts the population.*

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find the π-pulse duration for $\Omega/(2\pi)=2\,\mathrm{MHz}$.
2. What ideal population follows a $2\pi$ pulse?
3. What physical processes damp Rabi oscillations?

### Solutions

1. $t_\pi=0.25\,\mathrm{\mu s}$.
2. $P_e=0$ if the system started in its ground state.
3. Spontaneous emission, collisions, drive fluctuations, and phase noise.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The sine-squared law assumes resonance, coherent driving, a valid rotating-wave approximation, and negligible decay during the pulse. It is not a general solution for strongly open systems. MIT provides the course framework for optical Bloch equations; the numerical example is constructed independently.
