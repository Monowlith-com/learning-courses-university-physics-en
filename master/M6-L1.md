# M6-L1 – Electric Charge, Coulomb Force and Electric Field {#L1}

**Course area:** M6 – Bachelor: Electricity and Magnetism

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** Vectors, forces, SI units and inverse-square laws. [Previous lesson](M5-L8.md).

**Learning objective:** Explain charge and the field definition; superpose forces from several point charges as vectors.

## Charge and Conservation {#S1}

Electric charge $q$ is a conserved, signed property of matter. Its SI unit is the coulomb, $\mathrm C=\mathrm{A\,s}$. Total charge remains constant in a closed system even if individual objects exchange charge through contact or friction. Electrons carry $-e$ and protons $+e$, where $e=1.602176634\times10^{-19}\,\mathrm C$. Macroscopic charging results from an imbalance of positive and negative charge. Like charges repel; unlike charges attract. Forces act without mechanical contact; in electrostatics the interaction is described through a field defined at each point. The signs determine the force direction, not the inverse-square dependence on distance.

## From Force to Field {#S2}

For stationary point charges in vacuum let $r=|\boldsymbol r_1-\boldsymbol r_2|$. The unit vector $\hat{\boldsymbol r}_{12}$ points from source 1 towards charge 2. Coulomb's law is

$$
\boldsymbol F_{1\to2}=\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q_1q_2}{r^2}\hat{\boldsymbol r}_{12},\qquad k_e=\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\approx8.988\times10^9\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C^2}.
$$

The electric field is defined by $\boldsymbol E=\boldsymbol F/q_\mathrm{test}$ using an ideally small positive test charge. For a point source $Q$ we obtain

$$
\boldsymbol E(\boldsymbol r)=k_e\frac{Q}{|\boldsymbol r-\boldsymbol r_Q|^3}(\boldsymbol r-\boldsymbol r_Q).
$$

Its units are $\mathrm{N/C}=\mathrm{V/m}$. **Superposition** is vector addition: $\boldsymbol E_\mathrm{total}=\sum_i\boldsymbol E_i$. The field is a property of the sources and the observation point; an ideal test charge does not disturb it.

## Worked Example: Two Sources {#S3}

Two identical positive charges $Q=1.00\,\mathrm{nC}$ lie at $x=-0.10\,\mathrm m$ and $x=+0.10\,\mathrm m$. Their field contributions at the midpoint cancel exactly by symmetry: $\boldsymbol E(0)=\boldsymbol 0$. At $x=+0.20\,\mathrm m$, however, both contributions point to the right. The source distances are $r_1=0.30\,\mathrm m$ and $r_2=0.10\,\mathrm m$. With $k_e\approx8.988\times10^9$,

$$
E_x=k_eQ\left(\frac{1}{0.30^2}+\frac{1}{0.10^2}\right)\approx999\,\mathrm{N/C}.
$$

A positive $2.0\,\mathrm{nC}$ test charge experiences $F_x=qE_x\approx2.00\,\mathrm{\mu N}$. The diagram shows the source-field directions and vector addition at an observation point. Adding only magnitudes would be incorrect at the midpoint.

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Give the electric-field unit in terms of force and charge.
2. How does $|\boldsymbol F|$ change if the distance between point charges doubles?
3. Find $E_x$ halfway between equal charges $+Q$ on the left and $-Q$ on the right.

### Solutions

1. $\mathrm{N/C}$, equivalently $\mathrm{V/m}$.
2. It drops to one quarter.
3. Both contributions point from $+Q$ towards $-Q$; with separation $2a$, $E_x=2k_eQ/a^2$ to the right.

## Field Lines and Model Limits {#S5}

Field lines are a **visualisation**, not material strands. Their tangents show local direction and their approximate density indicates field strength. They originate at positive charge and terminate at negative charge or infinity. The point-charge model diverges as $r\to0$. Real charge distributions have finite size, and atomic or quantum descriptions may be needed at short distances. These formulas describe electrostatics in vacuum; polarisation in matter and time-dependent fields require additional theory. An actual test probe may disturb the field and the ideal test charge must be regarded as a limiting construction.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Charge conservation, Coulomb’s law and the vector field establish electrostatics. We next use flux and potential to describe fields efficiently.
