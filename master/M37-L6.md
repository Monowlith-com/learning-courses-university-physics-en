# M37-L6 – Multimessenger Astronomy and Chance Coincidences {#L6}

**Course area:** M37 – Master: Astrophysics and Cosmology

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M36: particle and count detection; M37-L5: cosmological observations · [Previous lesson](M37-L5.md).

**Learning objective:** Calculate the expected chance-coincidence count for independent stationary triggers and distinguish it from source identification.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Electromagnetic radiation, neutrinos and gravitational waves carry different information about astrophysical events. A time coincidence across detectors is a clue, not proof of a common source. Direction, timing, significance, background and selection effects must be assessed together. Real-time alerts coordinate follow-up observations.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

For two independent stationary Poisson trigger streams of rates $R_A$ and $R_B$, a symmetric time cut $|t_A-t_B|\le\Delta t$ over observing duration $T$ gives approximate expected accidental pairs $N_{\mathrm{chance}}\simeq2\Delta t\,R_AR_BT$ when windows are short and rates stationary. This counts event pairs, not necessarily independent physical events; sky positions are not used. Overlapping windows or time-varying rates require more careful analysis.

$$
N_{\mathrm{chance}}\simeq2\,\Delta t\,R_A R_B T\qquad\text{(independent stationary triggers)}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

Assume $R_A=1$ and $R_B=5$ triggers per day, $\Delta t=100\,\mathrm s$ and $T=365$ days. In common day units the approximation gives $N_{\mathrm{chance}}=2(100/86400)\cdot1\cdot5\cdot365\approx4.23$ chance pairs per observing year. This is a constructed statistics problem, not an IceCube result. The original diagram shows three information channels and a coincidence window.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What happens to $N_{\mathrm{chance}}$ if $\Delta t$ is halved?
2. What expectation results when $R_B=10$ rather than $5$ per day?
3. Why is a temporal match without direction not proof of one source?

### Solutions

1. About $2.11$ pairs.
2. About $8.45$ pairs.
3. Independent background triggers can fall in the same time window by chance.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The Poisson model assumes stationary independent streams and negligible boundary/selection effects. Real multimessenger analyses combine energy, sky-location uncertainty, exposure, detector sensitivity and trial corrections. The toy result assigns no actual astrophysical source.
