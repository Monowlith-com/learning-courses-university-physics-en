# M12-L4 – Harmonic Oscillator and Ladder Operators {#L4}

**Course area:** M12 – Bachelor: Advanced Quantum Mechanics

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** Harmonic motion, commutators, eigenvalues, and M12-L1–L2. [Previous lesson](M12-L3.md).

**Learning objective:** Derive the spectrum algebraically and explain zero-point energy and parity.

## A Local Model Near Equilibrium {#S1}

Near a stable minimum, a smooth potential can often be expanded to quadratic order. The resulting local model is $V(x)=m\omega^2x^2/2$. Quantum oscillators therefore apply far beyond a mechanical spring, including approximations to molecular normal modes, lattice motion and field modes. Unlike a classical oscillator, the quantum system cannot have precisely zero spread in both position and momentum. Even the ground state has nonzero kinetic energy and spatial extent.

## Creation and Annihilation Algebra {#S2}

With $[x,p]=i\hbar$, define

$$
a=\sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}\,x+\frac{i}{\sqrt{2m\hbar\omega}}p,\qquad [a,a^\dagger]=1
$$

The Hamiltonian becomes $H=\hbar\omega(a^\dagger a+1/2)$. For the number operator $N=a^\dagger a$, nonnegative expectation values and the ladder relations $[N,a]=-a$ and $[N,a^\dagger]=a^\dagger$ yield integer eigenvalues $n=0,1,2,\ldots$ for normalizable states and the spectrum 

$$
E_n=\hbar\omega\left(n+\frac12\right)
$$

Ladder action is $a|n\rangle=\sqrt n\,|n-1\rangle$ and $a^\dagger|n\rangle=\sqrt{n+1}\,|n+1\rangle$. The ground-state position density is Gaussian, excited wavefunctions have $n$ nodes and parity alternates with $n$.

## Example: Zero-Point Energy and Transition {#S3}

For a model vibration with frequency $f=1.00\times10^{13}\,\mathrm{Hz}$, $\omega=2\pi f$. The zero-point energy is $E_0=hf/2\simeq0.0207\,\mathrm{eV}$. Adjacent levels differ by $\Delta E=hf\simeq0.0414\,\mathrm{eV}$. Resonant electromagnetic excitation may drive transitions subject to the coupling and selection rules. The figure compares the parabolic potential with horizontal energy levels and a Gaussian ground-state density near equilibrium. An energy-level line must not be mistaken for a classical path in position space.

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Calculate $E_2/E_0$.
2. Evaluate $a|0\rangle$.
3. What happens to level spacing when $f$ doubles?

### Solutions

1. $(5/2)/(1/2)=5$.
2. The ground state is annihilated: $a|0\rangle=0$.
3. $\Delta E=hf$ doubles.

## Observability and Anharmonicity {#S5}

Anharmonic potentials generally have unequal level spacings, so the exact harmonic ladder-operator algebra no longer applies. An ideal quadratic potential has infinitely many bound levels, whereas a dissociating real molecule has only finitely many bound vibrational states. Although adjacent harmonic levels are separated by $\hbar\omega$, spectral predictions also need the coupling operator: not every energetically possible transition is optically allowed. Zero-point energy is not a reservoir from which an isolated oscillator can freely extract usable work.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

The equally spaced spectrum and zero-point energy follow from ladder operators of the harmonic Hamiltonian.
