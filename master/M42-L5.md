# M42-L5 – Particle Searches, Null Results and Poisson Upper Limits {#L5}

**Course area:** M42 – Master: Interdisciplinary Physics and Research Frontiers

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M36: particle physics and counting experiments; M30: Standard Model.

**Learning objective:** Derive a Poisson upper bound from an idealized zero-count result and distinguish it from evidence for a discovery.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Searches for axions, dark photons or supersymmetric particles test models beyond the established particle spectrum. A **null result** does not generically disprove an entire theory; it constrains parameter regions subject to a stated signal and detector model. The Particle Data Group catalogues such searches; listing a hypothesis is not evidence of discovery.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

For an ideal background-free Poisson count with expectation $\mu\ge0$, $P(N=0\mid\mu)=e^{-\mu}$. If $N=0$ is observed, a one-sided classical upper bound set by $P(N=0\mid\mu_{95})=0.05$ yields $\mu_{95}=-\ln(0.05)\simeq3.00$. With known efficiency $\varepsilon$ and exposure $\mathcal E$, the bound on a rate $R$ becomes $R_{95}=\mu_{95}/(\varepsilon\mathcal E)$.

$$
P(N=0\mid\mu)=e^{-\mu},\qquad \mu_{95}=-\ln(0.05)\approx 2.996.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

A **synthetic** search records zero events, assumes no background and has $\varepsilon=0.50$ over $10^6$ independent exposure units. Then $R_{95}\approx2.996/(0.5\times10^6)=5.99\times10^{-6}$ events per exposure unit. The 95% classical confidence construction has a repeated-sampling coverage interpretation; it is **not a 95% posterior probability** that the parameter lies below the reported value.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Determine $\mu_{90}$ from $e^{-\mu_{90}}=0.10$.
2. What happens to $R_{95}$ if efficiency halves with all else unchanged?
3. Why does a null result not prove that no hypothetical particles exist?

### Solutions

1. $\mu_{90}=-\ln(0.10)\approx2.303$.
2. The rate bound doubles when efficiency halves.
3. The bound applies only within the specified parameter and background-free detector model.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

Actual analyses account for background, statistical and systematic uncertainties, signal shapes, detector acceptance and multiple testing. The Poisson zero-count exercise is not a published exclusion curve or a discovery statistic.
