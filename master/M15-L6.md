# M15-L6 – Elementary Particles and the Standard Model {#L6}

**Course area:** M15 – Bachelor: Nuclear and Particle Physics

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** Quantum mechanics, electrodynamics, M15-L5 and relativistic energy basics. [Previous lesson](M15-L5.md).

**Learning objective:** Classify fermion generations, gauge bosons and the Higgs field in the Standard Model.

## Matter Fields in Three Generations {#S1}

The Standard Model contains fundamental fermions arranged in three generations. Each generation contains two quark flavours and a charged lepton with its neutrino; corresponding antiparticles exist. Quarks carry color charge and at low energies usually appear inside hadrons. A proton has two up and one down valence quarks, a neutron one up and two down. The valence description does not erase gluon and sea-quark contributions to proton structure in quantum field theory. Neutrinos have no electric charge and participate in weak interactions and gravity, the latter not being part of the Standard Model.

## Interactions and Gauge Bosons {#S2}

The model covers electromagnetic, weak and strong interactions. The photon couples to electric charge; $W^\pm$ and $Z^0$ mediate weak processes, while eight gluons transmit color interactions among quarks. Electromagnetism and the weak force are unified in electroweak theory. The Higgs field has a nonzero vacuum expectation value and its couplings contribute to masses of elementary fermions and weak gauge bosons. Much of the mass of composite hadrons instead arises from QCD dynamics. The quantized Higgs field has an observed scalar excitation, the Higgs boson. It is inaccurate to say that every particle receives all of its mass through one simple mechanism.

## Example: Quark Electric Charge {#S3}

Up- and down-quark electric charges are $Q_u=+2e/3$ and $Q_d=-e/3$. In the valence picture, proton and neutron charges are

$$
Q_p=2Q_u+Q_d=+e,\qquad Q_n=Q_u+2Q_d=0.
$$

Electric charge is additive and conserved. The figure tabulates three fermion generations and gauge-boson classes; cell sizes do not represent relative particle masses. Proton mass is about $938\,\mathrm{MeV}/c^2$ and cannot be obtained by adding small current-quark masses alone. Binding and field dynamics dominate.

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What is the proton valence-quark charge in units of $e$?
2. Which three interactions are in the Standard Model?
3. Why is the proton mass not explained solely by the Higgs field?

### Solutions

1. $+2/3+2/3-1/3=+1$.
2. Electromagnetic, weak and strong.
3. Much arises from QCD quark–gluon dynamics.

## Scope of the Standard Model {#S5}

The Standard Model is highly successful but not a complete theory of all known phenomena. It does not describe gravity, does not identify dark matter and does not derive its measured parameters from one simple principle. Neutrino oscillations establish nonzero neutrino masses and require an extension beyond the minimal massless-neutrino formulation. Its language is quantum fields and interactions: a table of colored blocks must not be interpreted as a mechanical assembly diagram of electrons or Higgs bosons. Experimental uncertainties and model dependence remain part of every physical claim.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

The Standard Model explains matter fields and three interactions; its open questions must be distinguished from tested predictions.
