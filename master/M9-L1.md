# M9-L1 – Stationary Action and Euler–Lagrange Equations {#L1}

**Course area:** M9 – Bachelor: Analytical Mechanics

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** Derivatives, integration by parts, Newtonian mechanics and oscillator energy. [Previous lesson](M8-L8.md).

**Learning objective:** Derive the Euler–Lagrange equation from stationary action and apply it to an oscillator.

## From Force to Action {#S1}

Newtonian dynamics expresses motion through the net force. Analytical mechanics starts from a different object: a number assigned to an entire path $q(t)$ between two times, called the action. For one degree of freedom, $S[q]=\int_{t_1}^{t_2}L(q,\dot q,t)\,\mathrm dt$. The Lagrangian $L$ has units of energy, while the action $S$ has units of energy multiplied by time. For many conservative mechanical systems, $L=T-V$, kinetic minus potential energy. The physical trajectory is not required to be the shortest geometrical path. Instead, small admissible path variations with fixed endpoints leave the action unchanged to first order. The construction can be generalized to several coordinates, fields and other physical theories.

## Variation and Boundary Terms {#S2}

Vary the path $q(t)$ by $q_\varepsilon=q+\varepsilon\eta$ with $\eta(t_1)=\eta(t_2)=0$. The first variation is

$$
\delta S=\int_{t_1}^{t_2}\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial q}\eta+\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q}\dot\eta\right)\mathrm dt.
$$

Integration by parts of the second term produces the boundary term $[(\partial L/\partial\dot q)\eta]_{t_1}^{t_2}$, which vanishes for fixed endpoints. Thus $\delta S=\int [\partial L/\partial q-\mathrm d(\partial L/\partial\dot q)/\mathrm dt]\eta\,\mathrm dt$. Since smooth $\eta$ is arbitrary in the interior, 

$$
\boxed{\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q}-\frac{\partial L}{\partial q}=0.}
$$

The equation holds for each independent coordinate in a multidimensional system. This derivation assumes sufficient differentiability and the specified admissible variations.

## Example: Harmonic Oscillator {#S3}

For a mass $m$ on an ideal spring of stiffness $k$, take $L=\frac12m\dot x^2-\frac12kx^2$. Then $\partial L/\partial x=-kx$ and $\partial L/\partial\dot x=m\dot x$. Euler–Lagrange gives

$$
m\ddot x+kx=0,
$$

the familiar harmonic equation. For $m=2\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $k=18\,\mathrm{N/m}$, $\omega=\sqrt{k/m}=3\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$ and $T=2\pi/3\,\mathrm s\approx2.094\,\mathrm s$. Initial data $x(0)=0.1\,\mathrm m$, $\dot x(0)=0$ lead to $x(t)=0.1\cos(3t)\,\mathrm m$. ## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Determine $\partial L/\partial\dot x$ for $L=\frac12m\dot x^2-V(x)$.
2. Derive the equation of motion for $V(x)=mgx$ when $x$ points upward.
3. Calculate the angular frequency for $m=2\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $k=18\,\mathrm{N/m}$.

### Solutions

1. $\partial L/\partial\dot x=m\dot x$, the canonical momentum in this example.
2. $\partial L/\partial x=-mg$, hence $m\ddot x=-mg$ and $\ddot x=-g$.
3. $\omega=\sqrt{18/2}=3\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$.

## Stationarity Is Not Always a Minimum {#S5}

The phrase “least action” is potentially misleading: $\delta S=0$ only specifies a stationary value. With fixed endpoints, the second variation can be positive, negative or indefinite. The familiar $L=T-V$ form assumes an appropriate conservative model; velocity-dependent potentials, time-dependent constraints and dissipation require additional care. Lagrangians are also not unique: adding a total time derivative $\mathrm dF(q,t)/\mathrm dt$ leaves the equations of motion unchanged with fixed endpoints. The observable content is the trajectory and its dynamics, not the absolute numerical value of an action shifted by a boundary contribution.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Varying an entire path yields local dynamics. Constraints will next reduce the number of necessary coordinates.
