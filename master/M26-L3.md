# M26-L3 – Lie groups, infinitesimal symmetries and conserved quantities {#L3}

**Course area:** M26 – Master: Advanced mathematical methods and model foundations

**Level:** Master · methodological specialisation

**Prerequisites:** M9: Noether’s theorem; M12: angular momentum; M26-L2: coordinates and vectors. [Previous lesson](M26-L2.md).

**Learning objective:** Derive a generator from continuous rotations and obtain a conservation law from a rotationally invariant Lagrangian.

## Guiding question and observation {#S1}

A symmetry is more than a visually unchanged picture: it relates physically equivalent descriptions. Continuous symmetries form differentiable groups under suitable assumptions. Their derivatives at the identity are generators that describe infinitesimal transformations.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

Plane rotations are represented by matrices $R(\theta)$. A finite rotation can be written as the exponential of an antisymmetric generator $J$. For a free particle or one in a central potential the angle $\varphi$ is cyclic: the Lagrangian has no explicit dependence on it. The Euler–Lagrange equation then implies conservation of the conjugate momentum.

$$
R(\theta)=e^{\theta J},\quad J=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad L=\frac m2(\dot r^2+r^2\dot\varphi^2)-V(r),\quad p_\varphi=mr^2\dot\varphi=\mathrm{const}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

Suppose a particle starts at $r_1=2\,\mathrm m$ with $\dot\varphi_1=0.5\,\mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$. If angular momentum is conserved and $r_2=1\,\mathrm m$, then $\dot\varphi_2=\dot\varphi_1(r_1/r_2)^2=2\,\mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}$. Also, $R(\pi/2)$ takes $(1,0)^T$ to $(0,1)^T$. The figure illustrates the group orbit of a unit vector.

![Circular orbit of two vectors related by a rotation through an angle.](../../../../media/course/U_PHY_151_EN.svg)

*The generator gives the infinitesimal change along the orbit of rotations.*

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Calculate $R(\pi)(1,0)^T$.
2. How does $\dot\varphi$ change if the radius is halved at fixed $p_\varphi$?
3. What assumption guarantees conservation of $p_\varphi$?

### Solutions

1. $(-1,0)^T$.
2. Angular velocity becomes four times larger.
3. The Lagrangian must be independent of $\varphi$, and the Euler–Lagrange equations must apply.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

Plane rotations form an abelian group; many three-dimensional and quantum symmetry groups are noncommutative. Noether statements require appropriate variational principles and boundary assumptions.

## Summary and outlook {#S6}

Continuous symmetries connect generators with conservation laws. Variational principles also give access to semiclassical approximations. [Next lesson](M26-L4.md).
