# M24-L6 – Reconstruction, Resolution, Noise and Artifacts {#L6}

**Course area:** M24 – Bachelor: Medical Physics and Imaging

**Level:** Bachelor · Elective

**Prerequisites:** M8: convolution and Fourier transforms; M16: error propagation; M17: inverse problems. [Previous lesson](M24-L5.md).

**Learning objective:** Distinguish image sharpness, contrast, noise and artifacts quantitatively and methodologically.

## Question and Observation {#S1}

Medical imaging devices first generate raw signals. A physical forward model and a reconstruction algorithm then convert them into an image. Images of the same underlying object may have very different noise and apparent sharpness even though the measured structures are unchanged. Strong sharpening may create artifacts, so visual appeal alone does not establish quantitative image quality.

## Physical Model and Equations {#S2}

In an approximately shift-invariant linear system, the true object $f(\mathbf r)$ is convolved with the point spread function $h(\mathbf r)$, and noise $n(\mathbf r)$ is added. Convolution blurs edges and suppresses high spatial frequencies. Two independent Gaussian blurs with standard deviations $\sigma_1$ and $\sigma_2$ add in variance. For ideal Poisson counting with expectation $N$, the ratio of mean to standard deviation is $N/\sqrt N=\sqrt N$.

$$
g(\mathbf r)=(h*f)(\mathbf r)+n(\mathbf r),\qquad \mathrm{SNR}_{\mathrm{counts}}\approx\sqrt N,\qquad \sigma_{\mathrm{tot}}^2=\sigma_1^2+\sigma_2^2.
$$

The counting formula does not universally describe reconstructed pixels: filtering and iterative processing often create correlations between neighbouring pixels. Modulation transfer and sampling influence resolution; motion and inaccurate models cause additional artifacts. Increasing photon counts may entail longer acquisition or greater exposure or activity depending on modality; maximising count statistics alone is not a clinical optimisation criterion.

## Worked Example and Teaching Diagram {#S3}

An invented detector collects on average $N=400$ events. Then $\sigma_N\approx20$, giving $5\,\%$ relative uncertainty. With $N=1600$, standard deviation becomes $40$ and relative uncertainty falls to $2.5\,\%$: quadrupling the expected counts halves relative uncertainty. Two independent Gaussian blurs with $\sigma_1=1\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\sigma_2=2\,\mathrm{mm}$ combine to $\sigma_{\mathrm{tot}}=\sqrt5\,\mathrm{mm}\approx2.24\,\mathrm{mm}$. ## Self-Assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What is relative Poisson uncertainty for $N=100$?
2. Calculate $\sigma_{\mathrm{tot}}$ for $\sigma_1=3\,\mathrm{mm}$ and $\sigma_2=4\,\mathrm{mm}$.
3. Why can correlations between reconstructed pixels invalidate a direct $\sqrt N$ rule?

### Solutions

1. $1/\sqrt{100}=10\,\%$.
2. $\sigma_{\mathrm{tot}}=\sqrt{9+16}\,\mathrm{mm}=5\,\mathrm{mm}$.
3. Algorithms combine measurements, introducing correlated errors; a reconstructed pixel is not an independent Poisson count.

## Validity Limits and Quality {#S5}

Shift invariance, linear response and Gaussian blur are approximations. Each imaging modality requires its own forward physics, quality metrics and safety considerations. The figure is entirely synthetic.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Spatial resolution, noise and contrast are not interchangeable. Improvements must be checked against artifacts and acquisition constraints.
