# M11-L2 – Photoelectric Effect, Photons and Compton Scattering {#L2}

**Course area:** M11 – Bachelor: Foundations of Quantum Physics

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** Conservation of energy and momentum, electromagnetic radiation, electronvolts; M7 and M10. [Previous lesson](M11-L1.md).

**Learning objective:** Calculate photon energy and momentum and explain the photoelectric and Compton effects using conservation laws.

## Threshold Frequency Rather than Mere Brightness {#S1}

In external photoemission, incident light releases electrons from a solid. Below a material-dependent threshold frequency this does not occur simply by waiting for each electron to accumulate energy from an arbitrarily intense monochromatic beam. A photon carries $E_\gamma=h\nu=hc/\lambda$. If this exceeds the work function $W$, the difference may appear as electron kinetic energy. The number of emitted electrons at a given frequency depends on absorbed photon flux and quantum efficiency, among other factors. Quantum efficiency is not universally one electron per photon: surface states, reflectivity and transport can strongly affect it.

## Photon Momentum and the Compton Shift {#S2}

The ideal energy balance is

$$
K_\mathrm{max}=h\nu-W,\qquad eU_\mathrm{stop}=K_\mathrm{max},\qquad \nu_0=W/h.
$$

Here $U_\mathrm{stop}$ denotes the positive magnitude of a retarding potential. A photon also carries momentum $p_\gamma=h/\lambda$. In scattering from an approximately free electron initially at rest, both energy and vector momentum must be conserved. Solving these conservation equations gives the Compton shift 

$$
\Delta\lambda=\lambda'-\lambda=\frac{h}{m_\mathrm ec}(1-\cos\theta).
$$

The prefactor $h/(m_\mathrm ec)\simeq2.426\,\mathrm{pm}$ is the electron Compton wavelength. Angle $\theta$ measures the photon’s deflection, not the recoil electron’s direction.

## Worked Photoemission and Scattering Angles {#S3}

At $\lambda=400\,\mathrm{nm}$ the photon energy is $E_\gamma\simeq1240/400\,\mathrm{eV}=3.10\,\mathrm{eV}$. For $W=2.20\,\mathrm{eV}$, one obtains $K_\mathrm{max}\simeq0.90\,\mathrm{eV}$ and a stopping-potential magnitude of about $U_\mathrm{stop}\simeq0.90\,\mathrm V$. The threshold wavelength is $\lambda_0=hc/W\simeq564\,\mathrm{nm}$; longer wavelengths do not cause ideal photoemission. In Compton scattering a $90^\circ$ photon deflection yields $\Delta\lambda\simeq2.426\,\mathrm{pm}$, while $180^\circ$ backscattering doubles that shift. ## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. A metal has $W=2.20\,\mathrm{eV}$. Calculate $K_\mathrm{max}$ for $\lambda=500\,\mathrm{nm}$.
2. Calculate the Compton shift for $\theta=180^\circ$.
3. Can higher intensity at $\nu<\nu_0$ defeat the single-photon threshold? Specify the limit of the model.

### Solutions

1. $E_\gamma\simeq2.48\,\mathrm{eV}$, hence $K_\mathrm{max}\simeq0.28\,\mathrm{eV}$.
2. $\Delta\lambda=2h/(m_\mathrm ec)\simeq4.853\,\mathrm{pm}$.
3. Not for single-photon photoemission. At very high intensities multiphoton processes can occur, which are a different mechanism.

## Idealizations and Measurement Practice {#S5}

Metal samples yield a distribution of electron energies because binding and transport differ; the ideal photoelectric equation predicts the upper energy edge. The free-electron Compton formula is best when binding effects are comparatively small; strong binding modifies the spectrum. Observing a wavelength shift reveals directed momentum transfer, not an absence of wave interference. Photon and wave descriptions are complementary treatments of the same quantized electromagnetic field.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Photoemission and Compton scattering make discrete photon energy and momentum transfer quantitatively testable.
