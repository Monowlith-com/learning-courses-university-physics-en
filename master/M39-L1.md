# M39-L1 – Brownian Motion and Stokes–Einstein Diffusion {#L1}

**Course area:** M39 – Master: Biophysics, Nonequilibrium and Complex Systems

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M13: statistical mechanics; M6: laminar flow; M38 · [Previous lesson](M38-L7.md).

**Learning objective:** Connect mean-square displacement, diffusivity and hydrodynamic friction for an idealized colloidal sphere.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Thermal molecular collisions lead to irregular motion of microscopic particles. In free homogeneous diffusion at long times, ensemble mean-square displacement grows linearly in time, while the mean signed displacement vanishes. This is an ensemble statement, not a deterministic trajectory for one particle.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

In one dimension, the diffusion equation with constant $D$ and vanishing boundary flux gives $\langle[x(t)-x(0)]^2\rangle=2Dt$. For a sphere of hydrodynamic radius $r_h$ in a Newtonian continuum fluid with no-slip boundary and small Reynolds number, $\zeta=6\pi\eta r_h$ and the Einstein relation yields $D=k_BT/\zeta$. The hydrodynamic and geometric radii need not coincide. The overdamped model omits the short-time ballistic regime.

$$
D=\frac{k_BT}{6\pi\eta r_h},\qquad\langle\Delta x^2\rangle=2Dt\quad(\text{1D}).
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

Choose illustrative $T=300\,\mathrm K$, $\eta=10^{-3}\,\mathrm{Pa\,s}$ and $r_h=1\,\mathrm{\mu m}$. Using $k_B=1.380649\times10^{-23}\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}$ gives $D\approx2.20\times10^{-13}\,\mathrm{m^2s^{-1}}=0.220\,\mathrm{\mu m^2s^{-1}}$. After $t=10\,\mathrm s$, one-dimensional RMS displacement is $\sqrt{2Dt}\approx2.10\,\mathrm{\mu m}$, not directed distance. The figure shows the ensemble trend.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. How does $D$ change when hydrodynamic radius doubles at fixed $T,\eta$?
2. What is $\langle\Delta x^2\rangle$ after five seconds in the numerical example?
3. Why cannot a single trajectory replace an ensemble mean?

### Solutions

1. $D$ is halved.
2. $2Dt\approx2.20\,\mathrm{\mu m^2}$.
3. Individual paths fluctuate; expected squared displacement is a statistical average.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

Stokes–Einstein may fail near interfaces, under confinement, in viscoelastic media, at high concentration or for nonequilibrium active particles. Dimensionality matters: in $d$ independent spatial dimensions, $\langle|\Delta\mathbf r|^2\rangle=2dDt$, not always $2Dt$.
