# M32-L6 – Pump–probe measurements and temporal resolution {#L6}

**Course area:** M32 – Master: Experimental Instrumentation and Research Methods

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M32-L5: short X-ray pulses; M7: waves and pulses · [Previous lesson](M32-L5.md).

**Learning objective:** Calculate effective temporal resolution from independent Gaussian pulse widths and timing jitter.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

In a pump–probe experiment an initial pulse excites a system and a delayed second pulse samples its changing state. The recorded delay dependence is a convolution of the physical dynamics with the instrument response. Both pulse durations and fluctuations of their relative arrival time limit attainable time resolution.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

For independent Gaussian pump and probe temporal profiles and Gaussian relative timing jitter, variances add: $\sigma_{\mathrm{eff}}^2=\sigma_{\mathrm{pump}}^2+\sigma_{\mathrm{probe}}^2+\sigma_{\mathrm{jitter}}^2$. Each $\sigma$ is a standard deviation along the same time coordinate, not an FWHM. For a Gaussian, $\mathrm{FWHM}=2\sqrt{2\ln2}\,\sigma\simeq2.355\sigma$. Widths expressed under different conventions must not be squared and added without conversion.

$$
\sigma_{\mathrm{eff}}=\sqrt{\sigma_{\mathrm{pump}}^2+\sigma_{\mathrm{probe}}^2+\sigma_{\mathrm{jitter}}^2}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For $\sigma_{\mathrm{pump}}=30\,\mathrm{fs}$, $\sigma_{\mathrm{probe}}=40\,\mathrm{fs}$ and $\sigma_{\mathrm{jitter}}=20\,\mathrm{fs}$, $\sigma_{\mathrm{eff}}=\sqrt{2900}\,\mathrm{fs}\approx53.85\,\mathrm{fs}$. Its Gaussian FWHM is about $126.8\,\mathrm{fs}$. The figure sketches a delay scan and the broader combined temporal response.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find $\sigma_{\mathrm{eff}}$ for $\sigma_1=3\,\mathrm{fs}$ and $\sigma_2=4\,\mathrm{fs}$ without jitter.
2. What is the FWHM of a Gaussian with $\sigma=10\,\mathrm{fs}$?
3. Why might the measured delay trace differ from the underlying dynamics?

### Solutions

1. $\sigma_{\mathrm{eff}}=5\,\mathrm{fs}$.
2. Approximately $23.55\,\mathrm{fs}$.
3. Because the observation is convolved with the pump and probe profiles and timing jitter.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

Quadrature addition requires independent Gaussian variables. Correlated jitter, asymmetric pulses and non-Gaussian responses require covariance terms or direct convolution. Temporal resolution does not substitute for calibrating the zero-delay point.
