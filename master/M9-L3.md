# M9-L3 – Symmetries, Cyclic Coordinates and Noether’s Theorem {#L3}

**Course area:** M9 – Bachelor: Analytical Mechanics

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** Euler–Lagrange equations, momentum, energy and polar coordinates. [Previous lesson](M9-L2.md).

**Learning objective:** Identify a cyclic coordinate and derive the relation between symmetry and conservation.

## What Remains Unchanged in a System {#S1}

Conservation laws are often first learned as separate statements: momentum is constant in the absence of net external force, and angular momentum is constant without torque. Analytical mechanics reveals a common structure. A continuous transformation that leaves the action invariant under appropriate conditions can possess a conserved quantity. An accessible case occurs when the Lagrangian has no explicit dependence on one coordinate, called a cyclic coordinate. Noether’s theorem treats more general continuous symmetries, including cases in which the Lagrangian changes by a total derivative. “Symmetry” means a precisely defined invariance of the mathematical model, not simply a regular-looking diagram.

## Cyclic Coordinates and Momentum Conservation {#S2}

For a generalized coordinate $q_j$, define canonical momentum $p_j=\partial L/\partial\dot q_j$. Euler–Lagrange implies

$$
\dot p_j=\frac{\partial L}{\partial q_j}.
$$

If $\partial L/\partial q_j=0$, then $p_j$ is constant. This is an elementary Noether correspondence. A free particle has Cartesian Lagrangian $L=\tfrac12m\dot x^2$ independent of $x$, so $p_x=m\dot x$ is conserved. Canonical momentum, however, depends on the chosen Lagrangian; in electromagnetic potentials it need not coincide with the kinetic momentum $m\boldsymbol v$. Symmetry must therefore be paired with the proper definition of the corresponding conserved quantity.

## Example: Angular Momentum in a Central Potential {#S3}

A mass $m$ moves in a plane under a potential $V(r)$ that depends on radius only. In polar coordinates,

$$
L=\frac m2(\dot r^2+r^2\dot\varphi^2)-V(r).
$$

The angle $\varphi$ does not appear explicitly, hence $\partial L/\partial\varphi=0$. Its conjugate momentum is $p_\varphi=mr^2\dot\varphi=\ell_z$, so angular momentum is conserved. For $m=2\,\mathrm{kg}$, $r=3\,\mathrm m$ and $\dot\varphi=0.5\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$, this gives $\ell_z=9\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2/s}$. If $r$ decreases, angular speed must increase provided no torque acts. ## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What follows from $\partial L/\partial q_j=0$?
2. Compute $\ell_z$ for $m=1\,\mathrm{kg}$, $r=2\,\mathrm m$, $\dot\varphi=3\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$.
3. Under which condition is $E=\sum p_j\dot q_j-L$ conserved in an autonomous Lagrangian model?

### Solutions

1. $\dot p_j=0$: canonical momentum is constant.
2. $\ell_z=mr^2\dot\varphi=12\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2/s}$.
3. No explicit time dependence of $L$ and validity of the ordinary Euler–Lagrange equations.

## Time Translation and Its Assumptions {#S5}

For a regular time-independent Lagrangian, the energy function $E=\sum_jp_j\dot q_j-L$ is constant along motion when there is no explicit time dependence: $\mathrm dE/\mathrm dt=-\partial L/\partial t$. In a simple $L=T-V$ model with quadratic kinetic energy, $E$ equals $T+V$. That identification is not automatic for arbitrary velocity dependence. Dissipation and external drives can break the assumed symmetries, invalidating an elementary conservation statement. Noether’s conclusion also presumes differentiable continuous transformations and suitable boundary and variational conditions. A purely discrete symmetry does not by itself generate the same continuous Noether charge.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Rotational invariance provides a conserved quantity. It next simplifies the motion of two gravitating bodies.
