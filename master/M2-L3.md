# M2-L3 – Vector Spaces, Matrices and Linear Systems {#L3}

**Course area:** M2 – Bachelor: Mathematical Methods for Physics

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** Vector components, linear equations and fractions. [Previous lesson](M2-L2.md).

**Learning objective:** Represent linear systems using matrices and explain rank and linear combinations.

## Why Matrices? {#S1}

A measuring instrument may mix several physical quantities: two sensors each report linear combinations of two unknown currents or forces. We must reconstruct the underlying quantities from the readings. Matrices collect such relationships compactly. A matrix has rows and columns; $A_{ij}$ denotes an entry. A vector space is a set of vectors closed under addition and scalar multiplication and satisfying the associated axioms.

## Linear Map and Solvability {#S2}

Matrix–vector multiplication and the linear system read

$$
\mathbf b=A\mathbf x,\qquad b_i=\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{ij}x_j.
$$

The columns of $A$ are images of the coordinate unit vectors. The system $A\mathbf x=\mathbf b$ is solvable when $\mathbf b$ lies in the column space. A solution, if it exists, is unique only when the null space of $A$ contains no vector other than zero. For a square matrix, this is equivalent to $\det A\ne0$. The **rank** counts the number of linearly independent columns (equal to the number of independent rows). Systems may be inconsistent or have infinitely many solutions.

## Example: Two Sensor Readings {#S3}

Suppose we seek $x$ and $y$; one sensor measures their sum while a second measures a weighted difference:

$$
\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\2&-1\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}x\\y\end{pmatrix}
=\begin{pmatrix}5\\1\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The first row gives $y=5-x$. Substituting into the second gives $2x-(5-x)=1$, hence $3x=6$, $x=2$ and $y=3$. Gaussian elimination would subtract twice the first row from the second to eliminate the first column. The determinant $-3$ is nonzero, so the two measurements suffice for a unique reconstruction under the model assumptions.

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Solve $x+y=7$, $x-y=1$.
2. What is the rank of a matrix with rows $(1,2)$ and $(2,4)$?
3. Can two dependent measurements uniquely determine two unknowns?

### Solutions

1. $x=4$, $y=3$.
2. Rank $1$: the second row is twice the first.
3. No. A consistent system has at least one free direction; otherwise it has no solution.

## Model Quality and Numerical Stability {#S5}

The existence of an exact solution does not imply robust reconstruction. If columns are nearly linearly dependent, small measurement errors can induce large changes in the solution. Condition numbers quantify this sensitivity. For overdetermined experimental datasets, least squares is often preferable to forcing an exact fit. Numerical Gaussian elimination requires pivoting; explicit matrix inversion is generally not the preferred method for solving large systems.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Matrix multiplication represents linear maps, while rank and null space determine solvability. Eigenvalues and eigenvectors then make coupled equations of motion tractable.
