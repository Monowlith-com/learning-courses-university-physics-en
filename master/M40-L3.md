# M40-L3 – Digital Detectors, Poisson Noise and Image Quality {#L3}

**Course area:** M40 – Master: Advanced Medical Physics and Quality Assurance

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M13: probability distributions; M32: measurement noise; M40-L2.

**Learning objective:** Distinguish idealized counting statistics of quantitative detector signals from resolution and artifacts.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Digital images emerge from a measurement chain of interaction, detection, electronics, sampling and processing. More registered quanta can reduce relative counting noise, but cannot fix a broad point-spread function or systematic calibration errors. Pixels are not necessarily independently measured spatial samples.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

For an ideal Poisson counter with expected event count $N$, $\mathrm{Var}(n)=N$ and $\sigma_n=\sqrt N$. When background and electronic noise are negligible and signal is proportional to $N$, $\mathrm{SNR}\approx N/\sqrt N=\sqrt N$. Spatial resolution is constrained separately by detector geometry, point-spread function and system motion. Real detectors add noise sources and correlations.

$$
\sigma_n=\sqrt{N},\qquad \mathrm{SNR}_{\mathrm{Poisson}}\approx\sqrt{N}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

An illustrative measurement window registers $N=400$ events on average. Its counting standard deviation is $20$ and ideal $\mathrm{SNR}=20$. With $N=1600$, SNR doubles to $40$ even though mean event count quadruples. The figure plots this square-root law. A change in registered events cannot be directly identified with patient dose without acquisition geometry and conditions.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What is ideal SNR at $N=900$?
2. By what factor must $N$ increase for a threefold SNR increase?
3. Why do extra quanta not correct an unsuitable detector point-spread function?

### Solutions

1. $30$.
2. By a factor of nine.
3. Spatial blur is governed by a different system response that was not changed.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

For multichannel, energy-resolving and ultrasound detectors, noise models, coherent signals, gain and temporal coupling differ. In ultrasound, pulse-echo depth $z=c\,\Delta t/2$ is a time-of-flight relation, not a Poisson relation; sound speed and attenuation require separate assumptions.
