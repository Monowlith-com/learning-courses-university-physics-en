# M31-L6 – Parallel computing, scaling and Amdahl’s law {#L6}

**Course area:** M31 – Master: Numerical and Computational Physics

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M31-L5: computationally demanding models; runtime analysis basics · [Previous lesson](M31-L5.md).

**Learning objective:** Calculate maximum ideal speedup from the parallel program fraction and processor count.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

A mathematically reliable numerical method can still be limited by computation time and memory. Parallel execution distributes work across cores or nodes. The main restriction is what fraction of a program can execute concurrently and what remains serial because of dependencies.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

Let $p$ be the parallelizable fraction of runtime for a fixed-size problem and $N$ the number of identical processors. In the ideal model the serial fraction $1-p$ does not speed up, while the parallel fraction is divided by $N$. Normalized runtime is $T_N/T_1=(1-p)+p/N$, giving $S(N)=1/[(1-p)+p/N]$. As $N\to\infty$, speedup is bounded by $S_{\max}=1/(1-p)$. In real systems synchronization, communication, memory bandwidth and load imbalance often reduce gains.

$$
S(N)=\frac{1}{(1-p)+p/N},\qquad S_{\max}=\frac1{1-p}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For $p=0.95$ and $N=20$, $S(20)=1/(0.05+0.0475)\approx10.26$. Even arbitrarily many processors cannot exceed the ideal limit of 20. The plot compares $p=0.95$ and $p=0.8$, approaching limits of 20 and 5 respectively.

![Amdahl speedup versus processor count for parallel fractions of 95 and 80 percent with asymptotic limits.](../../../../media/course/U_PHY_008_EN.svg)

*Even ideal parallel hardware is bounded by the serial part of a program.*

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Compute $S(4)$ for $p=0.75$.
2. What asymptotic limit applies at $p=0.9$?
3. Why may real speedup be smaller than Amdahl predicts?

### Solutions

1. $S(4)=1/(0.25+0.1875)\approx2.286$.
2. $S_{\max}=10$.
3. Communication, synchronization, load imbalance and hardware bottlenecks add overhead.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

Amdahl assumes fixed total work. Growing problem sizes and domain decomposition call for weak-scaling analyses as well. Actual scaling should be verified with controlled benchmarks and consistent accuracy requirements.
