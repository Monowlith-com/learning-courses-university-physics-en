# M35-L7 – Berry Curvature, Gauge Convention and Chern Number {#L7}

**Course area:** M35 – Master: Advanced Solid-State Physics and Materials Science

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M35-L6: SSH winding; M34: quantum states.

**Learning objective:** Transform the Berry connection under a local phase change and interpret quantized curvature flux for an isolated band.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Bloch eigenstates may be multiplied by a momentum-dependent local phase. The eigenvector phase itself is therefore not a unique observable. Berry connection encodes the momentum dependence of that phase, while its curl in two-dimensional momentum space is gauge-invariant Berry curvature. For a nondegenerate band separated from all other bands throughout the Brillouin torus, total normalized curvature flux is an integer.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

With convention $A_j(\mathbf k)=i\langle u(\mathbf k)|\partial_{k_j}u(\mathbf k)\rangle$ and $|u\rangle\mapsto e^{i\chi(\mathbf k)}|u\rangle$, one gets $A_j\mapsto A_j-\partial_{k_j}\chi$. Berry curvature is $\Omega_{xy}=\partial_{k_x}A_y-\partial_{k_y}A_x=i(\langle\partial_xu|\partial_yu\rangle-\langle\partial_yu|\partial_xu\rangle)$ and is gauge-invariant. For an isolated occupied band $C=(1/2\pi)\int_{\mathrm{BZ}}\Omega_{xy}\,d^2k\in\mathbb Z$. If $C\ne0$, no globally smooth periodic gauge exists; local patches are used.

$$
C=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathrm{BZ}}\Omega_{xy}(\mathbf k)\,d^2k\in\mathbb Z.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

Partition the Brillouin torus into four patches and prescribe a constructed Berry flux of $\pi/2$ through each patch. Their total flux is $2\pi$, so $C=1$. This is an illustrative flux accounting exercise, not evidence for a particular material or a claim of universally constant Berry curvature. The image shows the four flux contributions and the gauge issue.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What is $C$ if the total flux is $-4\pi$?
2. How does $A_j$ transform under $|u\rangle\mapsto e^{i\chi}|u\rangle$ using this convention?
3. Why is one globally smooth periodic eigenvector gauge obstructed when $C\ne0$?

### Solutions

1. $C=-2$.
2. $A_j\mapsto A_j-\partial_{k_j}\chi$.
3. The nontrivial Chern class obstructs a single globally smooth periodic gauge and requires local patches.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

Quantization requires a well-defined isolated band bundle over a closed Brillouin zone. Degenerate bands require a non-Abelian treatment. The sign depends on orientation and convention; Hall conductance requires additional physical assumptions.
