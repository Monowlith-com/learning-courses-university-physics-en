# M42-L3 – Geostrophic Balance, Rossby Number and the Beta Effect {#L3}

**Course area:** M42 – Master: Interdisciplinary Physics and Research Frontiers

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M38-L2: rotating flows; M14: fluid dynamics.

**Learning objective:** Compute geostrophic velocity from a horizontal pressure gradient and use Rossby number to assess validity.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Large-scale atmospheric and oceanic currents experience Coriolis acceleration in a rotating frame. When acceleration and friction are relatively small, the horizontal pressure-gradient and Coriolis terms can nearly balance. **Geostrophic balance** is a leading-order approximation rather than the full equations of motion; it is associated with small Rossby number away from the equator.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

With $x$ east, $y$ north and $z$ up, the Northern Hemisphere has $f=2\Omega\sin\varphi>0$. From $f\,\hat{\mathbf z}\times\mathbf u_g=-(1/\rho)\nabla_h p$ follow $u_g=-(\rho f)^{-1}\partial_y p$ and $v_g=(\rho f)^{-1}\partial_x p$. Rossby number $\mathrm{Ro}=U/(|f|L)$ compares advective acceleration with Coriolis effects. A local $\beta$-plane represents $f(y)\simeq f_0+\beta y$.

$$
u_g=-\frac{1}{\rho f}\frac{\partial p}{\partial y},\quad v_g=\frac{1}{\rho f}\frac{\partial p}{\partial x},\quad \mathrm{Ro}=\frac{U}{|f|L}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

At $45^\circ$ north, $f\approx1.03\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$. With $\rho=1.2\,\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$ and northward pressure increase $0.001\,\mathrm{Pa\,m^{-1}}$, the zonal velocity is $u_g\approx-8.1\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$: **westward**. Using $U=8.1\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ and $L=1000\,\mathrm{km}$ gives $\mathrm{Ro}\approx0.078$. The figure makes the sign convention explicit.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What happens to the sign of $u_g$ when $\partial_y p$ turns negative?
2. Calculate $\mathrm{Ro}$ for $U=16.2\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$ with otherwise unchanged values.
3. Why does the formula fail if applied unchanged at the equator?

### Solutions

1. The geostrophic zonal flow is then eastward.
2. $\mathrm{Ro}\approx0.157$.
3. Near the equator $f\to0$, violating the assumed leading-order Coriolis balance.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The calculation assumes a locally planar, frictionless, constant-density model. Vertical hydrostatics, turbulence, curvature and rapidly varying flow add further terms. Small Rossby number alone does not prove exact balance.
