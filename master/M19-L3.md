# M19-L3 – Precision Spectroscopy and Zeeman Splitting {#L3}

**Course area:** M19 – Bachelor: Advanced Experimental Methods and Research Practice

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** M11: spin and spectral lines; M15: detectors; M18: spectroscopy. [Previous lesson](M19-L2.md).

**Learning objective:** Calculate a simple Zeeman splitting and distinguish calibration from line identification.

## Question and Model {#S1}

Spectroscopy compares optical frequency or wavelength with transitions in atoms or molecules. Observed line positions also depend on instrumental response, calibration, temperature, magnetic field and resolution. An applied magnetic field splits previously degenerate magnetic sublevels. For suitable states in weak fields, the linear Zeeman approximation applies, but an observed line is always a difference between two energy levels subject to selection rules.

## Equations and Assumptions {#S2}

For one level with Landé factor $g_J$, the linear approximation is
$$
\Delta E_{m_J}=g_J\,\mu_{\mathrm B}\,m_J\,B.
$$
The frequency difference between adjacent sublevels of that same term is $\Delta\nu=g_J\mu_{\mathrm B}B/h$. This is a level separation, not automatically the splitting of a measured optical line: upper/lower level factors and allowed transitions matter. Strong fields, hyperfine interactions and altered angular-momentum coupling call for other models.

## Worked Example and Diagram {#S3}

Let $B=0.10\,\mathrm T$, $g_J=2$ and $\mu_{\mathrm B}/h\approx13.996\,\mathrm{GHz/T}$. Adjacent model sublevels are then separated by approximately $2.799\,\mathrm{GHz}$ in frequency. In this linear approximation the separation vanishes at $B=0$. The figure uses an illustrative triplet with $m_J=-1,0,+1$; it is not a spectrum of a specific atom and makes no claim about optical transition strengths.

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Which term changes with magnetic field in the linear Zeeman model?
2. Compute $\Delta\nu$ for $g_J=2$, $B=0.10\,\mathrm T$.
3. Why does a level separation not directly equal an optical line splitting?

### Solutions

1. The level shift is proportional to $g_Jm_JB$.
2. $\Delta\nu\approx2.799\,\mathrm{GHz}$.
3. An optical transition connects two levels with selection rules, and both levels may shift differently.

## Limits and Quality Control {#S5}

Real analysis requires wavelength calibration, the instrumental line-spread function, field homogeneity, polarisation selection and a check for overlapping features. One observed peak does not establish a Zeeman effect. Evidence improves when peak positions and polarisation change with $B$ as predicted. Optical sources and magnetic fields may carry significant hazards and require authorised local operating procedures.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Magnetic splitting is meaningful only together with a level model, transitions and instrument calibration.
