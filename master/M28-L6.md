# M28-L6 – Weakly interacting bosons and Bogoliubov excitations {#L6}

**Course area:** M28 – Master: Nonequilibrium and many-body physics

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M12: quantum mechanics; M13: Bose distribution; M27-L8: second quantization · [Previous lesson](M28-L5.md).

**Learning objective:** Calculate the Bogoliubov spectrum and derive its low-momentum sound limit.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

During Bose–Einstein condensation, many bosons can occupy the same quantum state. Weak interactions modify the elementary excitations. At small momentum, collective sound modes appear instead of purely free-particle modes.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

For a homogeneous three-dimensional Bose gas with weak repulsive contact interaction $g>0$ and condensate density $n_0$, consider quadratic fluctuations around a stationary condensate. The free energy of one mode is $\varepsilon_k=\hbar^2k^2/(2m)$. Bogoliubov diagonalization gives $E_k=\sqrt{\varepsilon_k(\varepsilon_k+2gn_0)}$. At small $k$, $\varepsilon_k\ll gn_0$ and therefore $E_k\simeq\hbar ck$ with $c=\sqrt{gn_0/m}$. At high $k$, the spectrum approaches $E_k\simeq\varepsilon_k+gn_0$. The linear dispersion reflects collective excitations of the condensed system.

$$
\varepsilon_k=\frac{\hbar^2k^2}{2m},\qquad E_k=\sqrt{\varepsilon_k(\varepsilon_k+2gn_0)},\qquad c=\sqrt{\frac{gn_0}{m}}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

If the free mode energy is $\varepsilon_k=0.5gn_0$, then $E_k=\sqrt{0.5\cdot2.5}\,gn_0\approx1.118gn_0$. The graphic plots both energies in units of $gn_0$ against normalized wavenumber $k/k_0$ with $k_0=\sqrt{mgn_0}/\hbar$. The Bogoliubov branch begins linearly, whereas the free energy depends quadratically on momentum.

![Bogoliubov excitation energy and free quadratic dispersion plotted against dimensionless momentum.](../../../../media/course/U_PHY_026_EN.svg)

*Interactions replace the free quadratic low-momentum behavior with a linear sound branch.*

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Derive $E_k\simeq\hbar ck$ at small momentum.
2. Find $E_k/(gn_0)$ if $\varepsilon_k/(gn_0)=1$.
3. What happens to $E_k$ for $g\to0^+$ at fixed $k$?

### Solutions

1. $E_k\approx\sqrt{2gn_0\varepsilon_k}=\hbar k\sqrt{gn_0/m}$.
2. $\sqrt3\approx1.732$.
3. $E_k$ approaches the free particle energy $\varepsilon_k$.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The model requires weak interactions and small condensate depletion. In lower dimensions, at high temperatures, or under strong correlations, this simple condensate picture can break down.
