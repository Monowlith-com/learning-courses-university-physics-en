# M1-L4 – Scalars, Vectors and Coordinates {#L4}

**Course area:** M1 – Bachelor: Foundations of Physics

**Level:** Bachelor · Introductory

**Prerequisites:** Pythagorean theorem and basic trigonometric functions. [Previous lesson](M1-L3.md).

**Learning objective:** Represent vectors in Cartesian components, add them and interpret their geometry.

## Distance and Displacement {#S1}

A person moves 3 m east and then 4 m north. The distance travelled is 7 m. The displacement from the starting point to the endpoint, however, has magnitude 5 m and a direction. A scalar quantity such as mass is described by a numerical value and a unit; a vector also carries directional information. Components depend on the chosen coordinate system.

## Components and Operations {#S2}

In a right-handed Cartesian system with orthonormal unit vectors $\mathbf e_x,\mathbf e_y,\mathbf e_z$,

$$
\mathbf a=a_x\mathbf e_x+a_y\mathbf e_y+a_z\mathbf e_z,
\qquad |\mathbf a|=\sqrt{a_x^2+a_y^2+a_z^2}.
$$

Vector addition operates component by component. For a planar vector at angle $\varphi$ from the positive x-axis, $a_x=a\cos\varphi$ and $a_y=a\sin\varphi$, where $a=|\mathbf a|$. Determine the angle in the correct quadrant from both components.

The dot product measures the directional alignment of two vectors:

$$
\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b=a_xb_x+a_yb_y+a_zb_z=|\mathbf a||\mathbf b|\cos\gamma.
$$

The cross product is a vector perpendicular to both operands; its magnitude is $|\mathbf a||\mathbf b|\sin\gamma$ and its direction follows the right-hand rule. In the Cartesian basis, for example, $\mathbf e_x\times\mathbf e_y=\mathbf e_z$. Order matters: $\mathbf b\times\mathbf a=-\mathbf a\times\mathbf b$.

## Example and Choice of Coordinates {#S3}

For $\mathbf s=3\,\mathrm m\,\mathbf e_x+4\,\mathrm m\,\mathbf e_y$, we obtain $|\mathbf s|=5\,\mathrm m$ and $\varphi=\arctan(4/3)\approx53.13^\circ$. A constant force $\mathbf F=2\,\mathrm N\,\mathbf e_x$ does work along this displacement:

$$
W=\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf s=6\,\mathrm{N\,m}=6\,\mathrm J.
$$

Rotate the coordinate axes anticlockwise by $90^\circ$, so $\mathbf e_{x'}=\mathbf e_y$ and $\mathbf e_{y'}=-\mathbf e_x$. The displacement components become $(4,-3)\,\mathrm m$. Its magnitude remains 5 m. The displacement itself has not changed.

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Calculate the magnitude and direction of the vector $(-3,4)\,\mathrm m$.
2. Determine $(1,2,0)\cdot(2,-1,0)$.
3. Determine $\mathbf e_y\times\mathbf e_x$.

### Solutions

1. $5\,\mathrm m$, with an angle of approximately $126.87^\circ$ from the positive x-axis.
2. $1\cdot2+2\cdot(-1)=0$; the two vectors are orthogonal.
3. $-\mathbf e_z$ by antisymmetry of the cross product.

## Limits and Conventions {#S5}

A zero vector has no distinguished direction. The simple component formulas used here assume an orthonormal Cartesian basis. In curvilinear coordinates, basis vectors may change with position; differentiation must then account for their variation. The cross product in this form belongs to oriented three-dimensional Euclidean space.

## Summary {#S6}

A vector is more than its magnitude. Components, dot and cross products enable quantitative statements about motion and forces. The next lesson considers their changes over time.
