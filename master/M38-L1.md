# M38-L1 – Seismic Refraction and Two-Layer Traveltimes {#L1}

**Course area:** M38 – Master: Geophysics, Atmospheric Physics and Climate Models

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M6: wave mechanics; M31: inverse problems; M37: astrophysics · [Previous lesson](M37-L7.md).

**Learning objective:** Compare direct and critically refracted waves in a horizontal two-layer model, distinguishing the meaning of an intercept and the minimum offset.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Seismic traveltimes constrain velocities and interfaces in the subsurface. Our toy model assumes two homogeneous isotropic layers with a horizontal boundary, with the lower layer faster than the upper one. A critically refracted head wave can form beyond a minimum source–receiver offset. The result is an inference from travel times rather than a direct photograph of subsurface geometry.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

For $v_2>v_1$, interface depth $H$ and incidence angle from the interface normal, $\sin i_c=v_1/v_2$. The direct arrival obeys $t_d=x/v_1$. Ray theory gives the head-wave arrival $t_h(x)=2H\cos i_c/v_1+x/v_2$, but only for $x\ge 2H\tan i_c$. The line extrapolates to $t_i=2H\cos i_c/v_1$ at $x=0$, where that head-wave path does not exist. The critical offset and the first-arrival crossover $x_{\times}$, where $t_d=t_h$, are different.

$$
t_h(x)=\frac{2H\cos i_c}{v_1}+\frac{x}{v_2},\qquad \sin i_c=\frac{v_1}{v_2}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For the constructed model $v_1=2\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$, $v_2=4\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$, $H=1\,\mathrm{km}$, one obtains $i_c=30^\circ$, $t_i\approx0.866\,\mathrm s$, $x_{\min}\approx1.155\,\mathrm{km}$ and $x_{\times}\approx3.464\,\mathrm{km}$. At $x=4\,\mathrm{km}$, $t_d=2\,\mathrm s$ and $t_h\approx1.866\,\mathrm s$: the head wave arrives first. The plot separates the extrapolated line from its physical domain.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find $t_i$ for $H=2\,\mathrm{km}$ at the same velocities.
2. Which arrival is earlier at $x=2\,\mathrm{km}$?
3. Why is $t_h(0)$ not a physical head-wave travel time?

### Solutions

1. $t_i\approx1.732\,\mathrm s$, proportional to layer depth.
2. $t_d=1\,\mathrm s$ and $t_h\approx1.366\,\mathrm s$; direct arrival.
3. The critical-refraction path along the lower interface requires a finite minimum offset.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The two-layer ray model ignores interface dip, lateral variation, anisotropy, attenuation and finite wavelength. Traveltime inversion is nonunique; hidden layers can evade simple refraction surveys.
