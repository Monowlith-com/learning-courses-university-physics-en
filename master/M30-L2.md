# M30-L2 – Canonical quantization and Fock states {#L2}

**Course area:** M30 – Master: Quantum field theory and the Standard Model

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M30-L1: free field equation; M25: harmonic oscillator · [Previous lesson](M30-L1.md).

**Learning objective:** Describe field modes as quantum oscillators and compute energy differences between Fock states.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

How do continuous field oscillations give rise to discrete particle excitations? Under canonical quantization, each independent free mode becomes a harmonic quantum oscillator. Particle numbers are occupation numbers of these modes; a Fock state may contain several identical bosons in one mode.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

As in M30-L1, we use natural units $\hbar=c=1$; hence the energy spacing of a free mode is $\omega_{\mathbf k}$. With $\hbar$ written explicitly, it is $\hbar\omega_{\mathbf k}$. In a finite volume, start with discrete momenta $\mathbf k$. Each mode obeys $[a_{\mathbf k},a_{\mathbf k}^\dagger]=1$ and $N_{\mathbf k}=a_{\mathbf k}^\dagger a_{\mathbf k}$. The states $|n\rangle$ have occupation numbers $n=0,1,2,\dots$; $a^\dagger|n\rangle=\sqrt{n+1}\,|n+1\rangle$. The Hamiltonian of a free mode becomes $H_{\mathbf k}=\omega_{\mathbf k}(N_{\mathbf k}+\tfrac12)$. The zero-point term is finite for one oscillator; summing it over infinitely many field modes requires separate treatment.

$$
E_n=\left(n+\frac12\right)\omega_{\mathbf k},\qquad E_{n+1}-E_n=\omega_{\mathbf k}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For $m=4$ and $|\mathbf k|=3$, the mode frequency is $\omega_{\mathbf k}=5$. The first three levels are $E_0=2.5$, $E_1=7.5$, and $E_2=12.5$. The diagram illustrates equal level spacings $\omega$; its arrows represent an increased occupation number, not motion in space.

![Four equally spaced energy levels of one quantized field mode with upward transitions.](../../../../media/course/U_PHY_096_EN.svg)

*In natural units $\hbar=c=1$, each additional excitation of a free bosonic mode costs energy $\omega_{\mathbf k}$.*

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. How does $a^\dagger$ act on $|2\rangle$?
2. What is the adjacent-level spacing for $\omega=5$?
3. What is the Fock vacuum?

### Solutions

1. $a^\dagger|2\rangle=\sqrt3\,|3\rangle$.
2. Five energy units.
3. The state without particle excitations in the considered free modes.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

This elementary Fock picture applies directly to free fields. Identifying states is more subtle in interacting theories. An absolute vacuum energy density cannot be inferred from an unregulated sum of zero-point energies.
