# M42-L4 – Material Efficiency, Life-Cycle Boundaries and Emissions Accounting {#L4}

**Course area:** M42 – Master: Interdisciplinary Physics and Research Frontiers

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M35: materials science; M38: climate models; M42-L3: model boundaries.

**Learning objective:** Evaluate a transparent materials-substitution scenario and identify the limits of simple emission factors.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Material efficiency includes durable design, material reduction per functional unit, reuse, repair and recycling. An environmental comparison needs both a **functional unit** and a **system boundary**: construction, production, use and end of life are not automatically covered by one factor. The IEA’s 2019 report illustrates strategies, but does not provide timeless emission factors applicable to all projects.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

For a simplified cradle-to-gate comparison, let a functionally equivalent part have mass $m=m_v+m_r$. An accounting example writes upstream emissions as $E=m_v e_v+m_r e_r$, with consistently defined factors $e_v$ and $e_r$. Real assessments must state recycling allocation, transport, manufacturing yield, functional equivalence and any avoided-burden assumptions.

$$
E=m_v e_v+m_r e_r,\qquad r_E=\frac{E_0-E_1}{E_0}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

A wholly **synthetic** comparison uses $1000\,\mathrm{kg}$ material. Scenario 0 uses only virgin input with $e_v=2.0\,\mathrm{kg\,CO_2e/kg}$, giving $E_0=2000\,\mathrm{kg\,CO_2e}$. Scenario 1 combines $700\,\mathrm{kg}$ virgin and $300\,\mathrm{kg}$ secondary input with assumed $e_r=0.5\,\mathrm{kg\,CO_2e/kg}$: $E_1=1550\,\mathrm{kg\,CO_2e}$, a calculated reduction $r_E=22.5\,\%$. This is **not a real manufacturing inventory**.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find $E$ for $500\,\mathrm{kg}$ virgin and $500\,\mathrm{kg}$ secondary material.
2. What boundary question must be resolved before calling the difference a “saving”?
3. Why should a scenario published in 2019 not be treated as a universal current material factor?

### Solutions

1. $E=1250\,\mathrm{kg\,CO_2e}$.
2. Functional unit, system boundary, recycling allocation and transport must be comparable.
3. Technology, electricity mix, recycling process and assumptions vary across time and geography.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

These numeric example values do not come from the IEA report. The model excludes product lifetime, other environmental impacts, costs, rebound and actual availability of recycled feedstock.
