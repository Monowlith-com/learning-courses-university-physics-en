# M26-L1 – Hilbert spaces, self-adjoint operators and spectral decomposition {#L1}

**Course area:** M26 – Master: Advanced mathematical methods and model foundations

**Level:** Master · methodological specialisation

**Prerequisites:** M8: linear operators and eigenvalue problems; M12: quantum mechanics. [Previous lesson](M25-L6.md).

**Learning objective:** Distinguish symmetry from self-adjointness, apply spectral projectors to a finite model and explain why a discrete eigenbasis is not always available.

## Guiding question and observation {#S1}

A quantum observable is not always described adequately by a list of eigenvectors. An orthonormal eigenbasis suffices in finite dimensions; position and momentum operators on infinite-dimensional spaces may exhibit continuous spectra. The rigorous framework combines a Hilbert space, a specified operator domain and a projection-valued spectral measure.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

A complex Hilbert space is a complete inner-product space. The domain $D(A)$ is essential: an operator is self-adjoint when $A=A^*$ including equality of the domains. Showing $\langle\phi,A\psi\rangle=\langle A\phi,\psi\rangle$ on a dense domain establishes symmetry, not necessarily self-adjointness. For self-adjoint operators the spectral theorem supplies orthogonal projectors $P(\Delta)$ associated with measurable energy sets $\Delta$. Summing over discrete eigenvectors is a special case.

$$
A=\int_{\mathbb R}\lambda\,\mathrm dP(\lambda),\qquad f(A)=\int_{\mathbb R}f(\lambda)\,\mathrm dP(\lambda),\qquad \Pr(A\in\Delta)=\langle\psi,P(\Delta)\psi\rangle.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

In a two-dimensional Hilbert space take $A=\operatorname{diag}(1,3)$ and normalized $\psi=(\sqrt3/2,1/2)^T$. The projector for $[0,2]$ is $P([0,2])=\operatorname{diag}(1,0)$. The probability is therefore $3/4$ and the expectation is $\langle A\rangle=(3/4)\cdot1+(1/4)\cdot3=1.5$. The figure contrasts discrete lines with a possible continuum schematically; it is not a spectrum of this two-level matrix.

![Schematic spectrum with two discrete spectral lines and a continuous region, which need not occur together in any particular model.](../../../../media/course/U_PHY_268_EN.svg)

*Discrete eigenvalues give individual lines; the projection-valued formulation can also represent a continuum.*

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find $\Pr(A\in[2,4])$ in the example.
2. Compute $\langle A^2\rangle$ for the same state.
3. Why does symmetry on a dense domain not automatically imply self-adjointness?

### Solutions

1. $1/4$.
2. $\langle A^2\rangle=(3/4)\cdot1+(1/4)\cdot9=3$.
3. The adjoint may have a larger domain; operator equality includes matching domains.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The numerical model is finite-dimensional. Unbounded operators require a specified $D(A)$ and integrability conditions for spectral calculus; the figure is not an experimental spectrum.
