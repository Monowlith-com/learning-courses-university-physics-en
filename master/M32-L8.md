# M32-L8 – Experimental design, randomization and factor effects {#L8}

**Course area:** M32 – Master: Experimental Instrumentation and Research Methods

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M16: measurement uncertainty; M32-L1–L7: instruments and data methods · [Previous lesson](M32-L7.md).

**Learning objective:** Compute main-effect and interaction coefficients in a coded ±1 full two-factor experiment.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Experimental design specifies factors, levels, replicates and measurement order. Varying only one parameter at a time can miss interactions. A full $2^2$ factorial study tests all four combinations of two two-level factors; randomization guards against ordering effects, while replication helps estimate residual variation.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

At coded levels $x_A,x_B\in\{-1,+1\}$ a simple model is $y=\beta_0+\beta_Ax_A+\beta_Bx_B+\beta_{AB}x_Ax_B+\varepsilon$. In a balanced unreplicated $2^2$ table, coefficients follow from four readings: $\beta_A=\tfrac14\sum_i x_{A,i}y_i$ and similarly for $B$ and $AB$; $\beta_0$ is the grand mean. With ±1 coding the high-minus-low main effect is $2\beta_A$. One observation per cell leaves no independent residual degrees of freedom for pure-error estimation.

$$
\beta_A=\frac14\sum_{i=1}^{4}x_{A,i}y_i,\qquad\operatorname{Effect}(A)=2\beta_A.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

Consider fabricated $2^2$ responses $y_{--}=8$, $y_{+-}=12$, $y_{-+}=10$ and $y_{++}=18$. The grand mean is $\beta_0=12$, with $\beta_A=(12+18-8-10)/4=3$, $\beta_B=2$ and $\beta_{AB}=1$. The observed main effects are $6$ and $4$ response units; the interaction coefficient is $1$, corresponding to a two-unit interaction contrast under this convention. The diagram displays all four treatment combinations.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find the A main effect for $\beta_A=2.5$ under ±1 coding.
2. Why are repeated readings at each factor combination valuable?
3. In $y=\beta_0+\beta_Ax_A+\beta_Bx_B+\beta_{AB}x_Ax_B$, which coefficient encodes the interaction of A and B?

### Solutions

1. $2\beta_A=5$.
2. Replication allows independent noise estimates and reproducibility checks.
3. $\beta_{AB}$.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The four treatment combinations describe effects within the selected factor ranges. With no replication, the saturated model cannot independently estimate pure experimental error. Randomization, blocking, calibration, safety controls and causal interpretation still require experimental justification. NIST supports the design framework; the statistical course clarifies the coefficient convention.
