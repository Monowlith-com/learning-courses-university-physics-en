# M16-L8 – Laboratory Records, Uncertainty Budget and Reproducibility {#L8}

**Course area:** M16 – Bachelor: Experimental Physics and Measurement Analysis

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** M16-L1–L7: measurement model, statistics, uncertainty, regression and tests. [Previous lesson](M16-L7.md).

**Learning objective:** Prepare a reproducible measurement record with raw data, calibration chain, uncertainty budget and result statement.

## From Setup to Raw Data {#S1}

A useful lab record lets an independent reader reconstruct what was actually measured. Record the measurand definition, instruments with relevant identifiers, setup, environment, settings, calibration information, time, raw observations and processing rules. Raw records are not replaced by rounded final values. Computational reproducibility calls for software versions, parameters, missing-data handling and, when appropriate, an executable script. *Reproducible* here first means obtaining the same result from the same data; an independent fresh measurement additionally tests experimental replicability. Report what has actually been checked and any access limitations.

## Uncertainty Budget and Result Statement {#S2}

An uncertainty budget lists every input quantity, estimate, standard uncertainty, evaluation type, distribution assumption, sensitivity and known correlations. For independent contributions, add squared terms; for dependent contributions include covariances. NIST guidance distinguishes combined standard uncertainty $u_c$ from expanded uncertainty $U$. A result statement may take the form

$$
y=(\hat y\pm U)\,\mathrm{unit},\qquad U=k\,u_c.
$$

State $k$, why it was selected, the meaning of the uncertainty and, where applicable, the intended coverage probability. Round the numerical result in accordance with the uncertainty rather than implying false precision.

## Example: Reporting a Voltage Measurement {#S3}

Suppose a corrected voltage estimate is $\hat y=5.003\,\mathrm V$. Independent standard uncertainty contributions for repeatability and calibration are $u_A=0.020\,\mathrm V$ and $u_B=0.015\,\mathrm V$. Then

$$
u_c=\sqrt{0.020^2+0.015^2}\,\mathrm V=0.025\,\mathrm V,\qquad U=2u_c=0.050\,\mathrm V.
$$

A transparent rounded report is $(5.00\pm0.05)\,\mathrm V$ with $k=2$. Claiming approximately 95% coverage additionally requires justification of the distributions and effective degrees of freedom. The diagram connects data capture, analysis, uncertainty budgeting and reporting.

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find combined standard uncertainty for independent $u_1=6$ and $u_2=8\,\mathrm{mV}$.
2. Find $U$ for $k=2$ and $u_c=0.025\,\mathrm V$.
3. Name four items required for another researcher to reproduce the analysis.

### Solutions

1. $u_c=\sqrt{6^2+8^2}=10\,\mathrm{mV}$.
2. $U=0.050\,\mathrm V$.
3. For example raw records, analysis formulae, software version and processing parameters; also conditions and calibration references.

## Auditability and Limitations {#S5}

A good report cannot replace careful experimental design. The method must suit the intended decision, traceability applies to a documented result, and edits must remain auditable. Log corrections with reason, date and retained originals. Update the uncertainty budget when replacing sensors or changing processing. The cited sources establish metrological concepts of traceability and uncertainty reporting; they do not independently mandate one digital archive format or laboratory retention period. Document those operational choices separately.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}
A complete result combines estimate, uncertainty, method, traceability and reproducible derivation.
