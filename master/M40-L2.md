# M40-L2 – MRI Gradient Encoding, Larmor Frequency and k-Space {#L2}

**Course area:** M40 – Master: Advanced Medical Physics and Quality Assurance

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M24: MRI foundations; M33: magnetic resonance and spectroscopy; M40-L1.

**Learning objective:** Distinguish angular frequency, ordinary frequency and spatial k-space coordinates.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

In MRI, magnetic moments precess in a static field. An added linear gradient links position to resonance frequency or accumulated phase. A spatial image is reconstructed from time-sampled complex signals. Larmor frequency alone does not determine spatial resolution.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

With $B(x)=B_0+G_xx$ and the gyromagnetic ratio $\gamma$ expressed in $\mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}\,T^{-1}}$, $\omega(x)=\gamma B(x)$. Ordinary frequency is $f=\omega/(2\pi)$. For a constant gradient direction and Fourier convention $e^{-i2\pi k_xx}$, the spatial-frequency coordinate can be defined as $k_x(t)=\gamma(2\pi)^{-1}\int_0^tG_x(t\prime)dt\prime$ in cycles per metre. Signal-acquisition signs and offsets depend on sequence conventions. The published correction to a nuclide table in an earlier overview is not used here as a numerical source.

$$
\Delta f=\frac{\gamma}{2\pi}G_xx,\qquad k_x(t)=\frac{\gamma}{2\pi}\int_0^tG_x(t\prime)\,dt\prime.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

Use the standard approximation $\gamma/(2\pi)\approx42.58\,\mathrm{MHz\,T^{-1}}$ for hydrogen nuclei as a physical model parameter. With $G_x=10\,\mathrm{mT\,m^{-1}}$ and $x=2\,\mathrm{cm}$, $\Delta f\approx8516\,\mathrm{Hz}$. After a constant gradient duration of $1\,\mathrm{ms}$, the magnitude of $k_x\approx426\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$. These numbers represent distinct quantities.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What is the frequency shift at $x=1\,\mathrm{cm}$?
2. What are the units of $k_x$?
3. Why does one k-space sample not determine a complete image?

### Solutions

1. Approximately $4258\,\mathrm{Hz}$.
2. Cycles per metre ($\mathrm{m^{-1}}$).
3. One sample carries only one component of the spatial-frequency information; imaging needs sufficient sampling and reconstruction.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The model ignores relaxation, field inhomogeneity, receive coils and gradient ramps. Illustrative gradients are not clinical sequence or safety recommendations; resolution additionally requires FOV, bandwidth and sampling assumptions.
