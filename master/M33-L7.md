# M33-L7 – Nonlinear Optics: Second-Harmonic Generation and Phase Matching {#L7}

**Course area:** M33 – Master: Atomic, Molecular and Optical Physics

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M15: wave optics and dispersion; M32-L4: spectroscopy; M33-L4.

**Learning objective:** Evaluate how second-harmonic output scales with pump intensity, crystal length, and phase mismatch.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

In a noncentrosymmetric medium, the induced electric polarization can contain a term quadratic in the driving electric field. An incident field at frequency ω therefore produces a polarization component at 2ω and can generate second-harmonic light. Nonlinearity alone is insufficient: waves accumulating different phases may cancel partly along the crystal.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

For $P=\epsilon_0[\chi^{(1)}E+\chi^{(2)}E^2+\cdots]$, the squared sinusoidal field contains a doubled-frequency component as well as a DC contribution. With constant pump intensity, a plane wave, an undepleted fundamental field, and negligible absorption, $I_{2\omega}\propto d_{\mathrm{eff}}^2I_\omega^2L^2\operatorname{sinc}^2(\Delta kL/2)$, where $\operatorname{sinc}(x)=\sin(x)/x$ and $\Delta k=k_{2\omega}-2k_\omega$. The proportionality constant includes refractive indices and frequency factors. Only at $\Delta k=0$ does the full $L^2$ scaling persist without phase loss.

$$
I_{2\omega}\propto I_\omega^2L^2\operatorname{sinc}^2\!\left(\frac{\Delta kL}{2}\right).
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

At perfect phase matching, doubling $L$ gives a factor of four in the undepleted-pump limit; doubling $I_\omega$ also gives a factor of four. For $\Delta kL=\pi$, the normalized phase-matching factor is $\operatorname{sinc}^2(\pi/2)=4/\pi^2\approx0.405$. The graphic shows the main peak at zero mismatch and the decaying side lobes.

![Normalized squared-sinc phase-matching curve with a central maximum and side lobes.](../../../../media/course/U_PHY_259_EN.svg)

*In the undepleted-pump limit, phase mismatch reduces second-harmonic output through a sinc² factor.*

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. How does $I_{2\omega}$ change if $I_\omega$ is halved, all else fixed?
2. What is $\operatorname{sinc}^2(0)$?
3. Which real effect limits indefinite $L^2$ growth at high conversion?

### Solutions

1. It falls to one quarter.
2. $1$ by the continuous limit.
3. Pump depletion; absorption and group-velocity walk-off can also limit conversion.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The displayed scaling is not a universal design rule for focused, ultrashort, or strongly converted pulses. Phase matching depends on direction, polarization, dispersion, temperature, and possibly periodic poling. The cited paper supports the scaling and sinc² factor; it is not a complete tensor derivation or experimental sign-off.
