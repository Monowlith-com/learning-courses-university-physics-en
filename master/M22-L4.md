# M22-L4 – Polymer Chains and Entropic Elasticity {#L4}

**Course area:** M22 – Bachelor: Biophysics

**Level:** Bachelor · Elective

**Prerequisites:** M13: statistical ensembles; M22-L1: random motion. [Previous lesson](M22-L3.md).

**Learning objective:** Derive end-to-end distances for simple polymer models and explain the statistical origin of entropic restoring forces.

## Question and Observation {#S1}

A long flexible biomolecule can adopt many spatial conformations. Many more coiled conformations are available than fully extended arrangements. Pulling the chain reduces the number of accessible conformations and hence produces an effective restoring force, even if the individual idealised segments are rigid. This is entropic elasticity.

## Physical Model and Equations {#S2}

Take a freely jointed chain of $N$ independent segments of length $b$. For the end-to-end vector $\boldsymbol R=\sum_i\boldsymbol b_i$, isotropy makes the cross terms in $\langle R^2\rangle$ vanish, so $\langle R^2\rangle=Nb^2$. With many segments and small extension, the almost Gaussian distribution $P(\boldsymbol R)\propto\exp[-3R^2/(2Nb^2)]$ gives the effective free energy $F(R)=3k_{\mathrm B}TR^2/(2Nb^2)+\mathrm{const}$. Thus

$$
\sqrt{\langle R^2\rangle}=b\sqrt{N},\qquad f\simeq\frac{3k_{\mathrm B}T}{Nb^2}R.
$$

Here $f$ is the force needed to hold the molecule at extension $R$ against the entropic pull. The linear approximation fails near the maximum contour length $L_c=Nb$. Real DNA also has bending stiffness and is often modelled by a worm-like chain at larger forces.

## Worked Example and Teaching Diagram {#S3}

For $N=100$ segments of length $b=1\,\mathrm{nm}$, the RMS end-to-end distance is $10\,\mathrm{nm}$. At $T=298\,\mathrm K$ and $R=5\,\mathrm{nm}$, the ideal small-extension force is $f\approx0.62\,\mathrm{pN}$. ## Self-Assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find $\sqrt{\langle R^2\rangle}$ for $N=400$ and $b=0.5\,\mathrm{nm}$.
2. What happens to the small-extension force when temperature doubles at fixed $N,b,R$?
3. Why does the ideal Hooke-like relation fail as $R$ approaches $Nb$?

### Solutions

1. $0.5\sqrt{400}\,\mathrm{nm}=10\,\mathrm{nm}$.
2. Because $f\propto T$, it doubles.
3. The Gaussian expression allows arbitrarily large $R$, but the segment chain has a finite contour length.

## Validity Limits and Quality {#S5}

The freely jointed chain neglects correlations, excluded volume, solvent quality and bending rigidity. Its linear force relation requires $R\ll Nb$ and sufficiently many segments. The illustrated conformations are schematic.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

The size of an ideal random polymer scales as $\sqrt N$. Small extension removes configurational entropy and produces a temperature-dependent restoring force.
