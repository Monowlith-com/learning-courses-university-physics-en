# M28-L8 – Fermi liquids, self-energy, and quasiparticles {#L8}

**Course area:** M28 – Master: Nonequilibrium and many-body physics

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M13: Fermi gas; M14: condensed matter; M28-L7: spectral function · [Previous lesson](M28-L7.md).

**Learning objective:** Interpret quasiparticle residue, linewidth, and lifetime using a retarded self-energy.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Electrons in a metal interact, yet often display long-lived excitations near the Fermi surface. Fermi-liquid theory treats these as quasiparticles. Their narrow coherent spectral contribution is not the whole single-particle spectrum: interactions redistribute weight to other excitations.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

Near a simple quasiparticle pole, expand the real self-energy $\Sigma^R(k,E)$ around the Fermi level. At $k=k_F$ and energy $E=0$ relative to the chemical potential, the quasiparticle residue is $Z=[1-\partial_E\operatorname{Re}\Sigma^R]^{-1}$. A negative retarded imaginary part represents damping. For weak damping, define the full energy linewidth $\Gamma_{\mathrm{qp}}=-2Z\operatorname{Im}\Sigma^R(k,E_*)>0$ and the usual population lifetime $\tau=\hbar/\Gamma_{\mathrm{qp}}$. Compare this with the Lorentzian in M28-L7, where $\Gamma$ denoted half the full width. Explicit definitions prevent a factor-of-two error.

$$
Z=\left[1-\left.\partial_E\operatorname{Re}\Sigma^R(k,E)\right|_{k_F,0}\right]^{-1},\quad \Gamma_{\mathrm{qp}}=-2Z\operatorname{Im}\Sigma^R(k,E_*),\quad\tau=\frac{\hbar}{\Gamma_{\mathrm{qp}}}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For $Z=0.75$, the real self-energy derivative at the Fermi level is $1-1/Z=-1/3$. For a full linewidth $\Gamma_{\mathrm{qp}}=0.040\,\mathrm{eV}$ and $\hbar\approx6.5821\times10^{-16}\,\mathrm{eV\,s}$, the lifetime is $\tau\approx1.65\times10^{-14}\,\mathrm s=16.5\,\mathrm{fs}$. The diagram combines a narrow coherent peak with a broad incoherent contribution. This is a spectral-weight model, not experimental data.

![Spectral distribution showing a narrow coherent quasiparticle peak and a broad incoherent background component.](../../../../media/course/U_PHY_092_EN.svg)

*The residue $Z$ measures the coherent quasiparticle fraction of the single-particle spectral weight.*

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What is the real self-energy derivative for $Z=1$?
2. Find $\tau$ for $\Gamma_{\mathrm{qp}}=0.020\,\mathrm{eV}$.
3. What does $1-Z$ represent in the simple spectral model?

### Solutions

1. Zero.
2. $3.29\times10^{-14}\,\mathrm s\approx32.9\,\mathrm{fs}$.
3. Spectral weight not concentrated in the coherent quasiparticle pole.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The quasiparticle approximation is a low-energy description. It can fail in one-dimensional Luttinger liquids or near certain quantum critical points. Linewidth, residue, and self-energy vary with energy and momentum.

## Summary and outlook {#S6}

Spectral weight and lifetime link many-body self-energy to measurable quantum matter. The next module turns to relativistic field and geometry concepts.
