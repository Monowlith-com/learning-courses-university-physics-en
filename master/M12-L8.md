# M12-L8 – WKB Approximation and Classical Turning Points {#L8}

**Course area:** M12 – Bachelor: Advanced Quantum Mechanics

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** Stationary Schrödinger equation, differential equations, and tunnelling; M12-L3. [Previous lesson](M12-L7.md).

**Learning objective:** Explain semiclassical phases, exponential attenuation and the limits of WKB.

## Between Classical Motion and Wave Description {#S1}

The WKB approximation (Wentzel–Kramers–Brillouin) links the Schrödinger equation to a local classical momentum. If wavelength is short compared with the variation scale of the potential, one may treat amplitude as slowly varying and phase as rapidly varying. It describes oscillations in classically allowed regions and exponential attenuation in forbidden ones. Their boundary is a classical turning point where $E=V(x)$. Precisely at that point the standard local WKB expression ceases to be directly valid.

## Local Momentum and Validity Condition {#S2}

With $p(x)=\sqrt{2m(E-V(x))}$, the leading WKB solution in an allowed region is

$$
\psi(x)\approx\frac{C_+}{\sqrt{p(x)}}e^{(i/\hbar)\int^x p(u)du}+\frac{C_-}{\sqrt{p(x)}}e^{-(i/\hbar)\int^x p(u)du}
$$

In forbidden regions define $\kappa(x)=\sqrt{2m(V(x)-E)}/\hbar$ and use growing or decaying exponentials. A schematic criterion is $|\hbar p'(x)|\ll p(x)^2$. At a turning point with $p=0$ the prefactor diverges; local Airy-function solutions and connection formulae are needed. For two simple turning points, leading Bohr–Sommerfeld quantization reads $\int_{x_1}^{x_2}p(x)dx\approx\pi\hbar(n+1/2)$.

## Example: Rectangular Barrier {#S3}

For a high, broad barrier with $V(x)>E$ between $x_1$ and $x_2$, leading tunnelling behaviour is

$$
T\propto\exp\!\left[-2\int_{x_1}^{x_2}\kappa(x)\,dx\right]
$$

With a constant offset $V_0-E=1.0\,\mathrm{eV}$ for an electron, $\kappa\simeq5.12\,\mathrm{nm^{-1}}$. Across $a=1.0\,\mathrm{nm}$ the exponential factor is $e^{-10.25}\simeq3.5\times10^{-5}$. The proportionality sign emphasizes that interface and reflection prefactors were not included. ## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. How is a classical turning point identified?
2. How does the exponential factor change when constant $\kappa a$ doubles?
3. Why must the local WKB wavefunction not be continued unchanged through a turning point?

### Solutions

1. There $E=V(x)$ and $p(x)=0$.
2. $e^{-2\kappa a}$ becomes $e^{-4\kappa a}$, its square.
3. The local approximation formally diverges, so a regular local solution and matching are required.

## Connection Formulae, Not Naive Continuation {#S5}

WKB becomes inaccurate when the potential varies too rapidly, the energy lies close to the barrier top, or multiple reflections create strong resonances. A rectangular step is abrupt, violating the slow-variation assumption; its WKB exponent is only a rough guide, while exact matching from M12-L3 is preferable. The quantization rule also depends on turning-point structure and boundary conditions. A careful calculation therefore states the potential, identifies semiclassical regions and specifies which connection formulae are used.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

WKB explains wave phases and tunnelling attenuation in semiclassical regions, but turning points require special matching.
