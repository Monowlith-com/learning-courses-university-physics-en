# M17-L3 – Root Finding and Nonlinear Equations {#L3}

**Course area:** M17 – Bachelor: Computational Physics and Simulation

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** M2: derivatives, continuity; M17-L1: roundoff. [Previous lesson](M17-L2.md).

**Learning objective:** Compare bisection and Newton iteration and specify meaningful stopping criteria.

## Physical Equilibria as Roots {#S1}

A condition $F(x)=0$ can represent force balance, an energy minimum or a resonance equation. Meaningful numerical roots require a permitted domain, units for $x$ and the physical interpretation of potentially multiple solutions. For continuous $F$, a sign change $F(a)F(b)<0$ guarantees at least one root in $[a,b]$ by the intermediate value theorem. It does not guarantee uniqueness or convergence of an arbitrary iteration.

## Bracketing and Local Iteration {#S2}

Bisection halves a bracketing interval after each sign test. After $n$ halvings the bracket width is $(b-a)/2^n$, so its midpoint differs from a contained root by no more than half that width. Newton's method uses the local derivative and can converge quadratically near a simple root. It may fail when $F'(x)$ is small, when the starting point is unsuitable or when an update leaves the physically admissible domain. Robust solvers often combine a bracket with faster local steps.

$$
x_{k+1}^{\mathrm N}=x_k-\frac{F(x_k)}{F'(x_k)}.
$$

$$
b_n-a_n=\frac{b_0-a_0}{2^n}.
$$

## Example: Square Root of Two {#S3}

For $F(x)=x^2-2$, the positive root is bracketed by $1$ and $2$. After $n=20$ bisections the interval width is $2^{-20}\approx9.54\times10^{-7}$, and the midpoint error relative to the enclosed root is at most $2^{-21}$. Newton iteration starting at $x_0=1.5$ uses $x_{k+1}=(x_k+2/x_k)/2$ and rapidly approaches $\sqrt2$. This contrasts a guaranteed bracket with a fast but local update.

**Executable Python example:**

```python
import math
left, right = 1.0, 2.0
for _ in range(20):
    mid = 0.5*(left+right)
    if (mid*mid-2)*(left*left-2) > 0:
        left = mid
    else:
        right = mid
print((left+right)/2, right-left)
x = 1.5
for _ in range(4):
    x = 0.5*(x+2/x)
print(x, math.sqrt(2))
```

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What bracket width results from $[1,2]$ after ten bisection steps?
2. Take one Newton step from $x_0=1.5$ for $F(x)=x^2-2$.
3. Why is a small residual alone an insufficient stopping criterion?

### Solutions

1. $1/1024\approx0.00097656$.
2. $x_1=1.5-0.25/3=1.4166667$.
3. A flat slope allows a small function value even far from the root.

## Stability and Stopping {#S5}

A small residual $|F(x)|$ does not always imply a small error in $x$: a flat function can yield a small residual far from the root. Stopping rules combine step or bracket width with a scaled residual and an iteration limit. Scales should match physical tolerances and observations. Check domain constraints before and after solving. Repeated roots can lack sign changes and require other strategies.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Bracketing offers robust bounds; fast local iterations need convergence diagnostics.
