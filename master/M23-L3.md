# M23-L3 – Thin Films, Reflection Phase and Interference {#L3}

**Course area:** M23 – Bachelor: Applied Physics, Photonics and Nanotechnology

**Level:** Bachelor · Elective

**Prerequisites:** M7: coherent superposition; M10: Fresnel interfaces. [Previous lesson](M23-L2.md).

**Learning objective:** Derive optical path differences in a thin film and explain the role of reflection phase.

## Question and Observation {#S1}

A transparent thin coating can reflect different colours with very different efficiency. Partial reflections form at the front and rear interfaces. The returning rays acquire a propagation path difference and may also have an additional π phase from reflection at a boundary. Thus a simple integer-wavelength rule is valid only when its phase assumptions are stated.

## Physical Model and Equations {#S2}

A wave traversing a homogeneous film of thickness $d$ and index $n_f$ at internal angle $\theta_f$ acquires a propagation phase relative to the first reflection of $\Delta\phi_{\mathrm{prop}}=(4\pi/\lambda_0)n_fd\cos\theta_f$. If the interface-reflection phase differences cancel, imposing $\Delta\phi_{\mathrm{prop}}=2\pi m$ gives

$$
2n_fd\cos\theta_f=m\lambda_0\qquad\text{(round-trip phase }2\pi m\text{ without interface phase shift)}.
$$

If exactly one reflected contribution has an additional $\pi$ phase shift, the same propagation condition is destructive rather than constructive for the sum of reflected fields. At normal incidence, a quarter-wave antireflection film has $d=\lambda_0/(4n_f)$, and cancellation is strong when reflected amplitudes also match. For transparent materials between ambient $n_0$ and substrate $n_s$, an ideal single-layer design often takes $n_f\approx\sqrt{n_0n_s}$. Multilayers and absorbing films are better analysed using complex Fresnel coefficients and transfer matrices.

## Self-Assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find the quarter-wave thickness at $\lambda_0=600\,\mathrm{nm}$ and $n_f=1.50$.
2. When does $2n_fd=m\lambda_0$ fail to be constructive for reflection?
3. Why is a path-only phase argument insufficient for strongly absorbing films?

### Solutions

1. $d=600/(4\cdot1.50)\,\mathrm{nm}=100\,\mathrm{nm}$.
2. A single relative $\pi$ phase shift from reflection reverses the interference condition.
3. Absorption introduces complex refractive indices and altered field amplitudes; transfer matrices account for both.

## Validity Limits and Quality {#S5}

The simple interference rule assumes a homogeneous, isotropic, weakly absorbing film and adequate coherence. Real coatings may be dispersive, polarisation-dependent and multilayered. A theoretical dip is not a guaranteed measured minimum.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Thin-film colours arise from superposing several reflected electric fields. Both propagation and interface-reflection phases matter.
