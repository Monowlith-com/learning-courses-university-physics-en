# M31-L5 – Inverse problems and regularization {#L5}

**Course area:** M31 – Master: Numerical and Computational Physics

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M20: linear algebra and optimization; M31-L4: uncertainty · [Previous lesson](M31-L4.md).

**Learning objective:** Formulate a Tikhonov-regularized objective and compute the effect of its regularization parameter.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

A forward model predicts observations from parameters; the inverse problem seeks parameters compatible with data. Inverse problems can be ill posed: small measurement errors may then cause large changes in reconstructed quantities. Regularization adds an explicit preference over candidate solutions.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

For the linear model $y=Ax+\epsilon$, the simplest Tikhonov form minimizes $J(x)=\|Ax-y\|_2^2+\lambda\|x\|_2^2$ with $\lambda>0$. Differentiating with respect to $x$ yields $2A^\top(Ax-y)+2\lambda x=0$ and hence $(A^\top A+\lambda I)x_\lambda=A^\top y$. With $\lambda>0$, the normal matrix is positive definite. At small $\lambda$, the data fit is prioritized; larger $\lambda$ damps the reconstructed solution. This penalty implicitly uses a zero reference and a quadratic isotropic prior preference.

$$
x_\lambda=(A^\top A+\lambda I)^{-1}A^\top y\quad(\lambda>0).
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

In the scalar example $A=0.5$ and $y=1$, the solution is $x_\lambda=0.5/(0.25+\lambda)$. Without regularization, $x_0=2$; at $\lambda=0.25$, $x_\lambda=1$. The absolute data residual rises from $0$ to $|0.5\cdot1-1|=0.5$, while the solution magnitude falls. The diagram shows this trade-off but does not identify an optimal $\lambda$.

![Reconstructed scalar x lambda decreases from two toward zero as Tikhonov regularization grows.](../../../../media/course/U_PHY_294_EN.svg)

*Regularization can damp unstable solutions while also altering the fit to observations.*

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find $x_\lambda$ when $A=1$, $y=3$, $\lambda=2$.
2. What is the absolute data residual for the example at $\lambda=0.25$?
3. Why is choosing $\lambda$ a modeling decision?

### Solutions

1. $x_\lambda=1$.
2. $0.5$.
3. It sets the balance between matching observations and preferred solution structure.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

Standard quadratic regularization favors small-amplitude solutions, but is not universal. Nonlinear forward models, correlated measurement errors, spatial structure or sparsity may require other objectives and priors. Numerically QR/SVD formulations are often preferable to explicitly inverting normal equations.
