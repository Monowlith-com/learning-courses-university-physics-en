# M30-L6 – Dirac fields and quantum electrodynamics {#L6}

**Course area:** M30 – Master: Quantum field theory and the Standard Model

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M30-L5: field interactions; M25: spin · [Previous lesson](M30-L5.md).

**Learning objective:** Distinguish spinor from scalar fields and identify the structure of a QED vertex.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Electrons carry spin one half and obey the Pauli principle. They cannot be represented by the bosonic scalar field of M30-L1. The Dirac equation uses spinors and matrices; quantum electrodynamics (QED) couples such charged fields to the electromagnetic gauge field.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

The Dirac matrices obey $\{\gamma^\mu,\gamma^\nu\}=2\eta^{\mu\nu}\mathbf1$ for signature $(+---)$. A free spinor field obeys $(i\gamma^\mu\partial_\mu-m)\psi=0$. Electromagnetic coupling is introduced by $D_\mu=\partial_\mu+ieA_\mu$ for the electron-field convention chosen here; observable results do not depend on a simultaneous arbitrary sign convention. The interaction produces a vertex with two fermion legs and one photon leg. Fermion-field excitations obey anticommutation relations.

$$
(i\gamma^\mu D_\mu-m)\psi=0,\qquad D_\mu=\partial_\mu+ieA_\mu.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

At low energies the dimensionless fine-structure constant is approximately $\alpha=e^2/(4\pi)\approx1/137$ in natural Heaviside–Lorentz units. A contribution with two electromagnetic coupling factors in its amplitude is of order $e^2$, but its full rate still depends on kinematics and diagram structure. The figure shows the elementary fermion–photon vertex.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Which statistics do Dirac fermions follow?
2. How many fermion and photon legs meet at a fundamental QED vertex?
3. What follows from $\{\gamma^0,\gamma^0\}=2\mathbf1$?

### Solutions

1. Fermi–Dirac statistics with anticommutation relations.
2. Two fermion legs and one photon leg.
3. $(\gamma^0)^2=\mathbf1$ in the chosen signature.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The drawn vertex is only the elementary QED interaction, not a complete experimental prediction. At high energies the coupling runs; precision calculations also require regularization, renormalization, and well-defined observables.
