# M29-L3 – Covariant derivative and the geodesic equation {#L3}

**Course area:** M29 – Master: General relativity and cosmology

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M26: vector and tensor calculus; M29-L2: metric and proper time · [Previous lesson](M29-L2.md).

**Learning objective:** Explain the geodesic equation through parallel transport and interpret connection coefficients in polar coordinates.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Even in flat geometry, a straight trajectory expressed in curvilinear coordinates can have nonzero coordinate acceleration. Christoffel symbols describe how the coordinate basis changes. Their presence alone does not establish physical curvature.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

The Levi-Civita connection is metric-compatible and torsion-free. Its coefficients are $\Gamma^\mu_{\alpha\beta}=(1/2)g^{\mu\nu}(\partial_\alpha g_{\nu\beta}+\partial_\beta g_{\nu\alpha}-\partial_\nu g_{\alpha\beta})$. Along an affinely parametrized geodesic, the tangent vector transports parallel to itself. In the Euclidean plane with polar metric $ds^2=dr^2+r^2d\varphi^2$, we have $\Gamma^r_{\varphi\varphi}=-r$ and $\Gamma^\varphi_{r\varphi}=1/r$. The radial equation therefore contains $-r\dot\varphi^2$. This does not imply intrinsic curvature of the plane.

$$
\frac{d^2x^\mu}{d\lambda^2}+\Gamma^\mu_{\alpha\beta}\frac{dx^\alpha}{d\lambda}\frac{dx^\beta}{d\lambda}=0.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

A point travels along a straight line of constant $x$ and varying $y$ in the Euclidean plane. Its Cartesian accelerations vanish, while $\ddot r$ and $\ddot\varphi$ are generally nonzero in polar coordinates. At $r=2\,\mathrm m$ and $\dot\varphi=0.5\,\mathrm s^{-1}$, the connection term is $-r\dot\varphi^2=-0.5\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The drawing places a Cartesian straight path on a polar grid.

![Polar coordinate grid with concentric circles and radial rays; a straight trajectory crosses curved coordinate lines.](../../../../media/course/U_PHY_049_EN.svg)

*Nonzero Christoffel symbols can be an effect of coordinates alone.*

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Name the two defining conditions of the Levi-Civita connection.
2. Evaluate $-r\dot\varphi^2$ for $r=4\,\mathrm m$ and $\dot\varphi=0.25\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$.
3. Is the Euclidean plane curved because $\Gamma^r_{\varphi\varphi}$ can be nonzero?

### Solutions

1. Metric compatibility and zero torsion.
2. $-0.25\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
3. No; the coefficients depend on coordinates but the Riemann tensor vanishes.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The geodesic equation describes free test bodies without nongravitational forces. Affine parametrization is essential for a zero right-hand side. Christoffel symbols are not tensors, and individual coefficients are not coordinate-independent observables.

## Summary and outlook {#S6}

The connection explains local changes of direction. The noncommutativity of covariant derivatives leads to measurable curvature in M29-L4. [Next lesson](M29-L4.md).
