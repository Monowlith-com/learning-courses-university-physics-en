# M15-L8 – Accelerators, Particle Detectors and Event Reconstruction {#L8}

**Course area:** M15 – Bachelor: Nuclear and Particle Physics

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** M6: Lorentz force, M15-L5–L7, four-momentum and counting statistics. [Previous lesson](M15-L7.md).

**Learning objective:** Combine acceleration, track curvature and calorimetric energy measurement.

## Acceleration and Beam Guidance {#S1}

Electric fields transfer energy to charged particles; magnetic fields ideally change direction at fixed particle energy. In a ring accelerator, radiofrequency cavities repeatedly accelerate particles, dipole magnets bend the orbit and quadrupole magnets focus the beam. Colliding beams often provide more usable center-of-mass energy than a stationary target for given beam energies. Event rate is the product of luminosity and process cross section. Beam quality, bunch geometry and stability are crucial practical factors. An accelerator diagram describes principles rather than current operating energies or powers of any named facility.

## Tracking, Calorimetry and Muon Systems {#S2}

General-purpose detectors often combine inner trackers, electromagnetic and hadronic calorimeters, and outer muon systems. In a uniform magnetic field $B$ with transverse motion, $p_T=|q|Br$. In convenient units,

$$
p_T[\mathrm{GeV}/c]\approx0.300\,|z|\,B[\mathrm T]r[\mathrm m],
$$

where $q=ze$. Track curvature gives charge sign and momentum; calorimeters record deposited energy, while muons often traverse substantial material. Neutrinos are inferred indirectly from momentum balance and event topology rather than directly tracked. Every detector has finite resolution and limited acceptance.

## Example: Momentum from Curvature {#S3}

A singly charged particle, $|z|=1$, in a $B=2.0\,\mathrm T$ field with idealized radius $r=1.5\,\mathrm m$ has

$$
p_T\approx0.300\times2.0\times1.5=0.90\,\mathrm{GeV}/c.
$$

At fixed field and charge, higher momentum corresponds to a larger radius. The graphic shows a curved track, a calorimeter layer and an outer penetrating-muon path. The given full-circle radius is a model quantity; in real detectors short track segments must be fitted while accounting for material effects. Momentum resolution often deteriorates for nearly straight tracks.

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find $p_T$ for $B=1.0\,\mathrm T$, $r=2.0\,\mathrm m$ and $|z|=1$.
2. What does a larger curvature radius imply at fixed $B$ and $q$?
3. Why does missing momentum not prove a specific new particle?

### Solutions

1. $p_T\approx0.60\,\mathrm{GeV}/c$.
2. Larger transverse momentum.
3. Neutrinos, unmeasured particles and detector effects can mimic the signature.

## Reconstruction and Uncertainties {#S5}

Reconstruction combines calibration, geometrical alignment, timing, track fits, clustering and identification criteria. Multiple scattering, energy loss, pile-up and detector dead time can bias measurements. Triggers reduce recorded event volume and their selection probabilities must be modeled later. Missing transverse momentum indicates unseen or unmeasured momentum carriers, not proof of one particular new particle. Analysis requires statistical and systematic uncertainties and independent validation, not merely attractive event displays.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}
Accelerators produce controlled collisions; track and energy measurements require calibrated reconstruction to yield credible events.
