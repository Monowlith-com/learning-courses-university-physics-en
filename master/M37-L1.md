# M37-L1 – Stellar Structure and Hydrostatic Equilibrium {#L1}

**Course area:** M37 – Master: Astrophysics and Cosmology

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M6: continuum mechanics; M22: thermodynamics; M36: nuclear and particle physics · [Previous lesson](M36-L8.md).

**Learning objective:** Explain a spherically symmetric stellar pressure gradient and estimate it in a uniform-density toy star.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

A long-lived star is not a static ball of gas without energy transport: its layers balance gravity, pressure, heat transport and energy generation. Quasi-static models describe enclosed mass, pressure, temperature and luminosity as radial functions. Nuclear reactions or other energy sources sustain thermal evolution, depending on the evolutionary stage.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

In a thin spherical shell, an outward pressure-gradient force balances inward gravity. For enclosed mass $m(r)$, density $\rho(r)$ and radius $r$, $dP/dr=-Gm(r)\rho(r)/r^2$. Combined with $dm/dr=4\pi r^2\rho$ this is a coupled system. A constant-density sphere $\rho=3M/(4\pi R^3)$ with boundary condition $P(R)=0$ gives $P_c=3GM^2/(8\pi R^4)$. This is an ideal mathematical comparison, not a realistic solar model.

$$
\frac{dP}{dr}=-\frac{Gm(r)\rho(r)}{r^2},\qquad \frac{dm}{dr}=4\pi r^2\rho(r).
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For the constructed example $M=2.0\times10^{30}\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $R=7.0\times10^8\,\mathrm m$, the constant-density sphere yields $P_c\approx1.33\times10^{14}\,\mathrm{Pa}$. Real stars are strongly stratified; this is not a measured solar core pressure. The graphic separates pressure support and gravitational pull in a notional shell.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. How does $P_c$ change if $R$ doubles at fixed $M$?
2. What sign has $dP/dr$ when $m,\rho,r>0$?
3. Why does a stellar model need an energy-transport relation in addition to $dP/dr$?

### Solutions

1. $P_c$ decreases by a factor of $16$.
2. Negative: pressure decreases outward.
3. Without heat transport and temperature, the equation of state and structure are not closed.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

Hydrostatic balance assumes approximately slow evolution. Uniform density, no rotation and no magnetic fields are restrictive simplifications; convection and radiative transport may dominate.
