# M16-L6 – Sampling, Filtering and Measurement Noise {#L6}

**Course area:** M16 – Bachelor: Experimental Physics and Measurement Analysis

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** M4: waves, M6: RC circuits, M8: Fourier transforms. [Previous lesson](M16-L5.md).

**Learning objective:** Identify aliasing, find RC cutoff frequency and correctly integrate a one-sided noise power spectral density.

## Analog Signals and Sampling {#S1}

An analog signal $x(t)$ is recorded at intervals $T_s$, yielding sample rate $f_s=1/T_s$. With suitable ideal assumptions, a signal band-limited below $f_{\max}$ can be reconstructed if $f_s>2f_{\max}$. Otherwise distinct analog frequencies can produce the same discrete sequence, an effect called *aliasing*. The theoretical condition alone is insufficient for physical sensors: components above Nyquist must be adequately suppressed by an analog anti-aliasing filter before acquisition. Observation duration, quantization and timing jitter also affect spectral analysis.

## RC Low-Pass and Noise Variance {#S2}

For a passive RC low-pass with output across the capacitor,

$$
H(f)=\frac{1}{1+i2\pi fRC},\qquad f_c=\frac{1}{2\pi RC}.
$$

In the classical equilibrium model, the resistor has a *one-sided* thermal voltage power spectral density $S_U(f)=4k_BTR$ for $f\ge0$, with unit $\mathrm{V^2/Hz}$. At the output, $S_{U,\mathrm{out}}(f)=S_U(f)|H(f)|^2$. Integrating over positive frequencies gives 

$$
\left\langle U_{\mathrm{out}}^2\right\rangle=\int_0^{\infty}\frac{4k_BTR}{1+(2\pi fRC)^2}\,df=\frac{k_BT}{C}.
$$

This is **voltage variance**, not power delivered to a load; with a two-sided PSD the spectral-density factors and integration limits must be changed consistently.

## Example: A Recorded Sinusoid {#S3}

A $9\,\mathrm{Hz}$ sinusoid is sampled at $f_s=10\,\mathrm{Hz}$. Samples $\sin(2\pi9n/10)$ coincide, apart from phase or sign convention, with a $1$-Hz sequence; the apparent alias frequency is $1\,\mathrm{Hz}$. For an RC low-pass with $R=1.0\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$ and $C=1.0\,\mathrm{\mu F}$,

$$
f_c=\frac{1}{2\pi(10^3)(10^{-6})}\,\mathrm{Hz}\approx159.2\,\mathrm{Hz}.
$$

The sampling panel shows the 9-Hz wave and sample points that are also compatible with a slowly varying oscillation. This shows frequency ambiguity, not a simulation of an anti-aliasing filter.

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. State the Nyquist frequency for $f_s=200\,\mathrm{Hz}$.
2. Find $f_c$ for $R=2\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$ and $C=10\,\mathrm{\mu F}$.
3. What are the units of the integral of a one-sided voltage PSD over frequency?

### Solutions

1. $100\,\mathrm{Hz}$.
2. $f_c=1/(2\pi\cdot0.020\,\mathrm s)\approx7.96\,\mathrm{Hz}$.
3. $\mathrm{V^2}$, the voltage variance.

## Measurement Chain and Nonidealities {#S5}

The thermal noise formula assumes a classical linear RC equilibrium model. Real amplifiers add voltage and current noise and frequency-dependent terms such as $1/f$ noise. Finite observation bandwidth changes noise variance. Digital smoothing cannot remove alias components that were already folded during acquisition. An RC filter with cutoff $f_c$ well above the Nyquist frequency cannot provide adequate anti-aliasing on its own. Datasheets must distinguish amplitude spectral density in $\mathrm{V/\sqrt{Hz}}$, power spectral density in $\mathrm{V^2/Hz}$, and bandwidth-integrated RMS values.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Sampling needs analog frequency protection; noise integrals require an explicit PSD convention, bandwidth and units.
