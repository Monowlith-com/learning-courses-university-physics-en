# M30-L9 – Flavor mixing and neutrino oscillations {#L9}

**Course area:** M30 – Master: Quantum field theory and the Standard Model

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M30-L8: electroweak sector; M25: superposition · [Previous lesson](M30-L8.md).

**Learning objective:** Distinguish flavor and mass eigenstates and calculate the two-flavor oscillation probability.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Weak processes produce neutrinos in flavor states that are generally superpositions of distinct mass eigenstates. Different phases accumulate during propagation, changing which flavor may later be detected. A related distinction between flavor and mass bases occurs for quarks through the CKM matrix; neutrino mixing uses the PMNS matrix.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

In a simplified two-flavor vacuum model, $|\nu_a\rangle=\cos\theta|\nu_1\rangle+\sin\theta|\nu_2\rangle$ and the orthogonal state is $|\nu_b\rangle=-\sin\theta|\nu_1\rangle+\cos\theta|\nu_2\rangle$. Highly relativistic propagation leads to an approximate phase difference $\Delta m^2L/(2E)$ and a transition probability $P_{a\to b}=\sin^2(2\theta)\sin^2(\Delta m^2L/(4E))$. In common laboratory units, the argument of the sine has a conversion factor of approximately $1.267$.

$$
P_{a\to b}=\sin^2(2\theta)\sin^2\!\left(1.267\,\frac{\Delta m^2\,[\mathrm{eV}^2]L\,[\mathrm{km}]}{E\,[\mathrm{GeV}]}\right).
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For the **teaching-only two-flavor model** with $\theta=30^\circ$, $\Delta m^2=2.5\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{eV}^2$, $L=295\,\mathrm{km}$ and $E=0.60\,\mathrm{GeV}$, the argument is $\approx1.557$. Since $\sin^2(2\theta)=0.75$, the probability is $P_{a\to b}\approx0.750$. This is explicitly **not** a complete prediction for an actual $\nu_\mu\to\nu_e$ experiment.

![Oscillating transition probability between zero and a maximum of 0.75 for a fixed mixing angle.](../../../../media/course/U_PHY_188_EN.svg)

*In the two-flavor approximation, transition probability oscillates with baseline divided by energy.*

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What is the maximum possible probability at $\theta=30^\circ$?
2. What happens at $L=0$?
3. Why is the two-flavor model insufficient for precision experiments?

### Solutions

1. $\sin^2(60^\circ)=0.75$.
2. The transition probability is zero.
3. Three flavors, CP phases, and matter effects can matter.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The formula assumes coherent propagation of ultrarelativistic neutrinos in vacuum and effectively two flavors. Real precision analyses use the three-dimensional PMNS matrix, mass spectrum, possible CP violation, and matter effects. The toy formula does not determine absolute neutrino masses.
