# M29-L7 – FLRW cosmology and Friedmann dynamics {#L7}

**Course area:** M29 – Master: General relativity and cosmology

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M29-L5: Einstein equations; M29-L6: clocks and light; M20: cosmological observations · [Previous lesson](M29-L6.md).

**Learning objective:** Distinguish scale-factor evolution in flat matter- and radiation-dominated models.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

On very large scales, the universe can be approximated as homogeneous and isotropic. An FLRW metric then describes spatial expansion through a scale factor $a(t)$. Different pressure–energy relations generate different expansion histories. This is a cosmological approximation, not a claim that local bound systems simply expand with the scale factor.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

Use a dimensionless scale factor normalized to $a(t_0)=1$, spatial flatness $k=0$, and initially $\Lambda=0$. The Hubble rate is $H=\dot a/a$. In terms of **energy density** $\varepsilon$ and pressure $p$, the Friedmann and continuity equations are $H^2=8\pi G\varepsilon/(3c^2)$ and $\dot\varepsilon+3H(\varepsilon+p)=0$. For constant equation of state $p=w\varepsilon$, integration gives $\varepsilon\propto a^{-3(1+w)}$. Pressureless matter ($w=0$) has $\varepsilon_m\propto a^{-3}$, while radiation ($w=1/3$) has $\varepsilon_r\propto a^{-4}$. A single component in the spatially flat model gives $a\propto t^{2/[3(1+w)]}$ when $w>-1$.

$$
H^2=\left(\frac{\dot a}{a}\right)^2=\frac{8\pi G}{3c^2}\varepsilon,\qquad\varepsilon\propto a^{-3(1+w)}\quad(k=0,\ \Lambda=0).
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

When the scale factor grows from $a=1$ to $a=2$, matter energy density falls to $1/8$, while radiation energy density falls to $1/16$. In a matter-only model with $a\propto t^{2/3}$, eight times the cosmic time gives four times the scale factor. The plot shows these two idealized power laws normalized to the same initial values.

![Two normalized curves of cosmic scale factor versus time: matter-dominated and radiation-dominated power laws.](../../../../media/course/U_PHY_101_EN.svg)

*In spatially flat single-component models, the scale factor grows as $t^{2/3}$ for matter and $t^{1/2}$ for radiation.*

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. How does $\varepsilon_m$ change when $a$ triples?
2. What power of $t$ describes the flat radiation-dominated model?
3. Which additional component can strongly affect realistic expansion even when $k=0$?

### Solutions

1. It falls to $1/27$ of its initial value.
2. $a\propto t^{1/2}$.
3. A cosmological constant or dark energy, set to zero in the example.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

Homogeneity, isotropy, individual perfect fluids, and $k=\Lambda=0$ are model assumptions, not universal facts. The displayed Friedmann equation uses energy density; if mass-equivalent density is used, the $c^{-2}$ factor is absent. No observational parameters or multi-component effects are fitted here.

## Summary and outlook {#S6}

Einstein dynamics relates energy density to expansion in an FLRW universe. Perturbations, horizons, and dark energy require further assumptions and data. The next module develops relativistic field theories.
