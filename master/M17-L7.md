# M17-L7 – Partial Differential Equations and Grid Stability {#L7}

**Course area:** M17 – Bachelor: Computational Physics and Simulation

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** M4: transport; M8: boundary-value problems; M17-L5: time integration. [Previous lesson](M17-L6.md).

**Learning objective:** Discretize one-dimensional diffusion and derive the stability bound for an explicit finite-difference scheme.

## Continuous Diffusion and Boundary Values {#S1}

The one-dimensional diffusion equation $\partial_tu=D\,\partial_{xx}u$ models heat or concentration transport under suitable assumptions. The constant diffusivity $D>0$ has units of $\mathrm{m^2/s}$. Evolution on a finite interval $0\le x\le L$ requires initial data and appropriate boundary conditions. In this example $u(0,t)=u(L,t)=0$. The spatial grid has nodes $x_j=j\Delta x$ and time levels $t_n=n\Delta t$. Grid spacing must be compared with physical diffusion lengths: an excessively coarse grid cannot resolve fine structures.

## Explicit FTCS Scheme {#S2}

Forward time and centered space (FTCS) finite differences give a recurrence relation. The dimensionless parameter $r=D\Delta t/(\Delta x)^2$ controls neighbor coupling. For this explicit one-dimensional scheme with constant $D$, von Neumann analysis requires $0\le r\le1/2$ for linear stability. This bound is not a universal rule for arbitrary equations, grids or higher-dimensional problems. Implicit backward-time centered-space methods have more favorable stability for linear diffusion but require solving a linear system at each step.

$$
u_j^{n+1}=u_j^n+r\left(u_{j+1}^n-2u_j^n+u_{j-1}^n\right),\qquad r=\frac{D\Delta t}{(\Delta x)^2}.
$$

$$
0\le r\le\frac12\quad\text{(FTCS, 1D, constant }D\text{)}.
$$

## Example: A Diffusing Sine Mode {#S3}

Choose $D=1\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$, $\Delta x=0.1\,\mathrm m$ and $\Delta t=0.004\,\mathrm s$, giving $r=0.4$. For initial data $u_j^0=\sin(\pi j/10)$, the center node has $u_5^0=1$. One FTCS step gives $u_5^1=1+0.4[2\cos(\pi/10)-2]\approx0.960845$. The corresponding analytic sine mode decays exponentially. The amplitude is dimensionless here and the large model diffusivity is chosen only to simplify the classroom calculation; physical applications require properly scaled $u$.

**Executable Python example:**

```python
import math
D, dx, dt = 1.0, 0.1, 0.004
r = D*dt/dx**2
u = [math.sin(math.pi*j/10) for j in range(11)]
v = u.copy()
for j in range(1,10):
    v[j] = u[j]+r*(u[j+1]-2*u[j]+u[j-1])
print(r, v[5])
```

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Calculate $r$ for $D=0.5\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$, $\Delta x=0.1\,\mathrm m$ and $\Delta t=0.006\,\mathrm s$.
2. Find the maximum stable FTCS time step for $D=1$ and $\Delta x=0.1$ in this model.
3. Why does $r\le1/2$ not prove physical correctness?

### Solutions

1. $r=0.5\cdot0.006/0.01=0.3$.
2. $\Delta t_{\max}=\Delta x^2/(2D)=0.005\,\mathrm s$.
3. Stability cannot correct a wrong model, boundary conditions or an unresolved grid.

## Stability, Consistency and Alternatives {#S5}

*Stability* prevents small numerical disturbances from growing unphysically; *consistency* means that the discrete equations approach the PDE as the mesh is refined. For appropriate linear initial-value problems, stability and consistency jointly imply convergence, but practical grid studies remain essential. Verify boundary enforcement and that a source-free diffusion mode does not grow unexpectedly. Unstable schemes may oscillate or blow up although real diffusion should smooth disturbances. Variable diffusivity $D$ requires an adapted flux discretization and stability analysis.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

FTCS illustrates finite-difference modeling; the bound $r\le1/2$ belongs to this specific one-dimensional scheme.
