# M37-L4 – Cosmic Microwave Background, Recombination and Decoupling {#L4}

**Course area:** M37 – Master: Astrophysics and Cosmology

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M29: FLRW spacetime; M37-L3: galaxies · [Previous lesson](M37-L3.md).

**Learning objective:** Calculate the temperature scaling of the CMB and distinguish recombination from last scattering.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

The cosmic microwave background (CMB) is an almost thermal relic radiation field. Early photons scattered frequently from free electrons in an ionized plasma. As the Universe cooled, neutral atoms formed more readily (recombination); the declining free-electron fraction allowed photons to travel longer distances without scattering (photon decoupling). Recombination and last scattering are physically linked, but neither instantaneous nor exactly simultaneous.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

Under adiabatic expansion of an ideal photon gas, temperature scales as $T\propto a^{-1}$ for cosmological scale factor $a$. With $1+z=a_0/a$, this gives $T(z)=T_0(1+z)$. Last scattering is often described broadly as occurring around $380,000$ years after the hot beginning at $z\sim1100$. Yet the visibility function distributes last-scattering events across a finite time/redshift interval; temperature scaling alone does not yield a precise decoupling time.

$$
T(z)=T_0(1+z),\qquad 1+z=\frac{a_0}{a}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

Using an illustrative $T_0=2.725\,\mathrm K$ and $z=1100$ gives $T=2.725\cdot1101\,\mathrm K\approx3000.2\,\mathrm K$. This illustrates radiation temperature at high redshift; atomic kinetics controls the actual ionization history. The graphic shows overlapping transitions rather than a single sharp instant.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find the temperature at $z=999$ for $T_0=2.725\,\mathrm K$.
2. How does a photon wavelength change if the scale factor doubles?
3. Why is one exact instant for last scattering misleading?

### Solutions

1. $2725\,\mathrm K$.
2. It doubles under cosmological redshifting of freely propagating photons.
3. Scattering probability and neutral fraction change continuously over a finite epoch.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The 380,000-year age is an approximate model statement, not a sharp measured phase boundary. Later reionization can scatter photons again. CMB anisotropies include Doppler, gravitational and projection effects.
