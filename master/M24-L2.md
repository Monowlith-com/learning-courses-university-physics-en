# M24-L2 – Computed Tomography, Projections and Backprojection {#L2}

**Course area:** M24 – Bachelor: Medical Physics and Imaging

**Level:** Bachelor · Elective

**Prerequisites:** M7: ray optics; M8: Fourier transforms; M17: numerical methods. [Previous lesson](M24-L1.md).

**Learning objective:** Turn X-ray transmission into a projection and explain why unfiltered backprojection is blurred.

## Question and Observation {#S1}

A radiograph superimposes attenuation along each ray path. Computed tomography collects projections from many directions. Numerous one-dimensional line integrals can then reconstruct a two-dimensional attenuation map. The challenge is not simply the number of measurements, but their coverage of directions and spatial frequencies.

## Physical Model and Equations {#S2}

For monoenergetic scatter-free radiation, the negative logarithm of normalised intensity equals the line integral of the local attenuation coefficient. In parallel-beam geometry with angle $\theta$ and detector coordinate $s$, the Radon transform collects these integrals. The Fourier slice theorem identifies the one-dimensional Fourier transform of $p_\theta(s)$ with a radial slice through the two-dimensional Fourier transform of $\mu(x,y)$.

$$
p_\theta(s)=-\ln\!\left(\frac{I_\theta(s)}{I_0}\right)=\int_{\ell(\theta,s)}\!\mu(x,y)\,dl.
$$

Ordinary backprojection distributes each projection value along its original ray paths, over-emphasising low spatial frequencies and blurring edges. In ideal continuous two-dimensional parallel-beam reconstruction, filtered backprojection multiplies the Fourier transform of each projection by a ramp proportional to $|\omega|$, then backprojects over $0\le\theta<\pi$. Normalisation depends on the Fourier convention. Actual fan-beam or cone-beam systems, stochastic detection, scatter and metal artifacts require modified models.

## Worked Example and Teaching Diagram {#S3}

For a line integral $p=0.80$, ideal transmission is $I/I_0=e^{-0.8}\approx0.449$. If a ray crosses $3\,\mathrm{cm}$ with $\mu_1=0.10\,\mathrm{cm^{-1}}$ and $2\,\mathrm{cm}$ with $\mu_2=0.25\,\mathrm{cm^{-1}}$, the integral is $p=0.30+0.50=0.80$. ## Self-Assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What transmission corresponds to $p=\ln4$?
2. Find $p$ for $\mu=0.12\,\mathrm{cm^{-1}}$, $x=5\,\mathrm{cm}$.
3. Why can a ramp filter increase noise in finite-count CT data?

### Solutions

1. $I/I_0=e^{-\ln4}=0.25$.
2. $p=0.12\cdot5=0.60$.
3. The frequency weight $|\omega|$ boosts high spatial frequencies and their accompanying measurement noise.

## Validity Limits and Quality {#S5}

The derivation assumes ideal parallel rays within a single slice. Clinical CT accounts for beam hardening, scatter, angular sampling, detector calibration and geometry-specific reconstruction.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Projection data are negative logarithms of transmission. Reconstruction requires a correct forward geometry and a controlled inversion.
