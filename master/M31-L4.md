# M31-L4 – Monte Carlo, Markov chains and autocorrelation {#L4}

**Course area:** M31 – Master: Numerical and Computational Physics

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M27: statistical physics; M31-L3: simulation · [Previous lesson](M31-L3.md).

**Learning objective:** Evaluate a Metropolis acceptance rule and explain how autocorrelation changes the effective sample size.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Many state spaces are too large to enumerate. Monte Carlo methods estimate expectations from samples. In Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC), consecutive draws are dependent; one thousand stored states do not automatically carry as much information as one thousand independent observations.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

In the canonical ensemble, $\pi(x)\propto e^{-\beta E(x)}$. For symmetric proposals $q(x\to x\prime)=q(x\prime\to x)$, Metropolis acceptance is $a=\min(1,e^{-\beta[E(x\prime)-E(x)]})$. After reaching stationarity, the variance of a sample mean depends on autocorrelations. If $\rho_k$ is the normalized lag-$k$ correlation, the idealized integrated autocorrelation time is $\tau_{\mathrm{int}}=1+2\sum_{k\ge1}\rho_k$, leading to $N_{\mathrm{eff}}\approx N/\tau_{\mathrm{int}}$. Practical ESS estimators need finite truncation rules and convergence diagnostics and are not identical to this infinite sum.

$$
N_{\mathrm{eff}}\approx\frac{N}{1+2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\rho_k}\quad\text{(stationary idealization)}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For a schematic chain with $\rho_k=0.5^k$, the sum is $\sum_{k\ge1}\rho_k=1$, so $\tau_{\mathrm{int}}=3$. A stored sample of $N=900$ draws provides an idealized $N_{\mathrm{eff}}\approx300$. For an energy increase $\Delta E=\ln2/\beta$, the Metropolis acceptance probability is $1/2$. The diagram shows decaying model autocorrelation, not measured simulation data.

![Geometrically decaying autocorrelation rho_k equal to 0.5 to the power k versus lag k, with half correlation at lag one.](../../../../media/course/U_PHY_182_EN.svg)

*Correlated draws contain less independent information than their raw sample count suggests.*

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What is Metropolis acceptance if $\beta\Delta E=\ln4$?
2. Find $N_{\mathrm{eff}}$ for $N=1200$ and $\tau_{\mathrm{int}}=4$.
3. Why should stationarity be assessed before interpreting ESS?

### Solutions

1. $1/4$.
2. $N_{\mathrm{eff}}\approx300$.
3. Stationary correlation formulas do not directly apply to a chain that is still drifting.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The stated Metropolis rule assumes symmetric proposals; otherwise the Hastings correction is needed. ESS summarizes asymptotic information for a particular estimand, not automatically the entire parameter space. Low autocorrelation alone does not prove sampling from the correct target.
