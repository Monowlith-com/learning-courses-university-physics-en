# M13-L3 – Canonical Ensemble and Partition Function {#L3}

**Course area:** M13 – Bachelor: Statistical Physics

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** Temperature and entropy from M13-L2; differentiation and basic probability. [Previous lesson](M13-L2.md).

**Learning objective:** Derive the Boltzmann distribution and calculate energy, free energy, and heat capacity from a partition function.

## A Small System in a Heat Bath {#S1}

In the canonical ensemble, temperature $T$, volume $V$, and particle number $N$ are fixed, whereas the system can exchange energy with a large heat bath. The probability of state $i$ with energy $E_i$ follows from the reservoir's entropy increase. Expanding the reservoir entropy at $E_{\mathrm{total}}-E_i$ yields a Boltzmann factor $e^{-\beta E_i}$ with $\beta=1/(k_BT)$. Normalizing the state weights defines the canonical partition function:

$$
Z=\sum_i e^{-\beta E_i},\qquad p_i=\frac{e^{-\beta E_i}}{Z}.
$$

The index $i$ runs over microstates, not just distinct energy values. Degenerate levels must be counted with their multiplicity.

## Thermodynamics from a Single Function {#S2}

Many thermodynamic properties can be obtained from the partition function. The mean energy is

$$
U=-\frac{\partial\ln Z}{\partial\beta},\qquad F=-k_BT\ln Z.
$$

Here $F$ is Helmholtz free energy and at fixed $N$, $dF=-S\,dT-p\,dV$. With $S=-(\partial F/\partial T)_{V,N}$ one obtains $U=F+TS$. Canonical energy variance gives the constant-volume heat capacity: 

$$
C_V=\frac{\mathrm{Var}(E)}{k_BT^2},\qquad \mathrm{Var}(E)=\frac{\partial^2\ln Z}{\partial\beta^2}.
$$

A single small system may show appreciable energy fluctuations; relative fluctuations often decrease with system size in a thermodynamic limit. The derivatives with respect to $\beta$ hold the remaining model parameters fixed.

## Example: A Two-Level System {#S3}

A nondegenerate two-level system has $E_0=0$ and $E_1=\varepsilon>0$. Its partition function is $Z=1+e^{-\beta\varepsilon}$. The Boltzmann weights give excitation probability and mean energy:

$$
p_1=\frac{1}{1+e^{\beta\varepsilon}},\qquad U=\varepsilon p_1.
$$

At $k_BT=\varepsilon$ we have $\beta\varepsilon=1$, so $p_1\approx0.2689$ and $U\approx0.2689\varepsilon$. The heat capacity is $C_V/k_B=x^2e^x/(1+e^x)^2$ with $x=\varepsilon/(k_BT)$; at $x=1$, $C_V\approx0.1966k_B$. The figure's bars show the two state probabilities for this parameter value. Excited-state occupation grows with temperature but for equal degeneracies remains at or below $1/2$.

*At beta times epsilon equal to one, excitation probability is about 0.269.*

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Calculate $Z$ for a two-level system with $\beta\varepsilon=\ln3$.
2. Find the excited-state probability $p_1$.
3. What is kept fixed when differentiating $U=-\partial_\beta\ln Z$?

### Solutions

1. $Z=1+1/3=4/3$.
2. $p_1=(1/3)/(4/3)=1/4$.
3. Volume, particle number, and Hamiltonian parameters, including the level spacing.

## Degeneracy and Modeling Limits {#S5}

Shifting the energy zero multiplies $Z$ by a simple factor without changing the normalized probabilities. With degeneracies $g_0$ and $g_1$, $Z=g_0+g_1e^{-\beta\varepsilon}$ and the high-temperature excitation probability $p_1$ becomes $g_1/(g_0+g_1)$. The identity $C_V=\mathrm{Var}(E)/(k_BT^2)$ describes canonical equilibrium with temperature-independent level energies; explicitly temperature-dependent effective Hamiltonians need extra care. A continuous classical partition function is an integral with an appropriate phase-space normalization. Large-system microcanonical and canonical ensembles often agree for local averages under suitable conditions, but not for every finite model or every phenomenon near a phase transition.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

The canonical partition function connects microstates to thermodynamic observables. Allowing particle exchange leads to the grand partition function.
