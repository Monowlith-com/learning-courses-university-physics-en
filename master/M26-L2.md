# M26-L2 – Manifolds, metrics and covariant derivatives {#L2}

**Course area:** M26 – Master: Advanced mathematical methods and model foundations

**Level:** Master · methodological specialisation

**Prerequisites:** M9: Lagrangian mechanics; M8: partial derivatives; M20: spatial coordinates. [Previous lesson](M26-L1.md).

**Learning objective:** Write the polar-coordinate metric, calculate its connection coefficients and distinguish nonzero connection coefficients from intrinsic curvature.

## Guiding question and observation {#S1}

A trajectory may be straight in Cartesian coordinates yet appear curved in polar coordinates. This is a coordinate effect, not physical curvature. Geometric equations must transform consistently under changes of coordinates. The metric specifies distances, while the connection describes how vector components are differentiated.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

On the Euclidean plane for $r>0$, the line element is $ds^2=dr^2+r^2d\varphi^2$. Thus $g_{rr}=1$ and $g_{\varphi\varphi}=r^2$. The Christoffel symbols of the Levi-Civita connection follow from first derivatives of $g_{ij}$. They need not vanish in polar coordinates even though the intrinsic Gaussian curvature of the plane is zero. Geodesics obey a coordinate-invariant statement with extra connection terms in the coordinate equation.

$$
\Gamma^k_{ij}=\frac12 g^{k\ell}(\partial_i g_{j\ell}+\partial_j g_{i\ell}-\partial_\ell g_{ij}),\quad \Gamma^r_{\varphi\varphi}=-r,\quad \Gamma^\varphi_{r\varphi}=\frac1r.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For a circle of radius $r=2\,\mathrm m$, an angular interval $\Delta\varphi=0.30\,\mathrm{rad}$ subtends an arc length $s=r\Delta\varphi=0.60\,\mathrm m$. A radial straight line at constant $\varphi$ is a geodesic; a circle at constant $r$ is not a free geodesic of the flat plane. The figure describes geometry rather than a gravitational field.

*The changing basis in polar coordinates generates connection terms even without intrinsic curvature.*

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find $ds$ for $dr=0$ and $d\varphi=0.01$ at $r=3\,\mathrm m$.
2. Find $\Gamma^r_{\varphi\varphi}$ at $r=3\,\mathrm m$.
3. Does a nonzero Christoffel symbol imply curved space?

### Solutions

1. $ds=r\,d\varphi=0.03\,\mathrm m$.
2. $-3\,\mathrm m$ in these coordinates.
3. No; the coefficients depend on coordinates, whereas curvature is tensorial.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The model treats the two-dimensional Euclidean plane away from the polar-coordinate singularity. It does not derive Einstein’s field equations.

## Summary and outlook {#S6}

Metric and connection separate measurable distances from coordinate-dependent derivatives. Their symmetries lead naturally to continuous transformation groups. [Next lesson](M26-L3.md).
