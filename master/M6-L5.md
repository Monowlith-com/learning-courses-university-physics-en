# M6-L5 – Magnetic Fields and Forces on Moving Charges {#L5}

**Course area:** M6 – Bachelor: Electricity and Magnetism

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** Cross product, circular motion and electric current. [Previous lesson](M6-L4.md).

**Learning objective:** Use the Lorentz force, the field of a long wire and Ampère’s circuital law.

## Magnetic Force {#S1}

A charge $q$ moving with velocity $\boldsymbol v$ in electric and magnetic fields feels the Lorentz force $\boldsymbol F=q(\boldsymbol E+\boldsymbol v\times\boldsymbol B)$. The magnetic contribution is perpendicular to $\boldsymbol v$ and $\boldsymbol B$, with magnitude $|F_B|=|q|vB\sin\theta$. Thus a purely magnetic field does no work on a point charge: $\boldsymbol F_B\cdot\boldsymbol v=0$. It can bend a trajectory but does not directly change kinetic energy. The cross product gives the force direction for positive charges, reversed for negative ones. Magnetic flux density $\boldsymbol B$ is measured in tesla, $1\,\mathrm T=1\,\mathrm{N/(A\,m)}$. Magnetic fields arise from electric currents and time-varying electric fields.

## Fields Produced by Currents {#S2}

For a very long straight wire in vacuum carrying steady current $I$, cylindrical symmetry gives the field magnitude at distance $r$ as

$$
B(r)=\frac{\mu_0I}{2\pi r}.
$$

The field runs tangentially around concentric circles. Here the result follows from Ampère’s circuital law $\oint\boldsymbol B\cdot\mathrm d\boldsymbol l=\mu_0I_\mathrm{enclosed}$. Time-varying electric fields require the full Maxwell–Ampère equation including displacement current. $\mu_0$ has unit $\mathrm{N/A^2}$; in the modern SI its numerical value is experimentally determined rather than defined to be exactly $4\pi\cdot10^{-7}$. Less symmetric fields are found through Biot–Savart or the Maxwell equations.

## Worked Example: Circular Motion {#S3}

A proton of mass $m_p\approx1.673\times10^{-27}\,\mathrm{kg}$ travels at $v=1.0\times10^6\,\mathrm{m/s}$ perpendicular to a homogeneous $B=0.20\,\mathrm T$ field. Without an appreciable electric field, the magnetic force magnitude is $F_B=evB\approx3.20\times10^{-14}\,\mathrm N$. Equating it to the required centripetal force,

$$
\frac{mv^2}{r}=|q|vB\quad\Longrightarrow\quad r=\frac{mv}{|q|B}\approx0.0522\,\mathrm m.
$$

The path is circular in the plane perpendicular to the field. A velocity component parallel to $\boldsymbol B$ gives a helix instead. The diagram marks velocity, magnetic field (out of the page) and force directed towards the circle centre. The result is classical and nonrelativistic.

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What is the magnetic force if a charge moves exactly parallel to $\boldsymbol B$?
2. Estimate $B$ at $r=0.010\,\mathrm m$ from a long wire carrying $I=5.0\,\mathrm A$, using $\mu_0\approx4\pi\times10^{-7}\,\mathrm{N/A^2}$.
3. Why can a magnetic field alone not accelerate a particle along its instantaneous velocity?

### Solutions

1. $F_B=0$ because $\sin0=0$.
2. $B\approx(4\pi10^{-7}\cdot5)/(2\pi\cdot0.010)=1.0\times10^{-4}\,\mathrm T$.
3. Force is perpendicular to $\boldsymbol v$, giving zero instantaneous power $\boldsymbol F\cdot\boldsymbol v$.

## Limits, Material Response and Units {#S5}

The long-wire formula assumes steady current, appropriate geometry and vacuum. Magnetic moments in matter respond to fields, requiring a distinction between $\boldsymbol B$ and magnetic field strength $\boldsymbol H$. Ferromagnets can be nonlinear and hysteretic, so a fixed relative permeability may be inadequate. The circular-orbit result assumes a uniform field, negligible collisions and losses. At relativistic speeds momentum is $p=\gamma mv$ rather than $mv$. Signs for $q<0$ and the cross-product order matter; exchanging the vectors reverses the force.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Magnetic forces bend charged-particle paths while currents produce circulating fields. The next topic is electromagnetic induction.
