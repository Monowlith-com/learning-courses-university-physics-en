# M39-L7 – Advection, Diffusion and the Péclet Number in Biological Systems {#L7}

**Course area:** M39 – Master: Biophysics, Nonequilibrium and Complex Systems

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M39-L2: reaction–diffusion; M39-L6: drift and noise · [Previous lesson](M39-L6.md).

**Learning objective:** Distinguish advective and diffusive solute fluxes and scale their competition with the Péclet number.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

In a microchannel, fluid flow carries dissolved material while molecular diffusion smooths concentration variations along and across the stream. Dominance depends on velocity, diffusivity and the chosen length scale. The Péclet number is a ratio of effects, not an additional transport force.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

For constant $D$ and incompressible flow $\mathbf u$, the total flux is $\mathbf J=\mathbf u c-D\nabla c$. Without reaction, concentration obeys $\partial_tc+\mathbf u\cdot\nabla c=D\nabla^2c$. With velocity $U$ and length $L$, $\mathrm{Pe}=UL/D=\tau_{\mathrm{diff}}/\tau_{\mathrm{adv}}$, where $\tau_{\mathrm{adv}}=L/U$ and $\tau_{\mathrm{diff}}=L^2/D$. A large $\mathrm{Pe}$ signals advective dominance on that scale, not zero diffusion near walls or in boundary layers.

$$
\mathbf J=\mathbf u c-D\nabla c,\qquad\mathrm{Pe}=\frac{UL}{D}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For illustrative microchannel parameters $U=10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}$, $L=10^{-4}\,\mathrm m$, and $D=10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m^2s^{-1}}$, $\mathrm{Pe}=100$. Timescales are $\tau_{\mathrm{adv}}=1\,\mathrm s$ and $\tau_{\mathrm{diff}}=100\,\mathrm s$. Increasing $D$ tenfold reduces $\mathrm{Pe}$ to ten. The figure represents timescales and directions schematically.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. How does $\mathrm{Pe}$ change when $L$ is halved?
2. What is $\tau_{\mathrm{adv}}$ when $U$ doubles?
3. Why does diffusion still matter at $\mathrm{Pe}=100$?

### Solutions

1. It falls to $50$.
2. $0.5\,\mathrm s$.
3. Near walls and transversely, large concentration gradients can make diffusion important.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The scaling does not predict exact flow profiles or transport with coupled reactions, turbulence or osmotic coupling. Flow fields require consistent boundary conditions or measurements. The length $L$ must match the geometry and direction of transport.
