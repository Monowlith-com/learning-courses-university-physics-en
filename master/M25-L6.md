# M25-L6 – Research integrity, documentation and reproducibility {#L6}

**Course area:** M25 – Bachelor: Interdisciplinary applications and scientific practice

**Level:** Bachelor · elective

**Prerequisites:** M16: data analysis; M18: laboratory records; M19: research data workflows. [Previous lesson](M25-L5.md).

**Learning objective:** Record data provenance and analysis choices so that a result can be traced and independently evaluated.

## Guiding question and observation {#S1}

A convincing plot is not yet an auditable research result. Raw measurements, calibration, filtering, exclusions and software versions must be traceable if others are to assess the findings. Good research practice also concerns results that contradict expectations. The DFG treats documentation as a condition of reviewability; this does not require exposing personal data without safeguards or publishing every dataset without checking rights and obligations.

## Physical model and equations {#S2}

A simple provenance chain starts with unchanged raw measurements, produces a processed dataset with logged parameters and links each plot to that exact dataset. Checksums can reveal file changes but do not prove scientific correctness or authorship. Computational reproducibility requires specified inputs, versions, random seeds and processing steps. Independent replication is different: it may involve fresh measurements and new experimental conditions. Documentation and transparent error reporting are part of scientific method, not merely administration.

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

In a teaching example, a sensor produces ten voltage readings and a calibration file specifies $g=2.0\,\mathrm{K/V}$. A raw reading of $1.50\,\mathrm V$ becomes $T=gU=3.00\,\mathrm K$. For later checking, neither the temperature value nor a plot alone is sufficient: one needs at least the original reading and unit, calibration version, calculation rule, processed file and processing timestamp. ## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Calculate $T$ for $U=2.00\,\mathrm V$ with $g=2.0\,\mathrm{K/V}$.
2. What can a matching file checksum establish, and what can it not establish?
3. Why must observations that contradict expectations also be recorded?

### Solutions

1. $T=4.00\,\mathrm K$.
2. It supports identity of the checked file bytes, not validity of the experiment.
3. Otherwise the data record becomes selectively biased, undermining review and reproducibility.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

A file hash alone is not complete evidence of provenance. Privacy, rights, embargoes and discipline-specific retention obligations require separate assessment; the example is not legal advice.

## Summary and outlook {#S6}

Documentation makes the path from observation to claim inspectable. Reliable provenance, transparent analysis choices and justified exclusions are elements of good research practice.
