# M6-L3 – Capacitors, Dielectrics and Field Energy {#L3}

**Course area:** M6 – Bachelor: Electricity and Magnetism

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** Gauss’s law, voltage and mechanical work. [Previous lesson](M6-L2.md).

**Learning objective:** Derive capacitance and field energy; compare dielectrics under different boundary conditions.

## Storing Charge {#S1}

A capacitor consists of two separated conductors that maintain separated charges $+Q$ and $-Q$. Its **capacitance** is $C=Q/U$ if the charge-voltage relationship is linear in the regime considered. The farad is $1\,\mathrm F=1\,\mathrm{C/V}$. High capacitance means large stored charge at fixed voltage, not necessarily high electric power. Charging moves charge against the field already present, and the work supplied by a source becomes field energy. The ideal capacitor model concerns a stationary configuration; charging and discharging require a circuit description including resistance.

## Plate Model and Energy {#S2}

For two extended parallel plates of area $A$ and separation $d$, the field away from the edges is approximately uniform. With a linear, homogeneous dielectric of relative permittivity $\varepsilon_r$ filling the gap,

$$
C=\varepsilon_0\varepsilon_r\frac{A}{d},\qquad E\approx\frac Ud.
$$

Adding an infinitesimal amount of charge $\mathrm dq$ requires $\mathrm dW=U(q)\,\mathrm dq=(q/C)\,\mathrm dq$. Integrating from $0$ to $Q$ gives

$$
W_\mathrm{el}=\frac{Q^2}{2C}=\frac12CU^2=\frac12QU.
$$

For a linear isotropic medium the field-energy density is $u=\tfrac12\boldsymbol E\cdot\boldsymbol D$. When voltage $U$ is held constant, adding dielectric increases stored field energy; for an isolated capacitor with constant $Q$, the energy decreases. The complete energy balance includes the source and mechanical work.

## Worked Example: Inserting a Dielectric {#S3}

A parallel-plate capacitor has $A=0.020\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and $d=1.0\,\mathrm{mm}$ with $\varepsilon_r=1$. Using $\varepsilon_0\approx8.854\times10^{-12}\,\mathrm{F/m}$,

$$
C_0=\varepsilon_0\frac{0.020}{0.001}\approx177\,\mathrm{pF}.
$$

At $U=100\,\mathrm V$, $Q=C_0U\approx17.7\,\mathrm{nC}$ and $W_0=\tfrac12C_0U^2\approx0.885\,\mathrm{\mu J}$. After disconnecting the source ($Q$ fixed), inserting an ideal dielectric with $\varepsilon_r=4$ raises capacitance to $708\,\mathrm{pF}$, lowers voltage to $25\,\mathrm V$ and lowers field energy to about $0.221\,\mathrm{\mu J}$. The diagram distinguishes fixed-voltage from fixed-charge operation.

*Dielectric increases capacitance; the boundary condition determines voltage and energy.*

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find the capacitance of a vacuum parallel-plate device with $A=0.010\,\mathrm{m^2}$ and $d=2.0\,\mathrm{mm}$.
2. How does $W=Q^2/(2C)$ change if capacitance doubles at fixed $Q$?
3. What is held constant when dielectric is inserted into a capacitor still connected to an ideal voltage source?

### Solutions

1. $C\approx8.854\times10^{-12}\cdot0.010/0.002=44.3\,\mathrm{pF}$.
2. The stored field energy halves.
3. Voltage is fixed; capacitance and charge increase in proportion to $\varepsilon_r$ in the linear model.

## Leakage, Fringe Fields and Safety {#S5}

The relation $C=\varepsilon A/d$ presumes approximately parallel extended plates with negligible edge effects and a homogeneous linear material. Real capacitors exhibit series resistance, insulation leakage, temperature and frequency dependence, and rated voltage limits. They can retain hazardous stored energy after a circuit is disconnected and must be discharged safely. In ideal parallel connections $C_\parallel=\sum_iC_i$; in series $1/C_\mathrm{series}=\sum_i1/C_i$. These expressions assume unambiguous nodes and negligible parasitic couplings.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Capacitance depends on geometry and material; stored field energy must be balanced under the correct boundary condition. Next we study currents and resistance.
