# M27-L3 – Angular-momentum coupling, singlet and triplet {#L3}

**Course area:** M27 – Master: Advanced quantum theory

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M11: spin and measurement; M12: angular-momentum algebra and tensor products. [Previous lesson](M27-L2.md).

**Learning objective:** Explain the coupling of two spin-1/2 degrees of freedom and classify their four states by total angular momentum.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Two spin-1/2 degrees of freedom have a four-dimensional product basis. This basis suits individual measurements, while total angular momentum is natural for rotationally symmetric interactions. Clebsch–Gordan coefficients connect the two descriptions.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

The total operator is $\mathbf J=\mathbf S_1+\mathbf S_2$. For $s_1=s_2=1/2$, the angular-momentum addition rule allows $j=0$ and $j=1$. The triplet has three $j=1$ components, whereas the singlet is the sole $j=0$ state. In the $m=0$ subspace, symmetric and antisymmetric combinations provide normalized states. The identity $J^2=J_1^2+J_2^2+2\mathbf S_1\cdot\mathbf S_2$ gives eigenvalues $\hbar^2/4$ for $\mathbf S_1\cdot\mathbf S_2$ in the triplet and $-3\hbar^2/4$ in the singlet.

$$
|1,0\rangle=\frac{|\uparrow\downarrow\rangle+|\downarrow\uparrow\rangle}{\sqrt2},\qquad |0,0\rangle=\frac{|\uparrow\downarrow\rangle-|\downarrow\uparrow\rangle}{\sqrt2}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For an isotropic exchange interaction $H=J_{\mathrm{ex}}\mathbf S_1\cdot\mathbf S_2/\hbar^2$, the triplet energy is $J_{\mathrm{ex}}/4$ and the singlet energy is $-3J_{\mathrm{ex}}/4$. If $J_{\mathrm{ex}}=4\,\mathrm{meV}$, the splitting is $E_{\mathrm{trip}}-E_{\mathrm{sing}}=4\,\mathrm{meV}$. The energy-level diagram shows the triplet's threefold degeneracy in the absence of an external field.

![Energy levels of a singlet and a threefold-degenerate triplet.](../../../../media/course/U_PHY_271_EN.svg)

*Exchange coupling splits total angular momentum into singlet and triplet sectors.*

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. How many coupled states are available to two spin-1/2 degrees of freedom?
2. Find $\langle\mathbf S_1\cdot\mathbf S_2\rangle$ for the singlet.
3. Which state has lower energy for $J_{\mathrm{ex}}>0$ in the specified model?

### Solutions

1. Four: one singlet and three triplet states.
2. $-3\hbar^2/4$.
3. The singlet at $-3J_{\mathrm{ex}}/4$.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The exchange model is isotropic and treats two distinguishable spin degrees of freedom. For identical particles the symmetry of the complete spin-and-spatial state also matters. The parameter $J_{\mathrm{ex}}$ is not an angular-momentum quantum number.
