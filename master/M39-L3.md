# M39-L3 – Biological Feedback Circuits, Hill Functions and Stability {#L3}

**Course area:** M39 – Master: Biophysics, Nonequilibrium and Complex Systems

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M39-L2; M31: ordinary differential equations; M13: stochastic processes · [Previous lesson](M39-L2.md).

**Learning objective:** Formulate an autoregulated production–decay model, find a fixed point and test local stability.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Cellular regulatory networks couple gene expression, signalling and metabolism. In simple negative autoregulation, increasing protein abundance reduces its production. This can stabilize a steady operating level; multi-component networks may also oscillate or show richer dynamics.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

Use the illustrative concentration dynamics $\dot x=\alpha/[1+(x/K)^n]-\gamma x$ for $\alpha,K,\gamma>0$. For $n=1$ the production rate decreases monotonically. Fixed points satisfy $f(x^*)=0$; local stability of a scalar ODE follows from $f^{\prime}(x^*)<0$. Since $f^{\prime}(x)=-\alpha/[K(1+x/K)^2]-\gamma<0$, there is at most one positive fixed point and it is stable. Negative single-component feedback alone does not imply bistability.

$$
\dot x=\frac{\alpha}{1+(x/K)}-\gamma x,\qquad f^{\prime}(x^*)<0\ \Rightarrow\ \text{locally stable}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

With constructed dimensionless $\alpha=2$, $K=1$, $\gamma=1$, we have $\dot x=2/(1+x)-x$. The fixed-point equation $x(1+x)=2$ gives $x^*=1$ (the other algebraic root $-2$ is physically inadmissible). The slope is $f^{\prime}(1)=-2/4-1=-1.5<0$. The teaching figure shows production and decay rates intersecting at $x=1$.

![Declining regulated production and rising linear decay, with a stable intersection at concentration one.](../../../../media/course/U_PHY_021_EN.svg)

*Dimensionless one-protein model; no experimental biological data.*

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What is the production rate at $x=0$?
2. What is the sign of $\dot x$ at $x=0.5$?
3. Is a negative concentration fixed point physically admissible?

### Solutions

1. Production is $2$ in the chosen units.
2. $\dot x=2/1.5-0.5\approx+0.833$; $x$ increases.
3. No, concentrations are nonnegative.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

This is a continuous deterministic one-variable model with a fixed production and linear decay law. Real cells have finite molecule numbers, intrinsic noise, delays and spatial structure. Multidimensional systems require eigenvalue or other stability analyses.
