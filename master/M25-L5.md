# M25-L5 – Models, idealizations and scientific validity {#L5}

**Course area:** M25 – Bachelor: Interdisciplinary applications and scientific practice

**Level:** Bachelor · elective

**Prerequisites:** M1: model building; M16: uncertainty; M17: numerical validation. [Previous lesson](M25-L4.md).

**Learning objective:** Distinguish idealization from measurement error and model discrepancy, and perform a simple residual-based model check.

## Guiding question and observation {#S1}

Why can an explicitly simplified model be scientifically useful? A frictionless pendulum or a point mass deliberately leaves out features of the real system. The model represents a selected aspect of reality and can make predictions under stated conditions. It is not invalid merely because its assumptions are not literally true. What matters is domain of validity, acceptable error and the possibility of testing predictions against independent observations.

## Physical model and equations {#S2}

Let $y_i$ be an observed value, $f(x_i;\theta)$ a model prediction and $\sigma_i$ its standard uncertainty. A residual $r_i=y_i-f(x_i;\theta)$ measures observation minus prediction. A normalized sum of squares $\chi^2$ can quantify fit under suitable assumptions; it is neither proof of the model nor automatically a probability statement without an uncertainty model. Systematic residual patterns can indicate missing physics, while apparently random residuals alone do not confirm all assumptions.

$$
r_i=y_i-f(x_i;\theta),\qquad \chi^2=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\frac{r_i}{\sigma_i}\right)^2.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

A model predicts $f(x)=2x$ at $x=1,2,3$. Measurements give $y=2.1;4.0;6.2$, each with $\sigma=0.2$. The residuals are $r=(0.1;0;0.2)$ and $\chi^2=(0.1/0.2)^2+0+(0.2/0.2)^2=1.25$. If the slope is estimated from these same data, the number of degrees of freedom changes. The figure shows a straight model line and three slightly displaced observations.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Calculate $r$ for $y=5.3$ and $f=5.0$.
2. How much does this residual contribute to $\chi^2$ when $\sigma=0.1$?
3. Give one distinct cause of a systematic pattern in residuals.

### Solutions

1. $r=0.3$.
2. $(0.3/0.1)^2=9$.
3. An omitted nonlinear term, a time-dependent parameter or an unmodeled calibration bias.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

Interpreting $\chi^2$ requires a defensible error and correlation model. Models may predict well while remaining underdetermined about unobserved mechanisms; no philosophical claim follows from a single fit statistic.

## Summary and outlook {#S6}

Idealizations are explicit choices about effects omitted for a particular purpose. Models remain testable when assumptions, residuals and domains of application are visible.
