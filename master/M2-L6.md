# M2-L6 – Gradient, Divergence, Curl and Potential Fields {#L6}

**Course area:** M2 – Bachelor: Mathematical Methods for Physics

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** Partial derivatives, vectors and dot products. [Previous lesson](M2-L5.md).

**Learning objective:** Compute divergence and curl and distinguish conservative from non-conservative fields.

## Local Balance and Rotation {#S1}

A flow field assigns a velocity to every location. Some regions act like sources or sinks, while others exhibit local rotation. Vector calculus quantifies different features: the gradient applies to scalar fields, divergence describes local net outflow per volume, and curl describes the tendency toward circulation. None of them should be inferred solely from the appearance of a few streamlines.

## Differential Operators {#S2}

For $\mathbf F=(F_x,F_y,F_z)^\mathsf T$ in Cartesian coordinates, define

$$
\nabla\cdot\mathbf F=\partial_x F_x+\partial_y F_y+\partial_z F_z,
$$

$$
\nabla\times\mathbf F=\begin{pmatrix}
\partial_yF_z-\partial_zF_y\\
\partial_zF_x-\partial_xF_z\\
\partial_xF_y-\partial_yF_x
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

If $\mathbf F=\nabla\phi$ for a smooth scalar potential $\phi$, then $\nabla\times\mathbf F=\mathbf0$ because mixed partial derivatives commute. The converse requires restrictions on the domain (such as simple connectivity); a curl-free field on a punctured region need not be a global gradient field.

## Example: Source and Rotational Field {#S3}

For the planar field $\mathbf F=(x,y,0)^\mathsf T$, $\nabla\cdot\mathbf F=2$, but $\nabla\times\mathbf F=\mathbf0$. It is the gradient of $\phi=(x^2+y^2)/2$. In contrast, the rotational field $\mathbf G=(-y,x,0)^\mathsf T$ has

$$
\nabla\cdot\mathbf G=0,\qquad
\nabla\times\mathbf G=(0,0,2)^\mathsf T.
$$

A small paddle wheel carried by $\mathbf G$ can rotate although there is no local net source. Divergence and curl are logically independent. Both divergence and curl of a velocity field have units of $\mathrm{s^{-1}}$.

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Determine $\nabla\cdot\mathbf H$ for $\mathbf H=(3x,2y,z)^\mathsf T$.
2. Compute $\nabla\times\mathbf H$ for the same field.
3. Is the rotating field $(-y,x,0)^\mathsf T$ the gradient of a smooth potential on all of $\mathbb R^3$?

### Solutions

1. $3+2+1=6$.
2. $\mathbf0$, because the mixed derivatives vanish.
3. No. Its curl is $(0,0,2)^\mathsf T$, which is nonzero.

## Potentials and Their Limits {#S5}

Electrostatics commonly uses $\mathbf E=-\nabla V$. The minus sign means the electric field points toward decreasing potential. With a time-varying magnetic field, however, $\nabla\times\mathbf E=-\partial_t\mathbf B$, so a purely scalar global potential generally does not suffice. The claim that curl vanishes also depends on field smoothness and on domains or singularities excluded from the model.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Divergence quantifies local sources and sinks, while curl quantifies local circulation. A gradient field is locally curl-free; the next step integrates fields along actual paths.
