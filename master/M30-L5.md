# M30-L5 – Path integrals, renormalization, and effective field theories {#L5}

**Course area:** M30 – Master: Quantum field theory and the Standard Model

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M30-L4: perturbative diagrams and amplitudes · [Previous lesson](M30-L4.md).

**Learning objective:** Interpret the path integral as a weighted sum over field configurations and estimate suppression of higher-dimensional operators.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

How can a theory work at low energy even when microscopic details are unknown? Path integrals provide an alternative description of field correlations. Effective field theory separates low-energy observables from a high scale at which additional degrees of freedom become important.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

Formally, the generating functional is $Z[J]=\int\!\mathcal D\phi\,e^{i(S[\phi]+\int J\phi)}$. This notation requires a suitable regularization and definition of the integration measure. An effective Lagrangian orders local operators by mass dimension. In four spacetime dimensions, a dimension-six operator $\mathcal O_6$ has coefficient $c_6/\Lambda^2$. At characteristic energy $E\ll\Lambda$, its amplitude-like contribution is typically suppressed by $(E/\Lambda)^2$ relative to dimension-four interactions, assuming no unusually large dimensionless coefficients or matrix elements. The renormalization group tracks how parameters run with the evaluation scale.

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{eff}}=\mathcal L_{\leq4}+\frac{c_6}{\Lambda^2}\mathcal O_6+\cdots.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For $E/\Lambda=0.10$, the expected dimensionless suppression factor for this amplitude contribution is about $0.01$. The diagram contrasts scales $E$ and $\Lambda$; it does not claim every observable rate decreases by exactly one hundredth. Interference with leading contributions can change observable scaling.

*Higher-dimensional operators yield controlled small corrections at $E\ll\Lambda$ when EFT assumptions hold.*

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find $(E/\Lambda)^2$ for $E=2$ and $\Lambda=20$.
2. Why is a dimension-six operator accompanied by $\Lambda^{-2}$?
3. Is a loop-corrected renormalized coupling generally scale independent?

### Solutions

1. $0.01$.
2. To give the Lagrangian density mass dimension four in four dimensions.
3. No; a renormalized coupling generally runs with the renormalization scale.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

Path integrals here are formal expressions; the compact notation does not resolve mathematical existence questions. An effective theory applies below its assumed cutoff, with a sufficiently controlled expansion.
