# M35-L2 – Drude Transport, Mobility and Hall Effect {#L2}

**Course area:** M35 – Master: Advanced Solid-State Physics and Materials Science

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M35-L1: band structure; M10: electromagnetism.

**Learning objective:** Calculate elementary Drude conductivity and the sign of the Hall coefficient for a single electron carrier type.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Electrical conduction arises when an applied field slightly perturbs carrier motion. The Drude model summarizes collisions in a momentum-relaxation time. The transverse Hall field can reveal carrier sign and, in restricted one-band conditions, carrier density. In multiband systems a single Hall number cannot generally be interpreted as the inverse carrier density.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

For isotropic electrons of density $n$, effective mass $m^*$ and constant relaxation time $\tau$, conductivity is $\sigma=ne^2\tau/m^*$, where $e>0$ denotes the electron-charge magnitude. Positive mobility is $\mu=e\tau/m^*$. In weak magnetic field for one electron-like carrier type, with a specified sign convention, the Hall coefficient is $R_H=-1/(ne)$. Real transport tensors depend on magnetic field, band anisotropy and multiple carrier types.

$$
\sigma=\frac{ne^2\tau}{m^*},\qquad R_H=-\frac1{ne}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

Take $n=2\times10^{28}\,\mathrm{m^{-3}}$, $\tau=10^{-14}\,\mathrm{s}$, $m^*=9.11\times10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $e=1.602\times10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}$. The results are $\sigma\approx5.63\times10^6\,\mathrm{S/m}$, $\mu\approx1.76\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^2/(Vs)}$ and $R_H\approx-3.12\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m^3/C}$. The figure illustrates the Hall sign and field geometry, not an actual specimen simulation.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What happens to $\sigma$ if only $\tau$ doubles?
2. What is the sign of $R_H$ for an ideal one-band electron conductor?
3. Why does $R_H=-1/(ne)$ generally fail in a multiband conductor?

### Solutions

1. It doubles.
2. Negative under the stated convention.
3. Different electron and hole populations contribute distinct densities, mobilities and field responses.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

Relaxation time is a model approximation rather than a universal material parameter. Quantum oscillations, strong fields, nanoscale dimensions or anomalous Hall terms require more advanced descriptions.
