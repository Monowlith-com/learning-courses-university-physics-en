# M12-L5 – Orbital Angular Momentum, Spin, and Coupling {#L5}

**Course area:** M12 – Bachelor: Advanced Quantum Mechanics

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** Vector spaces, commutators and spin; M11-L5 and M12-L1. [Previous lesson](M12-L4.md).

**Learning objective:** Explain and apply angular-momentum quantum numbers and addition of two angular momenta.

## Why Rotations Have Quantized Outcomes {#S1}

Orbital angular momentum $\mathbf L=\mathbf r\times\mathbf p$ generates spatial rotations of a particle's state. Intrinsic spin obeys the same angular-momentum algebra but is not the classical rotation of a tiny extended object. In a central potential, energy and $L^2$ can be diagonalized together. With spin–orbit interaction, total angular momentum $\mathbf J=\mathbf L+\mathbf S$ becomes central. Components along distinct axes cannot all have sharp values, whereas total squared angular momentum and one chosen projection can be sharp simultaneously.

## Operator Algebra and Angular-Momentum Addition {#S2}

The operators satisfy $[J_x,J_y]=i\hbar J_z$ and cyclic relations. States $|jm\rangle$ obey

$$
J^2|jm\rangle=j(j+1)\hbar^2|jm\rangle,\qquad J_z|jm\rangle=m\hbar|jm\rangle,
$$

where $m=-j,-j+1,\ldots,j$. When adding two independent angular momenta $j_1$ and $j_2$, allowed totals are 

$$
j=|j_1-j_2|,|j_1-j_2|+1,\ldots,j_1+j_2
$$

The product states $|j_1m_1\rangle|j_2m_2\rangle$ can be transformed into coupled $|jm\rangle$ states through Clebsch–Gordan coefficients. Dimensions agree: $(2j_1+1)(2j_2+1)=\sum_j(2j+1)$.

## Example: Singlet and Triplet {#S3}

Two spin-$1/2$ systems produce total quantum numbers $j=1$ and $j=0$. The triplet has three states: $|1,1\rangle=|\uparrow\uparrow\rangle$, $|1,0\rangle=(|\uparrow\downarrow\rangle+|\downarrow\uparrow\rangle)/\sqrt2$, and $|1,-1\rangle=|\downarrow\downarrow\rangle$. The singlet is

$$
|0,0\rangle=(|\uparrow\downarrow\rangle-|\downarrow\uparrow\rangle)/\sqrt 2
$$

Its total spin vanishes, even though each individual spin component is random and z-results are perfectly anticorrelated. The diagram decomposes a four-dimensional product space into a three-dimensional $j=1$ and a one-dimensional $j=0$ subspace.

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Which $j$ values arise for $j_1=1$ and $j_2=1/2$?
2. Check the dimension count.
3. Why is a $j=0$ singlet not a product of pure spin-z states?

### Solutions

1. $j=1/2,3/2$.
2. $2+4=6=(2\cdot1+1)(2\cdot\tfrac12+1)$.
3. It is a coherent entangled antisymmetric combination.

## Coupling Schemes and Selection Rules {#S5}

Which angular momenta remain separately conserved depends on Hamiltonian symmetries. In a strong external field a different coupling basis may be more useful than for an isotropic isolated system. Optical selection rules do not follow from $\Delta j$ alone: parity, polarization and transition-operator structure also matter. Two identical electrons must have an antisymmetric overall state, so spatial and spin parts cannot be combined arbitrarily.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Angular momenta combine into discrete totals; singlet and triplet illustrate coupled-basis decomposition.
