# M34-L6 – Quantum Error Correction with the Three-Qubit Code {#L6}

**Course area:** M34 – Master: Quantum Information and Quantum Technologies

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M34-L3: CNOT; M34-L5: quantum channels.

**Learning objective:** Derive the syndrome table for one X error and the resulting logical failure probability.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

An unknown quantum state cannot be cloned arbitrarily. Quantum error correction instead encodes quantum information coherently across a larger Hilbert space. The three-qubit repetition code maps α|0⟩+β|1⟩ to α|000⟩+β|111⟩. Parity checks can reveal which physical qubit suffered a bit-flip X error without reading the logical amplitudes.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

The stabilizers $Z_1Z_2$ and $Z_2Z_3$ have outcomes $(+1,+1)$ on the error-free codespace. Errors $X_1$, $X_2$ and $X_3$ produce $(-1,+1)$, $(-1,-1)$ and $(+1,-1)$ respectively. If every physical qubit flips independently with probability $p$, majority decoding fails for two or three flips. Hence $P_{\mathrm{fail}}=3p^2(1-p)+p^3=3p^2-2p^3$.

$$
P_{\mathrm{fail}}=3p^2(1-p)+p^3=3p^2-2p^3.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

At $p=0.1$, a physical bit-flip occurs with probability 10%, while ideal encoding and single-round correction yield $P_{\mathrm{fail}}=0.028=2.8\,\%$. The diagram maps four syndromes to no error, X1, X2 and X3. Additional noisy gates and syndrome measurements are deliberately excluded from this calculation.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Which syndrome identifies $X_2$?
2. What logical failure fraction follows for $p=0.2$?
3. Does the repetition code by itself correct arbitrary Z phase errors?

### Solutions

1. $(-1,-1)$.
2. $3(0.2)^2-2(0.2)^3=0.104$.
3. No. Additional encoding/stabilizers are necessary for phase-error correction.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The estimate assumes independent bit-flip-only noise and perfect syndrome extraction. Correlated errors, leakage, phase flips and faulty correction circuits can erase the advantage.
