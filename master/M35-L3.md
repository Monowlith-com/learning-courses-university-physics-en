# M35-L3 – Phonons, Lattice Dispersion and Electron–Phonon Coupling {#L3}

**Course area:** M35 – Master: Advanced Solid-State Physics and Materials Science

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M14: lattice vibrations; M35-L2: transport.

**Learning objective:** Derive acoustic dispersion and maximum angular frequency for a monoatomic mass-and-spring chain.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Atoms in a crystal oscillate collectively near equilibrium positions. Their quantized normal modes are phonons. A monoatomic chain possesses an acoustic branch with frequency proportional to wave number at long wavelengths. Phonons can transport heat and scatter electrons, but a phonon dispersion plot alone does not determine a transport lifetime.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

For mass $m$, spring constant $K$, lattice spacing $a$ and nearest-neighbor interactions, $m\ddot u_j=K(u_{j+1}-2u_j+u_{j-1})$. The wave ansatz $u_j\propto\exp[i(jka-\omega t)]$ yields $\omega^2=(4K/m)\sin^2(ka/2)$. The long-wavelength sound velocity is $v_s=a\sqrt{K/m}$ under an ideal harmonic-chain approximation. Electron–phonon scattering requires additional coupling parameters.

$$
\omega(k)=2\sqrt{\frac Km}\,\left|\sin\frac{ka}{2}\right|.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

Let $K/m=10^{26}\,\mathrm{s^{-2}}$ and $a=0.30\,\mathrm{nm}$. Then $\omega_{\max}=2\times10^{13}\,\mathrm{rad/s}$ at the zone boundary. At $ka=\pi/3$, $\omega=10^{13}\,\mathrm{rad/s}$; in the long-wavelength limit $v_s=3.0\times10^3\,\mathrm{m/s}$. The plot shows an ideal acoustic branch within the first Brillouin zone.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What maximum frequency follows for $K/m=4\times10^{26}\,\mathrm{s^{-2}}$?
2. Why is the mode acoustic near $k=0$?
3. Can the carrier relaxation time be inferred from this curve alone?

### Solutions

1. $\omega_{\max}=4\times10^{13}\,\mathrm{rad/s}$.
2. Because $\sin(ka/2)\approx ka/2$, so $\omega\propto|k|$.
3. No. One also needs interaction matrix elements, occupations and allowed transitions.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The harmonic one-atom chain has no damping or anharmonicity and only one acoustic branch. Optical branches require a multi-atom basis; real thermal and resistive properties cannot be predicted from this spring chain alone.
