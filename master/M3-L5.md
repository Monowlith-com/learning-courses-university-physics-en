# M3-L5 – Momentum, Collisions and the Centre of Mass {#L5}

**Course area:** M3 – Bachelor: Classical Mechanics

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** Newtonian dynamics, energy and vector calculations. [Previous lesson](M3-L4.md).

**Learning objective:** Use momentum conservation in collisions and relate centre-of-mass motion to external forces.

## Why Momentum Simplifies Collisions {#S1}

Collisions involve large forces over short intervals, making the detailed time dependence difficult to measure. We instead study momentum $\mathbf p=m\mathbf v$ and define a system boundary. For a system of interacting bodies, pairs of **internal** forces cancel in the sum under the Newtonian interaction model. Consequently the change in total momentum is set by the external impulse. This can relate velocities before and after a collision without resolving the details of the contact force.

## Impulse–Momentum Theorem and Centre of Mass {#S2}

For the total momentum,

$$
\mathbf P=\sum_i m_i\mathbf v_i,\qquad
\frac{\mathrm d\mathbf P}{\mathrm dt}=\sum\mathbf F_{\mathrm{ext}},\qquad
\Delta\mathbf P=\int\sum\mathbf F_{\mathrm{ext}}\,\mathrm dt.
$$

If the external impulse is negligible during the collision, $\mathbf P$ is conserved. With constant total mass $M=\sum_i m_i$, the centre of mass is

$$
\mathbf R_{\mathrm{CM}}=\frac{\sum_i m_i\mathbf r_i}{M},\qquad
M\mathbf a_{\mathrm{CM}}=\sum\mathbf F_{\mathrm{ext}}.
$$

Conserved momentum does not automatically imply conserved kinetic energy. In a perfectly inelastic collision the bodies move together afterwards, while some mechanical energy becomes deformation or internal energy.

## Worked Example: Two Carts Stick Together {#S3}

A cart of mass $m_1=2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ at $v_1=3.0\,\mathrm{m/s}$ hits a stationary cart of mass $m_2=1.0\,\mathrm{kg}$ on a straight track. They lock together after impact. Neglecting external impulse,

$$
v_f=\frac{m_1v_1+m_2v_2}{m_1+m_2}
=\frac{6.0\,\mathrm{kg\,m/s}}{3.0\,\mathrm{kg}}
=2.0\,\mathrm{m/s}.
$$

Initially $K_i=\tfrac12\cdot2.0\cdot3.0^2=9.0\,\mathrm J$; afterwards $K_f=\tfrac12\cdot3.0\cdot2.0^2=6.0\,\mathrm J$. **Three joules of mechanical energy** have been converted to other forms while total momentum remains $6.0\,\mathrm{kg\,m/s}$.

![Two carts before collision and a coupled pair afterwards with velocities](../../../../media/course/U_PHY_134_EN.svg)

*Momentum is 6 kg m per second on both sides; kinetic energy falls from 9 to 6 joules.*

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. A $4\,\mathrm{kg}$ cart travels at $2\,\mathrm{m/s}$. Find its momentum.
2. Two equal-mass carts with velocities $+2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ and $-2\,\mathrm{m/s}$ stick together. Find their final velocity.
3. In the main example, which energy quantity is not conserved, and by how much does it change?

### Solutions

1. $p=mv=8\,\mathrm{kg\,m/s}$.
2. Initial total momentum is zero, so $v_f=0$.
3. Kinetic energy drops from $9$ to $6\,\mathrm J$, a loss of $3\,\mathrm J$ from the mechanical-energy account. Total energy is not destroyed.

## What Conservation Does Not Claim {#S5}

In real experiments, friction and external impulses may change system momentum; isolation is often only approximate during a brief collision. For a two-dimensional collision, momentum must be balanced separately in each spatial direction. An “elastic collision” additionally requires equal total kinetic energy of the bodies before and after impact. It does not imply that no deformation occurs during contact.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

A well-defined system allows a short momentum balance alongside a separate energy check. Next comes rotational motion.
