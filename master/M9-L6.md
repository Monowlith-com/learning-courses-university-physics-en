# M9-L6 – Hamiltonian Mechanics and Phase Space {#L6}

**Course area:** M9 – Bachelor: Analytical Mechanics

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** Lagrangian mechanics, partial derivatives, energy and differential equations. [Previous lesson](M9-L5.md).

**Learning objective:** Perform the Legendre transform and obtain Hamilton’s equations and oscillator phase space.

## States from Coordinates and Momenta {#S1}

A second-order equation for $q(t)$ can be replaced by two coupled first-order equations. Hamiltonian mechanics represents the state as a point $(q,p)$ in phase space. Here $p$ is the canonical momentum conjugate to $q$, not necessarily mass times velocity in every model. With $n$ independent degrees of freedom, phase space has $2n$ dimensions. Time evolution traces a trajectory in this space, making initial values and conserved quantities easier to visualize. The formulation is especially useful in statistical mechanics, celestial dynamics and as a mathematical bridge to quantum theory.

## Legendre Transform and Canonical Equations {#S2}

Set $p_i=\partial L/\partial\dot q_i$ and assume that these relations can be solved locally for $\dot q_i(q,p,t)$. The Legendre transform is

$$
H(q,p,t)=\sum_i p_i\dot q_i-L(q,\dot q,t).
$$

Upon differentiation, terms containing $\mathrm d\dot q_i$ cancel by the momentum definition. Together with Euler–Lagrange $\dot p_i=\partial L/\partial q_i$, one obtains 

$$
\dot q_i=\frac{\partial H}{\partial p_i},\qquad\dot p_i=-\frac{\partial H}{\partial q_i}.
$$

Along a canonical trajectory, $\mathrm dH/\mathrm dt=\partial H/\partial t$. Thus a time-independent regular Hamiltonian $H$ is conserved. Whether its numerical value equals the physical total energy requires the standard conservative assumptions.

## Example: Elliptical Phase-Space Trajectory {#S3}

For the harmonic oscillator, $L=\tfrac12m\dot q^2-\tfrac12kq^2$ and $p=m\dot q$. The Hamiltonian is

$$
H=\frac{p^2}{2m}+\frac{kq^2}{2}.
$$

Hamilton’s equations yield $\dot q=p/m$ and $\dot p=-kq$, hence $m\ddot q+kq=0$. At positive energy $E$, the orbit satisfies $p^2/(2mE)+kq^2/(2E)=1$, an ellipse in the $q$-$p$ plane. For $m=1\,\mathrm{kg}$, $k=4\,\mathrm{N/m}$ and amplitude $A=0.5\,\mathrm m$, the energy is $E=\tfrac12kA^2=0.5\,\mathrm J$, and $p_{\max}=m\omega A=1\,\mathrm{kg\,m/s}$. The picture depicts a closed phase-space ellipse with labeled intercepts, not the oscillator mass’s spatial trajectory.

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find $H$ for $L=\tfrac12m\dot q^2-V(q)$.
2. Compute $p_{\max}$ for $m=1$, $k=4$ in SI units and $A=0.5\,\mathrm m$.
3. What is the phase-space dimension of a system with three degrees of freedom?

### Solutions

1. With $p=m\dot q$, $H=p^2/(2m)+V(q)$.
2. $\omega=2\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$, hence $p_{\max}=1\,\mathrm{kg\,m/s}$.
3. Six: three position variables and three canonical momenta.

## Regularity and Time Dependence {#S5}

The Legendre transform requires a sufficiently regular velocity Hessian $\partial^2L/\partial\dot q_i\partial\dot q_j$. Singular cases, including some gauge systems, impose constraints and need an extended treatment. Explicitly time-dependent Hamiltonians may exchange energy with external driving. A damped nonconservative oscillator shows a shrinking phase portrait, whereas the canonical dynamics considered here preserves phase-space volume. The orientation and shape of the diagram also depend on the chosen canonical coordinates.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Hamilton’s equations organize trajectories in phase space. Canonical transformations preserve the structure of those equations.
