# M6-L6 – Faraday’s Induction Law and Inductance {#L6}

**Course area:** M6 – Bachelor: Electricity and Magnetism

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** Magnetic flux density, oriented circuit loops and time differentiation. [Previous lesson](M6-L5.md).

**Learning objective:** Compute magnetic flux and induced emf; explain Lenz’s law and inductance.

## Magnetic Flux and Sign {#S1}

Magnetic flux through an oriented surface is $\Phi_B=\int_A\boldsymbol B\cdot\mathrm d\boldsymbol A$, with SI unit weber, $1\,\mathrm{Wb}=1\,\mathrm{T\,m^2}$. In a uniform field $\Phi_B=BA\cos\theta$, where $\theta$ is the angle between surface normal and field. Changing field magnitude, loop orientation or enclosed area can induce an emf. Positive loop circulation is tied to the surface normal by the right-hand rule. The sign is therefore fixed by a consistent orientation. **Lenz’s law** states that induced current acts to oppose the change in magnetic flux that produces it; this is a consequence of energy conservation.

## Induction and Coil Energy {#S2}

For a stationary conducting loop Faraday’s law relates circulation of electric field to changing magnetic flux:

$$
\mathcal E=\oint\boldsymbol E\cdot\mathrm d\boldsymbol l=-\frac{\mathrm d\Phi_B}{\mathrm dt},\qquad\mathcal E_N=-N\frac{\mathrm d\Phi_B}{\mathrm dt}
$$

for a coil with $N$ windings linked by the same flux. For moving conductors, motional emf involving $\boldsymbol v\times\boldsymbol B$ must also be included. For a linear inductor of fixed inductance $L$, the self-flux linkage is $N\Phi_B=LI$, giving $\mathcal E_L=-L\,\mathrm dI/\mathrm dt$. Stored energy is $W_L=\tfrac12 LI^2$. The henry satisfies $1\,\mathrm H=1\,\mathrm{V\,s/A}$. Inductance depends on geometry and magnetic materials.

## Worked Example: Linear Flux Change {#S3}

A coil has $N=200$ turns, each with area $A=0.010\,\mathrm{m^2}$. A uniform magnetic field perpendicular to the coil grows linearly from $0.10$ to $0.30\,\mathrm T$ over $0.050\,\mathrm s$. The flux change per turn is $\Delta\Phi_B=A\Delta B=0.010\cdot0.20=0.0020\,\mathrm{Wb}$. Hence the mean induced-emf magnitude is

$$
|\mathcal E_N|=N\frac{|\Delta\Phi_B|}{\Delta t}=200\frac{0.0020}{0.050}=8.0\,\mathrm V.
$$

For the orientation assigned to increasing positive linkage, the induced emf is negative: $\mathcal E_N=-8.0\,\mathrm V$. The illustration shows growing flux through a coil and the induced circulation opposing the increase. The resulting current cannot be found without the circuit resistance.

*The current-generated field opposes, but does not forbid, the flux change.*

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. A single loop of area $A=0.020\,\mathrm{m^2}$ experiences a perpendicular $\Delta B=0.50\,\mathrm T$ change in $0.10\,\mathrm s$. Find $|\mathcal E|$.
2. Find the energy stored in a linear $L=0.10\,\mathrm H$ inductor carrying $I=2.0\,\mathrm A$.
3. What must remain continuous across a switching instant in an ideal inductor?

### Solutions

1. $|\mathcal E|=A\Delta B/\Delta t=0.10\,\mathrm V$.
2. $W_L=LI^2/2=0.20\,\mathrm J$.
3. Current $I(t)$ remains continuous unless an unphysical impulsively infinite voltage is assumed.

## Induction in Practical Systems {#S5}

The ideal coil model assumes fixed geometry, negligible leakage flux and linear magnetic response. Real coils have winding resistance, parasitic capacitance and frequency-dependent losses; magnetic cores may saturate. In a resistor-inductor circuit driven by a voltage step, $L\,\mathrm dI/\mathrm dt+RI=U$ gives time constant $\tau=L/R$ and $I(t)=\frac UR(1-e^{-t/\tau})$. An ideal inductor’s current cannot jump discontinuously. Opening an inductive circuit can create large voltage transients and requires suitable suppression. Electromagnetic induction underlies generators, transformers and many sensors.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Induction follows changing flux linkage. Inductors store magnetic energy; next come AC circuits.
