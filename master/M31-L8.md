# M31-L8 – Verification, validation and uncertainty budgets {#L8}

**Course area:** M31 – Master: Numerical and Computational Physics

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M31-L1–L7: numerical models; measurement uncertainty basics · [Previous lesson](M31-L7.md).

**Learning objective:** Distinguish verification from validation and estimate grid-error convergence order from three resolutions.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

A simulation may be correctly implemented and still describe the physical world poorly. Verification asks whether equations and discretizations were implemented correctly and solved accurately enough. Validation asks whether the model describes relevant observations within its intended domain. Both assessments are needed and rely on different evidence.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

In a grid convergence study, evaluate one quantity of interest $Q_h$ at several grid spacings $h$. If leading error obeys $|Q_h-Q_*|\approx Ch^p$ with known reference value $Q_*$, halving $h$ yields $e_h/e_{h/2}\approx2^p$. Without a known reference, the order can under suitable conditions be estimated from three nested grids by Richardson differences: $p\approx\log_2(|Q_h-Q_{h/2}|/|Q_{h/2}-Q_{h/4}|)$. This relation needs the asymptotic convergence regime and the same well-defined output quantity. Measurement uncertainty, model discrepancy and parameter variability belong to other uncertainty components.

$$
p\approx\log_2\!\left|\frac{Q_h-Q_{h/2}}{Q_{h/2}-Q_{h/4}}\right|.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For an invented convergence example, choose $Q_*=1$ and $Q_h=1+h^2$. At $h=0.4$, $0.2$ and $0.1$, one gets $Q_h=1.16$, $1.04$, $1.01$. Consecutive differences are $0.12$ and $0.03$, whose ratio is four, so $p=\log_2(4)=2$. The figure shows quadratically decreasing error across labeled grid resolutions.

![Quadratic grid-convergence example with errors 0.16, 0.04 and 0.01 for successively halved mesh spacing.](../../../../media/course/U_PHY_313_EN.svg)

*Quadratically convergent grid sequence with decreasing errors.*

Observed second-order convergence supports verification but does not establish physical validation.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find $p$ if consecutive grid differences are $0.08$ and $0.02$.
2. Is comparing model outputs with measurements verification or validation?
3. Why are just one coarse and one fine mesh insufficient?

### Solutions

1. $p=\log_2(4)=2$.
2. Validation when independent physical observations are used.
3. Two grids cannot reliably establish observed order and may fall outside the asymptotic regime.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The figure uses constructed values and does not verify any real simulation. Rounding, solver termination, boundary conditions and model assumptions can mask numerical error. Grid convergence cannot substitute for validation against suitable experimental evidence.

## Summary and outlook {#S6}
The module closes with a traceable chain from model to numerical solution, numerical error and comparison with observations.
