# M32-L1 – Sensors, counting statistics and noise limits {#L1}

**Course area:** M32 – Master: Experimental Instrumentation and Research Methods

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M16: measurement uncertainty and sensors; M30: quantum-measurement context · [Previous lesson](M31-L8.md).

**Learning objective:** Estimate the relative counting uncertainty for independent events and distinguish background noise.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

A sensitive detector often counts individual photons or particles rather than measuring only a continuous voltage. Its uncertainty may include counting fluctuations, dark events, electronic noise, drift and correlated sources. Independent rare arrivals at a constant rate admit a useful Poisson model.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

For a Poisson random count $N$ of mean $\mu$, one has $\mathrm{Var}(N)=\mu$. At sufficiently large observed counts the standard deviation is estimated as $\sigma_N\simeq\sqrt{N}$. With negligible background, the signal-to-noise ratio relative to the mean is approximately $\mu/\sqrt{\mu}=\sqrt{\mu}$. Relative counting uncertainty therefore falls only as the inverse square root of count number. Subtracting independently measured background counts requires adding their properly exposure-scaled variances.

$$
\sigma_N=\sqrt{\mu},\qquad \mathrm{SNR}\simeq\sqrt{\mu}\quad\text{(ideal Poisson counts)}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For a mean of $\mu=400$ detected photons, $\sigma_N=20$ and the relative one-sigma fluctuation is $5\,\%$. Four times as many counts give $\sigma_N=40$ but only $2.5\,\%$ relative fluctuation. The figure compares absolute standard deviations with decreasing relative fluctuations; it is not experimental data.

![Count statistics: absolute standard deviation grows as count square root while relative noise falls.](../../../../media/course/U_PHY_213_EN.svg)

*Relative counting precision improves only with the square root of detected events.*

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find $\sigma_N$ and $\sigma_N/N$ for $N=900$.
2. By what factor must $N$ increase to halve relative Poisson noise?
3. Why does $\sqrt{N_{\mathrm{signal}}}$ underestimate uncertainty with nonzero background?

### Solutions

1. $\sigma_N\simeq30$ and $30/900=3.33\,\%$.
2. By a factor of four.
3. Background events and, if applicable, separate background subtraction add variance.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The Poisson approximation requires independent arrivals. Dead time, saturation, bunching, antibunching, drift and readout noise alter the statistics. The NIST source supports sensor applications, not an independent derivation of the Poisson formula.
