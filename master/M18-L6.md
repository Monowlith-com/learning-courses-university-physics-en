# M18-L6 – Spectroscopy: Wavelength Calibration and Resolution {#L6}

**Course area:** M18 – Bachelor: Physics Laboratory Practice

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** M7: optical spectra; M15: energy levels; M16: regression. [Previous lesson](M18-L5.md).

**Learning objective:** Fit a pixel-to-wavelength calibration and interpret spectral resolution correctly.

## Research Question and Model {#S1}

A spectrometer assigns a wavelength to detector intensities. A peak position initially specifies a pixel coordinate $p$, not a physical wavelength. Known reference lines calibrate a suitable model, for example $\lambda(p)=a+bp$ over a limited range. Wide spectral spans may require nonlinear dispersion; however a high-order polynomial should not be chosen merely because it passes through every calibration point. Record the measurement date, reference features and valid pixel range.

## Equations and Assumptions {#S2}

Spectral resolving power can be written $R=\lambda/\Delta\lambda$, where the definition of $\Delta\lambda$ must be stated, for example the instrumental full width at half maximum of a narrow reference feature. Varying line shapes, stray light and overlap complicate identification. The peak width in pixels is not automatically the intrinsic spectral width: the instrument response broadens what is observed. Width subtraction requires a justified convolution model; Gaussian and Lorentzian width rules are not interchangeable.

$$
\lambda(p)=a+bp,\qquad R=\frac{\lambda}{\Delta\lambda}
$$

## Worked Example and Diagram {#S3}

Synthetic two-point calibration: A 500 nm reference line falls at pixel 100 and a 600 nm line at pixel 300. Then $b=0.5\,\mathrm{nm/pixel}$ and $a=450\,\mathrm{nm}$. Under this model a peak at pixel 240 lies at $570\,\mathrm{nm}$. If the instrumental line width is $\Delta\lambda=2\,\mathrm{nm}$ at $600\,\mathrm{nm}$, the resolving power is $R=300$. These calibration values are constructed for teaching and are not readings from an identified spectrometer.

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find $a$ and $b$ from references (100, 500 nm) and (300, 600 nm).
2. What is the wavelength at pixel 240?
3. Calculate $R$ for $\lambda=600\,\mathrm{nm}$ and $\Delta\lambda=2\,\mathrm{nm}$.

### Solutions

1. $a=450\,\mathrm{nm}$ and $b=0.5\,\mathrm{nm/pixel}$.
2. $\lambda(240)=450+0.5\cdot240=570\,\mathrm{nm}$.
3. $R=600/2=300$.

## Limits and Quality Control {#S5}

Test the calibration against at least one independent line excluded from the fit. An offset in this check may indicate an unsuitable dispersion model or changed instrument geometry. Both reference wavelength uncertainty and pixel-centroid uncertainty contribute. For an asymmetric profile, the intensity centroid and the position of the maximum may differ. Preserve raw pixels, dark-signal correction, fits, residuals and calibration range. A spectral feature must not be identified by colour resemblance alone.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Without an independent calibration check and a clearly defined linewidth, a wavelength claim is fragile.
