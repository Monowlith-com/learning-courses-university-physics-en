# M21-L1 – Seismic Waves, Travel Times and Earth Structure {#L1}

**Course area:** M21 – Bachelor: Geophysics and Climate

**Level:** Bachelor · Elective

**Prerequisites:** M4: wave physics; M17: model fitting.

**Learning objective:** Distinguish P and S waves, calculate travel times and explain basic limits of Earth-interior tomography.

## Question and Observation {#S1}

An earthquake generates elastic disturbances that reach measuring stations as temporally separated signals. A compressional P wave usually arrives first, while a shear S wave tends to arrive later. Their travel times carry information about the material along their paths. A seismogram is a measurement of ground motion, not a direct picture of subsurface geology. Inferring structure from arrival times requires a model of wave speeds.

## Physical Model and Equations {#S2}

For a homogeneous, isotropic and linearly elastic material of density $\rho$, bulk modulus $K$ and shear modulus $\mu$, the bulk-wave speeds are approximately
$$
v_P=\sqrt{\frac{K+4\mu/3}{\rho}},\qquad v_S=\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{\rho}}.
$$
Shear waves require $\mu>0$; an ideal fluid with vanishing shear rigidity therefore does not support bulk S waves. For a straight ray of length $d$, the simplest forward model is $t=d/v$. If the same path length is assumed, the P-to-S arrival delay is $\Delta t=t_S-t_P=d(1/v_S-1/v_P)$. In a stratified Earth, gradients refract rays and boundaries produce reflections or conversions.

## Worked Example and Teaching Diagram {#S3}

In a synthetic example, a seismic ray follows an idealised path of $d=120\,\mathrm{km}$. Let $v_P=6\,\mathrm{km/s}$ and $v_S=3.5\,\mathrm{km/s}$. The P arrival occurs after $20.0\,\mathrm{s}$ and the S arrival after about $34.3\,\mathrm{s}$, leaving a $14.3\,\mathrm{s}$ delay. ## Self-Assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Calculate $v_P$ for $K=50\,\mathrm{GPa}$, $\mu=30\,\mathrm{GPa}$ and $\rho=3000\,\mathrm{kg/m^3}$.
2. For a $90\,\mathrm{km}$ ray, find the S–P delay if $v_P=6$ and $v_S=3\,\mathrm{km/s}$.
3. Why does one S–P delay not uniquely locate an earthquake source in three dimensions?

### Solutions

1. $v_P=\sqrt{(50+40)\cdot10^9/3000}\approx5.48\,\mathrm{km/s}$.
2. $\Delta t=90/3-90/6=15\,\mathrm{s}$.
3. Without further stations, a velocity model and an origin time, it constrains distance but not the full source position.

## Validity Limits and Quality {#S5}

The calculation assumes one path length through uniform, isotropic rock. Attenuation, scattering, reflections, source depth and velocity changes alter actual signals. A teaching diagram is not a geophysical tomographic reconstruction.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Seismic arrival times constrain Earth structure indirectly, through a travel-time and material model.
