# M20-L1 – Astrometry, Parallax and the Distance Ladder {#L1}

**Course area:** M20 – Bachelor: Astrophysics and Cosmology

**Level:** Bachelor · Elective

**Prerequisites:** M7: geometrical optics; M16: measurement uncertainty; M19: model criticism. [Previous lesson: Computational Records and Convergence](M19-L7.md).

**Learning objective:** Infer a distance from parallax and distinguish geometric from photometric distance estimates.

## Question and Observation {#S1}

Stellar distances cannot be measured with an ordinary ruler. Instead, observers use the apparent displacement of a nearby star against very distant background sources. Earth's orbital motion provides a known baseline. Stellar parallax is **half** the angular displacement between idealised observations roughly six months apart. This geometric method does not require knowing a star's luminosity. Apparent brightness, by contrast, combines intrinsic luminosity, distance and possible attenuation by dust.

## Physical Model and Equations {#S2}

For small angles, the parsec is defined by
$$
d[\mathrm{pc}]=\frac{1}{p[\mathrm{arcsec}]},
$$
where $p$ denotes annual trigonometric parallax in arcseconds. One arcsecond is $1/3600$ of a degree; the definition uses a baseline of one astronomical unit. For isotropic emission without attenuation, the measured flux is $F=L/(4\pi d^2)$. Apparent magnitude $m$ and absolute magnitude $M$ obey
$$
m-M=5\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{d}{10\,\mathrm{pc}}\right).
$$
This elementary relation presumes compatible photometric definitions and negligible extinction. Real measurements also require treatment of systematic angular offsets and statistical uncertainty.

## Worked Example and Teaching Diagram {#S3}

A synthetic measurement yields $p=0.050''$. The geometric distance is $d=20\,\mathrm{pc}$. For apparent magnitude $m=8.00$, the distance modulus is $5\log_{10}(20/10)=1.505$, giving $M\approx6.49$ if extinction is ignored. ## Self-Assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. A parallax of $0.10\,\mathrm{arcsec}$ is measured. What is the distance?
2. What is the distance modulus for $100\,\mathrm{pc}$?
3. How does observed flux change if distance doubles while luminosity is fixed?

### Solutions

1. $d=1/0.10=10\,\mathrm{pc}$.
2. $m-M=5\log_{10}(10)=5$.
3. $F$ falls to one quarter because $F\propto d^{-2}$.

## Validity Limits and Quality {#S5}

When parallax uncertainty is comparable to its measured value, naive inversion can bias distances; appropriate inference uses a statistical model and, where justified, prior information on stellar populations. Extinction and wavelength dependence can modify the magnitude relation. More distant galaxies require further methods in a distance ladder; parallax by itself is insufficient. ## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Parallax provides a geometric distance; brightness-based methods supplement it with astrophysical assumptions.
