# M36-L7 – Tracking Detectors, Magnetic Curvature and Momentum {#L7}

**Course area:** M36 – Master: Particle Physics, Collision Experiments and Accelerators

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M10: Lorentz force; M36-L6: particle beams.

**Learning objective:** Compute transverse momentum from an ideal circular-track radius in a uniform magnetic field.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Trackers register hit positions from which charged-particle trajectories are reconstructed inside a magnetic field. Bending orientation depends on charge sign, while radius constrains momentum at known field strength. Calorimeters measure deposited energy and muon chambers detect penetrating charged particles. These subsystems are complementary.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

For charge $q$, transverse momentum $p_T$ and uniform magnetic field $B$, $|q|v_TB=p_Tv_T/R$, so $p_T=|q|BR$. In practical units $p_T[\mathrm{GeV}/c]\simeq0.2998\,|q/e|B[\mathrm T]R[\mathrm m]$. The rounded $0.3$ comes from unit conversion, not an empirical fit.

$$
p_T[\mathrm{GeV}/c]\simeq0.2998\,|q/e|\,B[\mathrm T]R[\mathrm m].
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For a singly charged particle at $B=2\,\mathrm T$ and $R=1\,\mathrm m$, $p_T\approx0.600\,\mathrm{GeV}/c$. A second track of twice the radius at the same field and charge would have twice the transverse momentum. The figure is an original schematic of detector layers and two bent tracks.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find $p_T$ for $B=2\,\mathrm T$, $R=0.5\,\mathrm m$, $|q|=e$.
2. Find the radius for a singly charged track with $p_T=1.2\,\mathrm{GeV}/c$ at $B=2\,\mathrm T$.
3. Why does a neutral particle not leave a magnetic-curvature signature?

### Solutions

1. $p_T\approx0.300\,\mathrm{GeV}/c$.
2. $R\approx2.00\,\mathrm m$.
3. With no charge there is no magnetic Lorentz force; neutral particles require other detection methods.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

A circular path assumes constant field, negligible losses and a known projection. Real track fits require field maps, multiple-scattering and material corrections, alignment and uncertainty estimates.
