# M12-L1 – Hilbert Space, State Vectors and Measurement {#L1}

**Course area:** M12 – Bachelor: Advanced Quantum Mechanics

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** Complex numbers, linear algebra, eigenvectors, and spin from M8 and M11. [Previous lesson](M11-L7.md).

**Learning objective:** Use the Hilbert-space description of pure states, expectation values, and the Born rule correctly.

## States Are Not Measurement Results {#S1}

A pure quantum state is represented by a normalized vector $|\psi\rangle$ in a complex Hilbert space. Global phase has no measurable consequence: $|\psi\rangle$ and $e^{i\phi}|\psi\rangle$ give the same predictions. Relative phase between components, however, affects interference and is observable indirectly. Quantities are represented by self-adjoint operators. A superposition of eigenstates does not mean that one measurement produces several eigenvalues simultaneously. The formalism predicts probabilities for an operationally defined measurement; state preparation, detectors and experimental limitations remain essential.

## Bra–Ket Notation and Spectral Decomposition {#S2}

A ket $|\psi\rangle$ has a corresponding bra $\langle\psi|$. The inner product $\langle\phi|\psi\rangle$ may be complex, whereas a normalized state satisfies $\langle\psi|\psi\rangle=1$. For a discrete observable $A$ with eigenstates $|a_n\rangle$, $A|a_n\rangle=a_n|a_n\rangle$. For a nondegenerate eigenvalue $a_n$, the Born rule reads

$$
P(a_n)=|\langle a_n|\psi\rangle|^2
$$

More generally use the projector $P_a$ onto the whole eigenspace: $P(a)=\langle\psi|P_a|\psi\rangle$. The expectation value is $\langle A\rangle=\langle\psi|A|\psi\rangle=\sum_a aP(a)$. In a degenerate eigenspace a single arbitrarily selected eigenvector is not enough to describe the outcome.

## Example: Measuring a Spin Superposition {#S3}

Consider $|\psi\rangle=\sqrt{3}/2\,|+\rangle+(i/2)|-\rangle$ in the $S_z$ eigenbasis. Its norm is $3/4+1/4=1$, and an ideal $z$ measurement gives $P(+)=3/4$ and $P(-)=1/4$. The expectation value is

$$
\langle S_z\rangle=(3/4)(\hbar/2)+(1/4)(-\hbar/2)=\hbar/4
$$

Across many identically prepared systems the observed frequencies approach those predictions, while an individual result is always $+\hbar/2$ or $-\hbar/2$. ## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Normalize $|\chi\rangle=2|+\rangle+i|-\rangle$.
2. Find the probability of $|+\rangle$ after normalization.
3. Why need an expectation value not be an allowed individual result?

### Solutions

1. $|\chi_{\mathrm n}\rangle=(2|+\rangle+i|-\rangle)/\sqrt{5}$.
2. $P(+)=4/5$.
3. The expectation value averages many outcomes and need not itself be an eigenvalue.

## Scope and Interpretation {#S5}

The projection rule describes conditional repeat measurements only in an ideal projective-measurement model. Real detectors may require generalized POVM effects and open-system dynamics. A state amplitude does not assert that a particle carries a known classical value before measurement. A statistical mixture is represented by a density operator $\rho$ with $\rho\succeq0$ and $\mathrm{Tr}\,\rho=1$ rather than one ket. Its prediction is $P(a)=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho P_a)$. Coherent superposition and statistical mixture can share one observable's distribution yet differ when another measurement basis is used.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Hilbert space, the Born rule and projectors connect quantum states to statistically measurable outcomes.
