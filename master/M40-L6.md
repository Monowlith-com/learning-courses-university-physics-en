# M40-L6 – Regularized Image Reconstruction and External AI Validation {#L6}

**Course area:** M40 – Master: Advanced Medical Physics and Quality Assurance

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M31: inverse methods and numerical optimization; M40-L5.

**Learning objective:** Evaluate reconstruction priors and generalization of image-analysis models as separate issues.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Reconstruction and AI-driven image classification are different tasks. Reconstruction seeks plausible spatial information from measurements; diagnostic use additionally requires performance on representative independent data. High scores on the training data or related samples do not establish reliability in other settings.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

A simple linear inverse model is $y=Ax+n$. Tikhonov-type regularization minimizes $\|Ax-y\|_2^2+\lambda R(x)$. A penalty $R$ can stabilize an ill-posed problem but may introduce smoothing or bias. If an AI model trains on reconstructions, scanner changes, protocols, age distributions and patient overlap across dataset splits can distort evaluation. Case-level partitioning, external assessment and prospective monitoring are separate requirements.

$$
\hat{x}=\arg\min_x\bigl\{\|Ax-y\|_2^2+\lambda R(x)\bigr\}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For a purely mathematical single-pixel example, choose $A=1$, $y=2$, $R(x)=x^2$ and $\lambda=0.25$. From $2(x-2)+0.5x=0$ follows $\hat x=1.6$, compared with $x=2$ without regularization. This bias alone does not measure diagnostic utility. The figure shows separate flows for training, internal assessment and external validation.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What is $\hat x$ at $\lambda=1$ in the same one-pixel example?
2. Why are images from one person across training and test sets problematic?
3. Why is high PSNR not a substitute for diagnostic test performance?

### Solutions

1. $\hat x=2/(1+1)=1$.
2. It creates information leakage and an optimistic estimate of generalization.
3. A pixel similarity metric does not reliably capture clinically important errors.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

FDA guiding principles concern development practice, not automatic approval or legal clearance. A model can fail under distribution shift even with correct data partitioning. External validation must reflect the intended environment and quantify uncertainty.
