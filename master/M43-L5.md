# M43-L5 – Parameter Sensitivity, Identifiability and Measurement Times {#L5}

**Course area:** M43 – Master: Model Assessment and Scientific Research Practice

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M31: inverse problems; M43-L1: relaxation model; M43-L4: uncertainty budget.

**Learning objective:** Explain why some parameters cannot be identified from measurements at a single time.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

A model may fit the observations well while its parameters remain ambiguous. **Identifiability** asks whether different parameter values produce distinguishable predictions at all. Weak sensitivity makes estimation noise-sensitive, while identical predictions imply structural non-identifiability. Measurement times should be chosen to separate the signatures of different parameters.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

For $y(t;A,k)=A e^{-kt}$ with $A>0$ and $k>0$, the sensitivities are $\partial y/\partial A=e^{-kt}$ and $\partial y/\partial k=-At e^{-kt}$. At $t=0$, the signal depends on $A$ alone and has zero sensitivity to $k$. Two noiseless measurements at $t_0=0$ and $t_1>0$ identify $A=y(0)$ and then $k=-\ln[y(t_1)/A]/t_1$, provided $0<y(t_1)<A$.

$$
\frac{\partial y}{\partial A}=e^{-kt},\qquad \frac{\partial y}{\partial k}=-At e^{-kt}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

In a **synthetic** model set $A=8$ and $k=0.25\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$. Then $y(0)=8$ and $y(2\,\mathrm{s})=8e^{-0.5}\approx4.852$. The two plotted points suffice to determine both parameters in this idealized model. With measurements only at $t=0$, $k$ can take any value and is therefore unidentifiable.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What is $\partial y/\partial k$ at $t=0$?
2. Why does a second measurement time improve estimation?
3. Find $k$ from $A=8$, $t=2\,\mathrm s$ and $y(2)=8e^{-0.5}$.

### Solutions

1. $0$, regardless of $A$ and $k$.
2. A later observation depends on the decay rate, while $t=0$ isolates amplitude.
3. $k=0.5/2=0.25\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

Noise, unknown background, finite resolution and poorly chosen measurement times can strongly limit practical identifiability. Parameter correlations and the local Jacobian matter in larger models. Two observations are not a robust experimental design.
