# M12-L9 – Molecular Rotation and Vibration {#L9}

**Course area:** M12 – Bachelor: Advanced Quantum Mechanics

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** Angular momentum, harmonic oscillator and photon energy; M12-L4–L5. [Previous lesson](M12-L8.md).

**Learning objective:** Distinguish rotational and vibrational levels and estimate simple molecular spectra quantitatively.

## Diatomic Molecules as Quantum Systems {#S1}

A diatomic molecule has internal motions that can approximately be separated into rotation and vibration. Near the equilibrium bond length, harmonic motion is a useful model; orientation is described by a rigid rotor. These are distinct degrees of freedom that can couple in a real molecule. Spectra therefore often consist of bands with rotational structure rather than one isolated line. Separating electronic, vibrational and rotational energy scales is an approximation whose accuracy depends on molecule, excitation and temperature.

## Rotor, Oscillator and Selection Rules {#S2}

For atoms of masses $m_1,m_2$ the reduced mass is $\mu=m_1m_2/(m_1+m_2)$. The moment of inertia at bond length $r_e$ is $I=\mu r_e^2$. A linear rigid rotor has energies

$$
E_J=\frac{\hbar^2}{2I}J(J+1),\qquad J=0,1,2,\ldots
$$

A harmonic vibration has 

$$
E_v=\hbar\omega(v+1/2),\qquad\omega=\sqrt{k/\mu}
$$

Electric-dipole pure rotational transitions of a polar molecule typically obey $\Delta J=\pm1$; homonuclear diatomics have no permanent electric dipole and lack ordinary electric-dipole pure rotational lines. Infrared vibrational activity requires the dipole moment to change as the bond length changes.

## Example: Rotational Spacing and Isotope Effect {#S3}

Take a model with $I=1.00\times10^{-46}\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}$. The $J=0$ to $J=1$ transition has

$$
\Delta E=\frac{\hbar^2}{I}\simeq1.11\times10^{-22}\,\mathrm J,
$$

corresponding to $\nu=\Delta E/h\simeq1.68\times10^{11}\,\mathrm{Hz}$. If reduced mass doubles while geometry stays fixed, the rotational transition frequency halves because $I\propto\mu$. Vibrational frequency decreases only by $1/\sqrt2$. The figure separates the $J(J+1)$ rotational ladder from unequal spacings of levels in an anharmonic molecular potential.

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Compute $E_2/E_1$ for a rigid rotor.
2. How does $\omega$ change if reduced mass quadruples at fixed $k$?
3. Why is a pure rotational line absent for a homonuclear diatomic in the simplest electric-dipole model?

### Solutions

1. $E_2/E_1=6/2=3$.
2. $\omega$ is halved.
3. There is no permanent electric dipole and the electric-dipole rotational transition moment vanishes.

## Anharmonicity and Real Spectra {#S5}

The rigid-rotor model neglects rotational bond stretching and the harmonic model has no dissociation threshold. A Morse potential gives an instructive improvement, with finitely many bound vibrational levels. Real spectra can show rotation–vibration coupling, isotopic patterns, nuclear-spin statistics, Stark and Zeeman shifts, and Doppler or collision broadening. Temperature chiefly changes populations and thus line intensities as well as indirectly affecting the environment; it is not simply a universal rescaling of ideal energy gaps. Assigning an observed line requires the transition operator and its selection rules.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}
Rotation and vibration form distinct quantized energy scales; selection rules and molecular structure determine observed spectra.
