# M38-L5 – Radiative Transfer, Optical Depth and Emission {#L5}

**Course area:** M38 – Master: Geophysics, Atmospheric Physics and Climate Models

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M20: optics; M22: thermal radiation; M38-L4 · [Previous lesson](M38-L4.md).

**Learning objective:** Integrate a scalar directional radiative-transfer equation and separate assumptions about absorption, local thermal emission and absence of scattering.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Spectral radiance changes as light passes through matter because of absorption, emission and possibly scattering. Our restricted toy case uses an isothermal, non-scattering slab with a constant source function. Optical depth is then a dimensionless quantity measured along the chosen propagation path, not a geographic height or universal cloud property.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

We define $\tau$ increasing along the radiation path: $dI_\nu/d\tau=-I_\nu+S_\nu$. For local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE), thermal emission and no scattering, take $S_\nu=B_\nu(T)$. At constant temperature across optical thickness $\tau_*$, $I_{\nu,\mathrm{out}}=I_{\nu,\mathrm{in}}e^{-\tau_*}+B_\nu(T)(1-e^{-\tau_*})$. Directional path length must be incorporated in $\tau$. With scattering or non-LTE, $S_\nu$ generally differs from $B_\nu$.

$$
I_{\nu,\mathrm{out}}=I_{\nu,\mathrm{in}}e^{-\tau_*}+B_\nu(T)(1-e^{-\tau_*}).
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For a constructed monochromatic case with $I_{\nu,\mathrm{in}}=0$, $B_\nu=8$ arbitrary radiance units and $\tau_*=\ln 2\approx0.693$, the output is $4$ units. As $\tau_*\rightarrow0$, the slab adds negligible emission; at large thickness the emergent radiance approaches $B_\nu$. The drawing separates contributions at one frequency.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What is $I_{\nu,\mathrm{out}}$ at $\tau_*=0$ for arbitrary incident radiance?
2. What is the limit at $\tau_*\rightarrow\infty$ for the isothermal slab?
3. Why must strong scattering not be handled by this unchanged relation?

### Solutions

1. $I_{\nu,\mathrm{out}}=I_{\nu,\mathrm{in}}$.
2. $I_{\nu,\mathrm{out}}\rightarrow B_\nu(T)$.
3. Scattering couples directions and changes the source function.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The expression is spectral, homogeneous, isothermal, locally thermal and non-scattering. A grey model averages over spectral features; it is not an exact description of greenhouse-gas line absorption. Other texts may use the opposite path/optical-depth convention.
