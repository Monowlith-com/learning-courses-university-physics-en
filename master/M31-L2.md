# M31-L2 – Partial differential equations and grid methods {#L2}

**Course area:** M31 – Master: Numerical and Computational Physics

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M31-L1: time integration; M12: partial differential equations · [Previous lesson](M31-L1.md).

**Learning objective:** Construct an explicit finite-difference update for the heat equation and interpret its stability number.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Temperature, concentration and field amplitudes depend on both position and time. Grid methods replace spatial derivatives by differences of neighboring values. On a uniform one-dimensional mesh, the centered second difference has second-order spatial accuracy for smooth solutions.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

The heat equation is $\partial_t u=D\partial_{xx}u$ with diffusivity $D>0$. For spatial spacing $\Delta x$ and time step $\Delta t$, the explicit FTCS scheme gives $u_i^{n+1}=u_i^n+r(u_{i-1}^n-2u_i^n+u_{i+1}^n)$, where $r=D\Delta t/(\Delta x)^2$. The central point has weight $1-2r$, and the two neighbors have weight $r$ each. If $0\le r\le1/2$, these weights are nonnegative and preserve nonnegative data under suitable boundary conditions. In the linear one-dimensional case this is also the standard Fourier stability bound. Boundary conditions must be specified separately.

$$
r=\frac{D\Delta t}{(\Delta x)^2}\le\frac12\quad\text{(1D FTCS heat equation)}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

With $D=0.1\,\mathrm{m^2/s}$ and $\Delta x=0.2\,\mathrm m$, the required step size is $\Delta t\le(0.2)^2/(2\cdot0.1)=0.2\,\mathrm s$. The figure shows the local three-point stencil and a schematic temperature profile that smooths over time. Conservation of total heat depends on the boundary conditions; the example is not a measured material response.

*The explicit heat-equation update couples each new grid value to three values from the previous time level.*

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find $r$ for $D=0.2$, $\Delta t=0.05$, $\Delta x=0.1$ in SI units.
2. What happens to the stable time step when $\Delta x$ is halved?
3. Distinguish Dirichlet and Neumann boundary conditions.

### Solutions

1. $r=1$; the explicit scheme is unstable.
2. The largest admissible step becomes four times smaller at fixed $D$.
3. Dirichlet fixes field values at a boundary; Neumann fixes their normal derivatives.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The bound applies to the linear one-dimensional equation on a uniform grid. Geometry, variable coefficients, sources and multidimensional stencils change the restrictions. Finite volumes make discrete flux conservation explicit; finite elements use weak forms with different error analyses.
