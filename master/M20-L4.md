# M20-L4 – Compact Stars, Black Holes and Accretion {#L4}

**Course area:** M20 – Bachelor: Astrophysics and Cosmology

**Level:** Bachelor · Elective

**Prerequisites:** M9: two-body motion; M10: field theory; M12: quantum states. [Previous lesson: Stellar Structure, Hydrostatic Equilibrium and Evolution](M20-L3.md).

**Learning objective:** Estimate gravitational radii and elementary accretion power while distinguishing compact-object types.

## Question and Observation {#S1}

The endpoints of stellar evolution can be extremely compact. White dwarfs are mainly supported by electron degeneracy pressure; the structure of neutron stars is governed by dense nuclear matter. A black hole is not an ordinary body with a material surface. Its existence is not inferred from darkness alone: orbital motion, emission from surrounding material and dynamical measurements can reveal a highly concentrated gravitational field. Luminous accreting matter lies **outside** the event horizon.

## Physical Model and Equations {#S2}

For a non-rotating, uncharged black hole, the Schwarzschild model gives
$$
r_s=\frac{2GM}{c^2}.
$$
This is the event-horizon radius, not the size of a glowing accretion disc. An elementary accretion estimate uses
$$
L\approx\eta\dot M c^2,
$$
where $\dot M$ is the mass inflow rate and $\eta$ a model-dependent efficiency. Emission from accretion does not prove matter has crossed the event horizon. Relativistic dynamics are required close to the compact object.

## Worked Example and Teaching Diagram {#S3}

With $M=10M_\odot$ and $M_\odot\approx1.9885\times10^{30}\,\mathrm{kg}$, the radius is $r_s\approx29.5\,\mathrm{km}$. For a **purely illustrative** inflow $\dot M=10^{10}\,\mathrm{kg/s}$ and $\eta=0.10$, the estimated power is $L\approx9.0\times10^{25}\,\mathrm W$. Changing $\eta$ changes this power linearly. ## Self-Assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Estimate $r_s$ for a Schwarzschild black hole with $5M_\odot$.
2. What happens to $L$ when $\dot M$ doubles at constant $\eta$?
3. Why does luminous surrounding matter not imply a bright horizon surface?

### Solutions

1. $r_s\approx14.8\,\mathrm{km}$.
2. The model luminosity doubles.
3. Radiation is produced in hot matter outside the horizon.

## Validity Limits and Quality {#S5}

Rotation, magnetic fields, radiative transfer and feedback can markedly change real accretion luminosities. The Schwarzschild radius applies to an ideal non-rotating hole; neutron stars instead have material surfaces and different equations of state. The illustration is a teaching geometry, not an observational image.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Compact objects require distinguishing material surfaces, event horizons and the observable surroundings.
