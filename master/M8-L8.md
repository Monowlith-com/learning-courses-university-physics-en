# M8-L8 – Green Functions and Delta Distributions {#L8}

**Course area:** M8 – Bachelor: Mathematical Methods

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** Boundary-value problems, integration, linear operators and piecewise functions. [Previous lesson](M8-L7.md).

**Learning objective:** Derive a one-dimensional Dirichlet Green function and integrate a source distribution.

## Response to a Point Source {#S1}

A linear boundary-value problem can involve a source distributed in space: electrostatic charge, mechanical loading or heat generation within a rod. Because the governing operator is linear, first determine its response to an idealized source localized at a single point. This point response is called a Green function. The ideal source is represented by the Dirac delta distribution $\delta(x-\xi)$, which selects a function value at $\xi$ under integration. It is not an ordinary infinitely tall function that can be evaluated pointwise everywhere. Its precise use belongs to integrals or distribution equations. With homogeneous boundary conditions, an arbitrary distributed source can be reconstructed as a superposition of these point responses.

## Green Function from Boundary and Jump Conditions {#S2}

Consider operator $\mathcal L=-\mathrm d^2/\mathrm dx^2$ on $0<x<L$ with homogeneous Dirichlet boundaries. We seek $G(x,\xi)$ satisfying $-\partial_x^2G(x,\xi)=\delta(x-\xi)$ and $G(0,\xi)=G(L,\xi)=0$. Away from $x=\xi$, $G$ is linear in $x$. Impose continuity at $x=\xi$ and integrate the differential equation across a small interval around that point. This yields $G'_+(\xi)-G'_-(\xi)=-1$. Combined with the endpoint values, it gives

$$
G(x,\xi)=\frac{\min(x,\xi)\,[L-\max(x,\xi)]}{L}.
$$

As a function of $x$ this is piecewise linear with a kink at the source location. That kink represents the delta source in the distributional sense.

## Example: A Uniform Source {#S3}

For $-u''(x)=f(x)$ with $u(0)=u(L)=0$, the Green function constructs

$$
u(x)=\int_0^L G(x,\xi)f(\xi)\,\mathrm d\xi.
$$

Choose a constant source $f(x)=1$ in consistently scaled units. Direct integration over $0<\xi<x$ and $x<\xi<L$ yields 

$$
u(x)=\frac{x(L-x)}2.
$$

Differentiating twice verifies $-u''=1$, and $u=0$ at both ends. Its maximum occurs at $x=L/2$ and has value $u_{\max}=L^2/8$. The figure depicts the triangular Green function for a fixed source point $\xi$ and zero endpoint values. This point response must be distinguished from the parabolic solution generated by a uniform source.

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Compute $u(L/2)$ for $-u''=1$ with Dirichlet boundaries.
2. For $\xi=L/2$, find the maximum of $G(x,\xi)$.
3. Why is $G(x,\xi)$ not twice classically differentiable at $x=\xi$?

### Solutions

1. $u(L/2)=L^2/8$.
2. $G(\xi,\xi)=\xi(L-\xi)/L=L/4$.
3. The first derivative has a jump of $-1$; the second derivative is defined distributionally as $-\delta(x-\xi)$.

## Symmetry, Existence and Applications {#S5}

A Green function depends on the differential operator, its domain and every boundary condition. Different boundaries produce different Green functions; if the operator has a zero mode, uniqueness can fail or a compatibility condition may be required. The symmetry $G(x,\xi)=G(\xi,x)$ shown here follows from this self-adjoint operator with Dirichlet boundaries. Time-dependent problems require retarded, advanced or other Green functions according to the physical question. A retarded response respects causal structure rather than merely the form of a spatial operator. In discrete numerical models, inverse matrices or suitable solution operators play an analogous role, but the continuous delta distribution must not be inserted as an ordinary single-valued number.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}
Green functions superpose point sources to solve linear boundary-value problems. The methods of M8 will recur in field theories and quantum mechanics.
