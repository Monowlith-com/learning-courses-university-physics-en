# M9-L2 – Generalized Coordinates and Constraints {#L2}

**Course area:** M9 – Bachelor: Analytical Mechanics

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** Euler–Lagrange equations, vector kinematics and trigonometry. [Previous lesson](M9-L1.md).

**Learning objective:** Identify holonomic constraints and derive pendulum motion using an angle coordinate.

## Degrees of Freedom Instead of Redundant Coordinates {#S1}

An unconstrained point particle moving in a plane requires two position coordinates. Attaching it to an ideal inextensible string of length $\ell$ imposes $x^2+y^2=\ell^2$, leaving one degree of freedom. A holonomic constraint has the form $f(q_1,\ldots,q_n,t)=0$ and locally permits redundant coordinates to be eliminated. Generalized coordinates need not be distances; angles, relative positions and other parameters are allowed. A well-chosen coordinate system is not merely cosmetic. For ideal constraints, it can eliminate unknown reaction forces from the equations of motion because those reactions do no virtual work along admissible displacements.

## A Simple Pendulum as a One-Coordinate System {#S2}

For a planar pendulum choose angle $\theta$ from the downward vertical. The mass position is $x=\ell\sin\theta$ and $y=-\ell\cos\theta$, so $v^2=\dot x^2+\dot y^2=\ell^2\dot\theta^2$. With potential zero at the lowest point, $V=mg\ell(1-\cos\theta)$, hence

$$
L=\tfrac12m\ell^2\dot\theta^2-mg\ell(1-\cos\theta).
$$

Substitution into Euler–Lagrange yields $m\ell^2\ddot\theta+mg\ell\sin\theta=0$, or 

$$
\ddot\theta+\frac g\ell\sin\theta=0.
$$

String tension does not appear even though it enforces circular motion. A Cartesian formulation with a Lagrange multiplier could recover the constraint force.

## Example: Period in the Small-Angle Limit {#S3}

For small angular deflections measured in radians, $\sin\theta\approx\theta$, giving $\ddot\theta+(g/\ell)\theta=0$ and

$$
T_0=2\pi\sqrt{\frac\ell g}.
$$

Taking $\ell=0.994\,\mathrm m$ and $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ yields $T_0\approx2.00\,\mathrm s$. The ideal bob's mass cancels. An initial angle $\theta_0=10^\circ\approx0.1745$ rad makes the small-angle approximation reasonably accurate but not exact. The figure indicates the circular path, angle coordinate, tangential gravity component and radial string tension. Large excursions require the nonlinear sine equation, whose oscillation period exceeds $T_0$.

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. How many degrees of freedom does an ideal planar pendulum have?
2. Find $T_0$ for $\ell=0.25\,\mathrm m$ and $g=9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
3. Why does string tension not enter the angular equation?

### Solutions

1. One degree of freedom, such as $\theta$.
2. $T_0=2\pi\sqrt{0.25/9.81}\approx1.00\,\mathrm s$.
3. Tension is radial and does no virtual work along the allowed tangential displacement.

## Real Joints and Nonholonomic Constraints {#S5}

Not every constraint can be integrated into an equation involving positions alone. Rolling without slipping can introduce velocity-dependent nonholonomic restrictions. When a string breaks, a collision occurs or contact is lost, the set of admissible motions changes, and the original coordinate no longer describes the complete motion. Frictional constraint forces may perform virtual work along allowed displacements and require added generalized forces. An angular coordinate is also periodic: $\theta$ and $\theta+2\pi$ represent the same physical orientation despite different coordinate values. A coordinate choice is useful only within the domain where the underlying mechanical assumptions remain valid.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Generalized coordinates describe admissible motion efficiently. Symmetries of that description lead to conserved quantities.
