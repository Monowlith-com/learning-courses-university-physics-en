# M31-L3 – Molecular dynamics and velocity Verlet {#L3}

**Course area:** M31 – Master: Numerical and Computational Physics

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M31-L1: integrator stability; M13: Hamiltonian mechanics · [Previous lesson](M31-L2.md).

**Learning objective:** Apply a velocity-Verlet update using position, force and acceleration, and assess energy errors correctly.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Molecular dynamics advances many classically modeled particles from forces. A harmonic oscillator is a convenient test case: it has a known analytic solution and makes integration error visible. The time step needs to resolve the fastest relevant oscillations.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

For $m\ddot x=F(x)$, let $a_n=F(x_n)/m$. Velocity Verlet first computes $x_{n+1}=x_n+v_nh+\tfrac12a_nh^2$, then $a_{n+1}=F(x_{n+1})/m$, and finally $v_{n+1}=v_n+\tfrac12(a_n+a_{n+1})h$. For conservative position-dependent forces the method is time reversible and symplectic. It often preserves a nearby modified Hamiltonian better than explicit Euler over long intervals, but the physical energy $H=\tfrac12mv^2+V(x)$ is not exactly constant at every step.

$$
v_{n+1}=v_n+\frac{h}{2}\bigl(a_n+a_{n+1}\bigr).
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For $m=k=1$, $x_0=1$, $v_0=0$ and $h=0.2$, one gets $a_0=-1$, $x_1=0.98$, $a_1=-0.98$ and $v_1=-0.198$. Energy changes from $H_0=0.5$ to $H_1=0.499802$. The figure depicts an idealized nearly closed orbit and a schematic bounded energy deviation.

![Harmonic oscillator phase-space ellipse and small oscillatory energy deviation illustrating symplectic integration.](../../../../media/course/U_PHY_314_EN.svg)

*Velocity Verlet preserves geometric structure without keeping the original energy exactly fixed at each step.*

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find $x_1$ for $x_0=1$, $v_0=0$, $a_0=-1$, $h=0.1$.
2. What physical quantity must be recomputed after the position update?
3. Why does symplectic not mean exact energy conservation?

### Solutions

1. $x_1=0.995$.
2. The updated force or acceleration $a_{n+1}$.
3. Symplecticity preserves a geometric form; the original Hamiltonian may still oscillate slightly.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

Collisions, thermostats, constraints, rough potentials and oversized steps alter the mathematical picture. The drawn orbit is instructional, not a measurement from a molecular simulation. Energy diagnostics should separate drift, oscillation amplitude and step-size convergence.
