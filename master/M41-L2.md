# M41-L2 – Ring Resonators, Free Spectral Range and Quality Factor {#L2}

**Course area:** M41 – Master: Photonics and Ultrafast Optics

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M41-L1; M7: interference and resonance.

**Learning objective:** Calculate resonance spacing and quality factor from round-trip length, group index and linewidth.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

In a ring resonator, a guided mode returns a complex field amplitude after each round trip. Constructive phase accumulation creates discrete resonances. Two observables are distinct: free spectral range (FSR) is the spacing between adjacent resonances, while linewidth describes an individual resonance.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

For round-trip length $L=2\pi R$, the resonance condition is $\beta(\omega_m)L=2\pi m$. Differentiation with respect to $m$ gives the local spacing $\Delta\nu\approx c/(n_gL)$; the **group index** $n_g=c\,d\beta/d\omega$ incorporates dispersion. For small wavelength spacing, $\Delta\lambda\approx\lambda_0^2/(n_gL)$. A narrow isolated resonance has loaded quality factor $Q\approx\lambda_0/\Delta\lambda_{\mathrm{FWHM}}$; coupling affects this value.

$$
\Delta\lambda_{\mathrm{FSR}}\simeq\frac{\lambda_0^2}{n_g\,2\pi R},\qquad Q\simeq\frac{\lambda_0}{\Delta\lambda_{\mathrm{FWHM}}}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For $R=10\,\mathrm{\mu m}$, $n_g=4.0$ and $\lambda_0=1.55\,\mathrm{\mu m}$, $\Delta\lambda_{\mathrm{FSR}}\approx9.56\,\mathrm{nm}$. For a measured FWHM of $0.80\,\mathrm{nm}$, one obtains $Q\approx1550/0.80=1938$. The diagram distinguishes round-trip geometry, resonance spacing and linewidth. These are synthetic values, not vendor loss figures.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What happens to $\Delta\lambda_{\mathrm{FSR}}$ when radius is doubled?
2. Why must FSR calculations include dispersion?
3. What is $Q$ when $\Delta\lambda_{\mathrm{FWHM}}=0.40\,\mathrm{nm}$?

### Solutions

1. With all other parameters fixed, FSR halves to about $4.78\,\mathrm{nm}$.
2. Adjacent-mode spacing is set by the frequency derivative of $\beta$, not the phase index alone.
3. $Q=1550/0.40\approx3875$.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The approximation assumes locally slowly varying group index, spacing small compared with wavelength and a well-isolated line shape. Thermal drift, backscattering, mode splitting and complex coupling complicate actual spectra.
