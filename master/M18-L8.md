# M18-L8 – Laboratory Report, Research Data and Reproducibility {#L8}

**Course area:** M18 – Bachelor: Physics Laboratory Practice

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** M16: error budget; M17: computational reproducibility; M18-L1: measurement plan. [Previous lesson](M18-L7.md).

**Learning objective:** Document reports, raw data, scripts and limitations so that independent checking remains possible.

## Research Question and Model {#S1}

A scientific laboratory report must explain not only the final number but how that claim was obtained. Essential parts include question, model assumptions, apparatus and safety, calibration, raw data, processing, uncertainties, results and discussion. Present decisive reasoning in the body; long tables and complete scripts may be stored in traceable appendices or a linked data package. Separate observation, calculation and interpretation. A hypothesis modified after seeing the data must be identified as post hoc rather than portrayed as the original expectation.

## Equations and Assumptions {#S2}

Preserve raw data without overwriting it and write processed data to new traceably named files. A simple package uses `raw/`, `processed/`, `analysis/` and machine-readable metadata describing units, columns, equipment and calibration references. Record script version and parameter settings. If observations are excluded, record the criterion, timing, affected records and effect on the result. The FAIR principles aim at findability, accessibility under appropriate conditions, interoperability and reusability; they do not require unrestricted publication of every dataset.

## Worked Example and Diagram {#S3}

Example: A report states $x=(12.3\pm0.4)\,\mathrm{mm}$, explicitly calling $0.4\,\mathrm{mm}$ a combined standard uncertainty ($k=1$). This communicates the information more faithfully than `x = 12.327364 mm` when the apparatus does not support those digits. An $x\pm u_x$ interval is not automatically a 95% coverage interval. Where needed, explain distributions, correlations, coverage factors and effective degrees of freedom. A figure needs labelled axes with units, an informative caption and a distinction between measured and simulated points.

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Why should `raw/` and `processed/` be separate?
2. What does $x=(12.3\pm0.4)\,\mathrm{mm}$ with $k=1$ not imply?
3. Give a legitimate reason for restricting data access.

### Solutions

1. To preserve original observations for reprocessing and audit.
2. It does not automatically represent 95% coverage.
3. For example privacy, contractual or intellectual-property rights, or security considerations.

## Limits and Quality Control {#S5}

The DFG lists subject-specific research-data recommendations, but retention periods and access requirements must be checked against the applicable institutional policies. Personal information, contractual rights and safety constraints may justify restricted sharing. A reproducible report therefore explains data access and identifies data that cannot be distributed. Finally verify that each citation supports the statement attached to it. A general laboratory index establishes curricular context but does not validate a separately calculated formula.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}
Scientific auditability is a property of the entire data and reasoning chain, not of a single reported number.
