# M37-L7 – Gravitational Lensing and Einstein-Ring Geometry {#L7}

**Course area:** M37 – Master: Astrophysics and Cosmology

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M29: relativity and light deflection; M37-L6: observing channels · [Previous lesson](M37-L6.md).

**Learning objective:** Calculate the Einstein angle of an idealized point lens and explain the distance definitions.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

A massive foreground object bends light from a background source and may produce distorted multiple images. Perfect axial alignment with an axisymmetric point lens gives an Einstein ring. Its angular scale depends on lens mass and geometry. Real galaxy lenses are extended and may contain shear and other mass components.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

In the thin-lens, weak-field point-mass approximation, the Einstein angle is $\theta_E=\sqrt{(4GM/c^2)\,D_{LS}/(D_LD_S)}$. Here $D_L$ is the observer–lens angular-diameter distance, $D_S$ the observer–source distance and $D_{LS}$ the lens–source angular-diameter distance; they cannot generally be subtracted as Euclidean distances in an expanding cosmology. This relation alone does not reconstruct a galaxy mass distribution.

$$
\theta_E=\sqrt{\frac{4GM}{c^2}\frac{D_{LS}}{D_L D_S}}\qquad\text{(point lens)}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For a hypothetical point mass $M=10^{11}M_\odot$ and assigned values interpreted as angular-diameter distances $D_L=1\,\mathrm{Gpc}$, $D_S=2\,\mathrm{Gpc}$ and $D_{LS}=1\,\mathrm{Gpc}$, one finds $\theta_E\approx3.09\,\mu\mathrm{rad}\approx0.64\,\mathrm{arcsec}$. A realistic cosmology requires all three distances to be obtained consistently from one model. The figure depicts ideal ring formation.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. How does $\theta_E$ scale if the mass quadruples at fixed distances?
2. What happens mathematically for $D_{LS}=0$ in the point-lens model?
3. Why are three arbitrary independent distances not automatically consistent with a cosmological model?

### Solutions

1. The angle doubles.
2. $\theta_E=0$.
3. Angular-diameter distances are linked by spacetime geometry and redshifts.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The point-lens relation assumes weak deflection, a thin lens and ideal alignment. Extended lenses, microlensing, multiple planes and off-axis positions alter the images. Numerical values are a constructed dimensional comparison only.
