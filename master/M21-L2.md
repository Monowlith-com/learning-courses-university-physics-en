# M21-L2 – Gravity, Magnetic Fields and Geophysical Anomalies {#L2}

**Course area:** M21 – Bachelor: Geophysics and Climate

**Level:** Bachelor · Elective

**Prerequisites:** M3: gravitation; M6: magnetic fields; M17: inverse models. [Previous lesson](M21-L1.md).

**Learning objective:** Explain how physical property contrasts generate measured field anomalies.

## Question and Observation {#S1}

Gravity and magnetic field surveys reveal subsurface contrasts even when the rocks themselves cannot be seen. A measured departure from a computed reference field is called an anomaly. Excess mass may generate a positive gravity anomaly; magnetic signatures respond instead to magnetisation and field orientation. Neither instrument provides a unique geological map without additional modelling.

## Physical Model and Equations {#S2}

Newtonian point-mass potential per unit test mass is $\Phi=-Gm/r$ and gravitational acceleration is $\boldsymbol g=-\nabla\Phi$. For a small hidden excess mass $\Delta m$ at depth $h$ below a level survey line, the downward vertical acceleration anomaly in an idealised point-source model is
$$
\Delta g_z(x)=\frac{G\Delta m\,h}{(x^2+h^2)^{3/2}}.
$$
At sufficiently large distances, the magnetic field of a local magnetised body may be approximated by a dipole, whose response also depends on orientation. Measurements combine regional background, target, geometry and instrumental response; practical surveys require reference and elevation corrections.

## Worked Example and Teaching Diagram {#S3}

Set $\Delta m=10^{11}\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $h=1000\,\mathrm m$. Directly over the source, $\Delta g_z(0)=G\Delta m/h^2\approx6.67\cdot10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}\approx0.667\,\mathrm{mGal}$ using $1\,\mathrm{mGal}=10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. ## Self-Assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. How does $\Delta g_z(0)$ change if $h$ doubles while $\Delta m$ stays fixed?
2. Find $\Delta g_z(0)$ in mGal for $\Delta m=5\cdot10^{10}\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $h=1000\,\mathrm m$.
3. Why can different subsurface models yield similar observed anomalies?

### Solutions

1. Because $\Delta g_z(0)\propto h^{-2}$, it becomes one quarter as large.
2. $\Delta g_z(0)\approx0.3335\,\mathrm{mGal}$.
3. Changes in source depth, shape, extent and contrast can compensate each other in the data: the inverse problem is non-unique.

## Validity Limits and Quality {#S5}

The source is treated as a point mass below a planar survey line. Real bodies have extent; Earth curvature, topography and noise matter. Gravity anomalies alone do not determine rock magnetisation.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Field anomalies constrain subsurface contrasts, but do not uniquely identify hidden bodies.
