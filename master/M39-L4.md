# M39-L4 – Nonlinear Dynamics, the Lorenz System and Lyapunov Exponents {#L4}

**Course area:** M39 – Master: Biophysics, Nonequilibrium and Complex Systems

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M39-L3; M31: numerical integration; M19: dynamical systems · [Previous lesson](M39-L3.md).

**Learning objective:** Distinguish local trajectory stretching from global chaos and identify Lorenz equilibria correctly.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Nonlinear equations may amplify small differences in initial conditions. The Lorenz model originated as a drastic simplification of convective flow equations. Its three-dimensional trajectory is a mathematical teaching model, not measured weather. A positive asymptotic maximal Lyapunov exponent signals exponential sensitivity; one short episode of separation alone does not establish chaos.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

The dimensionless model reads $\dot x=\sigma(y-x)$, $\dot y=x(\rho-z)-y$, $\dot z=xy-\beta z$, with positive parameters. At the origin, the linearized $x$–$y$ dynamics form a $2\times2$ matrix. For $\rho>1$ its determinant is $\sigma(1-\rho)<0$, so the origin is unstable. Two further equilibria occur at $(\pm\sqrt{\beta(\rho-1)},\pm\sqrt{\beta(\rho-1)},\rho-1)$. Their stability depends on parameters; their mere existence does not imply chaos.

$$
\dot x=\sigma(y-x),\quad\dot y=x(\rho-z)-y,\quad\dot z=xy-\beta z.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For illustrative $\sigma=10$, $\beta=8/3$, $\rho=28$, the two nonzero equilibria are $(\pm\sqrt{72},\pm\sqrt{72},27)\approx(\pm8.485,\pm8.485,27)$. At the origin, the $x$–$y$ block has determinant $-270<0$ and eigenvalues of opposite sign. The figure sketches two possible projected trajectories and marks the equilibria; no Lyapunov exponent is inferred from this sketch.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What is the third equilibrium coordinate for $\rho=28$?
2. Why is the origin unstable for $\rho>1$?
3. Does temporary growth of a perturbation establish chaos?

### Solutions

1. $z^*=27$.
2. The linear $x$–$y$ block has negative determinant and thus one positive eigenvalue.
3. No. Appropriate long-time dynamical analysis is required.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

Lorenz equations are deterministic; measurement noise, numerical rounding and finite observation windows affect exponent estimates. The plotted phase portrait is a schematic rather than a numerically validated attractor. Chaos and long-term climate properties of real systems cannot be inferred solely from the three equations.
