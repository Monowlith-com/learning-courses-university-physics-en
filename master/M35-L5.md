# M35-L5 – Hubbard Model, Strong Correlations and Superexchange {#L5}

**Course area:** M35 – Master: Advanced Solid-State Physics and Materials Science

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M35-L1: tight binding; M12: spin; M28: many-body statistics.

**Learning objective:** Estimate the antiferromagnetic superexchange scale in a two-site Hubbard model.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

A band model favors delocalized electrons, while local Coulomb repulsion penalizes double occupation. The Hubbard model represents this competition through hopping $t$ and onsite interaction $U$. At half filling and large $U/t$, charge fluctuations become costly. Virtual hopping nevertheless induces a spin exchange interaction that favors a singlet for two localized neighboring spins.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

The elementary Hubbard Hamiltonian is $H=-t\sum_{\langle ij\rangle,\sigma}(c^\dagger_{i\sigma}c_{j\sigma}+\mathrm{h.c.})+U\sum_i n_{i\uparrow}n_{i\downarrow}$. For two sites, two electrons and $U\gg t>0$, second-order perturbation theory gives antiferromagnetic exchange $J=4t^2/U$ in $H_{\mathrm{eff}}=J(\mathbf S_1\cdot\mathbf S_2-1/4)$ within the singly occupied subspace. It is not a universal formula for arbitrary compounds or dopings.

$$
J\simeq\frac{4t^2}{U}\qquad(U\gg t>0).
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

Set $t=0.10\,\mathrm{eV}$ and $U=1.0\,\mathrm{eV}$, so $t/U=0.1$. Then $J\simeq0.040\,\mathrm{eV}$: within this effective two-spin model the singlet lies roughly $J$ below the triplets. The illustration shows the spin sectors and a schematic virtual doubly occupied intermediate state.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Calculate $J$ for $t=0.05\,\mathrm{eV}$ and $U=1\,\mathrm{eV}$.
2. How does $J$ change if $t$ is doubled at fixed $U$?
3. Why is $J=4t^2/U$ not an exact general materials law?

### Solutions

1. $J=0.010\,\mathrm{eV}$.
2. It is multiplied by four.
3. It assumes large U, half filling and a restricted single-band model.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

When $U\sim t$, this perturbative estimate fails. Band structure, geometry, dimensionality, disorder and other exchange paths can favor different magnetic or metallic phases.
