# M23-L2 – Waveguides, Total Internal Reflection and Modes {#L2}

**Course area:** M23 – Bachelor: Applied Physics, Photonics and Nanotechnology

**Level:** Bachelor · Elective

**Prerequisites:** M7: refraction; M10: Maxwell equations. [Previous lesson](M23-L1.md).

**Learning objective:** Calculate acceptance limits for a step-index waveguide and distinguish ray and modal descriptions.

## Question and Observation {#S1}

An optical fibre uses a core whose refractive index exceeds that of its cladding. At sufficiently oblique incidence, light undergoes total internal reflection at the boundary. This ray picture is helpful for multimode propagation, but the discrete patterns of a thin single-mode waveguide arise from Maxwell boundary conditions. The guided field also penetrates evanescently into the cladding.

## Physical Model and Equations {#S2}

For two lossless isotropic media with $n_1>n_2$, Snell's law reads $n_1\sin\theta_1=n_2\sin\theta_2$. The critical angle measured from the interface normal, and the numerical aperture of a step-index fibre in air, are

$$
\theta_c=\arcsin\!\left(\frac{n_2}{n_1}\right),\qquad \mathrm{NA}=\sqrt{n_1^2-n_2^2}\quad(n_0=1).
$$

The external acceptance angle relative to the fibre axis differs from the internal boundary angle measured from the normal. For launch from index $n_0$, the general expression is $n_0\sin\theta_{\max}=\sqrt{n_1^2-n_2^2}$. In a weakly guiding step-index fibre with core radius $a$, the dimensionless V-number $V=(2\pi a/\lambda)\mathrm{NA}$ helps classify modes. For the ideal model, single-mode operation approximately requires $V<2.405$; ray total reflection alone does not establish this limit.

## Worked Example and Teaching Diagram {#S3}

Let $n_1=1.48$ and $n_2=1.46$. Then $\mathrm{NA}=\sqrt{1.48^2-1.46^2}=0.2425$ and the acceptance half-angle in air is about $14.0^\circ$. The internal critical angle from the normal is roughly $80.6^\circ$. The diagram distinguishes these angles and sketches a cladding evanescent field.

## Self-Assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find $\mathrm{NA}$ for $n_1=1.50$ and $n_2=1.48$.
2. If $\mathrm{NA}=0.20$ and $n_0=1$, find the external acceptance half-angle.
3. Why is ray tracing inadequate for deriving the single-mode threshold?

### Solutions

1. $\mathrm{NA}=\sqrt{2.25-2.1904}\approx0.244$.
2. $\theta_{\max}=\arcsin(0.20)\approx11.54^\circ$.
3. Modes depend on field boundary conditions, wavelength and cross-section, not solely on reflection.

## Validity Limits and Quality {#S5}

Ray-model numerical aperture assumes ideal step-index profiles and low loss. Bending, absorption, scattering, polarisation and mode coupling require a full wave treatment. The diagram is schematic and not to scale.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Total internal reflection explains ray guidance; Maxwell boundary conditions explain the set of guided field modes and their dispersion.
