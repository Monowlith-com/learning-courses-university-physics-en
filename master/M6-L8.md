# M6-L8 – Electromagnetic Measurements and Applications {#L8}

**Course area:** M6 – Bachelor: Electricity and Magnetism

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** Lorentz force, electric field, current density and Faraday’s law. [Previous lesson](M6-L7.md).

**Learning objective:** Derive Hall and induction measurements, and assess units, signs and uncertainties.

## Hall Effect: Force Balance {#S1}

Charge carriers drifting in a magnetic field feel a sideways force $q\boldsymbol v_d\times\boldsymbol B$. Accumulated charge creates a transverse electric field $\boldsymbol E_H$. In steady state the electric and magnetic forces balance: $q(\boldsymbol E_H+\boldsymbol v_d\times\boldsymbol B)=0$. For mutually perpendicular current, field and transverse directions, $E_H=v_dB$ in magnitude. If one carrier species of number density $n$ and charge magnitude $|q|$ dominates, current is $I=n|q|v_dA$ through cross-section $A$. In a rectangular sample of thickness $t$ along the magnetic field and transverse width $w$, $A=wt$. Using $U_H=E_Hw$ yields

$$
|U_H|=\frac{IB}{n|q|t}.
$$

Its sign depends on carrier type, contact orientation and the chosen axes; a bare assertion of “positive Hall voltage” without geometry is meaningless.

## Inductive Measurement and Calibration {#S2}

Hall probes use calibration to measure magnetic flux density. At fixed current and known material parameters the simple single-carrier model predicts signal proportional to $B$. An **induction probe** instead uses $\mathcal E=-N\,\mathrm d\Phi_B/\mathrm dt$ and responds to changing linked flux. For a stationary coil in a homogeneous normal field $\mathcal E=-NA\,\mathrm dB/\mathrm dt$; a constant static field does not generate sustained induced emf. The two approaches are therefore complementary. Real instruments require calibrated offset, sensitivity, temperature drift and frequency response. Reports must specify sensor orientation and geometry as well as field magnitude.

## Worked Example: Hall Probe {#S3}

A rectangular sample carries $I=10.0\,\mathrm{mA}$. Its effective thickness along the field is $t=0.50\,\mathrm{mm}$; the assumed carrier density is $n=1.0\times10^{22}\,\mathrm{m^{-3}}$, charge magnitude $e=1.602\times10^{-19}\,\mathrm C$, and $B=0.20\,\mathrm T$. The one-carrier model gives

$$
|U_H|=\frac{0.0100\cdot0.20}{10^{22}\cdot1.602\times10^{-19}\cdot0.00050}\approx2.50\,\mathrm{mV}.
$$

As a separate comparison, a coil of $100$ turns with $A=0.0020\,\mathrm{m^2}$ undergoing $\mathrm dB/\mathrm dt=0.50\,\mathrm{T/s}$ produces $|\mathcal E|=NA|\mathrm dB/\mathrm dt|=0.10\,\mathrm V$. The figure distinguishes Hall contacts on a conductor from flux variation in an induction coil. These are idealised calibration cases, not universal sensor specifications.

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What happens to Hall voltage if $I$ doubles with unchanged $B$, $n$ and $t$ in the linear model?
2. What does a stationary coil output in a time-independent uniform field in the ideal model?
3. Why can reversing $B$ help separate Hall voltage from contact errors?

### Solutions

1. The Hall-voltage magnitude doubles.
2. No sustained induced voltage: $\mathrm d\Phi_B/\mathrm dt=0$.
3. The ideal Hall term changes sign, whereas a field-even parasitic offset does not; half the difference isolates the odd part.

## Measurement Strategy and Limits {#S5}

The simple Hall model fails with multiple carrier species, strong conduction anisotropy or nonlinear magnetic response. Misaligned contacts mix longitudinal voltage into the much smaller transverse signal; measuring both field polarities helps isolate the antisymmetric Hall term: $U_H\approx[U(+B)-U(-B)]/2$, provided parasitic terms are sufficiently symmetric. For $B=|U_H|n|q|t/I$, independent small relative standard uncertainties combine approximately as

$$
\left(\frac{u_B}{B}\right)^2\approx\left(\frac{u_U}{U_H}\right)^2+\left(\frac{u_n}{n}\right)^2+\left(\frac{u_t}{t}\right)^2+\left(\frac{u_I}{I}\right)^2.
$$

Correlations require covariance terms. Offset drift and frequency-dependent effects are not automatically covered by this simple expression.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Hall and induction probes exploit different electromagnetic laws. Interpreting results requires defined geometry, calibration and uncertainty analysis; this prepares more advanced electromagnetic applications.
