# M40-L4 – Absorbed Dose, Energy Deposition and Idealized Survival Models {#L4}

**Course area:** M40 – Master: Advanced Medical Physics and Quality Assurance

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M15: radiation interactions; M24: radiation protection; M40-L3.

**Learning objective:** Apply the definition of absorbed dose and properly delimit an abstract cell-survival model.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

For ionizing radiation, energy deposition in matter is a physically measurable quantity. Dose distribution, calibration, biological response and clinical intention are nevertheless distinct layers of radiation practice. The survival model used here sketches a laboratory response; it is not a treatment-planning rule.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

Absorbed dose $D$ is mean deposited energy $E_\mathrm{dep}$ per mass $m$; $1\,\mathrm{Gy}=1\,\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}}$. A common phenomenological model of surviving fraction after a single exposure is $S(D)=\exp(-\alpha D-\beta D^2)$ with material-dependent parameters in $\mathrm{Gy^{-1}}$ and $\mathrm{Gy^{-2}}$. Parameters vary with cell type and conditions. Dose is not dose rate or a radiation-weighted protection quantity.

$$
D=\frac{E_{\mathrm{dep}}}{m},\qquad S(D)=\exp(-\alpha D-\beta D^2).
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

Illustrative energy deposition $E_\mathrm{dep}=4\,\mathrm{mJ}$ in mass $m=2\,\mathrm g$ corresponds to $D=2\,\mathrm{Gy}$. Choosing *synthetic* cell-model parameters $\alpha=0.20\,\mathrm{Gy^{-1}}$ and $\beta=0.020\,\mathrm{Gy^{-2}}$ gives $S(2\,\mathrm{Gy})=e^{-0.48}\approx0.619$. This says nothing about patients, tumors or treatment doses.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What dose results from $2\,\mathrm{mJ}$ in $2\,\mathrm g$?
2. What does the example model give for $S(0)$?
3. Why must the cell model not be used as a clinical treatment recommendation?

### Solutions

1. $1\,\mathrm{Gy}$.
2. $1$.
3. It omits tissue heterogeneity, repair, fractionation, clinical data and many biological factors.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

Examples are strictly educational. Clinical irradiation requires calibrated instrumentation, independent checks, qualified personnel and applicable procedures; no limits or dose recommendations are derived here.
