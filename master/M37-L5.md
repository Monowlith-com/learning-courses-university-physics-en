# M37-L5 – Dark Matter, Dark Energy and Cosmic Expansion {#L5}

**Course area:** M37 – Master: Astrophysics and Cosmology

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M29: general relativity and FLRW; M37-L4: CMB · [Previous lesson](M37-L4.md).

**Learning objective:** Compute the expansion rate at a selected redshift in a simple flat matter–Lambda model.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Cosmic expansion is described by the Hubble rate $H=\dot a/a$. Standard cosmological models treat baryonic and cold dark matter as matter components with similar mean density scaling, while a cosmological constant has constant energy density. Dark matter and dark energy are distinct model ingredients; the Friedmann equation does not establish their microscopic nature.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

In an FLRW model, the terms in $H(z)^2/H_0^2$ scale according to equation of state: radiation as $(1+z)^4$, nonrelativistic matter as $(1+z)^3$, curvature as $(1+z)^2$ and a cosmological constant as a constant. For an idealized flat late-time model without radiation, $\Omega_{m0}+\Omega_{\Lambda0}=1$ and $H(z)=H_0\sqrt{\Omega_{m0}(1+z)^3+\Omega_{\Lambda0}}$. This is a model-dependent prediction, not a direct measurement of dark components’ microphysics.

$$
\frac{H(z)}{H_0}=\sqrt{\Omega_{m0}(1+z)^3+\Omega_{\Lambda0}}\quad\text{(flat matter--}\Lambda\text{ toy model)}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For specified $\Omega_{m0}=0.3$, $\Omega_{\Lambda0}=0.7$ and $z=1$, one obtains $H/H_0=\sqrt{0.3\cdot8+0.7}=\sqrt{3.1}\approx1.761$. At $z=0$, $H/H_0=1$ by construction. The figure plots relative contributions under the assumed model, not new measurements.

The figure does not show measurements or establish the microphysical nature of dark components.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find $H(2)/H_0$ with the same parameters.
2. Which term dominates at $z\gg1$ in this simplified matter–Lambda model?
3. Why must the approximation not be extrapolated unchanged into the radiation era?

### Solutions

1. $\sqrt{8.8}\approx2.966$.
2. The matter term proportional to $(1+z)^3$.
3. Radiation and the evolving cosmic inventory become important.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

Parameters are illustrative rounded values, not a specific Planck fit. Radiation and curvature were omitted; observational probes can have tensions and systematic uncertainties. A cosmological constant does not represent all models of dark energy.
