# M32-L2 – Lock-in amplification and phase-sensitive detection {#L2}

**Course area:** M32 – Master: Experimental Instrumentation and Research Methods

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M32-L1: noise and statistics; M7: harmonic oscillations · [Previous lesson](M32-L1.md).

**Learning objective:** Decompose a sinusoidal signal into in-phase and quadrature components and recover amplitude and phase.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

A weak periodic signal can be masked by broadband noise. A lock-in amplifier exploits a known reference frequency: multiplication by a phase-related reference followed by low-pass filtering removes high-frequency mixer terms. Bandwidth and time constant determine the trade-off between averaging time and rejection of noise.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

Let $v(t)=A\cos(\omega t+\phi)+n(t)$. Choose references $2\cos(\omega t)$ and $-2\sin(\omega t)$. Averaging over many periods with a suitable low-pass filter yields $X=A\cos\phi$ and $Y=A\sin\phi$ for the coherent part. The factor of two in the reference normalization matters; instruments may report differently scaled quadratures. Amplitude and phase follow as $R=\sqrt{X^2+Y^2}$ and the quadrant-aware $\phi=\operatorname{atan2}(Y,X)$.

$$
R=\sqrt{X^2+Y^2},\qquad\phi=\operatorname{atan2}(Y,X).
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

At $A=50\,\mathrm{\mu V}$ and $\phi=30^\circ$, the quadratures are $X\approx43.30\,\mathrm{\mu V}$ and $Y=25\,\mathrm{\mu V}$. The recovered values are $R=50\,\mathrm{\mu V}$ and $30^\circ$. The diagram shows the two-phase demodulation and its quadrature vector.

*Two orthogonal references provide amplitude and a quadrant-aware phase.*

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find $R$ for $X=3\,\mathrm{mV}$ and $Y=4\,\mathrm{mV}$.
2. What angle does $\operatorname{atan2}(1,-1)$ yield?
3. Why is $\arctan(Y/X)$ inadequate to identify the phase quadrant?

### Solutions

1. $R=5\,\mathrm{mV}$.
2. $135^\circ$.
3. Because the sign of $X$ and hence the quadrant may be lost.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

Frequency drift, reference-phase error, nonstationary noise and insufficient averaging violate the ideal relationships. Low-pass filters reduce effective noise bandwidth but can distort rapidly varying signals.
