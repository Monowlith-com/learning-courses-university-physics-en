# M32-L3 – Vacuum systems, cryostats and thermal loads {#L3}

**Course area:** M32 – Master: Experimental Instrumentation and Research Methods

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M5: thermodynamics; M32-L2: measurement chains · [Previous lesson](M32-L2.md).

**Learning objective:** Calculate an idealized pump-down time and distinguish main heat-load pathways in a cryostat.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Surface, beam and superconducting-sensor experiments often require tight control of residual gas and heat transport. Pumps, piping conductance, seals and material outgassing determine attainable pressure. A cryostat also has radiation shields, supports and electrical leads, all potential heat-load paths.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

For constant chamber volume $V$, effective pumping speed $S$ and gas load $Q$ (pressure times volume per time), the balance is $V\,\mathrm dp/\mathrm dt=-Sp+Q$. Its solution reads $p(t)=Q/S+[p(0)-Q/S]e^{-St/V}$. The ideal pressure time constant is $\tau=V/S$, and the steady limiting pressure is $p_\infty=Q/S$. Cryostat cooling additionally faces conduction, radiation and residual-gas heat transfer, which are outside this pressure-only equation.

$$
p(t)=p_\infty+[p(0)-p_\infty]e^{-t/\tau},\quad\tau=V/S,\quad p_\infty=Q/S.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

A model chamber has $V=40\,\mathrm L$ and $S=10\,\mathrm{L/s}$, so $\tau=4\,\mathrm s$. With $Q=0$, pressure decreases from $10\,\mathrm{Pa}$ to $10/e\approx3.68\,\mathrm{Pa}$ in one time constant. A nonzero gas load $Q=0.02\,\mathrm{Pa\,L/s}$ instead implies $p_\infty=0.002\,\mathrm{Pa}$ in the same model. This ideal curve is not a performance prediction for any particular pump.

![Ideal chamber pump-down curve with time constant tau and schematic chamber-to-pump connection.](../../../../media/course/U_PHY_307_EN.svg)

*Gas load and effective pumping speed set the ideal pressure evolution and limiting pressure.*

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find $\tau$ for $V=60\,\mathrm L$ and $S=5\,\mathrm{L/s}$.
2. Find $p_\infty$ at $Q=0.04\,\mathrm{Pa\,L/s}$ and $S=20\,\mathrm{L/s}$.
3. Name two paths by which heat enters a cryostat.

### Solutions

1. $\tau=12\,\mathrm s$.
2. $p_\infty=0.002\,\mathrm{Pa}$.
3. For example, conduction through supports and radiation from warm surfaces.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

Real pumping speeds and conductances depend on pressure regime and gas species; outgassing and leaks may dominate at low pressures. Cryogenic operating temperature does not follow from the pump-down model. Practical safety procedures, quench protection and materials selection are essential.
