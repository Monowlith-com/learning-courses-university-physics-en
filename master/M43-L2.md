# M43-L2 – Measurement Chains, Calibration and Systematic Deviations {#L2}

**Course area:** M43 – Master: Model Assessment and Scientific Research Practice

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M32: instrumentation and calibration; M43-L1: model predictions.

**Learning objective:** Invert a linear sensor calibration and separate the impact of an offset.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Between a physical quantity and a reported number lie the sensor, electronics, digitization, calibration and data processing. The measurement chain affects the result even when readings scatter very little. A stable unknown offset can produce high precision but poor trueness. Calibration references and operating conditions therefore require documentation.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

A linear sensor is described by $U=aT+b$. With known slope $a\ne0$ and offset $b$, inversion gives $T=(U-b)/a$. If $b$ is uncertain, its contribution to standard uncertainty follows from $\partial T/\partial b=-1/a$ in the linear approximation. A full uncertainty budget also includes signal uncertainty, covariances and slope uncertainty.

$$
T=\frac{U-b}{a},\qquad \frac{\partial T}{\partial b}=-\frac1a.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

A **synthetic** sensor has $a=2\,\mathrm{mV/K}$ and $b=0.5\,\mathrm{mV}$. For $U=10.5\,\mathrm{mV}$, the inferred value is $T=5\,\mathrm K$. Ignoring the offset would incorrectly yield $5.25\,\mathrm K$. Points in the chart are generated from the calibration model, not measured on an actual instrument.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What $T$ follows from $U=6.5\,\mathrm{mV}$?
2. What is the bias if $b$ is ignored?
3. What is $|\partial T/\partial b|$ in $\mathrm{K/mV}$?

### Solutions

1. $T=(6.5-0.5)/2=3\,\mathrm K$.
2. $5.25-5=0.25\,\mathrm K$.
3. $1/2=0.5\,\mathrm{K/mV}$.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

Linear calibration is valid only over its verified range and sufficiently stable conditions. Hysteresis, ageing, nonlinearity and uncertain reference values require further tests. A numerically subtracted offset is not automatically known exactly.
