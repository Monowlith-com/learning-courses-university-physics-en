# M27-L2 – Adiabatic theorem and geometric Berry phase {#L2}

**Course area:** M27 – Master: Advanced quantum theory

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M12: eigenstates and time evolution; M26: differential geometry. [Previous lesson](M27-L1.md).

**Learning objective:** Separate dynamic and geometric phases and calculate the Berry phase for spin transport around a closed loop.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

A quantum system can approximately remain in an instantaneous eigenstate when external parameters vary sufficiently slowly. It nevertheless acquires not only a dynamical phase from its energy but also a phase from the path taken through parameter space.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

For a nondegenerate eigenstate $|n(\lambda(t))\rangle$ separated by a finite gap, the adiabatic approximation gives two phases. The dynamic part integrates $E_n(t)$ over time. The geometric part is the path integral of the Berry connection $\mathcal A_n=i\langle n|\nabla_{\lambda}n\rangle$. A local gauge transformation changes the connection, but the phase around a closed loop changes only by an integer multiple of $2\pi$. For spin $1/2$ transported by a slowly rotating magnetic field, the geometric phase of the aligned state equals minus half the oriented solid angle.

$$
\gamma_n=\oint_C i\langle n|\nabla_{\lambda}n\rangle\cdot\mathrm d\lambda,\qquad \gamma_{+}=-\frac{\Omega_{\mathrm{solid}}}{2}\pmod{2\pi}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

The magnetic field direction traces a cone of polar angle $\theta=60^\circ$. After one azimuthal cycle, the tip encloses $\Omega_{\mathrm{solid}}=2\pi(1-\cos\theta)=\pi$. Hence $\gamma_+=-\pi/2$ modulo $2\pi$. The dynamic phase additionally depends on travel time and energy. The figure projects the path and enclosed surface on the unit sphere.

![Closed parameter-space path of a spin on a sphere and the enclosed solid angle.](../../../../media/course/U_PHY_003_EN.svg)

*The Berry phase of a spin is proportional to the oriented solid angle.*

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What condition underlies the simple nondegenerate adiabatic approximation?
2. Calculate $\gamma_+$ for a closed circle at $\theta=90^\circ$.
3. Is the Berry connection itself gauge invariant?

### Solutions

1. Slow changes relative to energy-gap timescales, without a relevant closing of the gap.
2. $\Omega_{\mathrm{solid}}=2\pi$ and $\gamma_+=-\pi$ modulo $2\pi$.
3. No; the closed Berry phase is gauge invariant modulo $2\pi$.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

Degeneracies can require non-Abelian holonomies. Adiabaticity can fail near avoided crossings. The sign of the spin phase assumes an explicitly oriented path and an eigenstate convention.
