# M21-L5 – Atmospheric Circulation and the Coriolis Effect {#L5}

**Course area:** M21 – Bachelor: Geophysics and Climate

**Level:** Bachelor · Elective

**Prerequisites:** M3: rotating frames; M4: fluid motion; M21-L3. [Previous lesson](M21-L4.md).

**Learning objective:** Explain how planetary rotation affects large-scale airflow and interpret a simplified Hadley cell.

## Question and Observation {#S1}

Unequal solar heating creates differences in temperature and pressure across latitudes. In a schematic tropical circulation, warm moist air rises near the equator and sinks farther poleward in an idealised subtropical region. Earth rotation deflects horizontally moving air relative to the rotating surface. This does not explain every local cloud, but frames major circulation patterns.

## Physical Model and Equations {#S2}

In Earth’s rotating frame, Coriolis acceleration is $\boldsymbol a_C=-2\boldsymbol\Omega\times\boldsymbol v$. For horizontal motion at latitude $\varphi$, the local Coriolis parameter is $f=2\Omega\sin\varphi$. Away from the equator, on sufficiently large spatial and temporal scales, an approximate geostrophic balance is
$$
f\boldsymbol k\times\boldsymbol v_g=-\frac{1}{\rho}\nabla_h p.
$$
The Coriolis force does no kinetic work because it is perpendicular to velocity. Hadley circulation reflects heating, convection and angular-momentum transport; a simple two-dimensional picture omits seasonal shifts and continents.

## Worked Example and Teaching Diagram {#S3}

At $\varphi=45^\circ$ and $\Omega=7.292\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$, $f\approx1.03\cdot10^{-4}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$. A horizontal speed of $10\,\mathrm{m/s}$ then corresponds to a Coriolis acceleration of about $1.03\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. The diagram shows a qualitative overturning circulation and distinguishes rising air, upper transport, descent and surface return.

## Self-Assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find $f$ at $\varphi=0^\circ$.
2. Calculate $|a_C|$ for $f=10^{-4}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$ and $v=20\,\mathrm{m/s}$.
3. Why may geostrophic balance fail for a small, short-lived vortex?

### Solutions

1. $f=2\Omega\sin0^\circ=0$.
2. $|a_C|=fv=2\cdot10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$.
3. Acceleration, friction and convection may dominate over the Coriolis force on small or fast scales.

## Validity Limits and Quality {#S5}

The diagram is neither an annual average of the real ITCZ nor a forecast. Geostrophic balance neglects friction and acceleration; it fails near the equator as $f\rightarrow0$.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Large-scale winds connect thermal forcing with planetary rotation; circulation cells are useful but simplified models.
