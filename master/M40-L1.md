# M40-L1 – CT Projection Data, the Radon Transform and Reconstruction Limits {#L1}

**Course area:** M40 – Master: Advanced Medical Physics and Quality Assurance

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M24: imaging methods; M31: inverse problems; M39 · [Previous lesson](M39-L7.md).

**Learning objective:** Interpret logarithmic CT projections as line integrals and explain principal limits of backprojection.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Computed tomography does not directly measure finished slices. The detector acquires projections at many angles; an inverse algorithm constructs a spatial map of attenuation coefficients. A sinogram organizes detector position and view angle; it is not itself an anatomical image.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

For an ideal monoenergetic, scatter-free ray, $I/I_0=\exp(-\int_\ell\mu(\mathbf r)\,d\ell)$. Taking the negative logarithm linearizes the path integral and provides a sample of the Radon transform. Filtered backprojection boosts high spatial frequencies before backprojection; noise, finite detector resolution, beam hardening and missing views limit the result. The simple logarithmic model is only approximate for polychromatic x-ray spectra.

$$
p_\theta(s)=-\ln\!\left(\frac{I_\theta(s)}{I_0(s)}\right)=\int_{\ell(\theta,s)}\mu(\mathbf r)\,d\ell.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

An illustrative ray crosses $2\,\mathrm{cm}$ of material with $\mu_1=0.10\,\mathrm{cm^{-1}}$ and another $1\,\mathrm{cm}$ with $\mu_2=0.20\,\mathrm{cm^{-1}}$. The projection is $p=0.40$, giving $I/I_0=e^{-0.4}\approx0.6703$. The figure shows a ray crossing two regions and a schematic angular acquisition.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What is $p$ if the first path length doubles?
2. What is $I/I_0$ for $p=0.7$?
3. Why can missing projection angles create direction-dependent artifacts?

### Solutions

1. $p=0.60$.
2. $e^{-0.7}\approx0.4966$.
3. Some spatial-frequency information is unmeasured, making the inversion underdetermined.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The calculation produces one projection measurement, not a full CT image or dose estimate. Conceptual filtering does not replace spectral detector, scatter or calibration corrections.
