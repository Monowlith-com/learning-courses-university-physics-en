# M32-L4 – Precision spectroscopy and spectral linewidths {#L4}

**Course area:** M32 – Master: Experimental Instrumentation and Research Methods

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M7: optics; M11: atomic physics; M32-L3: laboratory conditions · [Previous lesson](M32-L3.md).

**Learning objective:** Distinguish Gaussian and Lorentzian line shapes and determine the full width at half maximum (FWHM).

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Spectral features have finite width. Thermal velocity distributions in many gases lead to Gaussian Doppler broadening, while lifetime and collision processes can yield Lorentzian contributions in suitable approximations. Real measurements can exhibit Voigt-type mixtures convolved with instrumental response.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

A Gaussian centered on $\nu_0$ with FWHM $\Delta\nu$ may be written as $I(\nu)=I_0\exp[-4\ln 2\,((\nu-\nu_0)/\Delta\nu)^2]$. Half maximum occurs at $|\nu-\nu_0|=\Delta\nu/2$. A normalized Lorentzian with the same FWHM is $I_L/I_0=[1+4((\nu-\nu_0)/\Delta\nu)^2]^{-1}$. At equal FWHM the Lorentzian has heavier wings. Independent Gaussian broadenings combine by adding squared FWHMs; this does not apply directly to arbitrary line shapes.

$$
\frac{I(\nu)}{I_0}=\exp\!\left[-4\ln2\left(\frac{\nu-\nu_0}{\Delta\nu}\right)^2\right].
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For a Gaussian line centered on $500\,\mathrm{THz}$ with $\Delta\nu=2\,\mathrm{GHz}$, the half-maximum points occur at $\nu_0\pm1\,\mathrm{GHz}$. The spectrum is a model; frequency calibration, detector linearity and the instrument resolution function require separate evaluation.

![Comparison of normalized Gaussian and Lorentzian profiles at equal full width at half maximum.](../../../../media/course/U_PHY_267_EN.svg)

*Equal FWHM does not imply identical line shape or tail intensity.*

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. At what separation from $\nu_0$ is $I/I_0=1/2$ for $\Delta\nu=6\,\mathrm{MHz}$?
2. How do the Gaussian and Lorentzian wings differ at equal FWHM?
3. Find the combined Gaussian FWHM for widths of $3$ and $4\,\mathrm{GHz}$.

### Solutions

1. At $|\nu-\nu_0|=3\,\mathrm{MHz}$.
2. The Lorentzian has more slowly decaying wings.
3. $\sqrt{3^2+4^2}=5\,\mathrm{GHz}$.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The Gaussian model applies only to suitable broadening mechanisms. Saturation, frequency jitter, nonlinear detection and limited spectral resolution can bias inferred shapes. The NIST source discusses line profiles; the earlier Cornell lecture index supports only the topic placement.
