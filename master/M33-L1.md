# M33-L1 – Atomic Fine Structure and Angular-Momentum Coupling {#L1}

**Course area:** M33 – Master: Atomic, Molecular and Optical Physics

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M12: quantum numbers and angular momenta; M23: atomic spectroscopy; M32-L4: line shapes.

**Learning objective:** Compute L·S eigenvalues for a p electron with spin 1/2 and understand the limits of that model.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Why do apparently degenerate atomic levels generate multiple spectral lines? An electron’s orbital angular momentum couples to its spin. In a simple one-electron model, quantum numbers l, s, and j label the total angular momentum J=L+S. Different allowed values of j then acquire different energies even at fixed n and l.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

A simple effective spin–orbit Hamiltonian is $H_{\mathrm{so}}=\lambda\,\mathbf L\cdot\mathbf S/\hbar^2$. From $\mathbf J^2=(\mathbf L+\mathbf S)^2$, it follows that $\mathbf L\cdot\mathbf S=[\mathbf J^2-\mathbf L^2-\mathbf S^2]/2$. On $|l,s,j,m_j\rangle$, its dimensionless eigenvalue is $[j(j+1)-l(l+1)-s(s+1)]/2$. The size and sign of $\lambda$ depend on the atom and level; it is an effective energy parameter here.

$$
\Delta E_j=\frac{\lambda}{2}\left[j(j+1)-l(l+1)-s(s+1)\right].
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For a p electron, $l=1$, $s=1/2$, and $j\in\{1/2,3/2\}$. Taking $\lambda=2\,\mathrm{meV}$ gives $\Delta E_{3/2}=+1\,\mathrm{meV}$ and $\Delta E_{1/2}=-2\,\mathrm{meV}$, a modeled separation of $3\,\mathrm{meV}$. The figure sketches these shifted levels; it is not a scale drawing for a specific element.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Which values of $j$ are possible for $l=2$ and $s=1/2$?
2. Find $\Delta E_{1/2}$ for $l=1$, $s=1/2$, and $\lambda=4\,\mathrm{meV}$.
3. Why should the sketch not be transferred directly to complex multielectron atoms?

### Solutions

1. $j=3/2$ and $5/2$.
2. $\Delta E_{1/2}=-\lambda=-4\,\mathrm{meV}$.
3. Electron correlation, alternative coupling regimes, and field-dependent interactions can matter.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

A constant λ replaces a level-dependent microscopic interaction in this teaching model. Hyperfine structure, Zeeman effects, relativistic corrections beyond the model, and line-strength selection rules are omitted. NIST supports the quantum-number conventions, not the fabricated parameter.
