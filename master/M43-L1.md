# M43-L1 – Research Questions, Hypotheses and Testable Model Predictions {#L1}

**Course area:** M43 – Master: Model Assessment and Scientific Research Practice

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M31: numerical modelling; M39: relaxation processes; M42-L6: research documentation. [Previous module](M42-L6.md).

**Learning objective:** Turn a physical assumption into a quantitatively specified, potentially refutable prediction.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

A research question specifies a measurand, physical system, manipulated variable and relevant timescale. A testable hypothesis does not merely predict that a value decreases: it specifies a model and an observable consequence. **Falsifiability** does not guarantee truth; discrepancies may result from measurement error, incorrect initial conditions or model inadequacy. Alternative models and decision criteria should be documented beforehand.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

For linear relaxation towards a fixed equilibrium $y_\infty$, $dy/dt=-(y-y_\infty)/\tau$. Separation of variables yields $y(t)=y_\infty+(y_0-y_\infty)e^{-t/\tau}$. The time constant $\tau$ is the time after which the initial departure from equilibrium has fallen to $e^{-1}$; it is not the half-life $\tau\ln 2$. This model assumes constant parameters and one dominant relaxation timescale.

$$
y(t)=y_\infty+(y_0-y_\infty)e^{-t/\tau},\qquad t_{1/2}=\tau\ln2.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For a **synthetic** relaxation experiment, take $y_0=10$, $y_\infty=2$ and $\tau=3\,\mathrm{s}$. At $t=3\,\mathrm{s}$ the prediction is $y=2+8/e\approx4.943$. An independent observation could test this numerical prediction. The observation window, sensor precision and criteria for notable discrepancies should be specified before collecting data. The figure shows the model only.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What is $y(0)$?
2. Find the half-life for $\tau=3\,\mathrm{s}$.
3. Why does a single discrepant observation not immediately refute every version of the model?

### Solutions

1. $y(0)=2+8=10$.
2. $t_{1/2}=3\ln2\approx2.079\,\mathrm{s}$.
3. Measurement uncertainty, parameter errors and model alternatives must be examined separately.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

Multiple timescales, temperature-dependent rates, drift and perturbations can violate the single-exponential approximation. Fitting the same data does not constitute an independent prediction; agreement does not establish a unique physical mechanism.
