# M24-L1 – X-Ray Attenuation, Image Contrast and Absorbed Dose {#L1}

**Course area:** M24 – Bachelor: Medical Physics and Imaging

**Level:** Bachelor · Elective

**Prerequisites:** M6: electromagnetic fields; M7: optics; M15: interactions of radiation with matter. [Previous module: M23-L7](M23-L7.md).

**Learning objective:** Derive a basic transmission model, calculate half-value thickness and distinguish absorbed dose from radiation-protection quantities.

## Question and Observation {#S1}

Why do bones often stand out from soft tissue in radiographs? An X-ray source illuminates the body and a detector records the transmitted intensity. Different materials attenuate photons by different amounts. The detector signal is an intensity measurement, not a direct map of biological effects. Photoelectric absorption and scattering can both remove photons from the detected beam; scattered photons may also distort image contrast.

## Physical Model and Equations {#S2}

For a narrow, monoenergetic and scatter-free beam, the probability of interaction in a thin slab $dx$ is proportional to the remaining intensity. Integrating $dI=-\mu I\,dx$ from $0$ to $x$ gives exponential attenuation. Half-value thickness $x_{1/2}$ is the distance at which $I=I_0/2$. Absorbed dose $D$, by contrast, is absorbed energy $E_{\mathrm{abs}}$ per mass $m$, with $1\,\mathrm{Gy}=1\,\mathrm{J/kg}$.

$$
I(x)=I_0e^{-\mu x},\qquad x_{1/2}=\frac{\ln 2}{\mu},\qquad D=\frac{E_{\mathrm{abs}}}{m}.
$$

For several homogeneous layers, $I/I_0=\exp(-\sum_j\mu_j x_j)$; a spatially varying medium requires a line integral. Diagnostic X-ray beams are generally polychromatic, so energy-dependent attenuation produces beam hardening. Absorbed dose (Gy) and effective dose (Sv) are distinct; one detector intensity cannot yield an individual's biological risk. Image quality also depends on photon statistics, scatter, detector characteristics and processing.

## Worked Example and Teaching Diagram {#S3}

Take $\mu=0.20\,\mathrm{cm^{-1}}$ and $x=5.0\,\mathrm{cm}$. Then $\mu x=1$ and $I/I_0=e^{-1}\approx0.368$. The half-value thickness is $x_{1/2}=\ln2/0.20\approx3.47\,\mathrm{cm}$. If a hypothetical $0.020\,\mathrm J$ is uniformly deposited in $2.0\,\mathrm{kg}$, $D=0.010\,\mathrm{Gy}$. The drawing is purely an invented material model.

## Self-Assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What is $I/I_0$ when $\mu x=\ln2$?
2. Find $x_{1/2}$ for $\mu=0.10\,\mathrm{cm^{-1}}$.
3. Why is an absorbed dose of $1\,\mathrm{Gy}$ not the same physical quantity as $1\,\mathrm{Sv}$?

### Solutions

1. $I/I_0=1/2$.
2. $x_{1/2}=0.693/0.10\approx6.93\,\mathrm{cm}$.
3. Gray measures deposited energy per mass; protective quantities in sieverts involve different definitions and weightings and cannot be read directly from Gy.

## Validity Limits and Quality {#S5}

The model neglects beam spectrum, multiple scattering and geometry. All parameters are invented; they do not prescribe any clinical exposure. Medical use requires validated dosimetry, justification and optimisation.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

X-ray attenuation depends on both the ray path and material properties; absorbed dose is a separate energy-per-mass quantity.
