# M30-L1 – Classical fields and the Klein–Gordon equation {#L1}

**Course area:** M30 – Master: Quantum field theory and the Standard Model

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M10: special relativity; M13: variational principle; M25: quantum mechanics · [Previous lesson](M29-L7.md).

**Learning objective:** Derive the equation of motion of a real scalar field from a Lagrangian density and evaluate its relativistic dispersion relation.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

A field assigns a physical quantity to every spacetime point. Unlike the trajectory of one particle, field theory describes continuously distributed degrees of freedom. In a relativistic model, space and time derivatives enter one Lorentz-invariant action. A free real scalar field provides the simplest example.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

We use natural units $\hbar=c=1$ and signature $\eta_{\mu\nu}=\mathrm{diag}(+1,-1,-1,-1)$, an explicit convention change from M29. For $\mathcal L=\frac12\partial_\mu\phi\,\partial^\mu\phi-\frac12m^2\phi^2$, the Euler–Lagrange equation is $\partial_\mu(\partial\mathcal L/\partial(\partial_\mu\phi))-\partial\mathcal L/\partial\phi=0$. Substitution yields $(\Box+m^2)\phi=0$ with $\Box=\partial_t^2-\nabla^2$. A plane wave $\phi\propto e^{-i\omega t+i\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf x}$ obeys $-\omega^2+|\mathbf k|^2+m^2=0$.

$$
\omega^2=|\mathbf k|^2+m^2\qquad(\hbar=c=1).
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For $m=2$ and $|\mathbf k|=3$, we obtain $\omega=\sqrt{13}\approx3.606$. The two plotted curves show $\omega=\sqrt{k^2+m^2}$ for different masses. At $k=0$, the rest frequency remains $\omega=m$, whereas large momenta lead to approximately linear growth.

![Dispersion relations for two free scalar fields; the more massive field starts at a higher rest frequency.](../../../../media/course/U_PHY_255_EN.svg)

*Field mass changes the minimum frequency of a free relativistic mode.*

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What equation results when $m=0$?
2. Find $\omega$ for $m=4$, $k=3$.
3. What assumption is made by omitting interaction terms from $\mathcal L$?

### Solutions

1. $\omega=|k|$ for the positive-frequency branch.
2. $\omega=5$ in natural units.
3. The scalar field is free and has no modeled self-scattering.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The classical field equation is not yet a quantum theory. The curves represent free plane waves rather than measured particle spectra. Natural units and metric signature must remain consistent across formulas.
