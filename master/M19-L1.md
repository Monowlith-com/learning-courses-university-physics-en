# M19-L1 – Measurement Design, Blank Values and Uncertainty Budgets {#L1}

**Course area:** M19 – Bachelor: Advanced Experimental Methods and Research Practice

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** M16: uncertainty; M18: experimental planning and counting statistics. [Previous lesson](M18-L8.md).

**Learning objective:** Build an uncertainty budget for a difference measurement, account for correlation and identify its limits.

## Question and Model {#S1}

A demanding measurement begins with a measurement model rather than an instrument. A sample reading often requires subtracting a blank: $y=s-b$. Both the signal estimate $s$ and the background estimate $b$ require units, acquisition duration, uncertainty information and a traceable record. A blank is an experimental control; blinded analysis instead hides group identity until the analysis is frozen. These two meanings must not be conflated. Temperature drift, offset errors and common calibration can introduce correlations.

## Equations and Assumptions {#S2}

For standard uncertainties $u_s$ and $u_b$, linear propagation gives
$$
u_y^2=u_s^2+u_b^2-2\,\operatorname{cov}(s,b).
$$
If the readings are independent, the covariance vanishes. Unknown dependence requires a documented assumption rather than silently treating the terms as independent. Repeats inform random variability, but they cannot exclude calibration bias. Report a result as $y\pm u_y$ together with the uncertainty definition and units; extra decimal digits are not evidence of accuracy.

## Worked Example and Diagram {#S3}

In an illustrative experiment, $n_s=180$ and $n_b=40$ counts are observed in equal time intervals. Independent Poisson counts yield $n=140$ net events with estimated standard deviation $u_n=\sqrt{180+40}\approx14.83$ events. The net-count signal-to-noise ratio is about $9.44$. If blank acquisition lasted twice as long, its count must first be scaled to the sample interval and its variance multiplied by the square of that scaling factor. The diagram distinguishes total signal, blank and net estimate.

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What does covariance represent when $y=s-b$?
2. Find the net count and standard uncertainty for independent totals 180 and 40.
3. Why can ten repeated readings not eliminate a constant calibration bias?

### Solutions

1. It captures shared variation between $s$ and $b$; positive covariance reduces the variance of their difference.
2. $n=140$, $u_n=\sqrt{220}\approx14.83$ events.
3. The fixed offset remains in every repeat; an independent calibration check is required.

## Limits and Quality Control {#S5}

The Poisson assumption requires effectively independent arrivals and a sufficiently stable rate during acquisition. Dead time, drift or clustered arrivals require a different model. State exclusion rules, repeats and calibration controls before inspecting the result. A highly precise estimate can still be biased. The illustration is a teaching diagram with synthetic counts.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

A defensible measurement model connects corrections, covariance, controls and declared assumptions.
