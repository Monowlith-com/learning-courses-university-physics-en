# M36-L1 – Standard Model, Gauge Groups and Electric Charge {#L1}

**Course area:** M36 – Master: Particle Physics, Collision Experiments and Accelerators

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M30: quantum field theory; M34: quantum states.

**Learning objective:** Compute the electric charges of a left-chiral lepton doublet using a specified hypercharge convention.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

The Standard Model arranges matter fields and their interactions in a gauge structure. The strong interaction uses SU(3), while electroweak theory uses SU(2) × U(1). Electroweak symmetry breaking produces massive W and Z bosons and a massless photon; Higgs couplings are relevant for fermion masses. A gauge group is not a table of measured particle masses.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

Adopt the convention $Q=T_3+Y/2$ for electric charge in units of $e>0$. For a left-chiral lepton doublet $(\nu_L,e_L)$, $Y=-1$, while $T_3$ is $+1/2$ for the upper and $-1/2$ for the lower entry. Thus $Q(\nu_L)=0$ and $Q(e_L)=-1$. Some references define hypercharge without a factor of two; the physical charge does not change.

$$
Q=T_3+\frac{Y}{2}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For the doublet $L=(\nu_L,e_L)^T$ with $Y=-1$, the charges are $+1/2-1/2=0$ and $-1/2-1/2=-1$. The original diagram separates the two components and their contributions. This is a diagram of quantum numbers rather than a measured energy spectrum.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find $Q$ for the upper component ($T_3=+1/2$) of a left-handed quark doublet with $Y=1/3$.
2. Find $Q$ for its lower component.
3. Why must $Y=-1$ here not be combined with the different convention $Q=T_3+Y$?

### Solutions

1. $Q=+2/3$.
2. $Q=-1/3$.
3. That mixes normalizations; all quantum numbers must use the same convention.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The calculation concerns weak-isospin states in a fixed convention. It does not determine flavour mixing or numerical couplings. Right-handed lepton singlets have different SU(2) quantum numbers.
