# M33-L4 – Photon Statistics: Fock States, Coherent Light and Correlations {#L4}

**Course area:** M33 – Master: Atomic, Molecular and Optical Physics

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M13: probability distributions; M26: density operator; M33-L2.

**Learning objective:** Calculate Poisson photon-number probabilities and distinguish g(2)(0) for Fock and coherent states.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

A single quantized optical mode has discrete photon-number states |n⟩. A coherent state |α⟩ is a superposition of number states with a Poisson distribution and mean photon number n̄=|α|². Intensity fluctuations can distinguish coherent light, ideal single-photon light, and thermal light.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

For a coherent state the probability of $n$ photons is $P_n=e^{-\bar n}\bar n^n/n!$. Consequently the mean and variance both equal $\bar n$. The normalized zero-delay second-order correlation for one mode is $g^{(2)}(0)=\langle\hat n(\hat n-1)\rangle/\langle\hat n\rangle^2$. It equals one for ideal coherent states. For a Fock state $|N\rangle$ with $N>0$, it is $1-1/N$, hence zero for $|1\rangle$. The normalization is undefined for vacuum with $\langle n\rangle=0$.

$$
P_n=e^{-\bar n}\frac{\bar n^n}{n!},\qquad g^{(2)}(0)=\frac{\langle\hat n(\hat n-1)\rangle}{\langle\hat n\rangle^2}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For $\bar n=4$ we obtain $P_0=e^{-4}\approx0.0183$ and $P_1=4e^{-4}\approx0.0733$. The photon-number standard deviation equals $\sqrt4=2$. The graphic compares the Poisson distribution to an ideal single-photon state fixed at $n=1$. It depicts quantum-state statistics, not detector efficiency.

*Coherent light has Poisson photon statistics; an ideal single-photon state has fixed photon number.*

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Calculate $P_0$ for $\bar n=1$.
2. What is $g^{(2)}(0)$ for the ideal Fock state $|2\rangle$?
3. Why does $g^{(2)}(0)=1$ not alone imply a perfect laser experiment?

### Solutions

1. $e^{-1}\approx0.368$.
2. $1-1/2=0.5$.
3. Real measurements depend on modes, background, time resolution, and losses; the number does not specify the entire field.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

These expressions assume a well-defined ideal single mode and quantum state. Multimode fields, losses, detector noise, and time binning modify measured correlations. The Cornell reference is a lecture index; a separate quantum-optics chapter supports the formulas.
