# M22-L2 – Molecular Binding, Chemical Potential and Cooperativity {#L2}

**Course area:** M22 – Bachelor: Biophysics

**Level:** Bachelor · Elective

**Prerequisites:** M5: free energy; M13: partition function; M22-L1: thermal fluctuations. [Previous lesson](M22-L1.md).

**Learning objective:** Derive a binding isotherm, evaluate a dissociation constant and interpret cooperativity cautiously.

## Question and Observation {#S1}

A ligand may attach to a protein and subsequently detach. When the free ligand concentration is held fixed, reversible binding reaches a dynamic equilibrium in which binding and unbinding events balance statistically. A high occupancy is not evidence that a given ligand remains bound indefinitely.

## Physical Model and Equations {#S2}

For independent identical sites in the simple two-state reaction $P+L\rightleftharpoons PL$ with dilute ideal species, $K_d=[P][L]/[PL]$. Here $c=[L]$ is the free, rather than the total, ligand concentration. The occupied fraction $\theta=[PL]/([P]+[PL])$ is

$$
\theta=\frac{c}{K_d+c},\qquad K_d=\frac{k_{\mathrm{off}}}{k_{\mathrm{on}}}.
$$

For the simplest mass-action kinetic scheme, $K_d=k_{\mathrm{off}}/k_{\mathrm{on}}$ carries concentration units. The expression $\Delta G^\circ=-RT\ln K^\circ$ requires a dimensionless standard-state equilibrium constant $K^\circ$. A steeper empirical curve is sometimes described by $\theta=c^{n_H}/(K_{1/2}^{n_H}+c^{n_H})$. A Hill coefficient above one signals apparent cooperativity but does not by itself determine the exact number of sites.

## Worked Example and Teaching Diagram {#S3}

Take $K_d=2.0\,\mathrm{\mu mol/L}$ and free concentration $c=4.0\,\mathrm{\mu mol/L}$. The occupancy is $\theta=4/(2+4)=2/3\approx0.667$. When $c=K_d$, half of the independent sites are occupied. The graphic compares the two-state curve with a steeper empirical binding curve.

## Self-Assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find $\theta$ for $c=1\,\mathrm{\mu mol/L}$ and $K_d=2\,\mathrm{\mu mol/L}$.
2. Compute $K_d$ given $k_{\mathrm{off}}=0.2\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$ and $k_{\mathrm{on}}=10^5\,\mathrm{L\,mol^{-1}s^{-1}}$.
3. Why does a Hill coefficient of two not prove there are exactly two binding sites?

### Solutions

1. $\theta=1/(1+2)=1/3$.
2. $K_d=0.2/10^5\,\mathrm{mol/L}=2\,\mathrm{\mu mol/L}$.
3. The coefficient describes overall curve steepness; heterogeneity, allostery and experimental conditions may affect it.

## Validity Limits and Quality {#S5}

The simple relation assumes independent identical sites, equilibrium and controlled free concentration. Multiple sites, ligand depletion, nonspecific adsorption and nonequilibrium kinetics can change the curve. The figure is not experimental binding data.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

A dissociation constant connects half occupancy to a specific equilibrium model. Steep curves need additional mechanistic evidence before being interpreted as site-level cooperativity.
