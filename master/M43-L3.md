# M43-L3 – Control Experiments, Randomization and Blinded Analysis {#L3}

**Course area:** M43 – Master: Model Assessment and Scientific Research Practice

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M32: experimental design; M43-L2: measurement chain.

**Learning objective:** Calculate a synthetic group difference and critically assess its causal interpretation.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Controls probe the apparatus, while random assignment tends to balance known and unknown confounders in expectation across repeated studies. Blinded analysis can reduce analyst expectation effects. These techniques do not replace physical controls or adequate sample sizes. Analysis choices should, where possible, be recorded before treatment labels are revealed.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

A descriptive comparison uses the difference of group means $\Delta=\overline y_B-\overline y_A$. That number alone says nothing about uncertainty or causation. For independent samples and an appropriate variance model, the standard error of the difference also matters; randomization improves design balance in expectation but does not guarantee identical groups in a finite sample.

$$
\Delta=\overline y_B-\overline y_A,\qquad \overline y_G=\frac{1}{n_G}\sum_{i=1}^{n_G}y_{Gi}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For a **synthetic** comparison, take $A=(10,12,11)$ and $B=(13,14,12)$ in matching units. Then $\overline y_A=11$, $\overline y_B=13$ and $\Delta=2$. The figure shows all six values to expose the small sample size. A causal experimental interpretation requires appropriate allocation, a controlled measurement chain and explicit potential confounders.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find $\Delta$ for $A=(5,7)$ and $B=(8,10)$.
2. Why does a difference in means alone not establish causality?
3. What should be fixed before unblinding?

### Solutions

1. $\overline y_A=6$, $\overline y_B=9$, $\Delta=3$.
2. Confounding, selection, calibration bias and random fluctuations remain unresolved.
3. For example, exclusions, primary outcome and the analysis plan.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

With only three observations in each group, statistical uncertainty is substantial; the plotted points imply neither a confidence interval nor statistical significance. When randomization or blinding is infeasible or inappropriate, limitations and alternatives must be reported.
