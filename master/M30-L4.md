# M30-L4 – Interactions, Wick’s theorem, and scattering amplitudes {#L4}

**Course area:** M30 – Master: Quantum field theory and the Standard Model

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M30-L3: time-ordered correlators · [Previous lesson](M30-L3.md).

**Learning objective:** Explain the relation between an interaction term, Wick contractions, and an elementary scattering amplitude.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Observable scattering requires interactions. Quantum field theory often expands time-ordered products perturbatively in the interaction strength. Wick’s theorem resolves time-ordered products of free fields into normal-ordered terms and contractions; Feynman diagrams organize the terms.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

As a model choose a real scalar field with $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{int}}=-\lambda\phi^4/4!$. The four-field product produces a local vertex with four legs. The factor $4!$ compensates for the number of relevant permutations in Wick contractions. Under standard conventions the tree-level vertex is $-i\lambda$, so the contact contribution to $2\to2$ scattering obeys $i\mathcal M=-i\lambda$. Complete rates also require external-state normalization, phase space, possible symmetry factors, and additional diagrams where appropriate.

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{int}}=-\frac{\lambda}{4!}\phi^4,\qquad i\mathcal M_{\mathrm{tree}}=-i\lambda.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For an illustrative dimensionless coupling $\lambda=0.20$, the contact term gives $|\mathcal M_{\mathrm{tree}}|^2=0.040$. This number is **not** a cross section. The drawing shows four lines meeting at a contact vertex; it is a combinatorial representation, not a particle trajectory.

![Four incoming and outgoing field lines meet at a contact point labeled λ.](../../../../media/course/U_PHY_256_EN.svg)

*The local $\phi^4$ interaction produces a four-point vertex.*

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. How many legs meet at a $\phi^4$ contact vertex?
2. What happens to $|\mathcal M|^2$ if $\lambda$ doubles at tree level?
3. What is needed besides the amplitude to compute a cross section?

### Solutions

1. Four.
2. It increases by a factor of four.
3. At least integration over the allowed phase space with the appropriate flux and normalization.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The simple tree amplitude applies to the given interaction at leading perturbative order. Physical cross sections depend on energy and the model. Diagrams denote mathematical contributions to amplitudes, not photographs of microscopic paths.

## Summary and outlook {#S6}

Higher orders introduce loop integrals; their scale dependence motivates renormalization and effective theories. [Next lesson](M30-L5.md).
