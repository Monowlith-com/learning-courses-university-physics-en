# M28-L2 – Boltzmann equation, collision operator, and relaxation time {#L2}

**Course area:** M28 – Master: Nonequilibrium and many-body physics

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M5: kinetic gas theory; M13: statistical physics; M28-L1: transport equation · [Previous lesson](M28-L1.md).

**Learning objective:** Interpret the terms of the Boltzmann equation and solve a relaxation-time approximation.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

A dilute gas is described by a phase-space distribution $f(\mathbf r,\mathbf v,t)$. Free motion transports this distribution, forces change particle velocities, and collisions redistribute occupancies. This provides a connection between particle dynamics, thermal conductivity, viscosity, and other transport phenomena.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

The change in $f$ along a classical single-particle trajectory equals the collision operator $C[f]$. In a sufficiently dilute gas it can be constructed from binary collisions. A simple relaxation-time approximation uses $C[f]\approx-(f-f_{\mathrm{eq}})/\tau$. In a spatially uniform system without external forces, $\partial_t(f-f_{\mathrm{eq}})=-(f-f_{\mathrm{eq}})/\tau$ if $f_{\mathrm{eq}}$ remains fixed during relaxation. The result is exponential. A physically consistent collision approximation must preserve particle number, momentum, and energy, typically by choosing an appropriate local equilibrium distribution.

$$
\partial_tf+\mathbf v\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf r}f+\frac{\mathbf F}{m}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf v}f=C[f],\qquad f(t)-f_{\mathrm{eq}}=[f(0)-f_{\mathrm{eq}}]e^{-t/\tau}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

If the deviation at a fixed velocity is initially $\delta f(0)=0.30$ and $\tau=2\,\mathrm{ms}$, then after $4\,\mathrm{ms}$ it becomes $\delta f=0.30e^{-2}\approx0.0406$. The graph shows the normalized deviation. This is a solution of the relaxation equation, not a full solution of the nonlinear Boltzmann collision integral.

![Exponentially decaying normalized deviation from the equilibrium distribution as a function of time in relaxation-time units.](../../../../media/course/U_PHY_028_EN.svg)

*Within the relaxation-time approximation a deviation falls to $e^{-1}$ after one time $\tau$.*

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Which physical processes appear in the three terms on the left side of the equation?
2. Compute $\delta f(\tau)/\delta f(0)$.
3. Why must a collision approximation conserve particle number?

### Solutions

1. Time evolution, spatial streaming, and force-driven motion in velocity space.
2. $e^{-1}\approx0.3679$.
3. Ordinary elastic collisions neither create nor destroy particles.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

Molecular chaos, a dilute classical phase, and local binary collisions are assumptions of the ordinary Boltzmann model. Dense gases and quantum fluids require modified treatments.
