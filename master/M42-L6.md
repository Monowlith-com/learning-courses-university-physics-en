# M42-L6 – Good Research Practice, Reproducibility and Uncertainty {#L6}

**Course area:** M42 – Master: Interdisciplinary Physics and Research Frontiers

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M31: verification and validation; M32: instrumentation; M42-L5: counting statistics.

**Learning objective:** Estimate standard error in a simple independent measurement model and identify essential components of reproducible analysis.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Good research practice extends beyond archiving a final plot: measurement logs, calibration, selection criteria, analysis-code versions, processing steps and justified exclusions must be traceable. Reproduction may fail because raw data or parameter settings are unavailable. The DFG guidelines provide an integrity framework; subject-specific requirements and legal access restrictions still matter. FAIR means **findable, accessible under defined conditions, interoperable and reusable**, not necessarily unrestricted public release.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

For $N$ independent identically distributed measurements $X_i$ with finite standard deviation $\sigma$, the standard deviation of the mean is $\mathrm{SE}(\bar X)=\sigma/\sqrt N$. Unknown $\sigma$ is commonly estimated by sample standard deviation $s$. A shared systematic calibration bias **does not** shrink under this $1/\sqrt N$ scaling. Correlations may reduce the effective sample size.

$$
\mathrm{SE}(\bar X)=\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt N},\qquad \mathrm{SE}(\bar X)\propto N^{-1/2}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

A **synthetic** measurement study has $N=25$ independent observations with known spread $\sigma=4$ units. Its standard error is $\mathrm{SE}(\bar X)=0.8$ units. Increasing to $N=100$ reduces that random component to $0.4$. A shared offset of, say, $+1$ unit remains unchanged. A reproducible package therefore needs original data, configuration, computation environment, analysis version and a verification log.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. How many independent measurements are needed for $\mathrm{SE}=0.2$ with $\sigma=4$?
2. Does a larger sample remove a fixed calibration bias?
3. What metadata make excluded observations auditable?

### Solutions

1. $N=(4/0.2)^2=400$.
2. No. Systematic offset and sampling scatter are distinct uncertainty components.
3. Prespecified criteria, documented rationale, raw records and versioned processing steps.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The $1/\sqrt N$ rule should not be used uncritically for correlated, drifting, heavily selected or heavy-tailed measurements. Small-sample inference can require explicit distributional assumptions and intervals. The DFG framework does not replace subject-specific data security checks.
