# M31-L1 – Time integration, stiffness and stability {#L1}

**Course area:** M31 – Master: Numerical and Computational Physics

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M12: differential equations; M13: variational principles; M20: numerical foundations · [Previous lesson](M30-L9.md).

**Learning objective:** Derive the explicit Euler step-size stability condition from a test equation and select suitable time integrators.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

An equation of motion becomes computationally useful when its time evolution is evaluated with controlled error. Explicit integrators are simple but sensitive to the step size for rapidly decaying modes. Stiffness means that stability can require time steps far smaller than those needed merely for the intended temporal resolution.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

For the test equation $y\prime=-\lambda y$ with $\lambda>0$ and initial value $y_0$, the exact solution is $y(t)=y_0e^{-\lambda t}$. Explicit Euler yields $y_{n+1}=y_n-h\lambda y_n=(1-h\lambda)y_n$. Because every step uses the same amplification factor, its magnitude must remain below one. For this scalar problem with positive real $\lambda$, the result is $0<h\lambda<2$. This is a stability condition, not a guarantee of high accuracy. Stiff systems often favor implicit methods such as Radau or BDF; adaptive solvers assess estimated local errors using absolute and relative tolerances. Symplectic schemes are selected for Hamiltonian systems because of geometric structure preservation; they do not generally conserve the original energy exactly at every step.

$$
|1-h\lambda|<1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad 0<h\lambda<2.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For $\lambda=5\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ and $h=0.3\,\mathrm s$, one gets $h\lambda=1.5$ and hence $y_{n+1}=-0.5y_n$: alternating signs with decreasing magnitude. At $h=0.5\,\mathrm s$, the factor becomes $-1.5$ and errors grow. The diagram plots the amplification factor magnitude against $h\lambda$ and marks the stability limit.

![Explicit Euler amplification magnitude with a stable region below one for hλ between zero and two.](../../../../media/course/U_PHY_195_EN.svg)

*For the scalar test equation, the amplification magnitude separates damped stable steps from unstable ones.*

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find the amplification factor for $h\lambda=0.4$.
2. Is Euler stable with $h=0.25\,\mathrm s$ and $\lambda=10\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$?
3. Why are local error tolerance and stability different?

### Solutions

1. $1-0.4=0.6$; the discrete mode decays.
2. No; $h\lambda=2.5>2$ is unstable.
3. Small estimated local errors do not on their own guarantee damping of numerical disturbances.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The bound of 2 applies to this linear scalar test equation. Nonlinear dynamics, multiple eigenvalues, complex spectra and events require additional analysis. A useful approximation also needs to converge under time-step refinement.
