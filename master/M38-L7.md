# M38-L7 – Climate Feedback and Equilibrium Climate Sensitivity {#L7}

**Course area:** M38 – Master: Geophysics, Atmospheric Physics and Climate Models

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M22: thermal balance; M38-L4: TOA energy budget; M38-L6 · [Previous lesson](M38-L6.md).

**Learning objective:** Relate radiative forcing to a positive restoring-feedback convention and compute an idealized equilibrium warming.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Additional radiative forcing $F$ perturbs the global TOA budget. Temperature, water vapour, clouds and albedo then alter the net energy flux. Equilibrium climate sensitivity describes long-term global-mean surface warming from CO₂ doubling under a stated model/assessment definition. It is neither the instantaneous energy imbalance nor a single directly measured parameter.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

We define net flux $N$ positive downward, as in M38-L4, and choose a **positive restoring coefficient** $\lambda>0$: $N=F-\lambda\Delta T$. The mathematical equilibrium $N=0$ gives $\Delta T_{\mathrm{eq}}=F/\lambda$. IPCC literature also uses a feedback parameter $\alpha$ of the opposite sign: $\alpha=-\lambda$ for the same local linear balance. Both conventions agree if signs and units $\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}\,K^{-1}}$ remain explicit.

$$
N=F-\lambda\Delta T,\qquad \Delta T_{\mathrm{eq}}=\frac{F}{\lambda}\quad(\lambda>0).
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For illustrative forcing $F=3.6\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$ and $\lambda=1.2\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}\,K^{-1}}$, equilibrium warming would be $\Delta T_{\mathrm{eq}}=3.0\,\mathrm K$. At a transient $\Delta T=1\,\mathrm K$ the remaining imbalance is $N=2.4\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$. These parameters are deliberately chosen and are not new IPCC estimates. The plot shows the linear forcing–response relation and its equilibrium point.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What equilibrium warming results if $\lambda$ doubles at fixed $F$?
2. What sign has $\alpha$ in the indicated IPCC convention?
3. Why does $N=0$ not describe the instant when forcing first begins?

### Solutions

1. $1.5\,\mathrm K$.
2. $\alpha=-\lambda<0$ for a stabilizing response.
3. The ocean and other reservoirs delay adjustment; immediately after perturbation the flux imbalance is generally nonzero.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

This one-parameter linear model cannot replace coupled climate models. Feedback may depend on climate state, timescale, warming pattern and forcing type. The constructed 3 K must not be presented as a standalone IPCC range or an inference about contemporary observed warming.
