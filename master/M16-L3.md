# M16-L3 – Type A and Type B Uncertainty Evaluation {#L3}

**Course area:** M16 – Bachelor: Experimental Physics and Measurement Analysis

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** M16-L2: standard deviation; M16-L1: calibration. [Previous lesson](M16-L2.md).

**Learning objective:** Calculate standard uncertainties from repeated observations and prior information and combine them regardless of evaluation type.

## Statistical and Other Information {#S1}

Type A and type B classify *how* a standard uncertainty is evaluated, not whether the influence is random or systematic. Type A uses statistical analysis of observations; type B uses other information such as calibration certificates, specifications or physical models. With $n$ independent replicates, one often uses $u_A=s/\sqrt n$. Type-B information must be turned into a standard deviation using a justified probability model. A maximum permissible error is not automatically a standard uncertainty. For an assumed rectangular distribution over $[-a,a]$, $u_B=a/\sqrt3$. Using that conversion without support for the distribution would be unjustified.

## Standardizing and Combining Contributions {#S2}

If contributions $x_i$ are uncorrelated and local linearization is justified, the combined variance is the sum of squared standard uncertainties scaled by sensitivities. For a directly corrected scalar result $y=x+c$, with independent $x$ and $c$,

$$
u_c(y)=\sqrt{u(x)^2+u(c)^2}.
$$

Combined standard uncertainty is not the sum of absolute error bounds. An *expanded uncertainty* is reported as $U=k\,u_c$, with the coverage factor $k$ and justification. In particular, $k=2$ does not universally guarantee exactly 95 percent coverage for every distribution and sample size.

## Example: Digital Readout {#S3}

A digital display rounds to the nearest $0.1\,\mathrm V$. Assuming a uniform rounding error gives half-width $a=0.05\,\mathrm V$. Independent replicate readings give a standard uncertainty of the mean of $0.030\,\mathrm V$. Hence

$$
u_B=\frac{0.05}{\sqrt3}\,\mathrm V\approx0.0289\,\mathrm V,\qquad u_c=\sqrt{0.030^2+0.0289^2}\,\mathrm V\approx0.0416\,\mathrm V.
$$

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find $u_B$ for rectangular half-width $a=0.12\,\mathrm{mm}$.
2. Combine independent uncertainties of $3$ and $4\,\mathrm{mV}$.
3. Give two sources of information for a type-B evaluation.

### Solutions

1. $u_B=0.12/\sqrt3\approx0.0693\,\mathrm{mm}$.
2. $u_c=\sqrt{3^2+4^2}=5\,\mathrm{mV}$.
3. For example a calibration certificate and a component specification, each with a justified distribution.

## Common Misinterpretations {#S5}

Do not hide a known calibration bias inside an unspecified uncertainty: known significant effects should be corrected, while uncertainty in the correction remains. Linearization can fail for strongly nonlinear response functions or broad distributions; distribution propagation or Monte Carlo methods may then be useful. Type B is not inherently inferior to type A. Shared influence quantities, such as one thermometer used for two channels, induce correlations. The next lesson includes them explicitly in uncertainty propagation.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Evaluation types classify information sources; standard uncertainties combine according to sensitivities and correlations.
