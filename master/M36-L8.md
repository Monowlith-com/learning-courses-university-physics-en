# M36-L8 – Counting Experiments, Likelihood and Statistical Evidence {#L8}

**Course area:** M36 – Master: Particle Physics, Collision Experiments and Accelerators

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M13: statistics; M31: model validation; M32: counting measurements.

**Learning objective:** Apply the Asimov formula for a simple counting channel with known background and state its limitations.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Particle experiments distinguish candidate signals from known and unknown backgrounds. A simple counting channel is often described by a Poisson likelihood. Evidential strength depends not only on count excess but also background uncertainty and the statistical test. An observed excess is not automatically a discovery.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

For assumed signal expectation $s\ge0$ and exactly known background $b>0$, a simple Asimov counting model gives $Z_A=\sqrt{2[(s+b)\ln(1+s/b)-s]}$. For $s\ll b$ this approaches $s/\sqrt b$. It is the expected asymptotic significance in the model, neither an observed p-value nor a universal formula under uncertain background.

$$
Z_A=\sqrt{2\left[(s+b)\ln\!\left(1+\frac{s}{b}\right)-s\right]}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For $b=100$ and a hypothetical expected signal $s=25$, $Z_A\approx2.405$. The approximation $s/\sqrt b=2.5$ is slightly larger. The graphic compares the two model values, not an actual experimental significance.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find the approximate $s/\sqrt b$ for $s=10,b=100$.
2. How does that approximation change if $b$ quadruples at fixed $s$?
3. Why must the Asimov formula not be used without modification when background has uncertainty?

### Solutions

1. $1$.
2. It is halved.
3. The likelihood must incorporate background nuisance parameters and constraints.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

Assumptions are a single Poisson count, known background and an asymptotic interpretation. Look-elsewhere effects, systematic uncertainties, selection bias and multiple testing require separate treatment. Neither the example nor its image reports experimental significance.
