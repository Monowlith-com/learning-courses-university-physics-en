# M18-L5 – Optics Laboratory: Imaging and Diffraction {#L5}

**Course area:** M18 – Bachelor: Physics Laboratory Practice

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** M7: ray optics and interference; M16: scale calibration. [Previous lesson](M18-L4.md).

**Learning objective:** Determine focal length from object and image distances and diffraction angle from a grating model.

## Research Question and Model {#S1}

Ray and wave optics describe complementary regimes. For a thin lens in the paraxial approximation, $1/f=1/g+1/b$, where $g$ and $b$ denote positive real-object and real-image distances in the chosen convention. For actual lenses the relevant reference planes need not coincide with the geometric centre; principal planes, aberrations and off-axis rays matter when high accuracy is required. Lasers and intense light sources require site-specific protective measures; this lesson covers only geometric and mathematical teaching models.

## Equations and Assumptions {#S2}

For a normally illuminated grating with spacing $d$, diffraction maxima of order $m$ satisfy $d\sin\theta=m\lambda$. In the far field the angle can be inferred from calibrated geometry. The small-angle relation $\sin\theta\approx\theta$ applies only for small angles. A grating with 600 lines per millimetre has $d\approx1.67\,\mathrm{\mu m}$. For $\lambda=600\,\mathrm{nm}$ the first maximum has $\sin\theta\approx0.36$, hence $\theta\approx21.1^\circ$. Identify the order $m$: without it a wavelength estimate is ambiguous.

$$
\frac{1}{f}=\frac1g+\frac1b,\qquad d\sin\theta=m\lambda
$$

## Worked Example and Diagram {#S3}

Imaging example: An object is $g=0.30\,\mathrm m$ from an ideal thin lens and its real image $b=0.60\,\mathrm m$ beyond the lens. Then $f=gb/(g+b)=0.20\,\mathrm m$. The magnification magnitude is $|V|=b/g=2$, with image inversion. In actual measurements record distance origins, lens movement, focusing criterion and correlations between length readings. Extra decimal places do not cure ambiguous focus.

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find $f$ for $g=0.30\,\mathrm m$ and $b=0.60\,\mathrm m$.
2. Find $d$ for 600 lines per millimetre.
3. Why is $\sin\theta\approx\theta$ inaccurate at $\theta\approx21^\circ$?

### Solutions

1. $f=0.30\cdot0.60/0.90=0.20\,\mathrm m$.
2. $d=10^{-3}/600\,\mathrm m\approx1.67\,\mathrm{\mu m}$.
3. The angle is about $0.368$ radians but its sine about $0.36$; the approximation error grows with angle.

## Limits and Quality Control {#S5}

Each optical model has clear limits. The paraxial lens approximation fails for large angular apertures, and broadband light may introduce chromatic effects. Grating measurements require incident-angle control, calibrated geometry and attention to finite peak width as well as $d$ and $m$. Where possible compare multiple diffraction orders or object/image positions and inspect systematic residual patterns. Never improvise experiments with high-intensity beams.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Imaging and diffraction provide different observables, each with its own model and angular validity range.
