# M22-L7 – Single-Molecule Methods: FRET and Optical Tweezers {#L7}

**Course area:** M22 – Bachelor: Biophysics

**Level:** Bachelor · Elective

**Prerequisites:** M7: optics; M13: equipartition; M22-L4: elasticity. [Previous lesson](M22-L6.md).

**Learning objective:** Interpret idealised FRET efficiency in terms of distance and estimate optical trap stiffness from position fluctuations.

## Question and Observation {#S1}

An individual biomolecule can be studied indirectly through optical emission or held under load using a tightly focused optical trap. Two distinct methods are Förster resonance energy transfer (FRET), which probes coupling between a donor and acceptor, and optical tweezers, which use gradient forces to confine a microscopic object. Both require calibration and careful treatment of noise.

## Physical Model and Equations {#S2}

For one fixed donor–acceptor separation $r$ and a suitably defined Förster radius $R_0$, the ideal transfer efficiency $E$ is distance dependent. Near its minimum, a harmonic optical trap has potential $U(x)=\kappa(x-x_0)^2/2$. Equipartition gives $\langle U\rangle=k_{\mathrm B}T/2$ for the quadratic degree of freedom; hence the measured thermal position variance $\sigma_x^2$ determines its stiffness

$$
E=\frac{1}{1+(r/R_0)^6},\qquad \kappa=\frac{k_{\mathrm B}T}{\sigma_x^2}.
$$

FRET is not a calibration-free molecular ruler. $R_0$ depends on spectral overlap, donor quantum yield, refractive index and relative dipole orientation, and apparent signals require background, cross-talk and detector-efficiency correction. Estimating trap stiffness requires separating equilibrium thermal fluctuations from drift, detector noise and finite-bandwidth effects.

## Worked Example and Teaching Diagram {#S3}

In an idealised case, $r=R_0$ gives $E=1/2$, while $r=1.2R_0$ gives $E\approx0.251$. If a trap at $T=298\,\mathrm K$ has a genuine thermal position standard deviation $\sigma_x=10\,\mathrm{nm}$, then $\kappa\approx4.11\cdot10^{-5}\,\mathrm{N/m}=0.0411\,\mathrm{pN/nm}$. ## Self-Assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find ideal FRET efficiency when $r=R_0/2$.
2. How does $\kappa$ change if thermal position standard deviation doubles at constant temperature?
3. Why does dipole orientation matter in a FRET distance estimate?

### Solutions

1. $E=1/(1+1/64)=64/65\approx0.985$.
2. Since $\kappa\propto1/\sigma_x^2$, it falls to one quarter.
3. Orientation changes the Förster radius; without suitable assumptions or corrections, distance cannot be uniquely recovered from efficiency.

## Validity Limits and Quality {#S5}

The FRET relation assumes a dominant donor–acceptor separation and a defined $R_0$, neglecting dynamics during integration, photophysics and detailed corrections. Trap calibration assumes an equilibrium harmonic potential, adequate bandwidth and a calibrated detector. A licensing corrigendum for the cited FRET study is not evidence of a correction to its physical equations.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Single-molecule methods convert signals into structural or force-related quantities indirectly. Their accuracy depends on an explicit, verified calibration model.
