# M15-L5 – Scattering and Differential Cross Section {#L5}

**Course area:** M15 – Bachelor: Nuclear and Particle Physics

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** M12: scattering theory, M15-L3, spherical coordinates and wave interference. [Previous lesson](M15-L4.md).

**Learning objective:** Interpret cross section as rate per incoming flux and apply elastic scattering amplitudes.

## Event Rate and Scattering Geometry {#S1}

Scattering is characterized by observed event counts, incident beam properties and the detector's angular acceptance. Total cross section $\sigma$ has dimensions of area. It is not necessarily the physical outline of a point particle but a measure of reaction probability. For a thin target with areal density $n_T$ and incident particle rate $\dot N_{\mathrm{in}}$, a single-scattering approximation gives $\dot N_{\mathrm{sc}}\approx\dot N_{\mathrm{in}}n_T\sigma$. Colliding beams instead use luminosity $\mathcal L$ and $R=\mathcal L\sigma$. Acceptance, detection efficiency, multiple scattering and background must be considered independently in practice.

## Differential Cross Section {#S2}

The angular distribution is defined by

$$
\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}=\frac{d\dot N_{\mathrm{sc}}/d\Omega}{j_{\mathrm{in}}}
$$

For nonrelativistic elastic potential scattering with asymptotic wave $\psi\sim e^{ikz}+f(\theta,\phi)e^{ikr}/r$, one obtains 

$$
\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}=|f(\theta,\phi)|^2
$$

Scattering amplitude $f$ has dimensions of length. In general, kinematic factors, spins, multiple channels, particle identities and phase space must be included. Thus $|f|^2$ is not a universal inelastic relativistic scattering formula. Total cross section follows from $\sigma=\int(d\sigma/d\Omega)\,d\Omega$ over the physically allowed angular domain.

## Example: Isotropic Scattering {#S3}

Consider an ideal elastic scattering process isotropic over all solid angles with $d\sigma/d\Omega=2.0\,\mathrm{mb/sr}$. Since $\int d\Omega=4\pi\,\mathrm{sr}$,

$$
\sigma=4\pi\,(2.0\,\mathrm{mb/sr})\approx25.13\,\mathrm{mb}.
$$

An angular bin $\Delta\Omega=0.10\,\mathrm{sr}$ corresponds to an integrated bin cross section of about $0.20\,\mathrm{mb}$. The unit $1\,\mathrm b=10^{-28}\,\mathrm{m^2}$ is customary in nuclear and particle physics.

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find $\sigma$ for isotropic $d\sigma/d\Omega=1\,\mathrm{mb/sr}$.
2. Express $3\,\mathrm{mb}$ in SI area units.
3. When is $d\sigma/d\Omega=|f|^2$ valid without extra factors?

### Solutions

1. $\sigma=4\pi\,\mathrm{mb}\approx12.57\,\mathrm{mb}$.
2. $3\times10^{-31}\,\mathrm{m^2}$.
3. In the stated nonrelativistic elastic potential-scattering limit with consistent flux normalization.

## Scope and Corrections {#S5}

Scattering need not be isotropic. Coulomb scattering can be strongly forward-peaked, and identical particles require symmetrized or antisymmetrized amplitudes. Detectors usually measure folded distributions: resolution, angular acceptance and trigger selection affect the raw histogram. Finite target thickness brings absorption and multiple interactions. Luminosity in a collider has units of inverse area per time and must not be confused with cross section. One millibarn equals $10^{-31}\,\mathrm{m^2}$.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Cross sections connect rates and fluxes; amplitude formulas apply only under their specific assumptions.
