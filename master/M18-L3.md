# M18-L3 – Calorimetry: Energy Balance and Heat Losses {#L3}

**Course area:** M18 – Bachelor: Physics Laboratory Practice

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** M5: thermodynamics and specific heat capacity; M16: sensor errors. [Previous lesson](M18-L2.md).

**Learning objective:** Predict mixing temperatures using an energy balance and identify systematic heat losses.

## Research Question and Model {#S1}

Calorimetry relates temperature changes to energy exchanged as heat. For a substance without a phase transition and with approximately constant $c$, the change is $Q=mc\Delta T$. In an ideally insulated mixing experiment, heat lost by the warm part equals heat gained by the cool part. Real experiments require additional information: the temperature field must be sufficiently uniform, probes must sample it representatively, and the vessel has its own heat capacity. The result is initially a model test, not a direct universal material constant.

## Equations and Assumptions {#S2}

For two samples of the same substance with masses $m_h,m_c$ and initial temperatures $T_h,T_c$, the balance $m_hc(T_f-T_h)+m_cc(T_f-T_c)=0$ yields $T_f=(m_hT_h+m_cT_c)/(m_h+m_c)$. For different substances use their respective $mc$ values. The vessel adds a term $C_{\mathrm{cal}}(T_f-T_{\mathrm{cal},0})$. Heat exchange with the surroundings makes the energy sum for the chosen internal system nonzero. Record thermal contact, duration, initial temperatures and vessel assumptions.

$$
T_f=\frac{m_hT_h+m_cT_c}{m_h+m_c}\quad\text{(same material, ideal insulation)}
$$

## Worked Example and Diagram {#S3}

Teaching example: $0.20\,\mathrm{kg}$ of water at $60\,^\circ\mathrm C$ mixed with $0.30\,\mathrm{kg}$ at $20\,^\circ\mathrm C$ gives the ideal $T_f=36\,^\circ\mathrm C$. A lower observed equilibrium temperature could arise from a cooler vessel or heat loss, among other causes. It does not identify one cause without controls. A time trace $T(t)$ before and after mixing is more informative than one final value; any fitted extrapolation needs a stated model range. Handle hot liquids only under local safety instructions.

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Calculate the ideal mixture temperature for $0.20\,\mathrm{kg}$ at $60\,^\circ\mathrm C$ and $0.30\,\mathrm{kg}$ at $20\,^\circ\mathrm C$.
2. What are the signs of the two terms $mc(T_f-T_i)$?
3. Give two plausible systematic deviations.

### Solutions

1. $(0.2\cdot60+0.3\cdot20)/0.5=36\,^\circ\mathrm C$.
2. Negative for the initially warm part, positive for the cool part.
3. Vessel/environment heat exchange, incomplete mixing or thermometer lag.

## Limits and Quality Control {#S5}

For an initial uncertainty budget, masses and initial temperatures may be treated as independent only if the measurement chains justify it. A shared thermometer calibration error is correlated and must not be added as an independent random error. Test the zero-loss model before adding extra fit parameters. Heat loss means energy crossing a stated system boundary; energy is not destroyed. Good analysis names that boundary and distinguishes measurement uncertainty from model inadequacy.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Energy balances need explicit system boundaries and controlled apparatus contributions.
