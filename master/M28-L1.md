# M28-L1 – Langevin dynamics, the Fokker–Planck equation, and equilibrium {#L1}

**Course area:** M28 – Master: Nonequilibrium and many-body physics

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M13: statistical ensembles; M26: stochastic processes; M17: differential equations · [Previous lesson](M27-L8.md).

**Learning objective:** Derive a Fokker–Planck equation from a Langevin process and distinguish a stationary density from a vanishing current.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Microscopic collisions can act like random forces on slow degrees of freedom. Rather than following each collision, we calculate the evolution of a probability density $p(x,t)$. Stationarity initially means only that this density no longer changes; in general it is not identical to detailed balance.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

The Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process has linear restoring drift $-\gamma x$ and additive white noise. Here $W_t$ is a Wiener process and $D$ is the diffusion parameter. Probability conservation gives $\partial_t p=-\partial_xJ$ with $J=-\gamma xp-D\partial_xp$. On an unbounded one-dimensional domain with vanishing boundary current, stationarity implies $J=0$. Thus $\partial_x\ln p=-\gamma x/D$. Integration and normalization produce the stated Gaussian, with variance $D/\gamma$. In several dimensions a stationary density can instead support a circulating probability current.

$$
\mathrm dx=-\gamma x\,\mathrm dt+\sqrt{2D}\,\mathrm dW_t,\quad \partial_tp=\gamma\partial_x(xp)+D\partial_x^2p,\quad p_*(x)=\sqrt{\frac{\gamma}{2\pi D}}\,e^{-\gamma x^2/(2D)}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For $\gamma=2\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ and $D=0.5\,\mathrm{m}^2/\mathrm{s}$, the stationary variance is $\sigma_*^2=D/\gamma=0.25\,\mathrm{m}^2$. The standard deviation is $0.5\,\mathrm m$. The plotted Gaussian curves contrast a narrower initial density with the stationary density; they are schematic snapshots, not a complete trajectory.

![Two normalized Gaussian curves against position; the stationary probability density is wider than the initial density.](../../../../media/course/U_PHY_201_EN.svg)

*The stationary variance is fixed by the ratio $D/\gamma$.*

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find $\sigma_*^2$ for $D=0.8\,\mathrm{m}^2/\mathrm s$ and $\gamma=4\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$.
2. What additional condition does detailed balance impose on the current shown?
3. What is $\langle x(1\,\mathrm s)\rangle/\langle x(0)\rangle$ for $\gamma=2\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$?

### Solutions

1. $0.2\,\mathrm{m}^2$.
2. $J(x)=0$ at every position.
3. $e^{-2}\approx0.1353$; the mean decays exponentially.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

Markov and white-noise assumptions are approximations. With state-dependent diffusion, the drift depends on the Itô or Stratonovich interpretation. The solution uses appropriate boundary conditions.
