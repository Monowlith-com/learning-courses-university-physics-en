# M20-L3 – Stellar Structure, Hydrostatic Equilibrium and Evolution {#L3}

**Course area:** M20 – Bachelor: Astrophysics and Cosmology

**Level:** Bachelor · Elective

**Prerequisites:** M3: gravitation; M5: thermodynamics; M13: equations of state. [Previous lesson: Stellar Spectra, Temperature and Radial Velocity](M20-L2.md).

**Learning objective:** Derive mass conservation and hydrostatic equilibrium for a stellar model and explain their limitations.

## Question and Observation {#S1}

Why does a star not instantly collapse under its own gravity? During approximately stationary phases of evolution, inward gravity is balanced by an outward pressure force arising from a pressure that decreases with radius. A star is a stratified fluid rather than a solid shell. In many main-sequence stars, nuclear reactions supply energy that moves outward through radiation diffusion or convection. Whether a stellar object satisfies the static approximation depends on its evolutionary stage.

## Physical Model and Equations {#S2}

Assume spherical symmetry and let $m(r)$ be the mass enclosed by radius $r$. The mass of a thin spherical shell implies
$$
\frac{\mathrm dm}{\mathrm dr}=4\pi r^2\rho(r).
$$
Hydrostatic force balance then gives
$$
\frac{\mathrm dP}{\mathrm dr}=-\frac{Gm(r)\rho(r)}{r^2}.
$$
If $\varepsilon$ is the local energy generation rate per unit mass, a highly simplified stationary model obeys $\mathrm dL/\mathrm dr=4\pi r^2\rho\varepsilon$ when other energy sources or sinks can be neglected. Combined with an equation of state and energy-transport equations, these expressions form the basis of stellar structure models.

## Worked Example and Teaching Diagram {#S3}

For a hypothetical constant-density sphere, $m(r)=4\pi\rho r^3/3$. Integrating hydrostatic balance subject to $P(R)=0$ yields $P(r)=2\pi G\rho^2(R^2-r^2)/3$. Hence the central pressure is
$$
P_c=\frac{3GM^2}{8\pi R^4}.
$$
For $M=1.0\times10^{30}\,\mathrm{kg}$ and $R=5.0\times10^8\,\mathrm m$, $P_c\approx1.27\times10^{14}\,\mathrm{Pa}$. This is **not** a realistic solar model: actual density varies strongly with radius. The diagram depicts model layers and a schematic pressure profile.

## Self-Assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Derive $m(r)$ for uniform density.
2. How does $P_c$ change if radius is halved at fixed mass?
3. State two assumptions behind the hydrostatic equation above.

### Solutions

1. $m(r)=\int_0^r4\pi r^{\prime2}\rho\,\mathrm dr^\prime=4\pi\rho r^3/3$.
2. Since $P_c\propto R^{-4}$, pressure increases by a factor of 16.
3. Spherical symmetry and quasi-static force balance without substantial acceleration.

## Validity Limits and Quality {#S5}

Rapidly evolving stages such as collapse, supernova explosions and pulsations require time-dependent equations. Radiative and convective zones are schematic and differ with stellar mass and age. The constant-density model is useful only for demonstrating the pressure scale and its $R^{-4}$ dependence.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Stellar structure couples mass, force and energy balances with a material equation of state.
