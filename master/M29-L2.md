# M29-L2 – Metric, proper time, and light cones {#L2}

**Course area:** M29 – Master: General relativity and cosmology

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M10: Minkowski space; M26: tensors and coordinates; M29-L1: equivalence principle · [Previous lesson](M29-L1.md).

**Learning objective:** Compute proper time along timelike paths from the metric line element and identify null directions.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

A coordinate is not itself a clock reading. In relativity, a clock carried along a worldline measures proper time $\tau$. The spacetime metric determines invariant lengths and intervals. Light cones distinguish possible causal signals from spacelike-separated events.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

We adopt signature $(-,+,+,+)$ and $x^0=ct$. The general line element is $ds^2=g_{\mu\nu}dx^\mu dx^\nu$. For a timelike path, $ds^2=-c^2d\tau^2$; for light in geometric optics, $ds^2=0$. In a local Minkowski chart, $ds^2=-c^2dt^2+dx^2+dy^2+dz^2$. Substituting $v^2=(dx^2+dy^2+dz^2)/dt^2$ gives the usual time dilation law. In curved spacetime, the constant Minkowski coefficients are replaced by position-dependent metric components.

$$
d\tau=dt\sqrt{1-\frac{v^2}{c^2}}\qquad\text{(in an inertial Minkowski chart)}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

A clock travels at $v=0.6c$ for coordinate time $\Delta t=5\,\mathrm s$ in an inertial frame. Its elapsed proper time is $\Delta\tau=5\sqrt{1-0.36}=4\,\mathrm s$. In the $ct$–$x$ diagram, the lines $x=\pm ct$ are null directions. The particle worldline remains inside the future light cone.

![Spacetime diagram with horizontal position and vertical time axes, a light cone, and a timelike worldline inside the cone.](../../../../media/course/U_PHY_174_EN.svg)

*Null lines bound the local causal region; massive particles follow timelike worldlines.*

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What condition defines a null line element?
2. What proper time elapses in $10\,\mathrm s$ of coordinate time at $v=0.8c$?
3. Why is $ds^2$ invariant although individual $g_{\mu\nu}$ depend on coordinates?

### Solutions

1. $ds^2=0$.
2. $\Delta\tau=6\,\mathrm s$.
3. Metric components and coordinate differentials transform together, leaving their contraction unchanged.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The constant-$v$ formula assumes an inertial chart in flat spacetime. In curved spacetime or during acceleration, proper time must be integrated along the actual worldline. A single light-cone diagram does not determine global causal structure.
