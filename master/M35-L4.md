# M35-L4 – BCS Superconductivity, Cooper Pairs and the Energy Gap {#L4}

**Course area:** M35 – Master: Advanced Solid-State Physics and Materials Science

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M14: superconductivity; M35-L3: phonons; M28: statistical physics.

**Learning objective:** Apply the weak-coupling BCS gap ratio and identify its assumptions.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

A superconductor is not just a metal with an infinitely long Drude relaxation time. In conventional BCS theory an effective attraction near the Fermi surface produces coherent fermion pairing. A quasiparticle energy gap suppresses certain low-energy single-particle excitations. The condensate carries macroscopic phase coherence, although real samples may display vortices, critical currents and defects.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

For isotropic, weak-coupling $s$-wave BCS superconductivity under its standard assumptions, $2\Delta(0)/(k_B T_c)\approx3.53$. Here $\Delta$ is the single-quasiparticle excitation gap, while $2\Delta$ is the ideal threshold for creating two quasiparticles. The gap closes as $T\to T_c^-$; its complete temperature dependence requires the self-consistent BCS gap equation. The factor $3.53$ is not universal in strong-coupling or unconventional superconductors.

$$
\frac{2\Delta(0)}{k_B T_c}\approx3.53.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For an ideal BCS superconductor with $T_c=10\,\mathrm K$ and $k_B=8.61733\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm{eV/K}$, the estimate is $\Delta(0)\approx1.52\,\mathrm{meV}$, so $2\Delta(0)\approx3.04\,\mathrm{meV}$. The diagram shows an explicitly illustrative gap-closing curve, not a numerical solution of the full BCS self-consistency equation or experimental data.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. How does the weak-coupling zero-temperature gap change when $T_c$ doubles?
2. What does $2\Delta$ represent in ideal BCS theory?
3. Why is the factor $3.53$ not universal for all superconductors?

### Solutions

1. It doubles.
2. The idealized energy for creating two appropriate quasiparticle excitations.
3. Strong coupling, anisotropy and other pairing mechanisms can change the ratio.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The relation assumes a weak-coupling isotropic BCS state. It does not determine critical fields, unconventional pairing, contact resistance or engineering performance.
