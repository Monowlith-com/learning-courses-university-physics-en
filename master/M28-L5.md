# M28-L5 – Linear response, the Kubo formula, and causality {#L5}

**Course area:** M28 – Master: Nonequilibrium and many-body physics

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M10: electrodynamics; M13: equilibrium; M27: quantum operators · [Previous lesson](M28-L4.md).

**Learning objective:** State a causal susceptibility and calculate the real and imaginary parts of a relaxation model.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Experiments often measure how a system responds to a weak external signal. If the response is proportional to the perturbation, a susceptibility describes this relation. A physical response cannot precede its cause. Causality is therefore a mathematical constraint on the response function as well as an interpretive principle.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

Let the perturbation of the Hamiltonian be $H\prime(t)=-h(t)B$. In linear response, the expectation change in $A$ is described by the retarded equilibrium function $\chi_{AB}(t)=(i/\hbar)\theta(t)\langle[A(t),B(0)]\rangle$. A simple phenomenological relaxation model instead uses $\chi(t)=(\chi_0/\tau)e^{-t/\tau}\theta(t)$. We explicitly adopt the transform $\chi(\omega)=\int_0^\infty \chi(t)e^{i\omega t}\,\mathrm dt$. Thus $\chi(\omega)=\chi_0/(1-i\omega\tau)$, with real part $\chi_0/[1+(\omega\tau)^2]$ and imaginary part $\chi_0\omega\tau/[1+(\omega\tau)^2]$.

$$
\delta\langle A(t)\rangle=\int_{-\infty}^{t}\chi_{AB}(t-t\prime)h(t\prime)\,\mathrm dt\prime,\qquad \chi(\omega)=\frac{\chi_0}{1-i\omega\tau}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

At $\omega\tau=1$, the values are $\operatorname{Re}\chi/\chi_0=1/2$ and $\operatorname{Im}\chi/\chi_0=1/2$. Both plotted curves apply only to this single-relaxation-time model; real materials can combine many relaxation times and resonances. The model imaginary part reaches its maximum at $\omega\tau=1$.

![Real and imaginary parts of Debye susceptibility against dimensionless frequency omega times tau.](../../../../media/course/U_PHY_155_EN.svg)

*The Debye model has its dissipative maximum at $\omega\tau=1$.*

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What does the step function $\theta(t)$ enforce?
2. What is $\chi(0)/\chi_0$?
3. Calculate both components for $\omega\tau=1$.

### Solutions

1. It enforces causality: no response before the perturbation.
2. $1$.
3. Both are $1/2$ in units of $\chi_0$.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

Kubo response assumes a sufficiently weak perturbation and suitable equilibrium conditions. The sign of the imaginary part depends on Fourier convention. Debye relaxation is phenomenological rather than a universal response spectrum.
