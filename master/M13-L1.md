# M13-L1 – Microstates, Macrostates and Phase Space {#L1}

**Course area:** M13 – Bachelor: Statistical Physics

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** Combinatorics, energy conservation, probability, and thermodynamics from M2, M5 and M8. [Previous lesson](M12-L9.md).

**Learning objective:** Distinguish microstates from macrostates, count multiplicities, and motivate statistical state counting.

## From Single Particles to Macroscopic Systems {#S1}

A macrostate is specified by a few measurable quantities, such as internal energy $U$, volume $V$, particle number $N$, and possibly magnetization. A microstate, by contrast, fixes all dynamical degrees of freedom in the chosen model. In the microcanonical ensemble of an isolated system, energy, volume, and particle number are fixed; allowed microstates belong to a compatible energy shell. Statistical reasoning is needed because tracking the microscopic motion of a macroscopic object is impractical even though its macroscopic averages are reproducible. The bridge from microstates to observables depends on correlations, experimental resolution, and the relevant observation times.

## Equal A Priori Probability and Counting {#S2}

In a simple microcanonical model, accessible microstates have equal statistical weight. If there are $\Omega$ states, each has $p_i=1/\Omega$. The probability of a macrostate is proportional to its multiplicity $\Omega_M$, so distinct macrostates are generally not equally likely. For $N$ distinguishable independent two-state units with exactly $n$ units in the “up” state, choosing the $n$ positions gives

$$
\Omega(N,n)=\binom{N}{n}=\frac{N!}{n!(N-n)!}.
$$

For classical continuous degrees of freedom, one counts phase-space volume rather than discrete labels. Dimensional normalization and indistinguishability require care; the classical ideal gas has a Gibbs $N!$ correction. The equilibrium description rests on overwhelmingly numerous compatible states and its dynamical assumptions, not on microscopic motion coming to a stop.

## Example: Four Two-State Particles {#S3}

Four distinguishable spins can occupy $2^4=16$ configurations. Zero total magnetization requires exactly two spins up and two down; there are $\binom42=6$ such configurations. If the sixteen configurations are equiprobable, the probability of that macrostate is

$$
P(n=2)=\frac{\Omega(4,2)}{2^4}=\frac{6}{16}=0.375.
$$

The multiplicities for $n=0,1,2,3,4$ are $1,4,6,4,1$, and their sum is 16. The diagram enumerates the sixteen configurations and highlights the six with two up-spins. This is a model of noninteracting units with an appropriate ensemble; in a magnetic field, a canonical distribution at finite temperature generally assigns different weights to configurations of different energies.

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Compute $\Omega(5,2)$ for five distinguishable two-state units.
2. How many configurations exist for $N=4$, and what is the sum of their multiplicities?
3. Why are two macrostates not automatically equally probable?

### Solutions

1. $\binom52=10$.
2. $2^4=16$; $1+4+6+4+1=16$.
3. Their probabilities depend on the numbers of compatible microstates.

## Validity and Common Misconceptions {#S5}

The quantity $\Omega$ always refers to a precisely defined set of accessible states. Equal a priori probability on an isolated energy shell is a postulate of equilibrium statistical mechanics, not an assertion about every nonequilibrium preparation at every instant. Few-particle models show substantial fluctuations; large systems often concentrate statistical weight into a narrow range of macroscopic outcomes. Distinguishable model spins and identical quantum particles must not be confused. Classical points in phase space and quantum states are also distinct mathematical constructions. These qualifications prevent applying a simple combinatorial counting rule to a real gas without examining its assumptions.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Statistical mechanics relates a few macroscopic quantities to very many possible microstates. The next lesson turns this count into thermodynamic entropy.
