# M38-L4 – Global Radiation Budget and Climate Forcing {#L4}

**Course area:** M38 – Master: Geophysics, Atmospheric Physics and Climate Models

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M22: thermodynamics; M32: measurement methods; M38-L2: atmospheric flow · [Previous lesson](M38-L3.md).

**Learning objective:** Balance global top-of-atmosphere radiative fluxes, distinguishing positive net downward energy uptake from radiative equilibrium.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

A climate system receives shortwave solar radiation, reflects some of it, and emits longwave thermal radiation. At the top of the atmosphere (TOA), net flux diagnoses the energy budget but is not itself the near-surface temperature change. The atmosphere, surface and ocean can store and redistribute energy.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

We define $N$ positive downward: $N=(1-A)S_0/4-\mathrm{OLR}$. Here $A$ is planetary albedo, $S_0$ the incoming solar irradiance at the assumed orbit, and $\mathrm{OLR}$ the global mean outgoing longwave flux. The factor $1/4$ arises from the ratio of a sphere’s projected area to its surface area. At $N=0$ the mean TOA radiation budget is balanced; $N>0$ indicates net energy gain but not an instantaneous specified warming.

$$
N=\frac{(1-A)S_0}{4}-\mathrm{OLR}\qquad\text{(positive downward)}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

A constructed budget with $S_0=1360\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$, $A=0.30$ and $\mathrm{OLR}=237\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$ gives absorbed shortwave radiation of $238\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$ and $N=+1\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$. These are educational inputs, not current satellite observations. The graphic indicates the flux directions.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find $N$ if $\mathrm{OLR}=238\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$ with all other inputs fixed.
2. How does increasing albedo to $0.31$ change $N$, holding other inputs fixed?
3. Why is global mean solar irradiance $S_0/4$ rather than $S_0$?

### Solutions

1. $N=0$.
2. Absorption falls by $3.4\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$; $N=-2.4\,\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$.
3. Intercepted power scales with cross-sectional area, averaged over the full spherical surface.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

A global TOA budget suppresses spectral absorption, spatial/seasonal heterogeneity and cloud dynamics. Geostrophy models horizontal flow, not the global energy-budget term. Radiation transfer is addressed separately.
