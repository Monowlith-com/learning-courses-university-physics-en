# M41-L5 – Coherent X-ray Diffraction and Scattering Vectors {#L5}

**Course area:** M41 – Master: Photonics and Ultrafast Optics

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M41-L4; M35: crystal diffraction; M31: inverse problems.

**Learning objective:** Calculate the elastic scattering-vector magnitude and explain missing phase information in intensity measurements.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Coherent x-rays interfere after scattering from different locations in a specimen. Far-field diffraction patterns encode spatial frequencies of electron density. Detectors usually measure intensity rather than the complex field phase, so structural reconstruction poses an inverse problem.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

Elastic scattering preserves $|\mathbf k_i|=|\mathbf k_s|=2\pi/\lambda$, with scattering vector $\mathbf q=\mathbf k_s-\mathbf k_i$. For total scattering angle $2\theta$, $q=4\pi\sin\theta/\lambda$. In the kinematic single-scattering approximation, $I(\mathbf q)\propto|\widetilde\rho(\mathbf q)|^2$. The phase of $\widetilde\rho$ remains unmeasured, so coherent diffraction imaging needs further constraints and algorithms.

$$
q=|\mathbf k_s-\mathbf k_i|=\frac{4\pi}{\lambda}\sin\theta,\qquad I(\mathbf q)\propto|\widetilde\rho(\mathbf q)|^2.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

With $\lambda=0.100\,\mathrm{nm}$ and $\theta=15^\circ$, one finds $q\approx32.52\,\mathrm{nm^{-1}}$. The associated characteristic spatial scale $2\pi/q$ is $0.193\,\mathrm{nm}$. The figure includes incident/scattered ray geometry and a synthetic diffraction pattern; the latter is not a specimen reconstruction.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Compute $q$ for $\theta=30^\circ$ at the same wavelength.
2. Why do intensity detectors not directly reveal complex scattering fields?
3. How does scale $2\pi/q$ differ from guaranteed image resolution?

### Solutions

1. $q=62.83\,\mathrm{nm^{-1}}$.
2. The detector measures magnitude squared; complex phase is not separately recorded.
3. Practical resolution also depends on angular coverage, signal, noise, radiation damage and reconstruction assumptions.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The Fourier relationship models kinematic coherent scattering. Multiple scattering, partial coherence, detector gaps, finite samples and radiation damage can substantially alter inversion.
