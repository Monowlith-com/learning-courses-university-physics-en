# M19-L2 – Vacuum Technology, Residual Gas and Mean Free Path {#L2}

**Course area:** M19 – Bachelor: Advanced Experimental Methods and Research Practice

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** M5: kinetic gas theory; M16: metrology; M18: safety. [Previous lesson](M19-L1.md).

**Learning objective:** Model vacuum as a finite pressure regime, estimate a mean free path and identify measurement artefacts.

## Question and Model {#S1}

A laboratory vacuum is a reduced gas density, not literally empty space. Chamber pressure depends on temperature, pumping speed, conductance, leaks and gas desorption from surfaces. A residual-gas spectrum can help diagnose a system, but its intensities do not directly prove species concentrations without calibration. Pressure gauges rely on different operating principles; their readings are not interchangeable outside their stated ranges.

## Equations and Assumptions {#S2}

The ideal kinetic hard-sphere model predicts a mean free path
$$
\lambda=\frac{k_{\mathrm B}T}{\sqrt{2}\,\pi d^2p}.
$$
Here $T$ is absolute temperature, $d$ an effective collision diameter and $p$ pressure. Holding $T$ and $d$ fixed gives $\lambda\propto p^{-1}$. Comparing the path with chamber dimensions distinguishes flow regimes. This simplified relation does not automatically cover gas mixtures or strongly non-thermal conditions.

## Worked Example and Diagram {#S3}

Take $T=300\,\mathrm K$, $d=0.37\,\mathrm{nm}$ and $p=10^{-3}\,\mathrm{Pa}$. With $k_{\mathrm B}=1.380649\times10^{-23}\,\mathrm{J/K}$ the mean free path is about $6.81\,\mathrm m$. Ten times the pressure decreases it to about $0.681\,\mathrm m$. Both values can be compared with a chamber dimension of $0.1\,\mathrm m$ to assess the flow regime. The graphic shows the inverse relation on logarithmic scales; its points are model predictions, not observations.

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. How does $\lambda$ change if $p$ doubles at fixed $T$?
2. Estimate $\lambda$ at $p=10^{-2}\,\mathrm{Pa}$ using the example.
3. Why is a residual-gas peak not unambiguous evidence of one chemical species?

### Solutions

1. $\lambda$ is halved.
2. About $0.681\,\mathrm m$, because the pressure is tenfold.
3. Spectra may contain overlapping fragments, and sensitivity and calibration must be considered.

## Limits and Quality Control {#S5}

Real systems can be dominated by water vapour, oils, temperature variation, gauge sensitivity and surface outgassing. A mass-spectrum peak need not identify a unique molecule without further evidence and calibration. Glass under differential pressure, high voltage and cryogenic equipment require authorised laboratory procedures. This is a model lesson, not operating instructions for an unprotected system.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Pressure, temperature and collision model set the mean free path; spectra need separate calibration.
