# M21-L4 – Planetary Radiation Budget and Effective Temperature {#L4}

**Course area:** M21 – Bachelor: Geophysics and Climate

**Level:** Bachelor · Elective

**Prerequisites:** M5: thermodynamics; M7: radiation; M13: thermal spectra. [Previous lesson](M21-L3.md).

**Learning objective:** Set up a global radiation budget and compute the effective emission temperature.

## Question and Observation {#S1}

Earth receives solar radiation, reflects part of it and emits thermal energy to space. A balanced long-term global mean does not imply local equilibrium everywhere and at all times. Averaging over a spherical planet distributes incoming power over four times its illuminated cross-sectional area. Atmospheric downward thermal radiation is an additional process within the system, not an extra incoming solar source.

## Physical Model and Equations {#S2}

Let $S_0$ be the solar irradiance on a surface normal to sunlight, $\alpha$ the planetary albedo and $\sigma$ the Stefan–Boltzmann constant. A stationary global top-of-atmosphere model has
$$
F_{\mathrm{abs}}=\frac{S_0}{4}(1-\alpha),\qquad F_{\mathrm{out}}=\sigma T_e^4.
$$
Equating these fluxes gives
$$
T_e=\left[\frac{S_0(1-\alpha)}{4\sigma}\right]^{1/4}.
$$
The effective radiating temperature $T_e$ is not automatically the mean surface temperature. Greenhouse gases and clouds determine the altitudes and wavelengths from which radiation escapes to space.

## Worked Example and Teaching Diagram {#S3}

In a rounded teaching example, take $S_0=1360\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$ and $\alpha=0.30$. The average incoming solar flux is $340\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$, reflected flux $102\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$ and absorbed flux $238\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$. Hence $T_e\approx254.5\,\mathrm K$. ## Self-Assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Calculate $F_{\mathrm{abs}}$ for $S_0=1400\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$ and $\alpha=0.25$.
2. What happens to $T_e$ if absorbed flux increases by a factor of 16?
3. Why is $T_e$ not necessarily the average near-surface air temperature?

### Solutions

1. $F_{\mathrm{abs}}=1400\cdot0.75/4=262.5\,\mathrm{W/m^2}$.
2. Since $T_e\propto F_{\mathrm{abs}}^{1/4}$, the temperature doubles.
3. The level and spectrum of radiation emitted to space differ from surface conditions.

## Validity Limits and Quality {#S5}

This budget is globally averaged, stationary and spectrally simplified. Its values are rounded teaching inputs, not live observations. The outgoing thermal arrow represents radiation escaping at the top of the atmosphere, not necessarily photons travelling directly from the surface.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

A simple budget defines effective emission temperature; predicting surface temperature requires atmosphere–ocean physics.
