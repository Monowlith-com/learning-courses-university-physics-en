# M19-L6 – Research Data, Metadata and Reproducibility {#L6}

**Course area:** M19 – Bachelor: Advanced Experimental Methods and Research Practice

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** M18: laboratory notebooks; M17: computational environments; M19-L5: analysis plans. [Previous lesson](M19-L5.md).

**Learning objective:** Design a traceable dataset with provenance, units, checksums and access controls.

## Question and Model {#S1}

Research data are more than arrays of numbers. Reproducibility requires raw measurements, instrument configuration, calibration state, metadata, analysis code, parameters and traceable connections among them. A table lacking units or time references may not be reusable. FAIR principles aim at findability, accessibility under defined conditions, interoperability and reusability. FAIR does not mean all raw data must be openly released or retained forever regardless of context.

## Equations and Assumptions {#S2}

Each observation needs a stable raw format plus a field list documenting units, acquisition interval and origin. A manifest can record relative paths, MIME types, sizes, generating software and SHA-256 digests. A hash detects byte-level changes but establishes neither scientific accuracy nor authorship. Provenance links inputs, transformations and outputs. Retain the raw original and create a derived version instead of silently editing measurements in place.

$$
H(D)=\operatorname{SHA256}(D)
$$

## Worked Example and Diagram {#S3}

A small synthetic package could contain `raw/counts.csv` (time in seconds and counts), `meta/instrument.json` (instrument ID and calibration status), `analysis/fit.py` (software version and parameters) and `results/summary.csv` (net counts and uncertainty). A `manifest.json` links the four files; a README identifies them as a teaching example. The figure illustrates their dependencies. If exclusion rules change, retain a new, documented script and result version. Local checksum verification tests copy integrity, not the physical validity of the model.

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Name four data components to retain besides the measured numbers.
2. What does a file SHA-256 digest not guarantee?
3. Why does FAIR not automatically require open access?

### Solutions

1. For example units, calibration state, analysis code and parameters.
2. Neither scientific correctness nor authorship or reliable acquisition.
3. Accessibility may be governed by legitimate restrictions and legal limits.

## Limits and Quality Control {#S5}

Sensitive data may require restricted access, embargo or justified non-disclosure. Retention obligations depend on actual project, disciplinary and institutional rules; no universal period is asserted here. Check whether an independent reader could reconstruct the path from inputs to published tables. The steps in the diagram are teaching recommendations, not a universal DFG checklist.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Reproducibility relies on raw data, readable metadata, preserved provenance and documented processing.
