# M27-L5 – Quantum scattering and the scattering matrix {#L5}

**Course area:** M27 – Master: Advanced quantum theory

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M12: one-dimensional Schrödinger equation; M10: waves and boundary conditions. [Previous lesson](M27-L4.md).

**Learning objective:** Determine reflection and transmission amplitudes of a point barrier and distinguish an exact solution from the Born approximation.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Even a strongly localized interaction can reflect an extended matter wave. Scattering connects incoming and outgoing asymptotic states; measurable probabilities follow from squared amplitude magnitudes.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

A particle of energy $E=\hbar^2k^2/(2m)$ is incident from the left on $V(x)=g\delta(x)$. For $x<0$ write $\psi=e^{ikx}+re^{-ikx}$ and for $x>0$ write $\psi=te^{ikx}$. The wavefunction is continuous, but its derivative jumps by $\psi'(0^+)-\psi'(0^-)=2mg\psi(0)/\hbar^2$. This yields the dimensionless strength $\alpha=mg/(\hbar^2k)$, amplitudes $t=1/(1+i\alpha)$ and $r=-i\alpha/(1+i\alpha)$. Equal asymptotic speeds on either side imply $T+R=1$. In three dimensions, the scattering amplitude $f(\theta,\varphi)$ defines a differential cross section instead; the Born approximation is reliable only for sufficiently weak scattering.

$$
T=\frac{1}{1+\alpha^2},\qquad R=\frac{\alpha^2}{1+\alpha^2},\qquad \alpha=\frac{mg}{\hbar^2k},\qquad T+R=1.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

Let $\alpha=1$. Then $t=(1-i)/2$ and $r=-(1+i)/2$, giving $T=1/2$ and $R=1/2$. The figure compares both probabilities over a range of interaction strengths. Their sum always equals one because the model includes no absorption.

*Exact single-channel scattering conserves total probability flux.*

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What is the derivative jump condition at the delta potential?
2. Find $T$ when $\alpha=2$.
3. Why is a one-dimensional transmission coefficient insufficient for a general three-dimensional scattering experiment?

### Solutions

1. $\psi\prime(0^+)-\psi\prime(0^-)=2mg\psi(0)/\hbar^2$.
2. $T=1/(1+4)=0.2$ and $R=0.8$.
3. The scattered flux has an angular distribution, described for example by $\mathrm d\sigma/\mathrm d\Omega=|f|^2$.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The delta potential is an idealized one-dimensional interaction with a well-defined jump condition. Real three-dimensional scatterers require appropriate short-range models, boundary conditions and sometimes multiple channels. A unitary scattering matrix assumes that loss channels are absent.
