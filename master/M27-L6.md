# M27-L6 – Relativistic quantum mechanics and the Dirac equation {#L6}

**Course area:** M27 – Master: Advanced quantum theory

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M10: relativity and electrodynamics; M12: operators and wavefunctions. [Previous lesson](M27-L5.md).

**Learning objective:** Derive the relativistic dispersion relation and explain the role of anticommuting Dirac matrices in a linear Hamiltonian.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

The nonrelativistic Schrödinger equation uses quadratic kinetic energy. Special relativity instead imposes an energy-momentum relation with rest energy. Dirac linearized this relation at the cost of a multicomponent wavefunction.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

The four-momentum gives $E^2=p^2c^2+m^2c^4$. The Hamiltonian $H_D=c\boldsymbol\alpha\cdot\mathbf p+\beta mc^2$ satisfies $H_D^2=E^2$ as an operator identity when $\{\alpha_i,\alpha_j\}=2\delta_{ij}I$, $\{\alpha_i,\beta\}=0$ and $\beta^2=I$. Thus the $\alpha_i$ and $\beta$ are matrices and $\psi$ is a spinor. Expanding the positive energy branch at low momentum yields $E\simeq mc^2+p^2/(2m)-p^4/(8m^3c^2)+\cdots$. Negative-energy solutions also arise; their consistent interpretation belongs to quantum electrodynamics.

$$
H_D=c\boldsymbol\alpha\cdot\mathbf p+\beta mc^2,\qquad H_D^2=(p^2c^2+m^2c^4)I.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For an electron at $p=0$, the positive energy is $mc^2\approx0.511\,\mathrm{MeV}$. At $pc=0.3mc^2$, $E=mc^2\sqrt{1+0.3^2}\approx1.0440mc^2$. The kinetic increase is about $0.0440mc^2$, compared with the nonrelativistic estimate $p^2/(2m)=0.045mc^2$. The diagram compares both kinetic energies versus $p/(mc)$.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Which matrix relations eliminate unwanted mixed terms when squaring $H_D$?
2. Approximately what is the rest energy of an electron?
3. When does $p^2/(2m)$ become a poor kinetic-energy approximation?

### Solutions

1. In particular $\{\alpha_i,\beta\}=0$ and $\{\alpha_i,\alpha_j\}=2\delta_{ij}I$.
2. About $0.511\,\mathrm{MeV}$.
3. When $p$ becomes comparable to $mc$ or larger, relativistic corrections are significant.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

A one-particle Dirac approach does not describe all multiparticle and particle-creation processes. Consistent interactions with pair creation require quantum field theory. Negative energy branches must not be interpreted naively as an indefinitely stable one-particle energy reservoir.
