# M1-L6 – Experiment, Measurement Uncertainty and Research Practice {#L6}

**Course area:** M1 – Bachelor: Foundations of Physics

**Level:** Bachelor · Introductory

**Prerequisites:** Pendulum model, SI quantities and elementary statistics. [Previous lesson](M1-L5.md).

**Learning objective:** Plan a simple experiment, evaluate a measured result with uncertainty and document it transparently.

## From Prediction to a Series of Measurements {#S1}

A simple pendulum is to be used to estimate local gravitational acceleration. The goal is not to match an expected reference value as closely as possible, but to obtain a reproducible result with documented conditions. Use only a stable, approved suspension, a light bob and a clear swing area. Establish local safety rules before any laboratory experiment; work involving hazardous voltages, lasers, vacuum or radiation sources belongs only in appropriately supervised laboratories.

## Experimental Plan and Uncertainty {#S2}

Measure the effective length $\ell$ from the suspension point to the bob's centre of mass. Displace it only slightly and release it without an additional push. Time $N$ complete periods. Repeat independently $n$ times and also record initial conditions, instrument and resolution. From individual periods $T_i=t_i/N$, the mean and sample standard deviation are

$$
\bar T=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nT_i,
\qquad s_T=\sqrt{\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{i=1}^n(T_i-\bar T)^2}.
$$

For independent repetitions under comparable conditions, $u_A(\bar T)=s_T/\sqrt n$ is the standard uncertainty of the mean estimated from scatter. It does not cover constant systematic deviations. Instrument resolution, length measurement and model approximations must be assessed separately. The small-angle model gives

$$
g=\frac{4\pi^2\ell}{\bar T^2}.
$$

For small uncertainties and uncorrelated input estimates, linear uncertainty propagation gives

$$
\frac{u(g)}{g}\approx\sqrt{\left(\frac{u(\ell)}{\ell}\right)^2+4\left(\frac{u(\bar T)}{\bar T}\right)^2}.
$$

The factors follow from partial derivatives with respect to length and period. Correlations require additional covariance terms.

## Example with Synthetic Data {#S3}

The following numbers are constructed, not actual measurements. Let $\ell=0.500\,\mathrm m$, $u(\ell)=0.002\,\mathrm m$ and five total times for ten oscillations each be given:

| Repetition | Total time (s) |
|---|---:|
| 1 | 14.18 |
| 2 | 14.24 |
| 3 | 14.20 |
| 4 | 14.22 |
| 5 | 14.16 |

These give $\bar T=1.420\,\mathrm s$, $s_T\approx0.00316\,\mathrm s$ and $u_A(\bar T)\approx0.00141\,\mathrm s$. The model gives $g\approx9.789\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. If this example includes only the stated length uncertainty and statistical timing uncertainty, $u(g)\approx0.044\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$. Rounded: $g=(9.79\pm0.04)\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ (standard uncertainty, incomplete budget). A real experimental analysis would need to assess further contributions.

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. How does $u_A(\bar T)$ change if the number of independent repetitions is quadrupled while individual scatter remains the same?
2. What happens to $g$ if length is measured 1% too large but the period is correct?
3. What information belongs in a laboratory record besides the final result?

### Solutions

1. The standard uncertainty of the mean halves.
2. In the model used, $g$ is also overestimated by 1%.
3. Raw data, units, setup, instruments, experimental conditions, analysis method, uncertainty assumptions, discrepancies and traceable corrections.

## Sources of Error and Research Integrity {#S5}

Reaction times can partially cancel when timing but can also introduce bias. Large angles or an incorrectly determined effective length cause model or calibration discrepancies that repetitions do not remove. Measurements must not be adjusted retrospectively to make them fit. Exclusion criteria must be justified in advance; excluded data and reasons remain documented. Reproducibility requires a sufficiently complete description. The DFG code treats good research practice as an institutional and individual responsibility; it does not replace local safety instructions.

## Summary {#S6}

An experiment connects a model, measurement plan, raw data, traceable calculations and a justified uncertainty budget. The six foundational lessons thus provide a shared working basis for subsequent mathematical and physical modules.
