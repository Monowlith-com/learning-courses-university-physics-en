# M35-L8 – X-Ray Diffraction, Line Broadening and Scherrer Estimation {#L8}

**Course area:** M35 – Master: Advanced Solid-State Physics and Materials Science

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M14: lattices and Bragg reflections; M32: measurement methods.

**Learning objective:** Calculate an instrument-corrected Scherrer crystallite-size estimate and state its limitations.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

X-ray diffraction from periodic lattice planes produces Bragg peaks. Peak positions indicate lattice spacing, whereas peak widths reflect finite coherent domains, microstrain, instrumental resolution and profile shape. A crystallite is a coherently diffracting domain and need not equal a visible particle or aggregate. The Scherrer equation only provides a model-dependent size estimate.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

The common notation is $D=K_s\lambda/(\beta_s\cos\theta)$, where $K_s$ is a shape/profile factor, $\lambda$ the wavelength, $\theta$ half of the scattering angle $2\theta$, and $\beta_s$ the size-only breadth in radians along the $2\theta$ axis. For the explicitly assumed Gaussian profile convolution, $\beta_s=\sqrt{\beta_{\mathrm{obs}}^2-\beta_{\mathrm{inst}}^2}$; quadrature subtraction is not universal for Lorentzian or Voigt peaks. Microstrain is excluded from this illustration.

$$
D=\frac{K_s\lambda}{\beta_s\cos\theta},\qquad \beta_s=\sqrt{\beta_{\mathrm{obs}}^2-\beta_{\mathrm{inst}}^2}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

Use $\lambda=0.154\,\mathrm{nm}$, $K_s=0.9$, peak center $2\theta=40^\circ$, observed FWHM $0.30^\circ$, and instrumental FWHM $0.10^\circ$. Assuming Gaussian profiles gives $\beta_s=0.2828^\circ=4.9365\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{rad}$. At $\theta=20^\circ$, the estimated coherent-domain size is $D\approx29.9\,\mathrm{nm}$. The image shows a constructed peak and separate widths, not actual diffraction measurements.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What is $\theta$ when a reflection is at $2\theta=60^\circ$?
2. What happens to $D$ if only $\beta_s$ doubles?
3. Why is it inappropriate to call $D$ the particle diameter without qualification?

### Solutions

1. $\theta=30^\circ$.
2. The Scherrer estimate halves.
3. It characterizes coherent scattering domains and depends on line shape, microstrain and instrumental correction.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

Application requires a known wavelength, adequate peak profiles and a justified broadening model. Width corrections must use matching angle conventions and units; instrument-dominated peaks yield unstable estimates. Reliable materials characterization often requires multiple reflections and complementary techniques.

## Summary and outlook {#S6}

Under the assumed Gaussian model, the Scherrer equation estimates the size of coherently diffracting domains from an instrument-corrected X-ray peak width. Peak shape and possible microstrain limit this estimate; the crystallite size is not automatically the particle diameter. The module connects solid-state models with experimental materials characterization.
