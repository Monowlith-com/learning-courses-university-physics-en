# M26-L4 – Variational principles and the stationary-phase method {#L4}

**Course area:** M26 – Master: Advanced mathematical methods and model foundations

**Level:** Master · methodological specialisation

**Prerequisites:** M8: Fourier methods and integration; M9: action principles; M12: wavefunctions. [Previous lesson](M26-L3.md).

**Learning objective:** Find stationary points of a phase and evaluate the leading contribution to a one-dimensional oscillatory integral.

## Guiding question and observation {#S1}

Rapidly oscillating integrals often exhibit cancellation among contributions with different phases. Near points where the first derivative of the phase vanishes, local phase variation is slower and contributions may dominate. This connects variational principles to semiclassical approximations.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

Consider $I(\lambda)=\int a(x)e^{i\lambda S(x)}dx$ for large positive $\lambda$. Near an isolated interior point $x_0$ with $S\prime(x_0)=0$ and $S\prime\prime(x_0)\ne0$, expand $S$ to quadratic order. The leading contribution follows from an oscillatory Gaussian integral. Endpoints and multiple stationary points require further terms; degenerate points have different scaling laws.

$$
I(\lambda)\sim a(x_0)\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{\lambda\lvert S\prime\prime(x_0)\rvert}}\exp\!\left[i\lambda S(x_0)+\frac{i\pi}{4}\operatorname{sgn}S\prime\prime(x_0)\right].
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

Take $S(x)=x^2/2$, $a(x)=1$ over the whole real line, introducing a positive Gaussian regulator and then removing it. The only stationary point is $x_0=0$, with $S\prime\prime=1$. At $\lambda=4$ the regularized Fresnel integral tends to $I=e^{i\pi/4}\sqrt{\pi/2}$. Its magnitude is approximately $1.253$ and its phase is $\pi/4$. The figure separates the phase profile from its oscillatory real exponential.

![Parabolic phase above the rapidly oscillating real part of its complex exponential.](../../../../media/course/U_PHY_279_EN.svg)

*The phase varies slowly near its stationary point; away from it rapid oscillations tend to cancel.*

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Locate the stationary point of $S(x)=3(x-2)^2$.
2. How does the leading one-dimensional magnitude scale with $\lambda$?
3. Does the standard formula apply when $S\prime\prime(x_0)=0$?

### Solutions

1. $x_0=2$.
2. As $\lambda^{-1/2}$ for a nondegenerate interior point.
3. No; higher-order terms of the phase must be considered.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The formula requires a sufficiently smooth amplitude, a nondegenerate stationary point and suitable integration conditions. The whole-line Fresnel example with $a=1$ is a regulated limit.

## Summary and outlook {#S6}

Stationary phase provides a controlled approximation for oscillatory integrals. Multidimensional Gaussian integrals underpin many discretized path integrals. [Next lesson](M26-L5.md).
