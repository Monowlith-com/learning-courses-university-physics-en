# M28-L4 – Scaling, renormalization group, and critical fixed points {#L4}

**Course area:** M28 – Master: Nonequilibrium and many-body physics

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M28-L3: Landau–Ginzburg theory; M26: dimensionless couplings · [Previous lesson](M28-L3.md).

**Learning objective:** Distinguish relevant from irrelevant perturbations and derive a correlation-length exponent.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Close to a continuous phase transition, fluctuations appear on many length scales. A renormalization-group (RG) transformation removes short-scale information and describes the remaining physics using modified couplings. Critical systems are characterized by fixed points in this coupling space.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

Consider a small dimensionless distance $t$ from a fixed point. Under coarse-graining by a factor $b>1$, the linearized perturbation evolves as $t\prime=b^{y_t}t$. If $y_t>0$ it grows and is called relevant; if $y_t<0$ it is irrelevant. Near criticality the correlation length follows $\xi(t)\propto |t|^{-\nu}$. Under the same coarse-graining, the observed length becomes $\xi\prime=\xi/b$. Consistency of these laws requires $\nu=1/y_t$. This is a local scaling relation for one relevant variable, not a universal numerical value applying to all materials.

$$
t\prime=b^{y_t}t,\qquad \xi(t)\propto|t|^{-\nu},\qquad \nu=\frac{1}{y_t}\quad (y_t>0).
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

For the illustrative RG map with $b=2$ and $y_t=1$, $t=0.02$ becomes $t\prime=0.04$ after one step and $0.08$ after another. The corresponding exponent is $\nu=1$. The diagram shows a relevant direction in which flow arrows point away from $t=0$. It is a one-dimensional slice through a generally much larger coupling space.

![Renormalization-group flow with a fixed point at t equals zero and arrows on either side pointing away from it.](../../../../media/course/U_PHY_248_EN.svg)

*Relevant couplings grow away from a critical fixed point under coarse-graining.*

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What does a negative RG eigenvalue mean?
2. Find $\nu$ for $y_t=2$.
3. How does a small perturbation evolve after two steps with $b=2$ and $y_t=1$?

### Solutions

1. The perturbation shrinks under coarse-graining and is irrelevant.
2. $\nu=1/2$.
3. $t\to2t\to4t$.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The linearized RG map holds near a fixed point. Marginal variables with $y=0$, multiple relevant couplings, and anisotropic scaling require further analysis.
