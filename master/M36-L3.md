# M36-L3 – Scattering Kinematics, Pseudorapidity and Jet Separation {#L3}

**Course area:** M36 – Master: Particle Physics, Collision Experiments and Accelerators

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M30: scattering processes; M31: numerical reconstruction.

**Learning objective:** Compute angular separation in pseudorapidity–azimuth space for two reconstructed directions.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Collider detectors measure tracks and energy deposits from which objects such as jets are reconstructed. A direction relative to the beam axis is often expressed by $\eta=-\ln\tan(\theta/2)$. Azimuth is periodic. The quantity $\Delta R$ is a distance in $(\eta,\phi)$ coordinate space, not a literal three-dimensional opening angle or a complete jet algorithm.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

Wrap the azimuth difference onto $(-\pi,\pi]$. Then $\Delta\eta=\eta_1-\eta_2$ and $\Delta R=\sqrt{(\Delta\eta)^2+(\Delta\phi)^2}$. Geometrical proximity alone does not determine anti-$k_t$ clustering: the algorithm additionally uses transverse momenta and a specific iterative distance measure.

$$
\Delta R=\sqrt{(\eta_1-\eta_2)^2+(\Delta\phi)^2}.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

Take two constructed directions with $\eta_1=1.2$, $\eta_2=1.6$, $\phi_1=0.2$ and $\phi_2=0.5$ radians. The differences have magnitudes $|\Delta\eta|=0.4$ and $|\Delta\phi|=0.3$, so $\Delta R=0.5$. The diagram uses an illustrative radius $R_0=0.6$ to depict proximity.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find $\Delta R$ for $\Delta\eta=0.6$ and $\Delta\phi=0.8$.
2. Why must the difference between $179^\circ$ and $-179^\circ$ be wrapped?
3. Is $\Delta R<R_0$ a complete anti-$k_t$ algorithm?

### Solutions

1. $\Delta R=1$.
2. The short azimuth difference is $2^\circ$, not $358^\circ$.
3. No; transverse momenta and clustering steps are also required.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

Pseudorapidity approximates rapidity when particle mass is small compared with momentum. Detector acceptance, resolution, calibration and background affect reconstructed jets. The drawing illustrates a geometric criterion only.
