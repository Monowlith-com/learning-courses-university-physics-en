# M7-L8 – Lasers: Stimulated Emission and Cavity Threshold {#L8}

**Course area:** M7 – Bachelor: Optics

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** Photon energy, transitions, optical intensity and logarithms. [Previous lesson](M7-L7.md).

**Learning objective:** Explain population inversion and correctly calculate the gain threshold of a laser.

## Gain and Pumping {#S1}

A laser relies on stimulated emission in a suitable gain medium. An applied electromagnetic field can stimulate an excited transition to emit another photon in the same optical mode. Pumping creates the nonequilibrium populations from which optical amplification can arise. For the relevant transition an effective **population inversion** is needed: stimulated-emission gain must exceed absorption. A cavity repeatedly feeds a fraction of the light back through the medium, while a partially transmitting mirror extracts useful output. LASER stands for light amplification by stimulated emission of radiation. Not every real laser is perfectly monochromatic, perfectly coherent or restricted to one spatial or temporal mode.

## Round-Trip Gain and Loss {#S2}

For a linear cavity of physical length $L$, let $R_1$ and $R_2$ be the **intensity reflectances** of the two mirrors. Define small-signal gain $g$ and internal loss $\alpha$ as exponential intensity coefficients in $\mathrm{m^{-1}}$. During one round trip through the medium the light traverses $2L$, multiplying intensity by

$$
G_{\mathrm{rt}}=R_1R_2\exp\!\left[2(g-\alpha)L\right].
$$

At threshold gain exactly balances loss: $G_{\mathrm{rt}}=1$. Consequently 

$$
g_{\mathrm{th}}=\alpha+\frac{1}{2L}\ln\!\left(\frac{1}{R_1R_2}\right).
$$

This is **not** an electric-field amplitude formula; the factors change if one uses field gain or different loss conventions. Threshold means cancellation of linear small-signal losses, not unlimited exponential output-power growth.

## Worked Example: Threshold Gain {#S3}

Consider an ideal cavity with $L=0.300\,\mathrm m$, $R_1=0.990$, $R_2=0.900$, and $\alpha=0.0200\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$. Then $R_1R_2=0.891$, and

$$
g_{\mathrm{th}}=0.0200+\frac{\ln(1/0.891)}{0.600}\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}\approx0.212\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}.
$$

The required small-signal gain exceeds internal loss. Below threshold the round-trip multiplier is less than unity; above threshold the field may grow from noise until gain saturation and other nonlinear mechanisms limit it to a steady state. For a uniform cavity of index $n$, the approximate longitudinal mode spacing is $\Delta\nu\approx c/(2nL)$. The diagram distinguishes round-trip path, mirror loss and exponential gain.

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find $g_{\mathrm{th}}$ for $R_1=R_2=1$ and $\alpha=0.1\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$.
2. Which mirror quantity enters the stated threshold: amplitude reflectivity or intensity reflectance?
3. Calculate $\Delta\nu$ for $n=1$, $L=0.50\,\mathrm m$.

### Solutions

1. $g_{\mathrm{th}}=\alpha=0.1\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}$.
2. The stated $R_i$ are intensity reflectances.
3. $\Delta\nu=c/(2L)\approx300\,\mathrm{MHz}$.

## Lasers Beyond the Ideal Model {#S5}

The threshold expression assumes constant exponential coefficients based on the same intensity convention and a linear cavity. Distributed gain, modal profiles, scattering loss, facet effects, polarization coupling and spatial hole burning require more detailed models for complex lasers. Threshold alone says nothing about efficiency, beam quality, eye safety, linewidth or pulse duration. High optical power can permanently damage biological tissue; alignment and measurement therefore require appropriate laser-safety procedures and are not given as hands-on instructions here. 

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}
Lasers combine stimulated emission, optical feedback and a defined loss balance. Quantum and solid-state courses later extend these optical foundations.
