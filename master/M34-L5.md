# M34-L5 – Density Matrices, Decoherence and Quantum Channels {#L5}

**Course area:** M34 – Master: Quantum Information and Quantum Technologies

**Level:** Master · advanced specialization

**Prerequisites:** M34-L1: Bloch sphere; M34-L3: ideal gates.

**Learning objective:** Calculate the output state and measurement probabilities for an explicitly defined mixing channel.

## Guiding question and context {#S1}

Real qubits interact with their surroundings and control electronics. Pure vectors are insufficient when experiments involve mixtures or lost coherence. A density operator is Hermitian, positive semidefinite and has unit trace. A physical quantum channel is completely positive and trace preserving (CPTP), so it acts consistently even on entangled inputs.

## Physical model and derivation {#S2}

As a teaching example take $\mathcal E_p(\rho)=(1-p)\rho+pI/2$ with $0\le p\le1$. It mixes the input with the maximally mixed qubit and is not the only parameterization of a Pauli-error channel. For $\rho_0=|0\rangle\langle0|$ the output is $\operatorname{diag}(1-p/2,p/2)$. Every Bloch vector shrinks by $1-p$, reducing purity and measurement contrast.

$$
\mathcal E_p(\rho)=(1-p)\rho+\frac p2I,\qquad 0\le p\le1.
$$

## Worked example and teaching figure {#S3}

Start with $|0\rangle$ and set $p=0.2$. Then $P(0)=0.9$, $P(1)=0.1$, and the Bloch-vector length is 0.8. The figure compares the initial and shrunken z components and labels the measurement probabilities. The parameter value is illustrative, not a hardware benchmark.

## Self-check {#S4}

### Exercises

1. What is $P(1)$ at $p=0.4$ for input $|0\rangle$?
2. What is the Bloch length at $p=1$?
3. Why is positivity alone insufficient for arbitrary entangled inputs?

### Solutions

1. $0.2$.
2. $0$: the state is maximally mixed.
3. The channel extended with an untouched partner must still map density operators to positive operators.

## Limits of validity and quality {#S5}

The mixing channel does not represent every decoherence mechanism. Dephasing and amplitude damping affect states differently; one parameter is not a complete hardware characterization.
