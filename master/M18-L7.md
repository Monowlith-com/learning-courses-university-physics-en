# M18-L7 – Weak Signals, Background and Counting Statistics {#L7}

**Course area:** M18 – Bachelor: Physics Laboratory Practice

**Level:** Bachelor

**Prerequisites:** M13: statistical physics; M15: detectors; M16: uncertainty. [Previous lesson](M18-L6.md).

**Learning objective:** Model signal and background separately and estimate count uncertainty using Poisson statistics.

## Research Question and Model {#S1}

Many measurements detect discrete events such as photon arrivals, electronic pulses or accepted peaks. Independent rare events at a stationary rate can be approximated by a Poisson count $N$ in a fixed window, with $\mathbb E[N]=\operatorname{Var}(N)=\mu$. Dead time, time-varying rates and event correlations can invalidate that assumption. A weak-signal measurement records total counts, not just signal counts, because background persists even when the sought source is absent. A dedicated background observation is part of the model.

## Equations and Assumptions {#S2}

Let $N_t$ be a total count and $N_b$ an independent background count recorded for equal exposures. Estimate the net count as $N_s=N_t-N_b$. The Poisson variance of this difference is the sum of variances, approximately $N_t+N_b$. For unequal exposures, scale background by $\alpha=t_t/t_b$: $N_s=N_t-\alpha N_b$ with $u_s^2\approx N_t+\alpha^2N_b$. These standard relations describe counting uncertainty only; exposure calibration, dead-time correction and detector sensitivity require separate assessment.

$$
N_s=N_t-\alpha N_b,\qquad u_s=\sqrt{N_t+\alpha^2N_b}
$$

## Worked Example and Diagram {#S3}

Constructed example with equal exposures: the total region records 180 events and an independent background region records 40. The net count is $140$, with estimated standard uncertainty $\sqrt{180+40}\approx14.8$. The ratio $140/14.8\approx9.4$ is a useful descriptive scale, but without a specified testing framework is neither a complete p-value nor a universal discovery claim. Choosing the analysis window after inspecting data can inflate apparent evidence; criteria should be defined in advance.

## Self-assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find $N_s$ for $N_t=180$ and $N_b=40$ with equal exposures.
2. Estimate the Poisson standard uncertainty of the net count.
3. Why should a negative net estimate not be deleted automatically?

### Solutions

1. $N_s=140$.
2. $u_s=\sqrt{220}\approx14.8$.
3. It can be a valid consequence of background noise; automatically removing it would bias the analysis.

## Limits and Quality Control {#S5}

In spectra, background and peaks may overlap; a joint fit of background shape and signal profile may be preferable to a single-window subtraction. Examine residuals for temporal correlation, drift and systematic peak displacement. A negative net estimate does not imply a negative event count; it can result from background fluctuations when signal is weak. Do not replace it with zero without a documented analysis policy. Any real detector equipment and potentially hazardous sources require trained operators and local approval.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Background subtraction requires uncertainty from both counts and a transparent window definition.
