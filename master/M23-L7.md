# M23-L7 – Sensors, Noise, Filtering and Digitisation {#L7}

**Course area:** M23 – Bachelor: Applied Physics, Photonics and Nanotechnology

**Level:** Bachelor · Elective

**Prerequisites:** M16: uncertainty; M17: discrete data; M8: Fourier methods. [Previous lesson](M23-L6.md).

**Learning objective:** Describe a measurement signal chain and compute RC cutoff and alias frequencies.

## Question and Observation {#S1}

A sensor converts a physical quantity into voltage, current, frequency or counts. Before digitisation, analogue gain, impedance matching, interference suppression and bandwidth limitation may be necessary. An ADC does not reject high frequencies merely because its sample rate is low: signals outside the useful band can fold back into the sampled baseband. Noise statements should specify measurement bandwidth and, where appropriate, spectral density rather than an unexplained single noise-voltage number.

## Physical Model and Equations {#S2}

For uniform sampling at frequency $f_s$, Nyquist frequency is $f_{\mathrm N}=f_s/2$. A passive first-order RC low-pass with negligible output loading has transfer function $H(j\omega)=1/(1+j\omega RC)$ and magnitude

$$
f_{\mathrm N}=\frac{f_s}{2},\qquad |H(f)|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+(f/f_c)^2}},\qquad f_c=\frac{1}{2\pi RC}.
$$

At cutoff, the amplitude ratio is $1/\sqrt2$, or about $-3.01\,\mathrm{dB}$. An analogue antialiasing filter must attenuate unwanted input *before* sampling; a digital post-filter cannot undo aliasing already created. A useful signal band to $f_b$ requires evaluation of transition band, stopband and worst-case interfering content. Simply choosing $f_c<f_{\mathrm N}$ is not enough: one RC stage rolls off only 20 dB per decade. Broadband noise must be integrated over the effective noise bandwidth and interpreted using the chosen spectral-density convention.

## Worked Example and Teaching Diagram {#S3}

For $R=10\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$ and $C=10\,\mathrm{nF}$ the cutoff is $f_c=1/(2\pi RC)\approx1592\,\mathrm{Hz}$. Sampling at $f_s=10\,\mathrm{kHz}$ gives Nyquist frequency $5\,\mathrm{kHz}$. A poorly filtered 8 kHz input tone aliases to $|8-10|=2\,\mathrm{kHz}$; its surviving amplitude depends on analogue attenuation. The figure compares frequency response and folding.

## Self-Assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Calculate $f_c$ for $R=1\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$ and $C=100\,\mathrm{nF}$.
2. What is the alias frequency of a $7\,\mathrm{kHz}$ tone with $f_s=10\,\mathrm{kHz}$?
3. Why is a digital filter *after* conversion insufficient as the only alias protection?

### Solutions

1. $RC=10^{-4}\,\mathrm s$ and $f_c\approx1592\,\mathrm{Hz}$.
2. $f_{\mathrm{alias}}=|7-10|\,\mathrm{kHz}=3\,\mathrm{kHz}$.
3. The ADC has already superposed the frequencies in the sampled band; they cannot be unambiguously separated afterward.

## Validity Limits and Quality {#S5}

The RC model assumes ideal components and negligible loading. A real ADC front end depends on sampling architecture, input dynamics, stopband rejection and possibly internal oversampling. One manufacturer reference design demonstrates a particular implementation, not universal noise behaviour.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Measurement chains need appropriately calibrated conditioning before digitisation. Antialiasing is a system-level requirement on prefiltering and spectral management.
