# M22-L6 – Stochastic Reaction Networks and Molecular Noise {#L6}

**Course area:** M22 – Bachelor: Biophysics

**Level:** Bachelor · Elective

**Prerequisites:** M13: probability and ensembles; M17: numerical simulation. [Previous lesson](M22-L5.md).

**Learning objective:** Model a stochastic birth–death process, compute mean molecule counts and distinguish biological noise from mean dynamics.

## Question and Observation {#S1}

Some molecular species occur in only small copy numbers within an individual cell. Protein production and degradation proceed through discrete random reaction events. Molecule counts therefore fluctuate in time and from cell to cell even when the rates remain constant. A deterministic population mean is not itself a description of every observed cell.

## Physical Model and Equations {#S2}

In a linear birth–death model, molecules are created at a constant total rate $k_b$, and each existing molecule disappears independently at rate $\gamma$. The expectation satisfies

$$
\frac{d\langle n\rangle}{dt}=k_b-\gamma\langle n\rangle,\qquad \langle n(t)\rangle=\frac{k_b}{\gamma}\left(1-e^{-\gamma t}\right).
$$

The displayed time solution assumes $\langle n(0)\rangle=0$. For an arbitrary initial mean, $\langle n(t)\rangle=n_\infty+[\langle n(0)\rangle-n_\infty]e^{-\gamma t}$ where $n_\infty=k_b/\gamma$. The stationary distribution of this simplest model is Poisson: $P(n)=e^{-n_\infty}n_\infty^n/n!$, so $\operatorname{Var}(n)=n_\infty$. Feedback, bursts or state-dependent rates change that result.

## Worked Example and Teaching Diagram {#S3}

With $k_b=6\,\mathrm{min^{-1}}$, $\gamma=0.20\,\mathrm{min^{-1}}$ and an initially empty system, $n_\infty=30$ molecules and the relaxation time is $1/\gamma=5\,\mathrm{min}$. After five minutes the mean is $\langle n\rangle=30(1-e^{-1})\approx19.0$. The plot shows the expected mean, while the bars illustrate a theoretical stationary Poisson distribution rather than single-cell observations.

## Self-Assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Calculate the stationary mean for $k_b=4\,\mathrm{min^{-1}}$ and $\gamma=0.5\,\mathrm{min^{-1}}$.
2. For these rates, find the mean after $t=2\,\mathrm{min}$ starting from zero.
3. What is the coefficient of variation of a stationary Poisson process with mean eight?

### Solutions

1. $n_\infty=k_b/\gamma=8$.
2. $\langle n(2)\rangle=8(1-e^{-1})\approx5.06$.
3. $\mathrm{CV}=\sqrt{\mathrm{Var}(n)}/\langle n\rangle=1/\sqrt8\approx0.354$.

## Validity Limits and Quality {#S5}

The model assumes constant birth rate and independent linear removal. It omits regulation, cell division, extrinsic noise and multi-step reactions. Poisson statistics are a testable prediction of this model, not a universal law of gene expression.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Discrete chemical reactions generate intrinsic noise. Mean and variance measure different properties of the same system and should be considered together.
