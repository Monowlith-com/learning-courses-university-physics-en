# M21-L7 – Climate Archives, Proxy Data and Uncertainty {#L7}

**Course area:** M21 – Bachelor: Geophysics and Climate

**Level:** Bachelor · Elective

**Prerequisites:** M16: uncertainty analysis; M17: data models; M21-L4. [Previous lesson](M21-L6.md).

**Learning objective:** Critically interpret indirect climate reconstructions and correctly weight independent estimates.

## Question and Observation {#S1}

Before widespread instrumental climate observations, environmental information is recovered from archives such as ice cores, tree rings, corals and sediments. Measured isotope ratios, ring widths and chemical markers are proxies, not direct measurements of the desired temperature. Reconstruction needs chronology, calibration, a defensible physical or statistical response model and systematic-error assessment. Individual archives cover different locations and time spans.

## Physical Model and Equations {#S2}

A simple linear calibration model writes proxy observation $q=a+bT+\varepsilon$, with target temperature quantity $T$ and error term $\varepsilon$. If $a$ and nonzero $b$ are known, $\hat T=(q-a)/b$; with fixed calibration coefficients the proxy-only uncertainty is approximately $u_T=u_q/|b|$. Calibration and dating uncertainties must be added. For two or more **independent**, unbiased temperature estimates with standard uncertainties $u_i$, inverse-variance weighting gives
$$
\bar T=\frac{\sum_i T_i/u_i^2}{\sum_i1/u_i^2},\qquad u_{\bar T}=\left(\sum_i1/u_i^2\right)^{-1/2}.
$$
Correlated errors invalidate this simple uncertainty formula.

## Worked Example and Teaching Diagram {#S3}

Suppose two fictitious, assumed independent temperature anomalies are $T_1=1.2\pm0.3\,\mathrm K$ and $T_2=0.8\pm0.4\,\mathrm K$. Weights are $1/0.3^2$ and $1/0.4^2$, giving $\bar T=1.056\,\mathrm K$ and $u_{\bar T}=0.24\,\mathrm K$. The plot is an entirely synthetic proxy reconstruction with an illustrative uncertainty band. It is neither ice-core data nor a validated historic temperature estimate.

## Self-Assessment {#S4}

### Exercises

1. Find $\hat T$ for $q=5$, $a=1$, $b=2$ in appropriately compatible units.
2. What does inverse-variance weighting give for two independent estimates $0\pm1$ and $2\pm1$?
3. Why could common dating errors make the formally combined uncertainty overoptimistic?

### Solutions

1. $\hat T=(5-1)/2=2$ in the temperature unit defined by the calibration.
2. $\bar T=1$ and $u_{\bar T}=1/\sqrt2\approx0.707$.
3. Shared errors are correlated; averaging does not eliminate them as if they were independent.

## Validity Limits and Quality {#S5}

The synthetic markers do not describe actual climate history. Unbiasedness, normal errors, temporal stationarity, proxy independence and calibration validity require scrutiny. Chronology uncertainty acts along the horizontal time axis and is not captured by a vertical temperature-error band alone.

## Summary and Outlook {#S6}

Climate archives provide indirect evidence of past environments. Quantitative reconstructions must expose calibration and the full uncertainty structure.
